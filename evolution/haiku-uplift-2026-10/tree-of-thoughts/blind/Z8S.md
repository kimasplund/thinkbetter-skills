Go with the two-week option: cache the day's jobs read-only on the handset, let technicians fill in completion forms offline, and upload them when signal returns. Start the Harrowgate IT review this week, because that gate decides whether any of the options can land before winter.

**The deadline is set by the app review, not by development time.** Each new version of the app takes about eight weeks through Harrowgate's managed-catalogue review. Eleven weeks minus eight leaves three weeks: any build submitted after roughly week three cannot be live by storm season, whatever the engineering estimate. The two-week build submitted in week one or two is live around week ten or eleven, which is tight but workable. The five-week and nine-week options would finish development after the review window has closed. Ask Harrowgate's IT team now whether they will accept a pre-submission or expedite a security-only review, because that changes the picture for everything else.

**Why not the others:**

- **CRDT rebuild (9 weeks):** Merging changes automatically is the wrong model for the van stock count. Two handsets drawing from the same van while offline can both decrement the same part, and a CRDT will merge that into a number that is not a real stock level. Harrowgate bills cost centres from those counts, so the merge rule has to be a business decision made by the server, not a data-structure property. It also misses the review window.
- **Driftwell (3 weeks, about $2,000 a month):** Sending job data to a hosted sync service adds a sub-processor. Harrowgate's contract limits processing to the listed sub-processors, which today are only you and your hosting provider. Adding Driftwell needs a contract amendment and Harrowgate's agreement, which will not happen in 11 weeks. It also still needs the same server-side stock logic.
- **Replay queue (5 weeks):** The queue is the right idea, but the build misses the review window. Its extra scope (replaying every action against the API) is also not needed for the storm-season use case, where the only offline writes are completion forms.
- **Progressive web app (6 weeks):** The handsets have the browser disabled and only install apps from the managed catalogue, so a PWA cannot run on them at all.

**What the two-week build must get right:**

1. **Stock counts are decided on the server.** Forms queue on the device with the parts used. When they upload, the server applies the decrements in the order received and flags any part that goes negative or was double-drawn by the crew pair for dispatcher review. Do not show the handset a live stock figure while offline. Billing reads the reconciled server count.
2. **Expired sign-in tokens do not lose work.** Tokens last 12 hours and Harrowgate will not change that, so a technician will often come back online with an expired token. Keep queued forms encrypted on the device until the server accepts them, re-authenticate on reconnect, and retry the upload without asking the technician to re-enter anything.
3. **Cached jobs stay readable without a valid token.** Otherwise a technician who signs in before a two-day trip cannot open the day's jobs at all. This is a security decision that Harrowgate's security team has to sign off on, so raise it with them in the same review.
4. **Make the upload idempotent.** A form that uploads, loses its acknowledgement, and retries must not decrement stock twice.

**Assumptions:** I am assuming each app version starts its own eight-week review when submitted, that the two-week estimate includes the form queue and the upload retry described above, and that the cached-job and token behaviour is acceptable to Harrowgate's security team. If the review turns out to be shorter or can start on a pre-release, the replay queue becomes a reasonable second release. If Harrowgate says the eight weeks is firm and cannot be shortened, the two-week build is the only option that makes the storm-season date, so commit to it on Monday regardless.
