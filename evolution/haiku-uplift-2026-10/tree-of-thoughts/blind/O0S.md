Short answer: neither snowmaking nor the bike park works as currently written. The strongest path to still operating in ten years is to secure the upper-mountain lease first, then build a winter plan that fits the water licence. The bike park depends on a lease term you probably don't have, and snowmaking as costed exceeds your licence.

## What the numbers say

**Snowmaking (€2.1M, lower runs)**
- Consultant's water need: 9 ha × 4,500 m³/ha = **40,500 m³ per season**. Your licence allows **40,000 m³**. The proposal alone is already over the limit.
- The existing beginner guns used 31,000 m³ last winter. If they keep running alongside a lower-run system, total use would be roughly 70,000 m³, well above the licence.
- Funding is favourable: you own the lower land, so the regional fund's 50% applies. Net cost is about **€1.05M**.
- Action: check the 4,500 m³/ha figure against actual system specs and your own snowmaking data, and find out whether the licence can be increased or amended. Without that, a smaller footprint or consolidating the beginner guns into the new system would be needed to stay under 40,000 m³.

**Bike park (€850k, summer, upper slopes)**
- The upper slopes are leased, and the lease clause limits use to winter sports from 1 December to 15 April. A summer bike park appears to be outside the lease as written. This is the main blocker. Get the Grazing Commons Trust's written position before spending anything.
- The fund excludes leased assets unless at least 15 years remain on the lease. You renewed for ten years in 2025, so the bike park and the Summit refit are both excluded. Net cost would be the full €850k unless other finance is found.
- The "double annual revenue" claim is unverified. Ask for the model and its assumptions.

**Dry slope and tubing (€1.4M, lower land)**
- Owned land, so the 50% grant applies. Net cost about **€700k**.
- It does not depend on water or the leased land. It is the lowest-risk capital option on the table, though it may bring in fewer skiers than a snow-based offer.

**Upper-only operation with shuttle and €600k Summit refit**
- Excluded from the fund because the Summit chairlift and upper slopes are on the leased land with under 15 years remaining.
- The biggest hidden risk is the lease itself. A ten-year term from 2025 ends around 2035, which is inside your planning horizon. Without an extension, this option is not secure for ten years.

**Ardane Resorts approach**
- The €1.2M buyout grant must be repaid if the land is sold within ten years of 2019, so until about 2029. Any sale in that window triggers repayment. Waiting until after 2029 avoids that, but it would end community ownership, which is the thing you are trying to protect. Treat it as a fallback, not a plan.

## Recommended direction

1. **Make lease security the first priority.** Open talks with the Grazing Commons Trust on extending the lease to 15 years or more, and on whether any summer use is permitted. Every upper-mountain option, including the bike park, depends on this. The 15-year threshold also unlocks the regional fund for leased assets.
2. **Put snowmaking on the lower runs only at a water-compliant scale.** Plan within 40,000 m³ in total. Consolidate the beginner guns into the new system if that reduces water use, and verify the consultant's figure before committing €2.1M.
3. **Keep the dry slope as a low-risk hedge** on the owned lower land if snowmaking is delayed or scaled back.
4. **Hold the bike park until the lease allows summer use** and the revenue case has been independently checked.
5. **Keep Ardane as a fallback** and do not pursue it while the community can still fund a plan.

## Assumptions to confirm

- The 40,000 m³ licence is a seasonal cap that applies to the whole site, not just the new system.
- The 4,500 m³/ha figure is the consultant's estimate, not a measured value.
- The 2025 lease renewal kept the same 1 December to 15 April clause and did not extend the term.
- The regional fund's eligibility rules apply as described in the brief. Confirm the exact criteria with the fund before the November vote.
