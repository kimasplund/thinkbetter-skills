Short answer: back the dry slope and tubing lane on the lower land, make a lease extension your first priority, and hold off on snowmaking and on any sale. Here is why.

**What each option runs into**

- **Snowmaking (€2.1M)** hits the water licence before it hits the budget. The consultant's 4,500 m³ per hectare across 9 hectares comes to about 40,500 m³, which is more than the 40,000 m³ licence allows. The beginner guns already used 31,000 m³ last winter. Unless the consultant's figure is wrong or the licence can be changed, this cannot be built as proposed. Even at reduced coverage there is little headroom. It also doesn't fix the underlying problem: the lower runs had 18 skiable days, and extra snow only helps in winters cold enough to make it.
- **Summer bike park (€850k)** is blocked as proposed. The Trust's lease allows winter sports only from 1 December to 15 April, and a bike park on the upper slopes is a summer use. It would need the Trust's written consent to vary the lease. Because it is on leased land, it would also get no grant support, so the full €850k would be ours.
- **Dry slope and tubing lane (€1.4M)** is the only option that sits on land we own, is not limited by the lease or the water licence, and works in every season. It is grant-eligible at 50%, so our share would be about €700k. The main risk is demand, so test it before committing (for example, use the beginner-area visitor numbers and a season-pass pre-sale).
- **Upper-only operation with a shuttle (€600k chairlift refit)** is where the skiing is: the upper runs had 71 days. But the chairlift sits on leased land, so the grant excludes it and we would carry the full €600k. The lease also runs only to 2035, inside the ten-year window you are planning for.
- **Ardane Resorts' approach:** the €1.2M buyout grant must be repaid if the land is sold before 2029, so a sale now would cost at least that. It would also end community ownership, which may be the thing you most want to keep. Decline for now. If they come back, ask for terms in writing and factor in the repayment.

**The lever that matters most: the lease**

The grant excludes leased-land assets unless at least 15 years remain on the lease. The upper lease was renewed in 2025 for ten years, so roughly nine years remain. Extending it to at least 15 years from the application date would make the Summit chairlift refit grant-eligible, cutting our share from €600k to about €300k. It would also secure the upper skiing, which is the part of the business that has held up. Open that conversation with the Trust before November if you can.

**Recommended direction for the November vote**

1. Approve the dry slope and tubing lane on the lower land (€1.4M, about €700k after the grant), subject to confirming grant eligibility and a demand test.
2. Make a lease extension to 15 or more years a condition of any spending on the Summit chairlift or upper slopes.
3. Do not fund snowmaking until the water licence position is resolved and the consultant's water figure is corrected.
4. Ask the Trust whether it would consider a summer-use variation for a bike park. Treat that as an option, not a plan.
5. Decline the Ardane approach for now, and revisit after 2029.

**Trade-offs**

None of this restores winter numbers. Pass sales are falling because of snow, and the dry slope adds year-round income on the lower land without replacing the skiing. The upper ski area is what keeps the pass base alive, so protecting its lease is the single most important step.

**Assumptions**

- The Summit chairlift and the upper-slope facilities are on leased land, as the brief implies.
- The lower land's 9 hectares include the beginner area.
- The dry slope qualifies for the 50% grant, as the brief's wording suggests.
- The quoted costs are as given and I have not checked them independently.
