## Short answer

Don't sign with Hollis on the terms as offered, at least not in December. The deal runs into three hard limits you already described: your importer's cap, your café promise, and what your one machine can physically roast. Each of these can be worked on, but none of them can be waved away by signing. The best three-year growth path is to fix those limits first, then grow in steps.

## Why Hollis doesn't fit as offered

**Green coffee cap.** Roasting loses about 15% of the weight, so your current 21 tonnes roasted needs roughly 24.7 tonnes of green Alto Merida. Your importer caps you at 26 tonnes a year until 2028, which leaves about 1.3 tonnes of room. Hollis's 18 tonnes roasted needs about 21 more tonnes of green, so you'd be around 46 tonnes against a 26-tonne cap. Signing would commit you to supplying volume you're contractually not allowed to buy until 2028.

**Café promise.** Your café agreements say you won't sell the Alto Merida blend through supermarkets in the county. Hollis has six stores in your county. As written, that clause is breached on day one, and the café customers who matter most to your wholesale base would have a legitimate complaint.

**Roasting hours.** On one 5 kg machine, a 20-minute batch yields about 4.25 kg roasted. Between 8 am and 6 pm on weekdays you can run at most about 30 batches a day, which is roughly 127 kg roasted per day. Your current 21 tonnes is about 4,900 batches, or around 20 a day over 250 working days. That is already about two-thirds of the ceiling, and it ignores cleaning and cooling time. Hollis would add about 17 batches a day, taking you past 30. Even if the green cap and café promise were solved, the machine would not keep up.

**Organic certification.** The certificate covers your unit. Outsourcing the Hollis volume to a contract roaster would mean that roaster needs its own organic certification, and the product would need to be traceable through it.

## What each path can and can't do

| Path | Helps with the green cap | Helps with roasting hours | Notes |

| Hollis as offered | No | No | Breaches the importer cap and café promise |

## What I'd do

1. **This week, get a solicitor to read two contracts:** the importer contract (how the cap works, whether it can be amended, whether a second origin or a higher volume can be added) and the café agreements (the exact wording of the supermarket clause, and whether it covers the Hollis county stores specifically or only the Alto Merida name).

2. **Go back to Hollis with a version that fits.** Options: a different blend or single origin for the organic range, so the café clause and the importer cap don't apply; Alto Merida only through the 30-store chain outside your county, within the cap; or a start date later than February, tied to new roasting capacity. Ask for their deadline to be flexible given the constraints. If Faye wants to sign before the buyer leaves, a conditional letter of intent is safer than a binding contract you can't deliver.

3. **Ask the importer directly** whether they would raise the 26-tonne cap or agree to a second origin. A three-year growth plan with a clear volume forecast is a stronger request than a surprise order.

4. **Plan the roaster now, decide on it in the spring.** A 15 kg machine would roughly triple your daily output. It's worth the £68,000 once you have committed volume beyond what the 5 kg machine can handle, and the seven-month lead time means ordering by about the start of next year to avoid a capacity gap. Check the lease before ordering, since it may limit what equipment you can install and when you can run it.

5. **Grow in this order:** fix the contract limits, grow web subscriptions and existing wholesale within the cap, add a second origin or blend for new channels, then add the bigger roaster to support Hollis or a distributor. The own café and Varnland distributor can wait until the supply side is settled.

## Assumptions and uncertainties

- I've assumed your 21 tonnes is roasted weight sold, and that the 15% loss is weight lost from green to roasted.
- I've assumed a 5 kg batch and a 20-minute roast time apply to the 15 kg machine too, which would give roughly three times the output per batch.
- I've assumed about 250 working days a year and no other time constraints on the 5 kg machine.
- I don't know the exact wording of either contract, so the solicitor review is essential before you commit to anything.
- Hollis's price roughly doubling your profit is a strong reason to find a version of this deal that works. It's not a reason to sign one that breaks two contracts and can't be delivered on the current machine.
