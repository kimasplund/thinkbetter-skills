**Short answer:** Of the five proposals, the best chance of still being here in ten years is the one that keeps the upper winter operation healthy and adds a year-round use of the lower land you already own: the **dry slope and tubing lane**, paired with a firm commitment to the Summit chairlift. Snowmaking as costed does not work, the bike park is blocked by your lease, and the Ardane sale ends community ownership. The rest of this reply shows the arithmetic and the assumptions behind it.

**1. Snowmaking (€2.1M): the water numbers do not fit the licence**

- 9 ha × 4,500 m³/ha = **40,500 m³ per season**, which is already over your 40,000 m³ licence before anything else.
- The beginner-area guns used 31,000 m³ last winter. If those guns are separate from the 9 ha, total demand is about **71,500 m³**, roughly 79% over the licence.
- Grant effect: the lower land is owned, so this is eligible, and at 50% the net cost is about €1.05M. But the grant does not solve the water problem.
- Snowmaking only works if the licence is increased or demand is cut to about 8.9 ha or less with the beginner guns reduced. Ask the consultant to re-run the figures inside the licence before the board relies on them.

**2. Summer bike park (€850k): blocked by the lease, and not grant-eligible**

- The upper slopes are leased from the Grazing Commons Trust, and the lease limits use to winter sports between 1 December and 15 April. A summer bike park appears to breach that clause. This needs the Trust's written agreement to change the clause before anything else.
- The lease was renewed for ten years in 2025, so under 15 years remain. The tourism fund excludes it, so the full €850k falls on the community.
- The "double annual revenue" claim is not yet evidenced. Ask for the basis.

**3. Dry slope and tubing lane (€1.4M): the strongest fit**

- It sits on land you own, so it is grant-eligible. At 50%, the net cost is about **€700k**.
- It does not depend on snow or on the water licence.
- It gives the lower land a year-round use, which helps the lower-land asset that snowmaking cannot rescue.
- Assumption: the tubing lane is a meaningful revenue source. Get a demand estimate before committing.

**4. Upper-only operation with shuttle and €600k chairlift refit: a core you need anyway, but it gives up the lower land**

- The upper runs had 71 skiable days against 18 on the lower runs, so the upper slopes are where the business is. Their winter use is consistent with the lease.
- The Summit chairlift is on leased land, so the €600k refit is **not grant-eligible** and falls fully on the community. That cost is needed under almost any option that keeps the upper slopes open.
- Giving up the lower runs also gives up the owned asset that qualifies for grant. Shuttle costs are not in the brief and need estimating.
- Check whether selling or leasing the lower land would trigger the €1.2M buyout clawback (see point 5).

**5. Ardane Resorts approach: ends community ownership**

- The community bought the hill in 2019 with a €1.2M buyout grant repayable if the land is sold within ten years, so until 2029. A sale now would likely trigger repayment of up to €1.2M, unless the grant terms allow a pro-rata reduction. Check the grant agreement.
- A sale does not keep the club "still being here" in the sense the board means, so it should be rejected unless the other options are clearly failing.

**Recommended direction**

1. Keep the upper winter operation as the core. Plan the €600k chairlift refit now, and negotiate with the Trust for a lease extension to 15 or more years. That would restore grant eligibility for upper assets, and the lease ends around 2035, inside your ten-year horizon.
2. Pursue the dry slope and tubing lane on the owned lower land, net about €700k after grant.
3. Do not commit to snowmaking at the consultant's water figures. Re-run it within the licence first.
4. Only pursue the bike park if the Trust agrees to a summer use clause and the demand case is evidenced.
5. Reject the Ardane approach as a matter of course, given the clawback and loss of community ownership.

**Assumptions and questions to resolve before November**

- The 9 ha figure may include the beginner area. If so, the water gap is the total 71,500 m³ figure above.
- The tourism fund's exact rules on capital costs and how it treats snowmaking and dry slopes need confirming with the fund.
- The clawback's treatment of a partial land sale, and any pro-rata reduction, need checking against the 2019 grant agreement.
- The lease renewal and any clause change need written confirmation from the Grazing Commons Trust.
- The consultant's and the bike park's revenue claims need evidence.

This is a planning view based on the brief alone. Check the legal points (lease, clawback, grant eligibility) with a solicitor or the fund before the vote.
