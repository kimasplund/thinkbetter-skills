**Short answer:** don't sign with Hollis on the terms offered, and don't let the December deadline push you into it. Two of the constraints rule it out whatever machine you have. Growth is still possible, and the first steps are a conversation with your importer, a short read of the café contracts by a solicitor, and a decision on the roaster, not a rushed signature.

**Why the Hollis deal doesn't work as offered**

- **Importer cap.** Your Alto Merida green allocation is capped at 26 t a year. Your current 21 t of roasted coffee needs roughly 25 t of green (allowing for the 15% weight loss), so you have about 1 t of headroom. Hollis's 18 t would take you to roughly 46 t of green. Unless the importer raises the cap, you can't supply it.
- **Café promises.** Your supply agreements say you won't sell the Alto Merida blend through supermarkets in the county. Hollis's six county stores fall squarely inside that. Their other 24 stores may sit outside the wording, but a private-label organic range under the same blend name is a risk. Have a solicitor read the clause before you discuss terms.
- **Roasting hours.** Your 5 kg machine, running 8 am to 6 pm on weekdays, gives about 2,500 hours a year. At 20 minutes a batch and about 4.25 kg roasted per batch, the absolute ceiling is about 32 t a year, and you already use about two-thirds of that. Hollis would add roughly 1,400 hours, and loading, cooling and cleaning take time on top. The current machine is close to full.

**Recommended plan for the next three years**

1. **Now (October).** Tell Faye the Hollis terms can't be signed as written. Ask Hollis whether they'd take a different blend or origin, and whether a start date after the new roaster arrives would work. Ask the importer whether the 26 t cap can rise or whether a second origin is possible. Get a solicitor's view on the café clause.
2. **Decide on the 15 kg roaster by November.** Its seven-month lead time makes it the longest step, so the order date matters more than anything else. Any growth beyond roughly 24 t a year needs it, and it's the only path that takes you past the current ceiling without a third party. The risk is £68,000 sitting idle if the cap isn't resolved, so make the order conditional on the importer's answer if the supplier allows it.
3. **Grow the web shop and subscriptions in a non-Alto Merida blend.** Neither the importer cap nor the café promises restrict other coffees, so this is the growth you control most directly.
4. **Keep a contract roaster as a bridge.** If Hollis insists on February, a contract roaster can cover the start. Get their organic certification status and per-kg fee in writing. The fee will probably be the largest cut into the profit doubling Hollis's offer implies, so compare it against the figure before you rely on it.

**Assumptions I've made**

- The 5 kg machine takes 5 kg of green coffee per batch, and each batch loses 15%, leaving about 4.25 kg roasted.
- The 21 t figure is roasted coffee sold per year, so green use is about 25 t.
- Roughly 250 working days a year.
- A 15 kg batch also takes about 20 minutes. A larger drum may run longer, so confirm with the supplier.
- The importer cap covers green coffee you buy through that importer, not coffee from other sources.

**Remaining uncertainties**

- Whether the importer will raise the cap, and on what terms.
- Whether the café clause reaches a private-label range under a different name.
- Whether a contract roaster can meet your organic certification and volume needs.

**Bottom line:** Hollis at 18 t of Alto Merida isn't possible in February. The realistic route is a different blend or origin for Hollis, a 15 kg roaster ordered this autumn, and web growth in other coffees while the cap question is settled.
