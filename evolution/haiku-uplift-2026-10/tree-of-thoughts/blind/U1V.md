**Short answer:** Build the plan around the upper mountain, and make the first move a negotiation with the Grazing Commons Trust, not a vote on any of the five proposals as written. Two of the headline options fail on facts in your own brief. The most likely survivor, the bike park, can't proceed until the Trust agrees to change the lease.

## What breaks the two favourites

**Snowmaking on the lower runs doesn't fit your water licence.**
- 9 ha at about 4,500 m³/ha is roughly 40,500 m³ a season. That is already over the 40,000 m³ licence before anything else is counted.
- The existing guns used 31,000 m³ last winter. If they keep running, only about 9,000 m³ is left. That covers around 2 ha, not 9.
- Even if you got the water, the lower runs managed 18 skiable days. Snowmaking makes more of those days possible, but you'd be paying €2.1M to improve your weakest asset.
- The consultant's figures seem not to have been checked against the licence. Ask her directly, and ask what a licence increase would cost and how likely it is. That is a regulatory risk you can't price from here.

**The bike park is blocked by the lease, and by the grant rules.**
- The Trust lease limits you to winter sports between 1 December and 15 April. A summer bike park on the upper slopes, and summer use of the Summit chairlift, breaches that clause as it stands.
- The lease was renewed in 2025 for ten years, so about nine years remain. The tourism fund excludes leased land unless at least 15 years remain. So the upper-slope bike park would get no 50% grant. The full €850k would be yours to find, on land you could lose in 2035.
- "Double annual revenue" is the consultant's estimate. Ask for the assumptions: visitor numbers, spend per head, operating costs, and what comparable community bike parks actually earn. Revenue is not the same as surplus.

## Why the upper mountain is the core

- 71 skiable days against 18 is the clearest signal you have. Every serious option for the next ten years depends on the Summit chairlift and the upper slopes.
- That is why the upper-only operation is the sound base. It matches your reliable snow and cuts the cost of running terrain that is skiable about one winter day in five.
- Its problem is the same lease issue. A €600k chairlift refit on land with nine years left is a weak bet, and it gets no 50% grant. You're also planning for a ten-year horizon on a ten-year lease from 2025. The mountain you are relying on has to be secured past 2035 for any plan to hold.

## The lease negotiation unlocks the options

If the Trust agrees to both of the following, the picture changes:
1. Extending the lease so at least 15 years remain (a new term to 2041 or later). This makes the upper slopes eligible for the 50% tourism fund grant. The chairlift refit drops to about €300k net and a bike park to about €425k net.
2. Amending the seasonal clause to allow summer recreation. This legalises the bike park and makes the chairlift usable year-round.

The Trust may want something in return, such as a higher rent, a share of summer revenue, or environmental conditions. Find that out before the November vote. If the Trust refuses both, the bike park is off the table and the upper-only plan has to run on your own money and a lease that ends in 2035. You'd then need a fallback.

## The other two proposals

**Dry slope and tubing (€1.4M, lower land).**
- This is the one proposal that is clean on the facts. You own the land, so the 50% grant applies, bringing the net cost to about €700k. It needs no water and no Trust consent.
- The unknown is demand. No figure in your brief says what it would earn, and tubing and dry slopes often need staffing and insurance, and a steady stream of visitors. Ask for a business case. It is a reasonable use for the lower land if the numbers hold, and it gives you a second revenue stream that doesn't depend on the lease.
- It doesn't fix the core problem, which is falling season-pass sales for skiing.

**Selling to Ardane.**
- The €1.2M buyout grant must be repaid if the land is sold within ten years of the 2019 purchase, so before 2029. A sale agreed in the next couple of years would trigger that repayment, which comes off whatever price Ardane offers.
- That is a reason to be careful about timing and structure, not a reason to rule it out. Check whether the repayment applies to a sale of the company as well as the land, and what Ardane's price would be after repayment.
- Because it is only an informal approach, the board loses little by letting it sit while the lease talks play out. If the Trust talks fail and the money doesn't add up, it becomes the fallback, ideally after 2029.
- Be clear on what your members want. A community-owned hill sold to a resort company is a different outcome from a community-owned hill that survives.

## Recommended sequence

1. **Before the November vote:** open talks with the Trust on a 15+ year extension and a seasonal-clause amendment. Ask what they want in return.
2. **Ask the consultant to redo her work** with the water figures corrected and her revenue assumptions shown. Also ask the regional fund to confirm in writing how it will treat the leased land if the lease is extended.
3. **Put a conditional plan to the board:** the upper mountain as the base, with the chairlift refit and the bike park both dependent on the Trust's answer. Drop full-scale lower-run snowmaking. If you want snowmaking, scope a small one of about 2 ha near the lift base, within the licence headroom, and only if the guns' existing use can be cut.
4. **Cost the dry slope and tubing separately** as a lower-land project that is grant-eligible today.
5. **Park the Ardane approach** until you know the Trust's answer and the grant repayment position.

## What I'd still want to know

- Whether the Trust will engage, and on what terms. Everything above depends on this.
- Whether the beginner-area guns are on the lower runs or somewhere else, and whether their use can be reduced.
- The operating costs of the upper-only plan, and what the shuttle from the car park adds.
- Whether the lease extension would also have to cover the chairlift's end of life.
- How much cash the community can put in beyond the grants.

On the facts you have given me, the members' split between snowmaking and the bike park is a split between an option that doesn't fit the licence and one that needs the Trust's consent. Settle the lease first. The decision you then take in November will rest on terms you actually know.
