Short answer: don't sign the Hollis deal as offered, and don't let the December deadline push you into it. Here's why, and what I'd do instead.

**The Hollis volume doesn't fit your current constraints**

- **The importer cap.** Your importer caps Alto Merida green coffee at 26 t a year until 2028. You already buy about 21 t. Adding 18 t for Hollis means about 39 t of Alto Merida green, 13 t over the cap. A contract roaster doesn't get around this, because the cap is on the green coffee you buy, not on who roasts it.
- **The café agreements.** Your café supply agreements promise you won't sell Alto Merida through supermarkets in the county. Hollis has six stores in your county, so signing as offered would breach those agreements. That is a bigger risk than losing the deal. Check the exact wording before anything else. If it only covers county supermarkets, the out-of-county Hollis stores may be fine, but the importer cap still applies.
- **Roasting capacity.** One 5 kg machine running 20-minute batches inside an 8 am to 6 pm weekday window can do about 30 batches a day. That works out to roughly 37 t of green a year at five days a week for 50 weeks, before any allowance for loading, cooling or cleaning. You're at about 21 t now, so there is some headroom in theory and less in practice. 39 t doesn't fit even in theory.
- **Timing.** Hollis wants to start in February. A 15 kg roaster has a seven-month lead time, so even ordering now it won't arrive before about summer. A contract roaster could start sooner, but your organic certificate covers your unit only. Any other roaster would need its own organic certification before it could roast your organic product.

**What I'd do instead**

1. **Counter-offer Hollis.** Propose a volume that fits within the cap headroom, which is about 5 t above your current 21 t, and limit it to stores outside the county, or to a different organic blend or origin the importer cap doesn't cover. Private-label ranges often accept a different blend. A counter-offer before December is reasonable and tests how much Hollis really wants you. Don't commit to 18 t until the importer and café questions are answered.
2. **Talk to your importer now.** Ask about lifting the cap or adding a second origin, and what happens after 2028. Getting the answer in writing is the most valuable single step.
3. **Grow web subscriptions on a non-Alto Merida blend.** The cap covers only Alto Merida, so growth on other blends or origins isn't limited by it, and it uses the capacity you already have.
4. **Treat the 15 kg roaster as the real capacity answer.** If batch time stays similar, a 15 kg machine roughly triples output per hour (confirm with the manufacturer). Order for a summer start once the importer position is clear, and check that the lease allows a larger machine, with its extraction and noise.
5. **Defer your own café and the Varnland distributor.** Both need the same green supply under the same cap, and a café needs capital and attention you'll want for the roasting work. Revisit them once supply is settled.
6. **Use a contract roaster only as a bridge,** and only if it holds organic certification that covers the blend.

**Before deciding**

- Get the "double our profit" figure in pounds, after roasting loss, certification costs and any lost café margin.
- Ask Faye whether the December deadline is real. A buyer leaving isn't usually a reason to sign a three-year deal that breaks two existing contracts.

**Assumptions**

I've assumed the 21 t figure is all Alto Merida under the importer contract, the cap applies per year, the 20-minute batch time holds, and you roast about five days a week for 50 weeks. If any of these is off, the capacity figures change, but the cap and café-agreement conflicts do not.
