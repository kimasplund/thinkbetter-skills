**Short answer:** Neither of the two options your members are split over works as currently proposed. The direction I'd back is to protect the upper mountain, build the dry slope and tubing on the land you own, and open talks with the Grazing Commons Trust before the November vote. The bike park becomes a later option, contingent on those talks.

**1. Snowmaking on the lower runs fails on water.**
- At the consultant's own figure, 9 ha x 4,500 m³ = 40,500 m³ a season.
- Your licence allows 40,000 m³ in total, and the existing guns already used 31,000 m³ last winter. That leaves about 9,000 m³ of headroom, enough for roughly 2 ha, or about a fifth of the lower runs.
- So the €2.1M plan can't be run within the licence as it stands. A larger abstraction licence is uncertain and would need approval, and a drought or low-flow year could cut it further.
- Even with water, the lower runs sit at 650-900 m, where you only got 18 days. Snowmaking needs cold nights, so warming winters hurt it most.
- Unless the consultant shows a licence increase or a much lower water figure, I'd take this off the table.

**2. The bike park runs into your lease, and probably the funding rules.**
- The upper slopes are leased with a clause limiting you to winter sports between 1 December and 15 April. A summer bike park breaches that as written. The Trust would have to agree to amend it.
- The lease was renewed in 2025 for ten years, so about 9 years remain. The fund needs at least 15 years left on a lease for assets on leased land. As things stand, the bike park would probably get no 50% grant, so its real cost is the full €850k rather than about €425k.
- The "could double revenue" claim is the consultant's opinion. Ask for the assumptions: visitor numbers, ticket price, operating costs and season length.
- The bike park may still be a good idea. It depends on a lease deal with the Trust that you don't yet have.

**3. Selling to Ardane is the worst timing.**
- The €1.2M buyout grant must be repaid if the land is sold within ten years of 2019, so before 2029. That is roughly 2.5 years away, and the repayment would come out of any sale price.
- It would also give up community ownership, which is the thing you were set up to protect. I'd keep this as a fallback only. Check with a lawyer whether a share sale, as opposed to a land sale, triggers the clawback. I'm assuming it does until that's confirmed.

**4. The two options that hold up.**
- **Dry slope and tubing (€1.4M).** It sits on land you own, so it should qualify for the 50% grant, which brings your share to about €700k. It earns outside the ski season and doesn't depend on snow or on the Trust. It is the least exposed option.
- **Upper-only operation (€600k chairlift refit plus a shuttle).** The upper runs gave 71 skiable days against 18, so this is where your snow reliability is. The refit is on leased land and probably isn't grant-eligible with 9 years left, so check that. A shuttle also adds running costs and an access friction point, so test the numbers.

**Recommended plan**
1. Reject lower-run snowmaking as proposed.
2. Refocus winter operations on the upper runs and refit the Summit chairlift. Cost the shuttle and the lower-run closure honestly, including the effect on beginners and families, who may now have nowhere easy to learn. The existing beginner-area snow guns and their water may still serve that.
3. Apply to the tourism fund for the dry slope and tubing on the owned lower land.
4. Go to the Trust now and ask for two things. First, a lease extension to at least 15 years. Second, a variation allowing summer use. A 15-year term would also open the grant for the chairlift refit and the bike park. Make the bike park a stage-two decision once the Trust has agreed in writing.
5. Treat Ardane as a fallback only.

**Why this gives the best chance of being here in ten years:** it diversifies income across seasons, relies on the part of the hill that has reliable snow, puts grant money into assets you own, and avoids spending on a plan that the water licence and the lease clause both block. Pass-sales decline of a third in four years suggests you need non-snow revenue, not just more snow.

**Assumptions and things to verify before the vote**
- Whether the chairlift refit is needed in every scenario, and what the shuttle costs to run.
- The Trust's willingness to extend and to allow summer use, and any rent increase.
- Whether the tourism fund counts the dry slope and tubing as eligible, and its application deadlines.
- The consultant's demand and revenue assumptions for the dry slope, tubing and bike park.
- The exact clawback wording in the 2019 grant.
