**Short answer:** neither camp is right as the proposals stand. Snowmaking across the whole lower area doesn't fit your water licence. The bike park is the stronger direction, but it can't go ahead on your current lease. So the first job is a negotiation with the Grazing Commons Trust, and the November vote should be shaped around it.

## Why snowmaking fails as proposed

- Nine hectares at about 4,500 m³ per hectare is roughly 40,500 m³ a season. Your licence allows 40,000 m³ in total.
- The existing guns on the beginner area already used 31,000 m³. That leaves about 9,000 m³ of headroom, which covers roughly 2 hectares of new snowmaking, not 9.
- The consultant's figures therefore don't work unless the licence is raised substantially. That is a regulatory risk you can't control.
- The lower runs only had 18 skiable days, which suggests marginal temperatures as well as marginal snowfall. Even with water, you'd be making snow at the warm edge of what's viable.
- Net of the 50% grant (the lower land is yours, so it is eligible) the cost is about €1.05M. That is a lot to spend on a scheme that is water-capped to about a fifth of its intended area.

If members want snowmaking, the defensible version is a small, targeted scheme of about 2 hectares, such as a learner or return run, within the existing licence. Ask the consultant to re-cost that version. Do not approve the €2.1M scheme.

## Why the bike park is attractive but blocked

- It uses an asset you already have (the Summit chairlift) on the part of the hill that works, with 71 days a year. It also earns in the months when the lift is idle, and it doesn't depend on snow.
- Two lease problems stop it as things stand:
  1. **Use clause.** The lease limits you to winter sports between 1 December and 15 April. A summer bike park breaches that. You need a written variation from the Trust before spending anything.
  2. **Grant eligibility.** The tourism fund excludes assets on leased land unless at least 15 years remain. Your lease was renewed for ten years in 2025, so it runs to about 2035 and fewer than nine years remain. As things stand the bike park (and the chairlift refit) would get no 50% grant. With a grant the bike park would cost you about €425k. Without one it is the full €850k.
- The consultant's claim that it could "double annual revenue" needs checking. Ask for the visitor numbers, price and operating-cost assumptions behind it, and the revenue base it doubles. Summer revenue also doesn't fix falling season-pass sales, so treat it as diversification rather than a cure.

## The bigger issue: the lease

You asked about being here in ten years. The upper slopes, which carry your 71 days and the Summit chairlift, are leased until roughly 2035. Whatever the board picks, a lease that expires inside your planning window is the largest single risk to the plan. Securing a longer lease would:

- unlock the grant for any upper-slope project,
- make a bike park or a chairlift refit bankable,
- protect the only part of the hill that reliably has snow.

The ask to the Trust is one package: extend the term so that at least 15 years remain when you apply, which means expiry no earlier than about 2042, and vary the seasonal clause to allow summer recreation. Be ready to offer something in return, such as a rent uplift, a share of summer revenue, or trail maintenance obligations. It helps that they renewed on unchanged terms last year, but a variation is a different request, so don't assume a yes.

## The other options

- **Dry slope and tubing lane (€1.4M, about €700k after grant).** It is on land you own, so it is grant-eligible, and it needs no snow and no lease consent. It is a sensible fallback or later addition. But it is a lot of money for a modest draw, and it won't by itself win back season-pass holders.
- **Upper-only operation (€600k refit).** The logic is sound: drop the 18-day runs and concentrate on the 71-day runs. But the refit is on leased land, so it faces the same grant problem, and it makes you more dependent on the Trust than you are now. Treat it as the likely shape of the winter business, not as a separate bet.
- **Sale to Ardane Resorts.** Don't entertain it for now. The €1.2M buyout grant from 2019 must be repaid if the land is sold within ten years, so until 2029 a sale triggers that repayment, which cuts what the community would receive. Check with a lawyer how the clawback is worded, and whether it covers a sale of the company as well as of the land. The Trust's consent to any lease transfer would also be needed. Keep the approach as a fallback and at most a conversation, not a vote.

## Recommended direction

1. **Before the vote:** open talks with the Trust on a lease extension and a summer-use variation. Also ask the tourism fund to confirm in writing how it will treat the lease length, and ask the water regulator what a higher abstraction licence would take.
2. **In November, vote on a staged plan, not a single winner:**
   - Approve in principle the upper-slope strategy: a winter operation focused on the upper runs plus the summer bike park. Make the capital spend conditional on the lease being extended and varied.
   - Commission a rescoped snowmaking proposal for about 2 hectares within the existing licence.
   - Hold the dry slope and tubing lane in reserve as the grant-eligible option on owned land.
   - Leave the Ardane approach unanswered, or politely deferred.
3. **If the Trust refuses:** you'd be left with a business that depends on a lease that ends around 2035. In that case the plan changes: put effort into the owned lower land (small-scale snowmaking and the dry slope and tubing lane), keep the upper-only winter operation going while the lease lasts, and start a serious conversation with Ardane before 2035, when the repayment risk has also lapsed.

## What I'm least sure of

- Whether the 31,000 m³ already used counts against the same 40,000 m³ licence. I've assumed it does. If the beginner-area guns are licensed separately, the numbers change.
- The Trust's willingness to agree, and on what terms.
- The bike park's real revenue and operating costs, which are so far only the consultant's estimate.
- How the fund assesses the 15-year test, for example from the application date or the award date.

This is a decision you can make well in November, but only if the Trust conversation starts now.
