The direction I'd take is to build on the land you own: the dry slope and tubing lane as the core investment, with a smaller, water-limited snowmaking scope alongside it. Full snowmaking across the lower runs as costed doesn't fit your water licence, and the bike park can't be funded or run as proposed under the current lease.

**Why the pieces point that way**

- **The upper slopes are the weak point for the next ten years.** The Grazing Commons Trust lease was renewed for ten years in 2025, so it runs out around 2035, inside your horizon. Without a longer lease, the Summit chairlift and its 71 days of skiing could be lost before 2036. Starting lease talks is the most urgent step, whatever you choose.
- **The bike park needs the Trust's agreement.** The lease allows only winter sports between 1 December and 15 April, so a summer bike park falls outside it. With about ten years left, the lease is also below the 15-year threshold for regional funding, so the €850k would be entirely your cost. The consultant's doubled revenue figure doesn't account for either problem.
- **The upper-only plan has the same lease problem.** The €600k chairlift refit sits on leased land, so it gets no grant, and it depends on the same 2035 lease end. The shuttle from the car park also needs costing, and it isn't in the figures.
- **Snowmaking across the lower runs is limited by water.** Nine hectares at 4,500 m³ comes to about 40,500 m³ a season, which is already above your 40,000 m³ licence before the 31,000 m³ the beginner guns use. The headroom beyond current use is roughly 9,000 m³, about 2 hectares at the consultant's rate. The consultant's rate should be checked against your real figures, and a licence increase should be explored.
- **The dry slope and tubing lane is on land you own.** It should qualify for the 50% regional grant, roughly €700k net on €1.4M, subject to confirming eligibility. It works year-round, uses no water, and isn't limited to winter-only lease terms.
- **The Ardane approach would trigger repayment.** Selling before 2029 means repaying the €1.2M buyout grant, since the ten-year clock runs from the 2019 purchase. That would end community ownership and wouldn't keep the hill open for members.

**What I'd put to the November vote**

1. Commit to the dry slope and tubing lane on the lower land as the core project, after confirming grant eligibility.
2. Limit snowmaking to what the licence supports, roughly 2 hectares, unless a licence increase is obtainable.
3. Don't approve the bike park yet. Revisit it only if the Trust agrees to a summer use clause and a lease of at least 15 years, which would also make it grant-eligible.
4. Open lease talks with the Grazing Commons Trust now. A longer upper-slope lease matters more to your ten-year survival than any single facility.
5. Decline the Ardane approach for now, given the €1.2M repayment before 2029.

**Assumptions**

- The 31,000 m³ is current beginner-area use and is additional to what the new snowmaking would need.
- The consultant's 4,500 m³ per hectare is an estimate, so the real figure could move the water picture either way.
- Snowmaking and the dry slope both qualify for the regional grant on owned land, which should be confirmed with the fund.
