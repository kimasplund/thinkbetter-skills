Short answer: neither side of the split wins as the proposals stand. Two facts in your own paperwork break both. The direction with the best odds is an upper-mountain-centred plan, conditional on renegotiating the Trust lease, with the lower land used for something that doesn't need snow.

**Why snowmaking across the lower runs doesn't work as proposed**
- Water: 9 ha x 4,500 m³ = about 40,500 m³ a season. Your licence allows 40,000 m³ in total, and the beginner-area guns already used 31,000. That leaves about 9,000 m³ of headroom, enough for roughly 2 ha. Full coverage would need a licence increase of about 31,500 m³ (roughly 80% more than you hold), and nothing suggests that's obtainable. Ask the consultant how her plan fits the licence.
- Climate risk: the lower runs got 18 skiable days against 71 above. Spending €2.1M (about €1.05M net if funded) to make snow at the warmest, lowest elevation is a bet against the trend. Snowmaking also needs cold nights, not just water.
- Funding: the lower land is yours, so it would be eligible. That is its one strength.

**Why the bike park doesn't work as proposed**
- Lease clause: the Trust lease limits you to winter sports between 1 December and 15 April. A summer bike park breaches it. You need a written amendment first.
- Funding: the lease was renewed for ten years in 2025, so about 9 years remain. The fund requires at least 15 years on leased land. As things stand, the bike park (and the €600k chairlift refit) would get no 50% grant. That turns €850k into €850k, not about €425k.
- "Double annual revenue" is the consultant's estimate. Ask what it assumes: visitor numbers, price, season length, and whether it counts revenue or profit. Doubling a shrinking base is not the same as covering the gap.

**What I'd recommend**
1. Go to the Grazing Commons Trust now. Ask for (a) an extension taking the lease to at least 15 years remaining, and (b) a summer-use amendment. Everything on the upper slopes depends on this, including the chairlift, which is your 71-day asset. If the Trust says no, the bike park and the upper-only refit are both in doubt, and you need to know that before November.
2. If the Trust agrees, the bike park becomes roughly €425k net of grant, which is far more attractive, and it uses an asset you already have. Check the revenue claim first.
3. Use the owned lower land for something that doesn't need snow. The dry slope and tubing lane (€1.4M, about €700k net) is fundable and is not tied to the lease. Tubing can also run in winter when snow allows. Ask for evidence of demand before committing.
4. Consider limited snowmaking only within the water headroom (about 2 ha, perhaps linking beginner terrain), rather than the full €2.1M scheme.
5. Treat "upper-only" as the fallback, not the plan. Dropping the lower runs shrinks the offering while pass sales are already falling, and the refit has the same lease and funding problem.
6. Don't take the Ardane approach forward now. Selling the land before 2029 (within ten years of the 2019 purchase) triggers repayment of the €1.2M buyout grant, and you'd lose community control. Revisit it only if the other options fail, ideally after the clawback window.

**Before the vote, check**
- Current finances: how many months can you run at the present loss? Funding takes time and you must be able to carry the match funding (the other 50%).
- Whether the fund has a deadline or budget cap, and whether bike park and tubing both count as "eligible projects".
- Whether the water licence can be varied, and what it would cost.
- Whether the Trust is open to the lease change, and what it wants in return.

Assumptions: I took the lease as having about 9 years left as of late 2026, and assumed the grant covers 50% of eligible capital costs only. If any of that is different, the numbers change but the order of steps doesn't.

Confidence: high on the water and lease arithmetic, moderate on the overall recommendation, since revenue forecasts and the Trust's attitude are unknown.
