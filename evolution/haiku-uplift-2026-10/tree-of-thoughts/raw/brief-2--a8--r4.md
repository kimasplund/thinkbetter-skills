**Short answer:** Neither of the two camps' proposals works as written. Snowmaking on the lower runs fails on water. The bike park fails on the lease. The option most likely to still be here in ten years is an upper-mountain-centred plan: refit the Summit chairlift and make the upper slopes the core. Then make a lease renegotiation with the Grazing Commons Trust the gate for the bike park. Put the dry slope and tubing on your own lower land as a later, grant-funded add-on.

**Why snowmaking is out (unless the licence changes)**

- 9 ha x 4,500 m³ = about 40,500 m³ a season. That is more than your whole 40,000 m³ licence.
- The beginner-area guns already use 31,000 m³, so the headroom is about 9,000 m³. That covers roughly 2 ha of lower runs, not 9.
- To do what the consultant's figures assume, you would need a licence of roughly 71,500 m³. Her €2.1M estimate is not a plan until the regulator says that is possible.
- Even with water, the lower runs (650-900 m) managed 18 skiable days. Snowmaking needs cold nights, and those are the marginal ones. You would be spending €2.1M, or about €1.05M after the grant, on your least reliable ground.

**Why the bike park cannot be voted through as it stands**

- Your upper-slope lease limits you to winter sports between 1 December and 15 April. A summer bike park is outside that clause, so it needs a written variation from the Trust. Check this first, because the consultant's revenue forecast assumes it.
- The grant excludes assets on leased land unless at least 15 years remain. You renewed for ten years in 2025, so about nine remain. The bike park trails and the chairlift work would likely get no 50% grant, which makes the real cost €850k, not about €425k.
- A bike park built on land you hold for nine more years carries renewal risk. That risk sits at the edge of your ten-year horizon.
- "Double annual revenue" is the consultant's estimate. I would ask her for the assumptions behind it, such as visitor numbers, price and operating costs, before relying on it.

The idea itself is sound. It gives the chairlift summer income and diversifies away from snow. It just depends on the Trust.

**The other options**

- **Upper-only operation (€600k chairlift refit):** This matches the 71 vs 18 skiable days, so it concentrates spending where the snow is. It is also the base for the bike park, because both rely on the Summit chairlift. The refit is on leased land, so assume no grant until the lease term is fixed.
- **Dry slope and tubing (€1.4M):** This is on land you own, so it should qualify for the 50% grant, a net of about €700k. It works in any weather and gives the lower land a use after the ski runs go. I would phase it, not make it the lead, because I have seen no demand figures for it.
- **Sale to Ardane:** This is the weakest answer to "still being here". It ends community ownership. If the land is sold before 2029, the €1.2M buyout grant has to be repaid, which would lower what you net. Keep it as a fallback, not a plan, and check whether a sale of the company triggers the repayment.

**What I would put to the November vote**

1. Approve the Summit chairlift refit and an upper-focused winter operation, with the lower runs managed down.
2. Ask the Trust, before the vote if possible, for two things in writing: a variation allowing summer use of the upper slopes, and a lease extension so that at least 15 years remain (for example a new 20-year term). That would also unlock the 50% grant on upper-slope assets. The bike park would be approved subject to both.
3. Apply for the grant on the dry slope and tubing now, since the land is yours, and commit only if the grant is awarded and a demand check supports it.
4. Drop the snowmaking proposal unless the regulator confirms a licence increase of roughly 30,000 m³ or more.

**If the Trust says no to summer use or a longer lease:** the bike park cannot proceed. The plan then becomes the leaner winter operation on the upper runs plus the dry slope and tubing on your land. That is smaller and less exciting, but it avoids spending money on assets you cannot protect.

**Confidence and what I assumed:** I am fairly confident about the rejection of snowmaking (the water arithmetic is simple) and about the bike park's lease and grant problems. I am less sure about the final ranking, because I do not know your operating costs, your cash reserves, or how the Trust is likely to respond. I also assumed that "ten years" in the lease runs from 2025, that the refit would count as an asset on leased land, and that the buyout repayment applies to a sale of the land only. Please check each with the fund administrator and your solicitor.
