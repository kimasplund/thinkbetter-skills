**Assessment**

- Snowmaking as costed is not permitted. The consultant's water figure alone exceeds the licence, and the beginner guns already use most of the rest. Any version would need a smaller area or a licence increase, and it would still leave you dependent on the 18 skiable days on the lower runs.
- The bike park is blocked by the lease clause unless the Trust agrees to change it, and it gets no grant. Its doubling of revenue is the consultant's estimate and I have not been able to check it.
- The Ardane sale would trigger repayment of the €1.2M grant, so it is effectively off the table for now.
- Any plan built on the upper slopes is limited by the lease. It runs out about ten years from now, so the club may not be able to plan beyond it without a longer lease.

**Recommendation**

The best chance of still being here in ten years is the dry slope and tubing lane on the lower land. It is the only proposal that is grant-eligible, uses land you own, needs little water, and earns money outside the snow season, which reduces how much the 18-day result matters. Keep the Summit lift running for winter, since the lease allows that. Before the November vote, I would:

1. Ask the Grazing Commons Trust whether it will extend the lease to at least 15 years and whether it will allow summer use. This affects every upper-slope option, including the bike park.
2. Ask the regional tourism fund to confirm in writing whether the bike park and the chairlift refit are eligible.
3. Scale snowmaking to what the licence allows, and check whether the beginner guns sit inside the lower runs, before committing the €2.1M.

**Assumptions**

- The 9 hectares and the 4,500 m³/ha figure apply to the whole lower area, and the 31,000 m³ is the same water source under the same licence.
- The fund's rules work as your summary describes. I have not seen the fund's documents.
- The consultant's revenue claim and the Ardane terms are unverified.
