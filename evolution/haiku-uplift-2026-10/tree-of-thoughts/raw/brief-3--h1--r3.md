**Short answer:** Don't sign the Hollis deal as offered. As written, it can't be delivered and it would breach two of your existing commitments. But don't walk away either. A reshaped, smaller version could be your best growth move, and you have time to negotiate it.

I've assumed your 21 tonnes is roasted weight and that "5 kg" is green input per batch. Please check both.

## Why the deal doesn't work as offered

**1. The green coffee cap blocks it.**
- 18 t of roasted coffee at 15% weight loss needs about 21.2 t of green (18 / 0.85).
- Your current 21 t roasted already uses about 24.7 t green. The importer cap is 26 t.
- You have only about 1.3 t of green headroom. Hollis plus your current business needs about 46 t, which is roughly 20 t over the cap, and the cap runs until 2028. The deal runs three years, so it would also outlast the cap.
- A contract roaster does not fix this. The limit is on the green coffee, not on roasting capacity.

**2. It breaks your café agreements.**
- You promised the cafés you wouldn't sell Alto Merida through supermarkets in the county, and Hollis has six stores in your county.
- Signing as offered would put you in breach with 40 wholesale customers, who are most of your business. Losing even a few would wipe out the gain.

**3. Your machine can't do it.**
- Total roasted output would be about 39 t, which is about 46 t of green.
- At 3 batches an hour, 10 hours a day, 5 days a week, the theoretical maximum is about 37 t of green a year. Real life with cleaning, cooling and downtime is well below that.
- You would be over capacity before counting the lease hours.
- The 15 kg roaster costs £68,000 and takes seven months to arrive. Ordered today, it would land around May, after the February start. Larger roasting would also need your landlord's agreement and may breach the lease terms on noise and hours.

**4. Organic certification.** The certificate covers your unit. A contract roaster would have to be certified organic themselves, and you would have to bring them into your certification chain. That takes time and adds cost. Hollis wants an organic range, so a lapse here would put the whole deal at risk.

**5. The "doubles our profit" figure.** It probably ignores the extra green coffee, the roaster or contract roasting costs, packaging, and the risk to your café accounts. Please redo it with those included.

## What I'd do

**Step 1: Resolve the two hard blockers before anything else.**
- Ask your importer whether the cap can be raised, or whether you can buy more green coffee under a different blend or origin. Ask now, because it decides everything else.
- Read your café agreements closely, with a solicitor if possible. Check exactly what is restricted: the Alto Merida blend, which supermarkets, which area. Consider asking the cafés for a waiver, perhaps in return for something such as better pricing or exclusivity on a premium blend.

**Step 2: Counter-offer Hollis with a different product.**
- Offer a distinct private-label organic blend that is not Alto Merida, using a different origin that isn't under the importer cap. That also avoids the café promise, since it is not the restricted blend. Check the wording of the clause on this point.
- Start smaller, for example 5 to 6 t a year, which fits within your current machine and lease hours. Add stages that scale up if it sells, with volume tied to when you get a bigger roaster or extra green coffee.
- Ask for a longer start date, such as spring or summer, to fit the equipment lead time.
- Ask for a minimum purchase commitment, a price review linked to green coffee costs, and an exit if you can't source supply.

**Step 3: Deal with the December deadline.**
- The buyer leaving is Hollis's pressure, not a reason to take a bad contract. If the buyer moves on, you can still pitch their successor, so a deal that is delayed is not necessarily a deal lost.
- Tell Faye that a signed contract you can't fulfil is worse than none. Late or short deliveries to a supermarket cost you the account and your reputation, and the penalties usually fall on you.
- Aim to give Hollis a clear answer by early November, with your counter-proposal.

**Step 4: Grow in parallel with the low-risk options.**
- Push web subscriptions now. It is the best use of the spare capacity you have, with better margins than wholesale.
- Look at Varnland later, once you know your green coffee position.
- Hold off on the café and the 15 kg roaster until the sourcing and contract questions are settled.

## Questions to answer first
1. Can the importer raise the cap, and at what price?
2. Exactly what do the café agreements restrict, and would the cafés waive it?
3. Will Hollis accept a different blend and a smaller starting volume?
4. What are your real margins on Hollis volume once all costs are included?
5. What does your lease say about adding equipment?

If the answers to 1 and 2 are both no and Hollis insists on Alto Merida at 18 t, then decline politely and keep the relationship open for later.
