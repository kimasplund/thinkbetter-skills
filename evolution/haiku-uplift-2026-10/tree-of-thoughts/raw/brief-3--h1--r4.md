**Short answer:** Don't sign the Hollis deal as offered. It can't be delivered, and it would breach your café agreements. The best growth path over three years is a smaller, reshaped Hollis deal (or none), plus higher-margin growth you can already supply.

**Why the deal fails on your own numbers**

Assumptions: the 5 kg is green coffee per batch, and the 18 tonnes and your 21 tonnes are roasted weights.

1. **Green coffee cap.** At 15% weight loss, 21 t roasted needs about 24.7 t green. Your importer caps Alto Merida at 26 t until 2028. You have roughly 1.3 t of green headroom, which is about 1.1 t roasted. Hollis wants 18 t, which needs about 21 t more green. That is roughly 45.9 t in total, against a cap of 26 t. Even if the café business shrank, you couldn't supply both.
2. **Roasting capacity.** Your 5 kg machine does 3 batches an hour. Weekdays 8 to 6 gives at most about 30 batches a day, or about 37 t of green a year if you never stop. Real-world uptime is lower, and you're already using about two-thirds of it. 46 t of green is out of reach without a second machine or shifts you aren't allowed.
3. **Café agreements.** You promised the cafés you wouldn't sell Alto Merida through supermarkets in the county. Hollis has six stores in your county. Signing would breach those agreements and put your core wholesale customers at risk. Check the exact wording with a solicitor. It may be a hard bar on the county stores, not on the whole chain.
4. **The workarounds don't work in time.**
   - A 15 kg roaster costs £68,000 and takes seven months to arrive. Ordered today it arrives around May, so Hollis volume from February would go unmet. The lease hours also still limit you, and a bigger machine doesn't remove the green coffee cap.
   - A contract roaster must itself be certified organic. Your certificate covers only your unit. The green coffee cap still applies, and you'd lose control of the blend's quality.

"Roughly double our profit" is only true if you can deliver the volume. A missed supply commitment to a supermarket, or losing the cafés, would cost far more.

**What I'd do instead**

1. **Counter-offer to Hollis before December** (the buyer's deadline gives you leverage to ask, not a reason to sign):
   - A smaller starting volume that fits inside your headroom (about 1 t a year), growing in step with capacity.
   - Exclude the six county stores, or get the cafés' written consent.
   - Offer a different blend or origin for the private label. That avoids the Alto Merida cap and the exclusivity clause. It still needs organic-certified green coffee and a capacity check.
   - Add volume flexibility, a ramp-up period and a way out if supply fails.
   If Hollis refuses all of this, walk away. Faye's urgency is real, but a deal you can't deliver is worse than no deal.
2. **Grow web subscriptions.** They need no new machine, they carry the best margins, and they use your existing blend. Your green coffee headroom is small, so margin per kilo matters more than volume.
3. **Talk to the cafés and the importer.** Renegotiate the importer cap beyond 2028 or add a second origin. Update your café agreements so your next growth step isn't blocked.
4. **Defer the 15 kg roaster, the café and the Varnland distributor** until the constraints are fixed. A new machine only makes sense once you have the green coffee supply, a lease that allows enough hours (or a new unit) and a confirmed customer. The café is capital-heavy and pulls you away from roasting. A distributor in Varnland adds volume you can't supply and may raise export and organic-certification questions.
