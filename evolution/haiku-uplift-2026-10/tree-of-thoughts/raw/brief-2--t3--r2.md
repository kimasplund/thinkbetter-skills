The best chance of still being here in ten years is to bet on the upper slopes for winter, use the lower land you own for year-round income, and fix the lease before committing money. Of the five proposals, snowmaking and the bike park don't work as currently framed, and a sale to Ardane is the one option that ends the community's control of the hill.

**Why the proposals don't fit as written**

- **Snowmaking (€2.1M):** The consultant's 4,500 m³ per hectare across 9 hectares comes to about 40,500 m³ a season. Your licence allows 40,000, and the beginner guns alone used 31,000 last winter. Even if those guns were retired, the plan sits at or over the cap. It also serves the lower runs, which had only 18 skiable days last winter. Treat the water figure as a hard limit to verify with the consultant before anything else.
- **Bike park (€850k):** The upper-slope lease allows winter sports only, between 1 December and 15 April. A summer bike park on the Summit chairlift and new upper trails would break that clause unless the Grazing Commons Trust agrees in writing. The lease also has about ten years left, so the 50% grant doesn't cover upper-slope assets. The consultant's claim that revenue could double needs checking against both of these constraints.
- **Upper-only operation with shuttle and €600k chairlift refit:** This is the operation that actually earned the 71 days. The chairlift sits on leased land with under 15 years remaining, so the refit is not grant-eligible and the community pays the full €600k. The main risk is that the lease itself runs out around 2035, which falls just before your ten-year horizon.
- **Sale to Ardane Resorts:** The €1.2M buyout grant has to be repaid if the land is sold within ten years of the 2019 purchase, so a sale before 2029 would trigger repayment. A sale also ends community ownership, so it is off the table unless members choose that outcome deliberately.
- **Dry slope and tubing lane (€1.4M):** This sits on the lower land you own, so it is grant-eligible at 50%, roughly €700k net. It needs no water and no lease change, and it earns in months when there is no snow. The revenue potential is unknown, so get a demand estimate before committing.

**Recommended direction**

1. **Renegotiate the upper-slope lease now.** If the Trust extends it to at least 15 years remaining, the upper slopes and the Summit chairlift become grant-eligible. That would cut the €600k refit to roughly €300k of community money, and it removes the risk of the lease expiring around 2035.
2. **Ask the Trust whether summer use of the upper slopes can be permitted.** The bike park only works if this is granted, so treat it as a conditional option, not a plan.
3. **Build the dry slope and tubing lane on the lower land** as the lower area's year-round use, using the 50% grant.
4. **Keep the chairlift refit in the plan,** timed after the lease talks if possible, since the grant depends on them.

**Assumptions**

- The 4,500 m³ per hectare figure is the consultant's estimate, not a measured value.
- The 40,000 m³ licence total may or may not include the beginner guns' usage. The conclusion holds either way, because the 9-hectare plan alone is at the cap.
- Grant eligibility follows the rules as you described them, and the lease terms are as stated in your renewal.
- The bike park revenue and dry slope demand figures are unverified.
