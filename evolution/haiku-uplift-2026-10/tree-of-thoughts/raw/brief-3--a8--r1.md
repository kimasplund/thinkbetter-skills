**Short answer:** Don't sign the Hollis deal as offered. It can't be delivered with what you have, and it breaks a promise you've already made to your cafés. But don't walk away either. Counter with a smaller, phased deal on a different organic blend, and tell Faye you need to answer well before December.

(I've assumed the 21 t is roasted weight, that the 5 kg machine takes a 5 kg green charge, and that you roast about 245 weekdays a year. If the 21 t is green weight, the conclusions below get slightly looser but don't change.)

## Why the offer doesn't work as written

**1. The green coffee cap rules it out.**
- 21 t roasted at 15% weight loss needs about 24.7 t of green coffee. The cap is 26 t, so you're already using about 95% of it and have roughly 1.3 t of green headroom.
- Hollis's 18 t of roasted blend needs about 21.2 t of green. That leaves about 4.8 t of green (about 4 t roasted) for everyone else.
- Signing means your 40 cafés and web shop would shrink from about 21 t to about 4 t of Alto Merida until the cap changes. Your profit would not double. You'd swap higher-margin wholesale and web sales for supermarket volume, so the real gain is much smaller and could be negative. Check this with your numbers before anything else.

**2. The roaster can't physically do it.**
- Your current volume is about 4,900 batches a year, around 20 a day. Ten hours a day at 20 minutes per batch gives a ceiling of 30 batches a day, or about 7,500 a year, and that's with zero downtime for loading, cooling, cleaning and maintenance.
- Hollis alone would add about 4,200 batches. Together that's about 37 a day, which is over the ceiling. Realistically, running at about 80% of the ceiling, you could add only about 1,000 batches a year, which is roughly 4.5 t of roasted coffee.
- A 15 kg roaster would triple capacity, but with a seven-month lead time, an order placed in November arrives around June. It won't be there for a February start.

**3. The café agreements.** You promised your cafés you wouldn't sell the Alto Merida blend through supermarkets in the county, and six Hollis stores are in the county. Signing as offered puts you in breach with the customers who make up most of your business. Losing even a few of them would hurt more than Hollis would pay.

**4. Organic certification.** Your certificate covers your unit only. A contract roaster would need its own organic certification, and you'd have to add it to your scope with your certifier. Hollis's organic range would not tolerate a gap here. Do this early because certifier turnaround can be slow.

**5. The contract roaster doesn't fix the real problem.** It solves machine capacity and nothing else. It does not raise the green cap, and it does not resolve the café promise. It also thins your margin and adds the certification step. It's useful only as a bridge.

## What I'd do

**Counter-offer to Hollis before December:**
- **A different blend, not Alto Merida.** Source a second organic origin (from a different importer or an uncapped contract) and make a distinct Hollis blend, for example a private-label blend under their name. The importer cap only covers Alto Merida, and the café promise is only about the Alto Merida blend, so this resolves both at once. Have a solicitor read the exact wording of the café agreements first.
- **A phased volume.** Start with about 4 to 5 t in the first year, which your machine can take on without hurting café supply. Step up to 10 to 18 t once the larger roaster is installed and running, in year two. Ask for a volume range rather than a fixed 18 t, with a minimum you can always meet.
- **Price that covers the roaster.** Ask for a price or a term (three years is fine, perhaps with a volume commitment) that lets you recover the £68,000 machine. At the Hollis margin, work out how many tonnes it takes to pay it back.
- **A clause for the new origin.** Allow a delivery start of a few months after February for the new blend, with the first deliveries small.

If the buyer won't accept a different blend or phasing, the deal as offered isn't worth the risk. You'd be betting your café base on one account whose buyer is leaving in December.

**Meanwhile, order the 15 kg roaster now (or within weeks)** if the Hollis deal looks likely. Check first that the lease and your landlord allow it (power or gas, ventilation, noise) and that the hours restriction still works with a larger, faster machine. If the numbers don't clear, use a certified contract roaster for the first Hollis volumes instead.

## Other growth paths, in order

1. **Web subscriptions.** The best fit. Highest margin per kg, no extra capital, small volume, and no clash with the cafés. Growth here is limited by the 1.3 t headroom under the Alto Merida cap, so ask your importer now whether the cap can rise. Offering a second origin to subscribers also helps.
2. **Hollis on a second origin, phased as above.** This is the main growth route, but only on terms you can actually deliver.
3. **Varnland distributor.** It would use Alto Merida volume you don't have and adds export and organic-labelling paperwork. Revisit once the cap is lifted or the roaster is running.
4. **Your own café.** The least attractive. It needs capital and your attention, and it would use up what you need for the roaster. Return to it later.

## What to find out in the next two weeks
- Your margin per kg on cafés and on web sales versus the Hollis price. This tells you whether any Alto Merida should go to Hollis at all.
- Whether your importer will raise the cap, or supply a second organic origin, and on what terms.
- Whether the café agreements allow any exemption, and whether they define "supermarket" and "the blend" in a way a distinct Hollis product would escape. If you want to be safe, ask a few key cafés for a written waiver.
- Whether your landlord and certifier would allow a bigger roaster or a contract roaster.
- Whether Hollis will say yes to phasing. Ask their buyer now, not in December.

If Faye wants to move fast, the fast version is to send the counter-offer this month and sign by early December on revised terms. That meets the buyer's timing without taking on terms you can't keep.
