**Short answer:** don't sign the Hollis deal as offered. It can't be delivered as written, and parts of it would put your café contracts and importer contract at risk. But don't walk away either. A smaller, differently shaped Hollis deal is the best growth route for the next three years, and I'd put it to them well before December.

## Why the deal as offered doesn't work

I've assumed the 5 kg is green coffee per batch, and that the 15% weight loss means roasted output is 85% of green input.

**1. Your green coffee cap blocks it.**
- You sell about 21 t roasted a year, which needs about 24.7 t of green coffee.
- The importer caps Alto Merida at 26 t a year until 2028, so you have only about 1.3 t of headroom.
- Hollis wants 18 t roasted, which is about 21.2 t green. Your total would be roughly 46 t green against a 26 t cap.
- The only way to supply the Alto Merida blend to Hollis is to renegotiate the cap, or to sacrifice roughly 85% of your existing café and web business.

**2. Your roasting hours don't cover it.**
- Weekdays 8 am to 6 pm gives about 50 roasting hours a week. At three batches an hour that is about 2,500 hours a year at best, once you allow for holidays.
- Your current volume is about 4,940 batches, or about 1,650 hours, so you are already around two-thirds full.
- Hollis adds about 4,235 batches, or about 1,410 hours. The total is about 3,060 hours against roughly 2,500 available, and that is before cooling gaps, cleaning and sampling.
- You can't roast at weekends or at night, so the machine itself is the limit.

**3. The timing is impossible.** A 15 kg roaster has a seven-month lead time. Ordered today, it arrives in May at the earliest, and you would still have to install and commission it. February is out of reach by any route that involves buying a machine.

**4. The Alto Merida blend can't go to Hollis in your county.** Your café agreements promise you won't sell the Alto Merida blend through supermarkets in the county. Six of Hollis's 30 stores are in your county. A private-label version of the same blend would very likely be read as a breach, and the cafés are your core customers. Please have a solicitor read the exact wording, because "private label" and "the Alto Merida blend" may or may not be caught. Don't rely on my reading.

**5. A contract roaster only helps if it is certified organic.** Your certificate covers your unit and not anyone else's. The contract roaster would need its own organic certification and a proper chain-of-custody arrangement. That is feasible but adds cost, which eats into the margin that makes the deal look attractive. It also does nothing about the green coffee cap.

## What I'd do instead

**Counter-offer Hollis with a deal you can deliver.**
- **A different organic blend, not Alto Merida.** A new origin from a different importer is not subject to the 26 t cap and avoids the café exclusivity promise.
- **Stores outside your county only.** That means 24 of the 30 stores and keeps clear of the exclusivity promise. Alternatively, ask the cafés for a written waiver, but that is a harder conversation.
- **Start with about 6 to 8 t roasted a year, rising later.** Your spare machine time is roughly 850 hours, which is about 10 t of roasted output at the very most. Planning for 6 to 8 t leaves slack for breakdowns and peak weeks.
- **Start date of April or May rather than February**, with volume stepping up in years two and three once more capacity is in place.
- **Ask Hollis for a volume floor and a price review clause**, so you aren't investing in capacity for a customer who can cut orders. A three-year commitment from them should come with take-or-pay or minimum volumes.

If their buyer won't move on volume, shape or timing, it is better to lose the deal than to sign something you would have to breach. Faye's urgency about the December departure is understandable, but a signed contract you can't fulfil is worse than no contract. A new buyer may well offer similar terms, and Hollis's range will still need a roaster next year.

## Growing capacity for years two and three

- **Order the 15 kg roaster in the next few weeks** if the Hollis counter-offer or other growth looks likely. It would arrive around next May or June. Before ordering, check with your landlord that the lease, power supply, ventilation and emissions rules allow it. The weekday 8 to 6 hours still apply, but a 15 kg machine roughly triples output per hour, which solves the hours problem.
- **Check your organic certificate** covers the new machine, and tell your certifier before it is installed.
- **Negotiate the green coffee cap with your importer** for 2027 and 2028, but only if you can secure the cafés' consent. Don't build on it until it's agreed in writing.

## The other options you listed

- **Web subscriptions:** worth doing in parallel. They are low capital, they use the same Alto Merida allocation at higher margin per kilo, and they don't depend on anyone else's decision. Keep it modest because of the 1.3 t headroom.
- **Own café:** skip it for now. It needs capital and management time, adds little volume, and distracts from the roasting capacity problem.
- **Varnland distributor:** a good second step once the new roaster is in, but today it runs into the same green coffee cap and the same hours limit. Revisit in 2027.
- **Contract roaster:** useful as a stopgap for overflow only if it is certified organic, but I'd treat it as a bridge until your own larger machine arrives, not a long-term plan.

- Whether Hollis's price still doubles your profit at 6 to 8 t of a new blend, once you include a new origin's cost and any certification fees. Run those numbers before you reply.
- The exact wording of your café agreements and your lease. Both need a solicitor's eye before you commit to anything.
