## Short answer

Don't sign with Hollis as offered. Two of your existing constraints block it before the machine question even comes up: the importer's 26-tonne green cap and the promise in your café agreements not to sell the Alto Merida blend through county supermarkets. The best growth path is to use your existing headroom first (web subscriptions), fix the green-coffee cap before scaling, and only then decide on a bigger roaster.

## Assumptions

I've assumed the 21 tonnes and the 26-tonne cap are both green (unroasted) weight, that a batch is 5 kg green in and about 4.25 kg roasted out, and that you can run 10 hours a day on 5 weekdays for about 48 weeks a year. If the 21 tonnes is roasted weight, the numbers are tighter (see the note below).

## The numbers

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Machine time now (21 t green, 5 kg per 20-minute batch) | about 1,400 hours |
| Machine time available (10 h x 5 days x 48 weeks) | about 2,400 hours |
| Green coffee needed with Hollis (21 t + 18 t) | 39 t |
| Importer cap until 2028 | 26 t |
| Headroom under the cap | about 5 t |
| Machine time needed with Hollis (39 t) | about 2,600 hours, which does not fit in 2,400 |

- **The green cap is the binding constraint.** Hollis would take you to 39 t against a 26 t cap. Even with a bigger roaster or a contract roaster, you cannot legally buy that green coffee from this importer.
- **The machine is the second constraint.** Your current machine has spare capacity for about 5 t of extra green coffee, which is the same as the cap headroom. Hollis's 18 t would not fit in the machine's hours alone.
- **If the 21 t is roasted weight,** green volume is about 24.7 t. That leaves only about 1.3 t of cap headroom and about 1,650 machine hours in use.

## The café agreements

Your café contracts promise not to sell the Alto Merida blend through supermarkets in the county. Hollis has six stores in your county, so a private-label Alto Merida sale there is a direct conflict. Selling the same coffee under a different name may not get you out of it, because the clause is about the blend. A solicitor should read the exact wording before you go near Hollis.

## The certificate

Your organic certificate covers your unit. A contract roaster would need its own organic certification and traceability for the Hollis volume, and a bigger roaster in your unit may need the certifier told about it. Ask the certifier before you decide.

## What to do before December

1. **Ask the importer** whether the 26 t cap can be raised for 2027 and 2028, and on what notice.
2. **Ask Hollis** whether the deal can start later or be scaled down. A smaller first year (about 5 t, within your headroom) would be the only version that fits today. Also ask for an exit clause, a volume commitment, price terms and who carries the organic certificate. Tell them you need to check existing contracts first.
3. **Get a solicitor** to read the café agreements and the importer contract. If the café clause is an obstacle, a written waiver from the cafés is possible but needs care.
4. **Ask the organic certifier** about contract roasting and whether a larger machine changes the certificate scope.
5. **Model the profit honestly.** "Roughly double" profit needs to account for the lower Hollis price, the cost of buying 18 t of green coffee up front, extra machine or roaster costs, and the risk of losing some of the 40 cafés if the Hollis sale becomes known.
