**Short answer: don't sign with Hollis as offered.** On the figures you've given, the deal breaks your importer cap and probably your café agreements, and no roaster you can get in time covers it. Growth is still possible, but the binding constraint is green coffee supply, not roasting capacity.

## Why Hollis doesn't work as offered

**1. The importer cap is the hard limit.** Your Alto Merida green supply is capped at 26 t a year until 2028. Hollis's 18 t of roasted blend needs roughly 21 t of green (18 ÷ 0.85). Your current 21 t of roasted coffee needs roughly 25 t of green. That is about 46 t of green against a 26 t cap. Even if your "21 tonnes" already means green, you would be at about 39 t. A bigger roaster does not fix this.

**2. Your current machine can't do it.** I'm assuming the 5 kg figure is green input per batch, so each batch gives about 4.25 kg roasted:
- Hours available: 10 h a day × about 250 weekdays = 2,500 h. At 3 batches an hour, that is 7,500 batches, or about 32 t roasted a year at 100% utilisation.
- Today's 21 t takes about 1,650 h (about 66%). That leaves roughly 10 t of headroom before you allow for cleaning and loading, and less in practice.
- Hollis's 18 t would need about 1,410 more hours, for roughly 3,060 h in total against 2,500 available.

**3. The café promise probably rules it out.** Your café agreements say you won't sell Alto Merida through county supermarkets. Hollis has six stores in your county, and a private-label version of the same blend is still the blend. Get a solicitor to read the exact wording before anything else.

**4. Timing doesn't work.** A 15 kg roaster has a seven-month lead time. Ordering now means delivery around May, so a February start is impossible with new equipment. The December deadline is Hollis's pressure, not a reason to sign.

## Would a bigger roaster or a contract roaster help?

- **15 kg roaster (£68,000):** Roasting capacity would be ample. Assuming the batch time stays at 20 minutes (check with the maker), about 95 t a year is possible in the same hours. It still doesn't solve the green cap, the café clause or the lead time. Check that the lease, ventilation, power and smoke/chaff rules in the flat allow it.
- **Contract roaster for the Hollis volume:** This avoids the machine spend and the hours limit, but it doesn't fix the green cap. It also threatens your organic position: your certificate covers your unit, so beans roasted elsewhere would need that roaster to be certified and your certifier to approve the arrangement. Don't rely on it without that confirmed in writing.

## The other paths, roughly

- **Web subscriptions:** Probably the best next step. They sell retail margin with the capacity you already have, and they don't touch the cap or café clauses if the volume fits within your existing green allocation. The key question is how much green headroom you actually have.
- **Own café:** Doesn't obviously breach anything, but it uses roasting time and green coffee and adds fixed costs. Treat it as a separate business case.
- **Distributor in Varnland:** Check the importer contract for territory and volume terms, and check whether the café clause applies outside the county. If it doesn't, this could be a sensible way to use spare capacity at a wholesale price, but only within the cap.

## What I'd do before December

1. **Check your real green position.** Confirm whether "21 tonnes" is roasted or green, and how much of the 26 t cap you have left for this year and next.
2. **Get a solicitor to read the café agreements and the importer contract** for the exact clauses on supermarkets, territory and volume.
3. **Ask the certifier** whether contract roasting, or a second roasting site, would keep your organic status.
4. **Counter-offer Hollis** with something you can actually deliver:
   - a smaller volume that fits inside your green headroom;
   - no sales through the six county stores, or a different blend name and origin;
   - a later start date, tied to the machine and to importer approval;
   - a letter of intent with conditions, rather than a signed three-year contract.
5. **Ask the importer** whether the cap can be raised. If they say no, the Hollis deal can't be done with Alto Merida, whatever machine you buy.
6. **Don't let the December deadline drive this.** If Hollis won't wait for these checks, that tells you something about the deal.

**Assumptions:** 5 kg is green input per batch with 15% weight loss; "21 tonnes" is roasted output; about 250 weekdays a year; 20-minute batches; no change to the lease hours. If any of these are wrong, the numbers change, but the green cap and the café clause still apply.
