The best chance of still being here in ten years is the upper-mountain option: keep the Summit ski operation as the core business, keep the lower land you own, and do not accept the Ardane approach. Snowmaking as costed does not fit your water licence, and the bike park is blocked by your lease for now. Here is the reasoning, with the assumptions I made.

**1. Snowmaking across the lower runs fails on water alone**

- The consultant's assumption of 4,500 m³/ha across 9 ha is 40,500 m³ per season.
- Your licence allows 40,000 m³ per season, and the beginner guns already used 31,000 m³ last winter.
- Existing use plus the new system would be about 71,500 m³, against a 40,000 m³ cap. Even with the beginner area excluded from the calculation, the new system alone exceeds the licence.
- Headroom after existing use is about 9,000 m³, which covers roughly 2 ha at the consultant's rate.
- Before the board votes, ask the consultant to re-run the figures against the licence, and ask whether the 4,500 m³/ha rate can be cut. Even then, a licence increase would be needed for full coverage.

**2. The bike park is blocked by the lease and is not fundable**

- The upper slopes are leased, and the lease limits use to winter sports between 1 December and 15 April. A summer bike park on those slopes breaches it unless the Grazing Commons Trust agrees a variation in writing.
- The lease was renewed for ten years in 2025, so it runs to about 2035. The regional fund excludes leased-land assets unless at least 15 years remain. That means no 50% funding for the bike park, the chairlift refit, or other upper-slope capital until the lease is extended.
- The consultant's "double annual revenue" claim needs checking against this constraint and the cost of the trails.

**3. The dry slope and tubing lane is the only fundable option on land you own**

- You own the lower land, so the €1.4M dry slope and tubing lane is eligible for 50%, which is about €700k net.
- It works year-round and needs little water, so it does not depend on snow. It does not replace skiing, though, and on its own it does not restore the upper-mountain demand.

**4. Upper-only is the strongest on the numbers but has funding and land-sale risks**

- The upper runs had 71 skiable days against 18 for the lower runs, so the Summit chairlift is where the demand is.
- The €600k chairlift refit sits on leased land, so it is not fundable unless the lease is extended to at least 15 years remaining.
- The shuttle from the car park has no cost given, so add it to the plan.
- Giving up the lower runs is not the same as selling the lower land. Keep the lower land if you can, so the buyout grant clawback is not triggered.

**5. The Ardane approach carries the highest long-term risk**

- The community bought the hill in 2019 with a €1.2M grant that must be repaid if the land is sold within ten years, so any sale before 2029 triggers repayment.
- A sale also ends community control, which is the thing your ten-year question is about.
- Do not pursue it now. If the board wants to keep the option open, it should only be considered after 2029, and the terms should be judged against the value of an independent upper-mountain operation.

**Recommended direction for the November vote**

1. Keep the lower land and the community ownership.
2. Make the Summit upper-mountain ski operation the core, with the shuttle costed.
3. Open talks with the Grazing Commons Trust now. Aim for a lease extension to at least 15 years remaining, which would unlock the 50% fund for the chairlift refit and any upper-slope capital. Ask separately whether a summer use variation is possible for a bike park.
4. Treat the dry slope and tubing lane on the lower land as the year-round project to fund at 50%.
5. Drop the snowmaking proposal as costed. Revisit it only if the consultant's figures fit inside the licence.
6. Decline the Ardane approach for now.

**Assumptions and gaps**

- The 31,000 m³ used by the beginner guns is taken as part of the licence total for the season. If it is not, the calculation changes, though the new system still exceeds the licence alone.
- The lease expiry is taken as about 2035, assuming the ten-year renewal started in 2025.
- I assumed the regional fund rules apply as you described, and I have not checked the full eligibility criteria.
- I assumed the €600k chairlift refit and the €850k bike park figures are the full capital costs.
- This is not legal or funding advice. Check the lease variation, licence terms, and grant clawback with a solicitor and the fund administrator before the vote.
