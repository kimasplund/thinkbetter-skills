Short answer: don't sign the Hollis deal as offered. Two of your existing contracts block it, and the machine and timing rule out February. You can still grow, but the order matters.

These numbers rest on a few assumptions, which I've stated so you can correct them: the 21 t is roasted output; a 15% weight loss means 1 t roasted needs about 1.18 t green; a 5 kg batch yields about 4.25 kg roasted; and the 15 kg roaster runs at the same 20 minutes per batch as your current one (check this with the supplier).

**Why Hollis doesn't work as proposed**

1. **Importer cap.** Your current 21 t roasted needs about 24.7 t green, against a 26 t cap that runs to 2028. That leaves roughly 1.3 t of headroom. Hollis's 18 t roasted needs about 21 t more green, so you'd need around 46 t green in total. Signing would breach the importer contract.

2. **Café promises.** Your café agreements say you won't sell the Alto Merida blend through supermarkets in the county. Hollis has six stores there, and a private-label Alto Merida blend is very likely the thing those agreements exclude. Your café customers are most of your volume, so a breach could cost more than the Hollis deal earns. Get a solicitor to read the exact wording before anyone signs anything.

3. **Machine capacity.** On the current 5 kg roaster, the 10-hour weekday window allows at most 30 batches a day, about 127 kg roasted, or roughly 32 t a year on 250 weekdays with no downtime. You already use about two-thirds of that ceiling. Loading, cooling, cleaning and breaks make the realistic limit lower still. Hollis would take you to about 39 t, which the current machine can't do.

4. **Timing.** A 15 kg roaster has a seven-month lead time. Ordering in October gets you a machine around May 2027, so a February start can't be met with new equipment. Before ordering, check that your lease allows a larger roaster (ventilation, noise, smoke, power and floor load).

5. **Certification.** Your organic certificate covers your unit. A contract roaster or a second site would need its own certification or a scope change before any Hollis product could carry an organic claim. A contract roaster also doesn't fix the importer cap or the café clause, so it isn't a way round either problem.

**What I'd do instead**

- **Grow with a different coffee.** Alto Merida is effectively capped at about 22 t roasted a year. Any growth in volume has to come from another origin or blend, provided your importer contract and café agreements don't cover it. Check both before committing.
- **Offer Hollis something different.** An organic private-label blend from another origin, under its own name, might fit their range without touching either contract. Ask them whether they'd accept it, and ask for a later start (around mid-2027) that matches when a bigger machine could arrive.
- **Web subscriptions.** These are probably your best margin on existing capacity. Price them on a non-Alto-Merida blend, or within the green cap.
- **Varnland distributor.** This is worth pursuing if it can be supplied with a blend outside the importer cap. Check its exclusivity terms and whether its retail channels overlap with your café promises.
- **15 kg roaster.** Order it only once you have demand for roughly 20 t or more of coffee that isn't Alto Merida. At £68,000 it's a serious commitment, so price financing and check the lease first.
- **Own café.** This ranks last. It takes the most capital and attention, and it competes with the cafés that buy from you.

**Before the December deadline**

1. Ask a solicitor to review the café agreements and the importer contract. This is cheap compared with losing 40 cafés.
2. If Faye wants to keep Hollis warm, offer a non-binding heads of terms with conditions: importer consent, a café carve-out or a different blend, organic certification for the new scope, and a start date that fits the machine. Don't sign a binding supply contract.
3. Ask Hollis to extend the buyer's decision date, or to accept a 12-month trial.
4. Model the "double profit" claim properly. Include the 15% loss, extra roasting time and labour, packaging and Hollis's price over three years, and compare it with what the café and web customers pay.
5. Get quotes for the 15 kg roaster, a contract roaster, and certification changes, so you can compare the options side by side.

The core answer is that Hollis would need a different coffee, a bigger machine and a clear contract position, none of which you have yet. Growing with a second blend and ordering the machine once there's demand for it gives you a better three-year path than signing now.
