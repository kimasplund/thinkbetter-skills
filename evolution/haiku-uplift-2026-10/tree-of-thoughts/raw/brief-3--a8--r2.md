**Short answer:** Don't sign the Hollis deal as written, and don't let the December deadline push you into it. Three separate limits each block it, and the importer cap alone rules it out. The best growth path is to take a smaller, later Hollis deal on a different coffee, and to put your remaining Alto Merida into web subscriptions. Treat a Varnland distributor as a later option.

I've assumed your 21 tonnes is roasted weight. If it's green weight, the conclusion doesn't change.

## Why the Hollis deal doesn't work as offered

**1. The green coffee cap.** You lose about 15% in the roaster, so 18 tonnes of roasted coffee needs about 21.2 tonnes of green. Your 21 tonnes of current sales already uses about 24.7 tonnes of green. Your importer caps you at 26 tonnes until 2028.

| | Roasted (t) | Green needed (t) |
|---|---|---|
| Current sales | 21 | about 24.7 |
| Hollis | 18 | about 21.2 |
| Total | 39 | about 45.9 |
| Cap | about 22 | 26 |

Headroom today is only about 1.3 tonnes of green, or about 1.1 tonnes roasted. To serve Hollis you would have to give up most of your café and web business to make room. That swaps customers rather than growing. It would also leave you dependent on one buyer, and the Alto Merida blend is the core of your business. The cap runs for the whole first two years of a three-year deal.

**2. The roasting capacity.** The 5 kg machine does 3 batches an hour. Weekday roasting from 8 to 6 gives you about 2,500 hours a year at most. You use roughly 1,650 hours now (about 4,900 batches), so you're at about two-thirds of the ceiling. The combined 46 tonnes would need about 9,200 batches and over 3,000 hours. That is more than the lease allows even if the machine never stopped. The lease also rules out roasting at night or on weekends. A 15 kg roaster would fix the capacity problem, but it costs £68,000 and takes seven months to arrive. If you ordered it today it would arrive around May, not February. Signing in December would put delivery in July or later.

**3. The café agreements.** You promised the cafés you wouldn't sell the Alto Merida blend through supermarkets in the county. Six Hollis stores are in your county. Selling the blend to Hollis for those stores breaches those agreements. That puts your 40 café relationships at risk, and they are your core business.

A contract roaster wouldn't solve this. The cap still applies to the green coffee. The exclusivity promise still applies to the blend. The roaster would also need its own organic certification, because your certificate covers only your unit.

## What I'd do instead

**1. Put the headroom to work first (now to spring).** Use the roughly 1.1 tonnes of spare Alto Merida on web subscriptions. These likely carry better margin than wholesale, use no extra machine time worth worrying about, and don't touch the café agreements. This is low risk and you can start immediately.

**2. Counter-offer Hollis before December.** Propose a smaller, later deal that sidesteps all three limits:
- **A different organic coffee.** Offer a separate blend or origin that isn't Alto Merida. It would sit outside the importer cap and outside the café promise. Ask your importer whether they can supply a second origin, or find another certified organic supplier.
- **A ramped volume.** Start at roughly 4 to 6 tonnes in year one and step up as capacity arrives. That fits the machine, with room left over, though you'd want to check the hours first.
- **A later start.** Aim for around July rather than February, when a bigger roaster could be in place.
- **Stores outside the county.** Hollis has 24 stores outside it, so the exclusivity clause doesn't apply to them. Check the wording of your agreements, because they may be broader than you remember.

Hollis's buyer wants volume, but a buyer who wants an organic range will often accept a different product and a phased start. If they won't move, walking away is better than signing something you can't deliver. Missing deliveries on a three-year supply contract would cost you far more than the profit it promises.

**3. Order the 15 kg roaster only once a revised deal is signed.** Then the £68,000 is backed by a contract. Because of the lead time, order it right after signing so it arrives before volumes step up. It would triple batch size, which more than covers a larger Hollis commitment and lets you add subscriptions. A contract roaster is a reasonable bridge for the first months of the new blend, provided it's certified organic.

**4. Open talks with your importer now.** The cap lasts until 2028 and it limits every growth path that uses Alto Merida. Ask whether they will lift it, or whether you can add a second origin. Larger and steadier volumes may be a selling point for them.

## Other paths, briefly

- **Own café:** This is a different kind of business, with high fixed costs and management time. It uses roasted coffee but adds nothing to your supply problem, and you'd be competing with your own wholesale customers. I'd leave it.
- **Varnland distributor:** This is a possible later step once you have a larger roaster and a second source of green coffee. It's outside the county, so the café agreements don't block it, but today you have no spare Alto Merida to sell. It would also add export and organic paperwork.
- **Contract roaster alone:** This fixes capacity but not the cap or the café promise.

## Before you decide

1. Check whether Hollis's price already accounts for the lost café sales. The "double profit" figure only holds if the extra volume is additional, and under the cap it wouldn't be.
2. Reread the exact wording of the café agreements, and talk to a solicitor before any conversation with Hollis about the Alto Merida name or blend.
3. Check with your organic certifier what a second blend or a larger roaster would require.

**Bottom line:** The offer needs about 46 tonnes of green coffee and over 3,000 roasting hours a year. You have a 26 tonne cap and about 2,500 hours at best. It would also breach your promises to the cafés. Grow through subscriptions now, renegotiate Hollis onto a different coffee at a smaller, later start, and buy the bigger roaster only against a signed contract.
