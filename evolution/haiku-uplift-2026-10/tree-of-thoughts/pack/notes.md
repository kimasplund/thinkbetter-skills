# Notes on planted keys

**Brief 1 (field-service software, offline mode).** The two options a quick reply favours are the CTO's CRDT rebuild (merges automatically) and Driftwell (fastest). Each is ruled out by a constraint mentioned in passing. Driftwell would be a new sub-processor under the contract (k1, the easiest key). The 11-week deadline is mostly taken up by Harrowgate's eight-week app review, so only about 3 weeks of build time remain (k2, hard because the 11 weeks are prominent and the review is one sentence near the end). The shared van stock, decremented from two handsets that can both be offline, means stock counts cannot be merged automatically without corrupting billing (k3, hard because it is written as plain workflow description). The fixed 12-hour tokens clash with two-day offline periods, so every offline design needs re-authentication before upload (k4, hard because the token fact reads as security trivia). The disabled browser quietly rules out the PWA and is deliberately left as an unscored extra. A strong answer is a scoped read-only-plus-forms release in about 2 weeks, with parts logged as events reconciled on the server and queued work kept through re-login, and richer sync later.

**Brief 2 (community ski area).** The members' two favourites, snowmaking and the bike park, both fail. Snowmaking at 4,500 m3/ha across 9 ha needs 40,500 m3, more than the entire 40,000 m3 licence, and the numbers sit in different paragraphs (k1, hard). The lease's winter-only clause, described as "the usual clause", forbids the summer bike park (k2). Renewing for ten years in 2025 leaves about nine, below the fund's 15-year minimum, so the upper-only plan's chairlift refit and anything else on the upper slopes gets no grant (k3, hard because it needs the renewal date, today's date and the fund rule put together). The sale exit would trigger repayment of the 1.2M buyout grant (k4, easier). The grant-eligible land is the lower land, which has the least snow, so a strong answer pairs a self-funded upper winter operation with grant-backed, snow-independent use of the lower land, and may suggest negotiating a longer lease.

**Brief 3 (coffee roaster).** The Hollis deal looks attractive and the partner is pushing it, and its most visible obstacle is roaster capacity. The arithmetic (about 32-33 t a year ceiling against 39 t needed, with a seven-month lead time for a new roaster) is k1. The binding constraint is less visible: the 26 t green-coffee cap allows only about 22 t roasted of Alto Merida, which they already almost use up, so the deal is impossible and every growth path built on that blend is capped (k2, hard because a reply that fixes capacity thinks it has solved the problem). The county exclusivity promise in the café agreements conflicts with six Hollis stores (k3). The contract-roaster workaround fails because the organic certificate covers only their own unit and Hollis wants organic coffee (k4, hard because it only matters once in-house capacity has been ruled out). A strong answer declines or restructures Hollis (a different coffee, no stores in the county) and grows through paths not tied to the capped blend.
