Notes (J-A, practitioner lens). The fixed points I checked every reply against: current use of about 24.7 t green against a 26 t cap leaves about 1.3 t of headroom, so all Alto Merida growth is capped until 2028. Hollis needs about 46 t green in total. The machine ceiling is about 2,500 h, roughly 37 t green. The 15 kg roaster cannot arrive by February. The six county stores breach the café clause. A contract roaster needs its own organic certification and fixes none of the other limits. Growth has to come from a second origin or blend.

- N6L: Covers the four blockers clearly. It then says 6 to 8 t of Hollis "could fit under the cap", which is wrong when headroom is about 1.3 t, and it calls web subscriptions "within the cap" without sizing that.
- K0H: The capacity arithmetic is solid, but it never works out the 1.3 t green headroom it keeps pointing to. The second-origin route is mentioned only in passing.
- Q8K: Accurate numbers, a useful path-by-constraint table and a sensible order of steps. Its point that the café clause hits on day one is well made.
- J1F: Says the key thing outright: Alto Merida tops out at about 22 t roasted, so any growth has to be another coffee. The heads-of-terms suggestion is tight, actionable and well conditioned.
- V4T: Treats 21 t roasted as green and adds 18 t roasted to it. That gives a false 5 t of headroom, which it then recommends offering to Hollis. Growing web sales on a non-Alto Merida blend is a good idea.
- D6D: Strong. It covers the café waiver trade, phased volume on a second origin, and a review clause on green price. The "28 tonnes total" figure is muddled.
- Q9F: Shows that taking Hollis swaps customers rather than adding them, and that signing in December means a July machine. Every step is ordered: subscriptions now, renegotiate, then a roaster only against a signed contract.
- Q0F: Gets machine capacity wrong (it calls 20 batches a day the ceiling) and is inconsistent about the 15 kg output. It says to order the roaster now despite its own cap logic, and it wants more cafés on Alto Merida when there is almost no headroom.
- W1W: Accurate, and it adds the price and mix lever and the customer-swap point. It is a little lighter on a concrete second-origin plan.
- Y1O: Concise and correct, with a counter-offer sized to about 1 t of headroom. Less of a three-year plan than the best replies.
- B7B: Strong. It shows Hollis-only would leave 4.8 t green for everyone else. It also covers protections, a waiver with something in return, and the contract roaster as a bridge only.
- X1Y: Its main case assumes 21 t is green, which gives a 5 t headroom and a 5 t Hollis offer. Its table gets the Varnland cap logic backwards, and it never offers a non-Alto Merida blend.
- W6E: Accurate and specific: about 850 spare hours, a 6 to 8 t different-blend counter, and take-or-pay terms. It oddly ties renegotiating the cap to the cafés' consent.
- F7K: A good comparison table. It sets a November decision on the roaster, conditional on the importer's answer, and grows web sales on other origins. "Growth beyond 24 t needs it" is loose.
- H9C: The most usable reply. It quantifies the customer swap and realistic machine headroom (about 4.5 t), and it says to compare margins before any Alto Merida goes to Hollis. It also prices the roaster payback and gives Faye a fast route that still meets the deadline.
- Y0Y: Solid and well structured, with a waiver idea and protections. It doesn't size subscription growth against the 1.3 t headroom.
