# J-B scores, brief-2

Facts checked against the brief: 9 ha x 4,500 = 40,500 m3, which exceeds the 40,000 licence on its own; 31,000 existing use leaves about 9,000 m3 (about 2 ha). The lease clause bars summer use. The ten-year lease from 2025 gives fewer than 15 years, so upper assets get no grant, and it ends around 2035, inside the ten-year horizon. The buyout clawback applies to a sale before 2029. Lower-land projects (snowmaking and the dry slope) are both grant-eligible.

- Z5F: All the main constraints, correct arithmetic, a conditional vote plan with a fallback, and a clear confidence statement. Lease expiry is only touched on ("edge of horizon"). Approving the €600k refit without a grant before the lease is secured is a small tension.
- N1J: The most complete. Treats lease expiry inside the horizon as the biggest risk, computes 2042, plans what to offer the Trust and a fallback if it refuses, and lists its uncertainties precisely (licence scope, how the 15-year test is applied).
- U1V: Catches all of it, including the need to secure the lease past 2035, and gives grant-adjusted nets. It slips by saying 2 ha of snowmaking needs existing use cut, when its own headroom figure says it doesn't. A bit long.
- Q5S: Concise and accurate on the lease horizon. Doesn't work out the 2 ha headroom, says "about ten years" left (about 9), and has no fallback or what-would-change.
- E4A: Good constraints, but says the dry slope is "the only option that is grant-eligible", which contradicts its own snowmaking point. The recommendation doesn't really land.
- T6G: Clear recommendation. Calls the dry slope "the only fundable option on land you own" (snowmaking is too) and treats 71 snow days as "demand". No fallback.
- Q4H: Tight and accurate. Its match-funding and cash-runway check is a non-obvious, useful point. Doesn't flag lease expiry inside the horizon. One garbled line ("€850k into €850k").
- I9N: A solid conditional plan with a correct €725k net. Misses lease expiry inside the horizon. The aside about selling the lower land sits uneasily with the clawback.
- F1U: Flags lease expiry inside the window. Never questions the bike park revenue claim, asserts that pass sales fall "because of snow" without support, and its demand test is odd.
- O0Z: Good on the constraints, and its point about beginners losing terrain is non-obvious. Recommends the ungranted refit now. No explicit what-would-change.
- O0S: Flags the horizon risk, but pushes snowmaking "at a water-compliant scale" as priority 2 on the 18-day runs, with no headroom figure. Its recommendation is vague.
- B7T: Good, with both a what-would-change and advice on handling the member split. Says "about 8 years remain" (it is about 9). Lease expiry inside the horizon is only in its assumptions.
- B3J: Puts lease expiry inside the horizon first, which is right. Asserts that Ardane "wouldn't keep the hill open" without support, and gives no fallback.
- L5M: Gets the logic backwards: says that if the 9 ha includes the beginner area, the gap is the 71,500 figure (it would be 40,500). Its "8.9 ha" point is muddled, the table repeats the text, and it plans the ungranted refit now.
- O6G: A compact table, but calls the dry slope "the only proposal that is grant-eligible", which contradicts its own table, and says "10 years left". Thin on next steps.
- O4J: Excellent. Lease expiry against a 2036 horizon, keeping the lift on minimum spend until the lease is clear, staged snowmaking for a learner strip, a what-would-change section, and explicit assumptions. Its 8.5-year figure is a little imprecise.
