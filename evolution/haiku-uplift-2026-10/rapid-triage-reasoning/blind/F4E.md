Short answer: confirm the freezer van before 08:30, stop the heaters, keep everything out of the skip, and keep the electrics out of that room. Mould can start within 48 to 72 hours in a warm, muggy room, so freezing buys you the time you don't have otherwise.

**Do these in order**

**1. Now (07:45 to 07:55): make the room safe and stop the damage getting worse**
- Turn off the strongroom lighting circuit at the consumer unit, not just the wall switch. Water came through the light fitting. Do not run extension leads, fans or heaters into that room until an electrician has checked it.
- Unplug the heaters. The hardened catalogue edges show what heat does to wet coated paper. It bakes it and speeds up mould. Take that stack away from the heater, but don't try to peel it apart.
- Cool and ventilate the room instead. Open windows or doors, and use a fan outside the wet area only to move air through. Don't blow it directly at the boxes. If anyone can bring a dehumidifier, that helps a lot.
- Volunteers should wear gloves and a mask. Wet paper and mould are an irritant.

**2. Before 08:30: say yes to Polar Quay**
- Freezing is the right call for the glossy prints and the stuck catalogues in particular. Coated paper dried wet fuses into a solid block, and once it is frozen it can be separated later by a conservator or a freeze-drying service. Handled wet and unfrozen, it can't be saved.
- I'd freeze all 60 wet boxes, ledgers and letters included. You can't air-dry that volume in a hall by tonight, and anything not dry within about two days will go mouldy.
- Cost is roughly 60 × £9 = £540 a month. That is affordable, but ask them on the phone for the minimum term, any collection or handling charge, and a written quote or email. Ask whether they will take the boxes as they are.
- Phone them before 08:30, not after. If they can only take part of the load, send the glossy prints, the catalogues and the most sodden Cobbold ledgers first.

**3. Packing, which takes about 30 minutes**
- Leave the contents in their boxes and don't unpack, sort, press or separate anything. Moving wet paper tears it.
- Put each box in a bin bag or freezer bag if you have one, or at least wrap it, so it doesn't drip. If the bottom of a box is falling apart, slide it onto a board or a tray instead.
- Number each box and keep a simple list of the number, shelf location and contents (for example "Cobbold ledgers 1890s" or "catalogues"). You will need this for the insurer and for retrieval.
- Photograph the room, the shelves and the box labels before you move anything. Use your phones.
- If any boxes are only slightly damp, not soaked, put them aside. Those can be dried flat in the hall at normal room temperature, with no heat, using fans on low and some paper towel or blotting paper between the leaves. If you are unsure, freeze them.

**4. The skip: say no for now**
- Tell the agent the material is the Trust's property and is being removed this morning. Offer to get it out of the building before 09:00, since the frozen boxes will be gone with the van anyway, which deals with the smell.
- Don't accept "the insurer will pay for it anyway" at face value. Insurers pay for the loss, but once it is in a skip it is gone, the claim gets harder to evidence, and these are unique records from the Cobbold boatyard that can't be replaced with money.
- Put the agent's request in writing or email it back to yourself, so you have a record. Dump nothing until the loss adjuster has seen it.

**5. Today, not Tuesday**
- Ring your insurer's claims line now (many have a 24-hour number) and tell them what happened, what you have done and that you are freezing the material. Ask them to confirm in writing that freezing and the storage costs are covered, and ask whether they can bring the loss adjuster forward. Also check your policy for cover for emergency salvage.
- Don't wait until Tuesday to ask them. If they object to freezing, you want to hear that before it's done, but it's still the right action given the risk, and you can tell them that.
- Keep every receipt: plumber, freezer, bags, hire of equipment.

**6. This week**
- Contact a conservator or a disaster-recovery service for the freeze-drying and separation of the photographs and catalogues. Your regional archive service, the National Archives advice team or a local record office can usually advise and sometimes help. Do this before the storage bills mount.
- Have the strongroom dried properly and checked for damp and mould before anything goes back. Don't return anything to a room that's still muggy.
- Make sure any material lent to you (if any of the Cobbold material is on deposit) is reported to the owner.

**What I'm assuming:** the rooms upstairs are no longer leaking, the plumber's isolation holds, and the freezer will take wet boxes as they are. If you can't get the van by 08:30, the fallback is to get the boxes out of the warm room into the coolest, best-ventilated space you have, with no heaters, and to freeze on Monday. Prioritise the prints and catalogues for any space or time that is limited.
