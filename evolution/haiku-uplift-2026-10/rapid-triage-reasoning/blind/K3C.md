Do these in order. The 08:30 deadline is the one that matters most.

**Next 5 minutes**

1. **Stop the heaters now.** Unplug both and don't run extension leads into the strongroom. The ceiling and light fitting got wet, so mains power in there is a shock risk. Leave the light off at the wall. Heat is also the wrong treatment: it hardens and distorts the glossy papers, which is what happened to the catalogue edges, and it speeds up mould. Keep the fans, but run them from a dry area, pointing at the room to move air. Don't aim them at the stacks.
2. **Tell the landlord's agent that nothing goes in the skip.** Say it politely and put it in writing (text or email) straight away. The insurer will pay for these only if it can assess them, and the loss adjuster is coming Tuesday. Once they're destroyed, the claim and the records are both lost, and the Cobbold material can't be replaced. Offer to move the wet boxes out of the strongroom before 09:00 so the smell stops being the agent's problem. Take photos of everything first, including the leak, the ceiling, the light fitting and the boxes in place.
3. **Phone your insurer's claims line.** Report the loss and ask them to bring the adjuster forward. Ask them to confirm in writing that nothing should be discarded. Also ask whether they'll cover emergency freezing and salvage costs, and whether they will approve the Polar Quay cost.

**Before 08:30: confirm the freezer collection**

Say yes to Polar Quay. Freezing stops mould (which starts within about 48 hours at 22 °C and high humidity) and stops further damage. It buys you time to dry the material properly later. The cost is small: 60 boxes at £9 a month is about £540 a month, and the insurer should reimburse it. If they won't, you can afford a month or two while it's sorted. Missing the van means waiting until Monday in warm, muggy conditions, which is the risky option. Ask Polar Quay how they want the boxes packed, and whether they can supply crates or bags.

**What goes to the freezer, and what doesn't**

- **Freeze:** all the wet ledgers and letters, still in their boxes or wrapped, with no attempt to separate them. Freezing wet paper is a standard salvage method. Freeze the stuck-together catalogues and photographic prints too. Glossy coated paper that dries while stuck together fuses permanently, so these need freezing while still wet. Do not try to peel them apart.
- **Photographs:** if you can, interleave or wrap the prints in freezer or greaseproof paper (not cling film) to stop them sticking further. If there's no time, freeze them as they are.
- **Items that are only damp, not wet:** if some boxes are merely damp, they can stay out and air-dry, spread out and interleaved with clean blotting paper or unprinted newsprint, in the hall at room temperature with air moving. Don't bother if you're short of time. Freezing is the safer choice for anything uncertain.

**Handling**

- Wear gloves. Floodwater from a sink pipe is relatively clean, but mould spores aren't harmless, and a mask is sensible.
- Lift wet boxes from underneath and support the base. Wet paper tears easily, and wet cardboard boxes collapse.
- Don't try to open, separate or wipe anything. Don't use paper towels on the pages.
- Move the boxes to a staging area (the hall) and pack them for the van. Label each box with its number and contents so it can be tracked for the insurance claim.
- Keep a list as you go. Rough counts are fine at this stage.

**Volunteers**

Split the six into roles. Two photograph and list, two carry and pack, one stays on the phones to the insurer and the agent, and one is the point of contact for the van. Take breaks, and make sure nobody works in the strongroom while the ceiling is still wet and an electrical fault is possible.

**Afterwards**

- Get an electrician to check the strongroom light fitting and circuit before anything is switched back on.
- Run a dehumidifier in the strongroom (powered from a safe supply) and ask a conservator about drying it out. The Trust's insurer may know one, or you could contact the National Archives' advice service or a local record office. Ask for emergency advice on vacuum freeze drying of the frozen material. It's usually the best way to recover stuck glossy papers, and it's something the insurer may fund.
- Keep all receipts.

**Assumptions:** I've assumed the boxes can be loaded within the van's time window, the van has the capacity for roughly 60 boxes, and the policy covers water damage. If the van can't take everything, prioritise in this order: the glossy catalogues and prints (because they can't be saved otherwise), then the Cobbold ledgers and letters.
