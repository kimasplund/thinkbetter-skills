Short version: don't run the splash as it stands, and don't send Vosk's lawyer your copy. Swap in the held-over feature, keep the story alive for tomorrow, and spend the 55 minutes making the swap reversible and the story verifiable. Pulping 38,000 copies is expensive. A defamation claim over a forged document, if it turns out to be forged, is worse. Holding costs you one day, a possible loss to the Gazette, and nothing permanent.

Why hold:
- Everything rests on one document from one source. The only authentication you have is a routing stamp and initials, and those are exactly what a forger would copy.
- Someone is now specifically claiming forgery. You can't dismiss that, and you can't verify the document in 55 minutes.
- The press can't be recalled but the swap can. You have about 20 minutes of swap time against a 23:00 deadline, so the hard decision point is about 22:35 and you shouldn't wait for it.
- The cost of being beaten by the Gazette is limited. Being first with a forged document is not.

Do now, in this order:

1. 22:05-22:15, try to reach Ines. Call, text and ring her sister or a plus-one, and say plainly that it's a hold-or-run call on the splash. Also try the deputy editor, the managing editor, the editor-in-chief, and the paper's lawyer or legal helpline. Tell them you'll hold unless someone with authority says otherwise. If someone senior does answer, they decide. If nobody does, the decision is yours as the person responsible, so make it, write it down and say so.

2. Call Dagny now. Ask:
   - How did the source get the document, and how well has the source performed before?
   - Has anyone else, for example a second person at the building control office or someone on the Larchmont project, confirmed the sign-off was faked? A real forgery claim would normally have other evidence behind it.
   - Is there anything on the page that can be checked, such as the officers' names, the stamp format, the reference number and the dates?
   Tell her you're not killing the story and that you want it right and in the paper tomorrow or the day after. If she can get a second source or a check on the officers in the next half hour, that changes things.

3. Do not send Vosk's lawyer the scan. The offer is the sort of thing a person would make if they wanted to learn what you hold and whether it identifies your source. The scan may carry marks that point to the leaker, such as handling traces, a particular copy or metadata. Reply by email, and keep it short and neutral. Say you've received the message, that you take the allegation seriously, that you're not going to share source material, and that you'll put specific questions to him and to Vosk in writing and give them a fair deadline to respond. This is a record of fairness without giving anything away. Have legal review it if you can reach them, but don't wait on that for the swap.

4. 22:15 onward, tell production to prepare the swap. The subs should start on the held-over feature now so it's ready. Don't cancel the splash yet. Set a decision point of 22:35 at the latest. If the situation has not clearly improved by then, call the swap.

5. Stop the digital and wire release, which is easy to forget and which is the real danger tonight.
   - Unschedule the 00:01 web publication and the app alert to 70,000 readers. Remove the schedule rather than just editing the text, then confirm in the CMS that nothing is queued, and have a second person check. An app alert to 70,000 people cannot be retracted.
   - Send a kill or hold notice on the group wire to all three sister titles: "Courier splash for 00:01 embargo is WITHDRAWN. Do not publish, do not alert, do not rewrite from the 21:00 copy. Reason: pending verification. Further notice to follow." Ring the night desk at each as well as sending it, because an embargoed wire story is easy to miss, and any of them running it takes the risk on your behalf. Ask each to confirm in writing.
   - Check the 21:00 wire copy hasn't gone anywhere beyond the three titles.

6. Watch the Gazette without being driven by it. If they run a story, it will be based on what they have, and you may be able to see whether they have the same document. If they publish something wrong or unsupported, that is a reason to be glad you held. If they publish a verified story, you lose a day on a story you can still follow with more.

7. 22:35, make the call. Run only if you have both a credible second route to the facts (a second source or a verification of the document) and a senior person agreeing. Otherwise swap. If you run something, the only version I would consider is a reduced one saying that Vosk is being investigated or questioned, and only if legal has cleared it. The unmodified splash is not that.

8. After the swap, before you leave:
   - Write a short handover note for Ines covering what happened, who you tried, what you decided, what you told the wire and the lawyer, and the open questions.
   - Preserve everything: the scan, the emails, the lawyer's message and your sent reply. Don't forward or alter the original file.
   - List what has to happen tomorrow.

Tomorrow's checks, for Ines and Dagny:

Ask building control's press office (opens 08:30) a neutral question about whether the report and its reference exist and were issued, without naming or exposing the source. Check the named officers independently, and the Larchmont records through public planning and building control registers.

If the document turns out to be genuine and you have it confirmed, you run a stronger story a day later. If it is a forgery, you will have avoided putting a false accusation of fire-safety fraud on 38,000 front pages, a web page and 70,000 phones, and you will also have learned someone is trying to plant stories on your newsroom.
