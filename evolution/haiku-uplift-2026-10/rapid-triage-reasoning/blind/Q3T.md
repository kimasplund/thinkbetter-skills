Stop the volunteers before anyone plugs anything in. Here is what to do, in order, from now until 08:30.

**1. Right now (before 08:00)**
- **No fans, fan heaters or extension leads in the strongroom.** Water has been coming through the light fitting, so the room may be wet and the wiring may be compromised. Switching the light off at the wall is not isolation. Ask someone to turn off the circuit for that room at the consumer unit (fuse box). If you can't identify it, turn off the main switch, which is safer while the room is wet. Don't touch the wiring or the light fitting yourself.
- **Keep the heaters away from the paper.** The catalogue that was dried by a heater at 07:00 has hardened, and heat will cause more of the same. Do not use heat on anything else.
- **Don't lay anything out to dry on the hall tables.** Stuck photographic prints and glossy catalogues can't be separated by air-drying. Laying them out also spreads mould spores and damp.

**2. By 08:30: book the freezer**
- **Confirm Polar Quay Cold Storage.** Freezing is the standard first step for wet paper and photographs, because it stops mould and lets the stuck stacks be separated later under controlled conditions. At £9 a box per month, 60 boxes is about £540 a month, which is affordable from the £4,000 while the insurance claim is settled. Ask them whether they can take the photo prints and catalogues in the same load. If the van can only come on Monday, you lose two days, but freezing is still the right choice.
- **Put the wet boxes in the freezer, not the skip.** Do not let the agent dispose of anything yet.

**3. Before 09:00: call the landlord's agent and the insurer**
- **Tell the agent no to the skip.** The smell is a real problem, but the boxes are records of the Cobbold boatyard. Once they are in a skip they can't be recovered, and the insurer may refuse the claim if you destroyed evidence before the loss adjuster saw them. Ask the agent to put a temporary cover or bin bag over the boxes, or to keep them in the strongroom with the door shut and the room ventilated, until the freezer van collects.
- **Call the insurer's emergency or claims line now.** Most policies have a 24-hour number. Report the leak, say that you are freezing the material to stop mould, and ask whether they approve it and what they will pay for. Keep the name and reference number. Do not assume they will pay "for it all"; that is for them to confirm.

**4. Record everything**
- Photograph the room, the ceiling, the light fitting, every stack of boxes and the stuck catalogues, with the date and time visible. Note which boxes are wet and which are damp. Keep the catalogue that was heated, unchanged, as evidence.
- Write a short log of who did what and when, including the 07:00 heater trial.

**5. Things to do next, not today**
- Ask the loss adjuster (Tuesday) for a salvage plan and a conservator's advice on the photographs and ledgers.
- Ask the plumber for a written note of the cause and the time the water was shut off. The insurer will ask for it.
- Keep the volunteers out of the strongroom until the electrics have been checked by a qualified electrician.

**Assumptions:** I have assumed the strongroom's circuit is still live, the insurer's policy covers water damage from a burst or leaking pipe, and the freezer can take the boxes without special packing. Check the first point with an electrician and the second with the insurer.
