Short version: stop the heat, stop the skip, say yes to the freezer van, and keep the strongroom electrics off. Here is the order I would do it in.

**Next 5 minutes**

1. **Keep the strongroom light off, and don't run extension leads in there.** Water came through the light fitting. Do not run fans or heaters in that room until an electrician has checked it. Get the circuit isolated at the consumer unit if you can. Mains power plus standing water is the one thing here that can hurt a person, so it comes first.
2. **Take the heater away from the catalogues now.** Hard edges mean the coated paper is drying unevenly and may be fusing. Heat on stuck coated paper makes the damage permanent. Don't use heaters on any of the material. Fans are fine, but only in the hall, on moving air, not pointed straight at the items.
3. **Tell the landlord's agent that nothing goes in the skip.** The wet records are the Trust's property and your insurance claim. The loss adjuster can't value or settle for what has been destroyed. The agent's insurer comment doesn't help you either, because you don't know your own cover and what it needs. Offer a compromise instead. Everything wet is out of the building, or at least out of the strongroom, before 09:00. The smell is a reason to move things quickly. It isn't a reason to throw them away.

**Before 08:30: say yes to Polar Quay for the things that need it**

Freezing is the standard way to stop mould and buy time. Mould starts within about 48 hours in warm, damp conditions, and 22 °C and muggy is exactly that. Freezing also means nobody has to handle fragile wet paper in a rush. A specialist can later vacuum freeze-dry it.

- **Freeze:** the stuck catalogues and the glossy photographic prints. Wet coated paper and photographs are the items most likely to bond permanently if they dry in contact. Freeze them as they are and don't try to peel anything apart. Also freeze any ledger or letter box that is still soaked through.
- **Don't freeze or send:** anything that is only damp. That can be air-dried today.
- Cost: 60 boxes at £9 a box is about £540 a month. That is affordable out of £4,000 for a month or two, but it will add up. Ask Polar Quay for a minimum term and a per-box release price. Ask your insurer in the first call whether the storage cost is covered.
- If the van can't take all 60, send the coated paper and photographs first.

**How to pack in the next hour**

- Handle wet items as little as you can. Work in pairs. Keep each item in its box or stack and don't try to separate or unfold it.
- Put each box in a clean polythene bag or wrap, or in a crate lined with polythene. Label each with a number and a short note, such as "Cobbold ledgers 1890s". Make a quick list as you go.
- Photograph the room, the leak and each box before it moves. This is evidence for the claim.
- Keep a short list of what goes where: freezer, hall drying, and anything you decide is unrecoverable.

**What the volunteers do in the hall**

- Spread out only the boxes that are damp rather than soaked. Open them, stand ledgers upright with the pages fanned a little, and interleave loose letters with clean blotting paper or plain unprinted newsprint. Change the paper as it gets wet.
- Cool, dry, moving air. Open windows if it's dry outside, use fans at a distance, and use a dehumidifier if you can find one. No heaters, no sun.
- Do not touch the ink on anything. Ledgers and letters may have water-soluble ink, so don't wipe or blot the writing itself.
- Wear gloves. If you see mould, and the smell may mean you will, keep those items separate and wear a mask.

**Calls to make this morning**

1. **The insurer's emergency line.** Don't wait for Tuesday. Tell them what you have done and why, and ask what they need. They may have a recommended salvage firm. Ask them to confirm that the freezer cost is covered.
2. **An archive salvage or conservation contact**, for example your county record office or a conservator. Ask them to advise on the freezer items and on later drying. Many local record offices help with disasters like this.
3. **An electrician**, to check the strongroom circuit before anyone goes back in with power.

**What I'm assuming**

- The insurer covers this kind of damage, but I have not seen your policy. Check the terms, and do not treat the agent's statement as a guarantee.
- You can afford a month or two of freezer storage. If the cost looks to be a problem, tell the insurer that now.
- The freezer holds only the worst items. If space or money forces a choice, the coated paper and photographs go first, then the soaked boxes, and the merely damp ones stay with you.

If you can only do three things before 08:30, do these: unplug the heater, say yes to the van, and tell the agent that nothing goes in the skip until the insurer has been told.
