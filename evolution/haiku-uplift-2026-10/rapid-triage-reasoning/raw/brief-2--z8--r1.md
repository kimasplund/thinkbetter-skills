## Do this in the next 45 minutes

**1. Confirm Polar Quay by 08:30.** Freezing stops mould and further swelling and staining, and it buys you time to get proper conservation advice. Frozen material can be thawed and dried later, so this is the step you can most easily undo. Ring them now and ask:
- whether they accept wet paper and photographs in the condition they are in, and whether they want the boxes bagged or wrapped first;
- how they bill for a partial load, and whether the van can take all of the boxes this morning.

At 60 boxes that is about £540 a month, which is affordable from the £4,000 while the claim is sorted. If the van can only take some, freeze the ledgers and letters first and the photographs and catalogues next, since those are the most at risk. Do not wait for the insurer to approve the cost before confirming. Tell them you are freezing to prevent further loss.

**2. Stop the heaters and extension leads.** Water has been running through the light fitting, so the electrics in that room are not safe to load. The heaters also damage the stuck catalogues and glossy prints. The one that went by a heater at 07:00 is already showing the damage you would expect, so keep the heaters well away from everything else. Fans can run on a proper socket only after an electrician has checked the light fitting and ceiling. Do not run them from extension leads.

**3. Do not lay anything out to dry in the hall.** Drying stuck prints and catalogues in open air will cause more damage than freezing. Do not separate the stuck stacks. Leave them as they are for the conservator or freezer team.
