It's 07:45 on Saturday. I chair the Harrowby Local History Trust, and overnight a pipe under the kitchen sink upstairs leaked through the ceiling of our strongroom, mostly through the light fitting. The plumber has shut off the water and left; the strongroom light is off at the wall switch.

About 60 archive boxes on the lower shelves are soaked: mostly ledgers and letters from the old Cobbold boatyard, 1870s to 1950s, plus around 300 glossy photographic prints and a run of sale catalogues on shiny paper, now stuck together in their stacks. The room is about 22 °C and muggy.

Six volunteers have arrived with fans and two fan heaters. They want to run extension leads into the strongroom and start laying everything out to dry on the tables in the hall. One of them put a stack of the stuck catalogues by a heater at 07:00 as a trial; the edges have gone hard.

Polar Quay Cold Storage will take boxes into their −20 °C freezer at £9 a box per month, but their van can only collect this morning if I confirm by 08:30. Otherwise it's Monday.

The landlord's agent wants the wet boxes in the skip before the charity shop downstairs opens at 09:00, because of the smell, and says the insurer will pay for it all anyway. The insurer's loss adjuster can come on Tuesday.

The Trust has about £4,000 in the bank. What do I do right now?
