I'll be honest: I'm not sure we should be doing this at all. I'm on the election committee at Ostwick Housing Co-operative (412 units), and this year our board election and a vote on a $1.9M roof special assessment are moving online. Links go out next Monday. Part of me thinks we should have stayed on paper. Can you try to break this and tell me whether my doubts are justified?

- Our management company, Varrow Property Services, emails each unit a unique voting link (a token in the URL) at the address on file.
- One vote per unit, and our bylaws require a secret ballot. Voting is open 10 days; quorum is 50% of units. Members can change their vote until close, and the last submission counts.
- If someone loses their link, they call the Varrow office. Staff confirm the unit number and the leaseholder's full name, deactivate the old link and email a fresh one to whatever address the caller gives.
- A member can hand their vote to another member by uploading a signed proxy form (scanned PDF) in the portal. Varrow's office manager approves it, and that unit's ballot then appears on the proxy holder's page.
- To plan reminder calls, board members and the office manager see a live dashboard with turnout by floor and running totals for each question.
- Each submission is logged with timestamp, token ID and choices. At close the office manager exports the results to CSV and emails them to us, and we certify.
- Dorota Weiss, our vice-chair, has full portal admin rights as our liaison with Varrow. She's running for re-election.

The roof vote is contentious. We're volunteers with no IT background, and I'm not convinced anyone has really thought this through. Be blunt.
