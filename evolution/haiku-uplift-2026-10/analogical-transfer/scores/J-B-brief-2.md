Notes (J-B, brief-2)

- G5T: Covers reorder-not-shrink, category 5 starvation, self-report bias, the landlord-holds-deposit incentive, and the forcing-event fix for late settlements. The ~250 hearings figure and other estimates are flagged as assumptions. The section on what ED triage scales do brings in outside knowledge, but it is reasonable. A little long.
- G0C: Tight and sharp. Says not to set a hearing date at filing, moves the forcing event earlier, and pre-registers a decision rule. The claim that an ED queue "clears overnight" is an invented or wrong analogy fact. "Probably within what six adjudicators can do" is a leap, though it is partly hedged in the checks list.
- Y7V: Sound on capacity and self-report. The "landlord-side claim with a time limit" example is invented. Claims a 320-per-arm split shows large effects in 2-3 months, yet its primary outcome is time to hearing on a 14-month queue. Misses the deadline-effect insight. Endorses the ED fit too readily.
- L7D: Clean queue arithmetic check, and harm-based categories are a good idea. Makes little of the landlord-delay incentive, and the settlement section misses why settlements bunch late. Moderate, honest assumptions.
- V2W: The retrospective test of the categories on existing cases is a cheap, sharp way to reduce uncertainty. States as fact that "landlords do not write reasons", which the brief does not say. Treats settlement as slot release, not as a deadline effect.
- B6N: Good insight: the delay transfers value to landlords, pressure to settle low, escrow, and the forcing event. But "landlords have no equivalent box" is invented, and the two-year clearance arithmetic is wrong (it needs ~440 more a month, not 390). One sentence is muddled ("which is where triage's value lies"). Long.
- B4E: The most complete diagnosis. It makes the deadline-effect point explicit (month 13 vs week 4-6), contacts backlog parties to find dead cases, caps the priority lane, sets a long-stop date, and raises the risk that settlement pressure hurts unrepresented tenants. Decision rules and stated confidence are well calibrated. The three-year clearance figure is a bit low (~900/month needed, not 850). Long.
- R8L: The analogy-mapping table is visible method scaffolding. "Stakes are similar since the landlord holds the deposit" misreads the incentive, and it frames fairness as favouring tenants rather than noting that delay favours landlords. Short, with honest assumptions.
