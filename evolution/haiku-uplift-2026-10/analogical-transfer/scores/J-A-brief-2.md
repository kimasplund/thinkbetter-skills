- B4E: The most complete. It covers the deadline effect, the landlord incentive to stall, a capped priority lane, a long-stop date, contacting backlog parties to find dead cases, and decision rules with numbers. The "850 a month to clear in three years" figure is slightly low (closer to 900). Long.

- V2W: Tight and practical. A retrospective test of whether the free-text box predicts anything is a cheap, smart check. It is thinner on the deadline mechanism, and the pilot length is vague ("two or three months is short").
- L7D: Sound and balanced, with a useful legal-fit check and the advice to define the goal first. It does not address the landlord's incentive to delay, and the settlement fixes are brief.
- Y7V: Good pilot design (baseline, power, stop conditions, capacity questions for before January) and it notes the landlord incentive. It invents an example of a landlord claim with a time limit, and claims large effects would show within months in a 14-month queue.
- G0C: Clear "don't adopt as proposed". Good on streaming by complexity, not setting hearing dates at filing, and a two-arm comparison. Some unsupported claims (an ED queue "clears overnight"; 256 hearings "probably within" capacity).
- G5T: Excellent practitioner detail: resource-based streaming, readiness checks, landlord non-response moving a case up, capping category 1, a long-stop date, and who staffs the 48-hour review. Its uncertainty section is honest. Long.
