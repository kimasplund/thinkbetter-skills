# Handover: drying the 212 water-damaged survey maps

Prepared for Tobias. The maps are safe for now: they are in chest freezers at −20 °C and will stay stable there while you work through the decisions below. Nine days have passed since the sprinkler failure, so there is no need to rush, but do not let the freezers stop.

## What we have

- 212 hand-coloured survey maps, 1790s–1840s, soaked in the sprinkler failure.
- 151 maps belong to the Archive outright.
- 61 maps are the Brekke deposit, held under a 2009 loan agreement. Clause 7 says deposited items "shall not be removed from the Archive premises for any purpose without the prior written consent of the Lender." The Brekke executor can only be reached through their solicitor.
- Dr Aino Pell (freelance conservator) has examined samples. Watercolour washes are in gum arabic, and there is verdigris green along boundaries. Pell is the expert whose judgement we rely on.

## Decisions already made

- **Kestrel Document Rescue is dropped.** Their process heats items to 40–60 °C. Pell says that is acceptable for office paper but will bleed and cockle the water-soluble media on these maps. Kestrel rang yesterday offering 30% off (€13.30 per map). The discount does not change the heat problem, so do not reopen this without Pell's agreement.

## Options still open

1. **Nordlicht Recovery, offsite vacuum freeze-drying.** €38 per map plus €420 flat transport. Pell thinks freeze-drying is probably fine for the verdigris, but wants a test run on two of the least significant maps first.
   - Cost for all 212: 212 × €38 + €420 = €8,476. This is over Valtera's €6,000 cap.
   - Cost for the 151 Archive-owned maps only: 151 × €38 + €420 = €6,158. This is still €158 over the cap.
   - The 61 Brekke maps cannot go offsite without the lender's written consent (see clause 7).
2. **Air-drying in-house on interleaving.** Table space holds about 25 maps per batch, roughly 3 days per batch. For 212 maps that is about 9 batches, or roughly 27 working days. Cost is low, but it is slow and carries cockling risk. Ask Pell whether it suits the watercolours.
3. **Keep everything frozen until next fiscal year.** Safe in the short term, but the maps stay unusable and the question stays open.

## Money

- Valtera Mutual's adjuster will reimburse drying up to €6,000 total, to any accredited vendor. Check that Nordlicht is on Valtera's accredited list before committing.
- Our remaining conservation budget this year is €1,100. This is the money for anything Valtera does not cover, such as Pell's fee and in-house materials. Pell's fee is not in my notes, so get a quote.

## Suggested next steps

1. **Brekke consent.** Write to the Brekke solicitor now and ask for written consent to move the 61 deposit maps offsite. Start this early because it is the slowest item.
2. **Pell test run.** Ask Pell to run the two-map freeze-drying test with Nordlicht. Ask her in the same message whether in-house air-drying is acceptable for the watercolours.
3. **Valtera check.** Confirm Nordlicht's accreditation and ask Valtera how the €6,000 cap applies to a split job.
4. **Nordlicht quote.** Ask for a revised quote that fits the cap. Options include splitting the job, reducing the transport cost, or prioritising the verdigris maps.
5. **Decide the split.** Once consent and the test results are in, choose between offsite freeze-drying for some or all maps and in-house air-drying for the rest.

## Assumptions

- Pell's test results will be available before the main job is booked.
- Valtera's €6,000 cap covers all drying costs, including transport.
- The 30% Kestrel discount is not a reason to reopen Kestrel.
