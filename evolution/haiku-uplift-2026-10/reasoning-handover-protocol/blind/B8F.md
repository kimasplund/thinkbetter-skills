**Handover for Tobias: choosing and contracting a drying method for the water-damaged maps**

**Situation**
Nine days ago a sprinkler head failed over the map store and soaked 212 hand-coloured survey maps from the 1790s-1840s. They are in our chest freezers at -20 °C. Frozen is a stable holding state, so nothing is deteriorating now. Your job is to choose a drying method and contract it.

**Whose maps are they**
- 151 maps are ours outright.
- 61 maps are the Brekke deposit (2009 loan agreement). Clause 7 says: "Deposited items shall not be removed from the Archive premises for any purpose without the prior written consent of the Lender."
- The Brekke executor is reachable only through their solicitor.
- Any offsite drying of the 61 needs that written consent first. Without it, those 61 can only be dried here.

**The material**
Dr Aino Pell (freelance conservator) examined samples. The maps have watercolour washes in gum arabic and verdigris green on the boundaries. Both are water-sensitive.

**Options**

| Option | Cost | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Nordlicht Recovery, offsite vacuum freeze-drying | €38/map + €420 flat transport | Preferred. Pell thinks it is probably fine for verdigris, but wants a test run first. |
| Kestrel Document Rescue, offsite vacuum thermal drying | €19/map | Rejected. The process thaws and heats items to 40-60 °C. Pell says this is fine for office paper but will bleed and cockle water-soluble media. |
| Air-dry in-house on interleaving | No vendor cost | Space for about 25 maps at a time, about 3 days per batch. |
| Keep everything frozen until next fiscal year | No vendor cost | Possible fallback. See the open questions on insurance. |

**About Kestrel's 30% discount**
Kestrel rang yesterday offering 30% off, which is €13.30 a map. The saving is real, but the problem was never price. The process damages the colour media on every one of these maps, so I would not reopen it. If you want to be thorough, ask Pell one question: would a cooler, lower-temperature Kestrel process be acceptable? I would not expect a yes.

**Money**
- Valtera Mutual's adjuster says they reimburse drying costs up to €6,000 in total, with any accredited vendor.
- Our remaining conservation budget this year is €1,100.
- Available funds: €7,100 maximum.

The sums, using Nordlicht:
- All 212 maps: 212 × 38 + 420 = €8,476. That is €2,476 over the insurance cap and €1,376 more than our €1,100 budget. It does not fit.
- Our 151 maps only: 151 × 38 + 420 = €6,158. That is €158 over the cap, which our budget can absorb.
- The most that fits exactly inside the €6,000 cap is 146 maps: 146 × 38 + 420 = €5,968.
- The 61 Brekke maps sent offsite would add about €2,318, which we cannot fund. (I am assuming the €420 transport is flat and shared.)

**Recommended plan**
1. Test first. Ask Pell to choose the two least significant maps from our own 151, not Brekke, and have Nordlicht freeze-dry them as a trial. Check the verdigris and washes for bleeding or cockling before committing the rest. Ask Nordlicht whether they will do a two-map trial and at what price.
2. If the test passes, send our 151 (or 146) to Nordlicht, with the €158 overage coming from our €1,100 budget. If you would rather stay inside the cap, air-dry the other 5 here.
3. Dry the 61 Brekke maps in-house. That is about 3 batches (25, 25, 11) over roughly 9 days, at no vendor cost. It also needs no consent, because the maps stay on site. Get Pell's view first on whether air-drying is acceptable for these particular maps. I have not yet asked her.
4. If the test fails, the fallback is to air-dry everything in-house. 212 maps is about 9 batches, or roughly 4 weeks. Pell should advise on handling and sequence.

**Start now: write to the Brekke solicitor**
Even if you dry the Brekke maps here, I recommend writing to the solicitor this week. I do not know whether the Lender has been told about the damage. If not, tell them, and ask in the same letter whether they would consent to offsite treatment if we later want it. Replies through a solicitor are slow, so send the letter early.
