**Short answer:** Pattern C is the best option on the table, but as Priya wrote it, it is not approvable. Its first sailing breaks the harbour bylaw, and the fix costs the 06:20 train connection. My confidence in C is lower than either analyst's number suggests.

**Where things stand**

- Priya kept three of seven timetable patterns. Only Pattern C is described in the notes, so I can't compare the other two.
- Priya rejects a charter on physical grounds. The Dunlin's stern ramp doesn't fit the Vellmouth linkspan.
- Owen says the charter is out only because of cost, and that the Dunlin is back on if the board finds money. That conflicts with Priya's ramp finding. Cost is not the only blocker, so the charter is not a real fallback yet.

**The main problem: the first sailing**

- Priya's 05:15 first sailing from Vellmouth falls inside the harbour bylaw's no-movement window (23:00 to 05:30). It cannot run as written.
- The earliest legal departure is 05:30. At 55 minutes, that arrives at Inch Harbour around 06:25, which misses the 06:20 train. The connection that justified the early sailing is lost.
- Owen's crew and fuel test used the 05:15 sailing, so his "it holds" result also needs re-running on the legal timetable.

**How sure we can be**

- Priya's 70% and Owen's 80% can't be combined. Owen tested crew hours and fuel, not demand. Neither analyst tested whether the loadings are right.
- The loadings come from the September 2024 survey. The Corran Bridge road route was closed from March to November 2024, so that survey was taken while road alternatives were unavailable. It may over- or under-state ferry demand. We can't tell which from the notes.
- The Inch Harbour park-and-ride opened in January 2026, after the survey. It may take car traffic off the crossing, which affects loadings.
- My judgment: as written, Pattern C is not ready for the board. Once it is made legal and the connection is re-checked, I'd put confidence that C is the right pattern at roughly 55 to 60%. That figure is my estimate, not a calculation from the two analysts' numbers. Demand uncertainty is the largest single risk, and it is not yet tested.

**Before the board decides (in priority order)**

1. Re-plan Pattern C with the first sailing at or after 05:30. Check the connection. Either accept a later train, or ask the harbour authority whether an exception to the bylaw is possible. Don't assume one exists.
2. Get a written check from the port or yard on whether the Dunlin's ramp fits the Vellmouth linkspan. Until then, drop the charter as an option, or treat it as an option only if the ramp check comes back positive.
3. Re-run Owen's crew-hours and fuel check on the revised timetable.
4. Refresh or sanity-check the loadings. A short count at Vellmouth and Inch Harbour would be the best evidence. At minimum, compare the 2024 survey against the Corran closure period and the new park-and-ride.

**Assumptions I've made**

- Inch Harbour is the mainland side, where the 06:20 train departs.
- The 55-minute crossing time is the same in both directions and at all hours.
- The other two patterns and the full loading figures are not in the notes, so I haven't assessed them.
