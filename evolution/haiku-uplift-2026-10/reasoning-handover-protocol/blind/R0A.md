**Short version:** Pattern C is not yet a safe recommendation. Both analysts are fairly confident, but their notes contain a hard conflict nobody has noticed, and their evidence is thinner than two reports make it look. I would put honest confidence in C as written well below either of their numbers, and I would not average them. Details below.

## State of play

- **Situation:** The Tamsin retires in April. The Elder will run Vellmouth (island) to Inch Harbour (mainland) alone, 55 minutes each way. The board decides after your week back.
- **Work done:** Priya looked at seven timetable patterns and kept three. Pattern C is her best: near-hourly service, first sailing from Vellmouth at 05:15 so islanders can make the 06:20 mainland train. Owen stress-tested C on crew hours and fuel. It passes on both: a split shift keeps crews within the 12-hour duty limit, and fuel is up 4% on today.
- **Charter option:** Rejected, but the two analysts give different reasons (see below).

## Problems you need to know about

1. **The 05:15 departure appears to break the harbour bylaw.** Priya's own notes say no vessel movements at Vellmouth between 23:00 and 05:30. A 05:15 sailing leaves Vellmouth inside that ban. Neither analyst flagged it. Owen tested crew and fuel, not legal operability. The headline feature of C, the train connection, is therefore currently not allowed. The earliest legal departure is 05:30, which arrives at 06:25, after the 06:20 train. Even 05:15 would give only about 10 minutes to reach the train, which is tight. So C's main benefit probably fails as designed. Needs checking: whether the bylaw has an exemption or a permit route for scheduled ferry services, and whether the train leaves from Inch Harbour or somewhere further on.

2. **"Near-hourly" with one vessel needs checking.** A round trip is 110 minutes of sailing before turnarounds. One boat cannot leave each terminal hourly. At best it leaves each end about every 2 hours, which could mean a sailing roughly hourly counting both directions. I can't tell which Priya means. Pin this down before the board sees "hourly".

3. **Both loadings come from one source, and it is probably unrepresentative.** Priya and Owen both rely on the September 2024 survey. That is one dataset, not two confirmations. It was also taken while the Corran Bridge road route round the estuary was closed (March to November 2024), which likely pushed extra people onto the ferry. And the new Inch Harbour park-and-ride opened in January, after the survey, which could change who uses the ferry and when. So demand figures may be overstated, shifted, or both. I don't know the size or direction of the effect. A fresh count or spot check would settle it.

4. **The charter rejection has two different reasons.** Priya says the Dunlin's stern ramp doesn't fit the Vellmouth linkspan, which is a physical constraint. Owen says it is out only because of cost, and that it returns if the board finds money. Both can't be fully right. If the ramp really doesn't fit, money doesn't help, and Owen's note could mislead the board into thinking a second vessel is a funding question only. Confirm the linkspan point with the harbour authority or an engineer before the board hears either version. Also, no other second-vessel option is recorded as having been checked.

## How sure can we be?

- Priya says about 70% and Owen about 80%. Don't average them or treat them as two votes. They share the same demand data, and Owen's check covered only crew and fuel. Neither number accounts for the bylaw conflict.
- **My view:** C is probably workable as a service pattern (crew hours and fuel are plausible), but with the 05:15 sailing as written I would put confidence in it at roughly 40-50%, mainly because of the bylaw. If the bylaw question is resolved (permit or a revised start time) and the train connection survives, it recovers to about 60-65%. Going higher needs fresh demand data. These are my judgement, not computed figures.
- **Unchecked and worth a second look:** the fuel figure (+4% looks low for a timetable with more sailings, so I'd want to see the basis); the split-shift duty calculation against the actual first and last sailing times; and how the other two shortlisted patterns compare, since they were dropped without notes on why.

## For your week, in priority order

1. Ask the harbour authority about the 23:00-05:30 bylaw: is there an exemption for scheduled passenger services, or would the first sailing have to be 05:30 or later?
2. Confirm which train, from which station, and whether a 05:30 or later departure can still connect. If not, work out what the train connection is worth and whether another pattern serves it better.
3. Clarify what "near-hourly" means in sailings per direction per day.
4. Get Priya and Owen to settle the Dunlin reason in one sentence.
5. Decide whether to refresh demand data before the board meets, or to tell the board plainly that loadings come from a 2024 survey taken during the bridge closure.
6. Ask Owen to re-run crew hours against the revised first sailing, and to give the fuel basis.

If the board must decide before items 1 and 2 are answered, I'd present C as a provisional preference with these two conditions, not as a recommendation.
