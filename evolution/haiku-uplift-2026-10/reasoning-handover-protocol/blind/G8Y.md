# Handover: blossom-end rot in compartment 3

Daniel, this is where the problem stands and what to do first. Figures are from the notes Lindqvist left; where I had to assume something, I say so.

## What we know

- The calcium supply is not the failure point. Feed solution lab tests in weeks 32, 34 and 36 were on target every time, and leaf calcium is normal. Calcium is reaching the plant.
- The problem is calcium reaching the fruit. Calcium moves with transpiration. Fruit transpires very little, so it depends on the plant pulling water (and calcium) through the xylem. Compartment 3 has night-time humidity of 92–95%, against 85–88% elsewhere, and its vent motors only open to 40%. Low night-time transpiration is the most likely cause.
- Compartment 3 also runs a higher drip EC (3.9, against 2.8 in compartment 1 and 3.0 in compartment 2). High root-zone salinity reduces water and calcium uptake further. It may also mean salts are building up in that compartment's substrate.
- Blossom-end rot is 6–9% of fruit, nearly all in compartment 3.

## What not to do

- **Do not raise calcium nitrate.** The feed supplier's advice is wrong for this problem. Feed calcium is already on target, and more calcium nitrate would push compartment 3's EC higher, which makes uptake worse.
- **Do not spend time on foliar calcium.** Grenmark's Clean Leaf programme (renewed in July) prohibits foliar applications of any kind after first fruit set. Any foliar calcium is off the table for this crop.
- **Do not repeat the feed lab tests or leaf tissue analysis.** They have already answered the question: supply is fine.
- **Do not treat the vent motor purchase as the first step.** It is expensive and slow (€48,000, 10-week lead time). See below.

## What to do first, in order

1. **Measure before changing anything.** In compartment 3, measure drain EC and substrate EC at several points, and check the EC of the water source. Compare with compartments 1 and 2. This tells you whether the high EC is a feed problem or a build-up problem.
2. **Bring compartment 3's EC down.** If drain EC is clearly above feed EC, flush or lower the feed EC for that compartment. Confirm the drip lines in compartment 3 deliver evenly (emitter flow, blockages) because uneven delivery can make the EC reading misleading.
3. **Get night-time transpiration going with what you already have.** Check whether the vent motors are actually working as designed, whether the climate computer's night humidity setpoints and vent logic are set correctly for compartment 3, and whether the vents can be opened further manually. Use air circulation fans at night to move air over the canopy. Each of these costs little or nothing.
4. **Watch the gas budget before using heat to dry the air.** The fixed-price contract has 114,000 m³ left (410,000 minus 296,000) to the end of March. Anything above that is billed at spot, about four times the contract rate. Heating at night to lower humidity is only worth it within that allowance. Work out the weekly gas use and how many weeks of allowance are left before you rely on heat.
5. **Decide on the vent motors only after steps 1–4.** Ten weeks of lead time from now lands in late December. The crop runs until week 2, so the new motors would only help for the last few weeks of the crop. Assuming the motors would be fitted and working, the value depends on whether the fruit still setting then matters for the final harvest, and whether the motor itself is the limiting factor. Check this before paying €48,000. Note that the lead time is quoted; confirm it and whether it includes fitting.
6. **Track the result.** Count blossom-end rot on each truss, per compartment, weekly. Compare compartment 3 with the others after each change so you can tell which step worked.
