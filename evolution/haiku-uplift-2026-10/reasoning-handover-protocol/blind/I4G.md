## State of play: Vellmouth–Inch Harbour service after MV Tamsin retires

**Bottom line:** Pattern C is the leading option, but it is not ready for the board. Neither analyst's notes show that it passes the harbour bylaw, and the timetable as described may not be physically possible with one vessel. My honest confidence in C as it stands is well below the 70% and 80% in the notes, and I would not present either figure to the board.

### Where the two analyses agree
- Pattern C is the best of the three patterns kept (Priya considered seven).
- Chartering a second vessel is out for now.
- Both used the September 2024 passenger survey for loadings.

### Problems to resolve before the board

1. **The first sailing breaks the harbour bylaw.** Priya's notes give a 05:15 first sailing from Vellmouth. The bylaw bans vessel movements at Vellmouth from 23:00 to 05:30, so 05:15 is inside the ban. Neither analyst flagged this. Owen's stress test covered crew hours and fuel, not the bylaw.

2. **The bylaw fix may lose the 06:20 train.** The earliest legal departure is 05:30. With the 55-minute crossing, that arrives at about 06:25, after the 06:20 train. I have assumed the train leaves from the mainland side at Inch Harbour, so please check that. The schedule needs a different connection or a different train time.

3. **The "near-hourly" service may not be feasible with one vessel.** Each crossing takes 55 minutes, so one round trip is 110 minutes before any turnaround time. With even 10 minutes at each end, the Elder departs each terminal about every 2 hours 10 minutes, or about every 65 minutes combined across both ends. That is not near-hourly from each side. I could not see the actual timetable, so please check Pattern C against the vessel's cycle. If the stress test used a timetable that doesn't fit the cycle, Owen's crew-hour and fuel results do not hold.

4. **The charter question is contradictory.** Priya rejected the MV Dunlin because its stern ramp does not fit the Vellmouth linkspan, which is a physical constraint. Owen says charter is out only on cost, and that the Dunlin is back if money is found. Owen's version ignores the fit problem. Unless someone confirms the Dunlin can use the linkspan, it is not a real option, whatever the budget.

### How sure can we be?

- **Both confidence figures rest on the same September 2024 survey.** That is one data source, not two independent checks, so they should not be combined or treated as corroborating each other.
- **The survey may be skewed.** The Corran Bridge road route was closed from March to November 2024, so a September 2024 survey may have captured demand that was pushed onto the ferry. The road route reopened in November 2024.
- **The park-and-ride at Inch Harbour opened in January.** The survey predates it, so it may overstate ferry demand from car-based travellers.
- **Owen's 80% covers only crew hours and fuel.** Those tests do not address demand, the bylaw, or the vessel cycle, so the 80% is not a measure of the whole plan.
- **Crew hours and fuel are unverified.** The split-shift claim cannot be checked without the timetable, and the 4% fuel rise is only a figure, not a costed plan.

My judgment: C is still the most promising option, but with the bylaw breach, the vessel-cycle question and stale demand data, I would put confidence at well under 50% until those are fixed. I would not state a precise figure to the board.

### Suggested next steps before the board (about one week)
- Re-cut Pattern C with a first sailing at 05:30 or later, check the 06:20 connection, and check the timetable against the Elder's real round-trip time.
- Re-run the crew-hour and fuel tests on the corrected timetable.
- Settle the Dunlin linkspan question with the port or the vessel owner, so the board can see whether charter is a real option.
- Check whether a demand update is possible, using the park-and-ride opening and the road reopening as adjustments. If not, present loadings as uncertain.
- Present the board with a clear statement of what is verified, what is not, and why.

### Assumptions I made
- The 06:20 train departs from the mainland side at Inch Harbour.
- "Near-hourly" means departures from each terminal at roughly hourly intervals.
- I have not seen the full timetable, the seven patterns, or the survey, so my points on feasibility and demand are based only on the notes.
