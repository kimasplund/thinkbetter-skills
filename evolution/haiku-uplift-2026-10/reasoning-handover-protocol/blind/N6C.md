# Handover: Blossom-end rot in Compartment 3
Lindqvist Glasshouses | For: Daniel Achterberg, Head Grower

## Situation
- Since mid-August, 6-9% of fruit has blossom-end rot (BER). Nearly all of it is in compartment 3 (of 3; 4 ha in total, truss tomatoes).
- The crop runs to week 2. It is now about week 42, so roughly 14-15 weeks remain (assumed from today's date).
- Your job is to choose and implement a fix. The previous head grower and the owner spent three weeks on diagnosis. This document records what they found and what they ruled out.

## What we know

| Finding | Detail |
|---|---|
| Feed calcium is fine | Lab tests in weeks 32, 34 and 36 were all on target. Leaf tissue calcium is normal. |
| Compartment 3 climate is different | Night humidity is 92-95%, against 85-88% in the other compartments. The old vent motors only open to 40%. |
| Drip EC is much higher in compartment 3 | Week 36: C1 2.8, C2 3.0, **C3 3.9**. |

**Working theory:** calcium is in the feed and in the leaves but is not reaching the fruit. Calcium moves with the transpiration stream, and the fruit gets little of it when night transpiration is low. High humidity causes that, and the vents can't fix it.

**Open point, and your first priority:** the EC gap is not explained anywhere in our notes. The humidity theory doesn't account for it.
- A drip EC of 3.9 is about 1 unit above the other compartments. High EC makes it harder for roots to take up water, and it competes with calcium uptake. It could be making BER worse.
- It might be deliberate, for example EC raised to steer the crop in the humid compartment. It might also be a dosing, mixing or recipe problem specific to C3.
- We don't know which. Find out in your first days: check the C3 dosing and recipe settings, compare drip EC with drain EC, and look at the drain volume. Please also check whether the lab samples were taken from the C3 line or from a shared tank. The notes don't say.
- Correcting a wrong EC is cheap, fast and reversible. Do this before spending money on anything else.

## What not to spend time on

1. **Raising calcium nitrate.** Our feed supplier's rep keeps recommending it. The calcium is already on target in the feed and in the leaves. Adding more raises EC further, and C3 is already the high-EC compartment. We have deliberately not done it. Don't revisit it unless new test results show a real calcium shortfall.
2. **Foliar calcium sprays.** This is the usual BER rescue, but it is not available to us. Our buyer Grenmark Foods renewed us in their "Clean Leaf" programme in July, which allows no foliar applications of any kind after first fruit set. A spray risks the programme. Don't trial it, even on a small area, without talking to the owner and checking the programme terms with Grenmark first.
3. **More feed lab tests for calcium.** Three clean results are enough. Spend the effort on climate and EC.
4. **The new vent motors as a fix for this crop.** They cost €48,000 and have a 10-week lead time. Ordered this week, they arrive around week 52 or later, at the very end of the crop. That is probably too late to help this crop. Treat them as a decision for next season's budget, and use the BER data you collect now to justify it. (If the owner wants to order anyway, that is his call. It isn't the quick fix.)

## Constraint: gas
- Our fixed-price contract covers 410,000 m³ to the end of March. We have used 296,000, which leaves **114,000 m³**.
- Anything above the contract is billed at spot, currently about 4 times the contract rate.
- The obvious way to cut humidity in a glasshouse is to heat and ventilate together, which burns gas. In winter that could use up the remaining 114,000 m³ well before March.
- Before you commit to a heating-based fix, work out how much extra gas it needs per week and project it to the end of March. Then decide whether the BER loss costs more than the extra gas would. If you have the figures, compare it with the value of the BER fruit lost.
- I don't have our weekly gas burn rate in these notes. You will need it from the owner or the climate computer.

## Options to consider
These are starting points, not recommendations. Everything below stays within the Clean Leaf rule, because none of it is applied to the leaves.

1. **Check and correct C3 EC** (see section 2). Do this first, since it is cheap and might be the main cause.
2. **Get night humidity down in C3 with what we have.** Options include a short heat-and-vent pulse, adjusted night setpoints, and horizontal air-flow fans if C3 has them. Cost this against the gas constraint above. Measure whether it brings night humidity toward 88%.
3. **Adjust irrigation and crop steering for C3** so the plant is under less stress at night. This needs your judgement. I'm not prescribing a recipe.
4. **Crop and fruit management in C3.** Consider fruit load and removal of affected fruit. Keep the grading and loss numbers so we can see whether any change helps.
5. **Vent motors.** Plan for next season (see section 3).

## What I'd ask you to do in week 1
- Read the C3 EC settings and compare drip and drain EC. Confirm whether the high reading is deliberate.
- Get the weekly gas figures and calculate the gas remaining at the current rate.
- Start a weekly record for each compartment of BER percentage, night humidity and drip EC, so that any change you make can be judged against it.
- Agree with the owner how much extra gas spend he will accept.
- Check the Clean Leaf terms with Grenmark if anyone proposes anything that touches the leaves.

## Gaps in what we know
- We don't know why C3 EC is 3.9.
- Whether the lab samples came from the C3 line is unconfirmed.
- Our weekly gas consumption is not in these notes.
- Humidity is our best explanation, but it is not proven. A good test is to improve one thing in C3 and see whether the BER rate falls over the following 2-3 weeks. Fruit set now shows up as rot some weeks later, so don't expect fast results.
