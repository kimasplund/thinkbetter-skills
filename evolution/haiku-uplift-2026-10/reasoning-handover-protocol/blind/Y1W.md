**State of play: MV Elder solo service, Vellmouth–Inch Harbour (after MV Tamsin retires in April)**

**Bottom line:** Pattern C is not ready for the board. Both analysts' confidence figures (about 70% and about 80%) overstate what the work supports, and the timetable as written breaks a harbour bylaw. Fix that first, then the rest of the case can be tested properly.

**1. The timetable breaks the harbour bylaw (not flagged by either analyst)**
- Pattern C has the first sailing from Vellmouth at 05:15.
- The harbour authority bylaw bans vessel movements at Vellmouth from 23:00 to 05:30.
- 05:15 falls inside that window. The earliest legal departure is 05:30.
- A 05:30 departure arrives at Inch Harbour around 06:25, so it misses the 06:20 mainland train. The islanders' main connection is therefore lost under the bylaw. This undermines the core purpose of Pattern C.
- Owen's crew-hours and fuel checks were run on the 05:15 version, so they need rerunning once the start time is corrected.

**2. Near-hourly service with one vessel needs checking**
- Each crossing takes 55 minutes, so one round trip is at least 110 minutes before turnaround time at each end.
- A single Elder therefore cannot give a departure every hour from each side. The realistic frequency is closer to one departure per side every two hours or so.
- Neither note explains how near-hourly service works with one vessel. This must be resolved before the pattern is judged on anything else. Either the frequency claim is wrong, or the plan needs a second vessel.

**3. The charter option is contested, not closed**
- Priya rejected the MV Dunlin on physical grounds: its stern ramp does not fit the Vellmouth linkspan.
- Owen rejected it on cost only, and assumes the Dunlin is back on if the board finds money.
- These are different reasons. If the ramp does not fit, money does not solve it. Someone with linkspan or vessel knowledge should confirm the fit question before the board is told the Dunlin is an option.
- If the Dunlin is viable, it may be the only way to get the hourly frequency in point 2. That makes the question more important, not less.

**4. The loadings are weak evidence**
- The loadings come from a passenger survey in September 2024.
- The Corran Bridge road route was closed from March to November 2024, so the survey ran during a closure. Travel patterns then were likely distorted, for example by more or fewer people using the ferry.
- The council's Inch Harbour park-and-ride opened in January, which may change demand at the Inch Harbour end. The survey predates it.
- Demand figures should be treated as uncertain until refreshed or checked against current sailing and ticket data.

**5. How sure can we be?**
- Priya's 70% for C predates the bylaw finding. It should be treated as unsupported.
- Owen's 80% comes from a stress test of the same timetable, so it does not add independent confidence. It also does not cover the bylaw breach or the frequency issue.
- Honest view: we cannot put a meaningful percentage on Pattern C today. Confidence is low until items 1 to 4 are resolved. Once they are, a fresh estimate is worth making.
- Priya's rejection of the other four patterns and Owen's crew and fuel checks are the parts most likely to survive. The three kept patterns other than C were not described in the notes, so I cannot assess them.

**Suggested actions before the board (one week)**
1. Rework Pattern C with a first sailing no earlier than 05:30, and check whether any connection can be kept.
2. Confirm how frequency is achievable with one vessel, or drop the near-hourly claim.
3. Get a definitive answer on whether the Dunlin fits the linkspan. Separate that from the cost question.
4. Flag the 2024 survey as dated and possibly distorted by the bridge closure. Say whether a refresh is possible before the decision.
5. Rerun crew-hours and fuel on the corrected timetable.
6. Present the board with a confidence level described in words (for example low, pending items 1 to 4), not the 70% and 80% figures.

**Assumptions in this summary**
- The bylaw applies to all vessel movements at Vellmouth, including the first sailing.
- Vellmouth is the island side and the 06:20 train is at Inch Harbour.
- The notes describe what the analysts found, and I have not checked the underlying data.
