## Problems to resolve

1. **The 05:15 first sailing breaks the harbour bylaw.** Priya herself recorded that no vessel movements are allowed at Vellmouth between 23:00 and 05:30. A 05:15 departure is a movement inside that window. Owen's stress test covered crew hours and fuel, not the bylaw, so his result doesn't clear it.
   - The earliest legal departure is 05:30. With a 55-minute crossing that arrives at 06:25, after the 06:20 train. The reason for choosing 05:15 therefore fails.
   - Even 05:15 was tight. It arrives at 06:10, which leaves a 10-minute connection to the train.
   - Options: ask the harbour authority for a dispensation or exemption, or give up the 06:20 connection and target a later train. A third option is to have the Elder overnight on the mainland side and re-plan the early run, but check whether that works for the islanders it is meant to serve.
   - This is the first thing to check this week. It is a phone call, not an analysis.

2. **The two notes disagree on why the charter was rejected.**
   - Priya says the Dunlin is physically unusable because its stern ramp doesn't fit the Vellmouth linkspan.
   - Owen says it is out only because of cost, and that it comes back if the board finds money.
   - These can't both be right. If Priya is correct, extra money doesn't help, and the board could be told something false. Ask her what the ramp finding is based on (survey, drawings or an operator statement). Until then, treat the Dunlin as ruled out on fit, with cost as an unverified second reason.

3. **The loadings are a single source, and a biased one.**
   - The September 2024 survey was taken while the Corran Bridge was closed (March to November 2024). Anyone who would normally drive round the estuary was pushed onto the ferry, so the loadings probably overstate normal demand.
   - The Inch Harbour park-and-ride opened in January, after the survey. It may shift how people use the crossing, in either direction.
   - Both analysts used these same numbers. Owen's 80% is not independent confirmation of Priya's 70%. His check tested crew and fuel on the same demand assumptions, so the two confidences shouldn't be averaged or added up.
   - The consequence is that frequency, vessel capacity and the case for a second vessel all rest on possibly inflated demand. The error could cut either way. A smaller true demand makes the Elder alone easier, but it also weakens the justification for early and late sailings.

4. **"Near-hourly" with one vessel needs checking.** A 55-minute crossing means a full round trip takes well over 110 minutes with turnarounds. One vessel can run an hourly sailing in a given direction only if departures alternate between ends, which gives each end a sailing about every two hours. I don't have the timetable, so I can't tell which one C means. Confirm what "near-hourly" means at each terminal, because the board will read it as hourly from both ends.

5. **Smaller points.** Owen's fuel figure of +4% is measured against today, which includes two vessels. Check the baseline is like-for-like. Also confirm the split shift has been agreed with the crew and isn't just a calculation.
