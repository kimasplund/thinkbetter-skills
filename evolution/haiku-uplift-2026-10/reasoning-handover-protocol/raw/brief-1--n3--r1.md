# Handover: water-damaged survey maps (for Tobias)

## Situation
A sprinkler head failed over the map store nine days ago. 212 hand-coloured survey maps (1790s-1840s) were soaked and are now frozen at -20 °C, which holds them stable. Your job is to choose and contract a drying method.

## The two facts that shape everything
1. **Ownership split.** 151 maps are ours. 61 are the Brekke deposit. Clause 7 of the 2009 loan agreement says deposited items "shall not be removed from the Archive premises for any purpose without the prior written consent of the Lender". So none of the 61 can go to any offsite vendor until we hold written consent. The Brekke executor can only be reached through their solicitor.
2. **Money.** Valtera Mutual reimburses drying up to EUR 6,000 in total, from any accredited vendor. Our remaining conservation budget this year is EUR 1,100. That makes EUR 7,100 of usable funds.

## Options
| Option | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Kestrel (offsite thermal vacuum drying) | **Ruled out** | Dr Aino Pell, our freelance conservator, examined samples. The maps have watercolour washes in gum arabic and verdigris green on the boundaries. Kestrel's process heats items to 40-60 °C. She says that is fine for office paper but will bleed and cockle water-soluble media. |
| Nordlicht (offsite vacuum freeze-drying) | **Leading option** | EUR 38 per map plus EUR 420 flat transport. Pell thinks it is probably fine for the verdigris, but wants a test run first (see below). |
| Air-dry in-house on interleaving | Possible fallback | Table space for about 25 maps at a time, roughly 3 days per batch. |
| Keep frozen until next fiscal year | Weak | Delays the problem. I have not checked how long the maps can safely stay frozen. |

**Kestrel's 30% discount (EUR 13.30 per map, offered yesterday).** I would not reopen this. The objection is the heat damaging the media, not the price, and a lower price doesn't change that. Only revisit it if Pell says a gentler Kestrel process exists, and I have no sign that it does.

## Numbers
- Nordlicht, our 151 maps only: 151 x 38 + 420 = **EUR 6,158**. That is EUR 158 over the Valtera cap, which the EUR 1,100 budget covers easily.
- Nordlicht, all 212 maps: 212 x 38 + 420 = **EUR 8,476**. That is EUR 1,376 more than the EUR 7,100 we can draw on. It only works if someone else pays the difference, for example Brekke's own insurer or the Brekke estate.
- In-house air-drying of the 61 Brekke maps: three batches (25, 25, 11), so about 9 days of table time. Its cost isn't covered by Valtera as far as I know, so check.

## Suggested plan
1. **This week, in parallel.** Ask Brekke's solicitor in writing for consent to move the 61 maps offsite for freeze-drying. Tell them about the damage if that has not already been done, and ask who bears the cost for their items. The solicitor is the slow step, so start now.
2. **Test run.** Ask Pell to pick the two least significant maps, from our 151 and not from Brekke. Send them to Nordlicht first and have Pell assess the results, particularly the verdigris. Ask Nordlicht whether a pilot batch means a second EUR 420 transport charge. If it does, our 151 maps cost EUR 6,578, which is still within the EUR 7,100.
3. **If the test passes,** send the remaining 149 of our maps to Nordlicht.
4. **Brekke maps.** If consent arrives and the money is found, send them to Nordlicht too. If not, ask Pell whether in-house air-drying is acceptable for these media, since it is also wet-handling. Otherwise they stay frozen until consent or funding is settled. Do not move them without written consent.

## Things I have not confirmed
- Is Nordlicht accredited with Valtera, and does Valtera need pre-approval or an invoice format before it pays? Ask the adjuster.
- Does Valtera's cover extend to the Brekke items, which we hold on deposit?
- How long Nordlicht's quote holds, and their turnaround time.
- When the fiscal year ends, and whether the freezers have capacity and a safe long-term hold.
- Whether there is any mould on the maps. Ask Pell what she saw.
