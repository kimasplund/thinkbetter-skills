**Short version:** Pattern C is not safe to take to the board as it stands. Neither analyst's confidence figure (70% and 80%) should be used. I'd put it well below either, until the items below are resolved. Two of them are potential deal-breakers and are quick to check.

## State of play

- Seven timetable patterns were considered and three kept. Pattern C is the front-runner. It is near-hourly, with a first sailing from Vellmouth at 05:15 to connect with the 06:20 mainland train.
- Owen's stress test found C workable on crew hours (split shift within the 12-hour duty limit) and fuel (+4% on today).
- Chartering the MV Dunlin as a second vessel is currently out. The two analysts give different reasons (see below).
- Both analysts used the same loadings data, the September 2024 passenger survey.

## Problems that need your attention

1. **The 05:15 first sailing breaks the harbour bylaw.** Priya's own notes say no vessel movements at Vellmouth between 23:00 and 05:30. A 05:15 departure is inside that ban. Neither analyst joined the two facts up. This is not a detail. The whole reason for C's first sailing is the 06:20 train, so C as designed cannot run without a bylaw exemption.
   - Even if the sailing were legal, a 05:15 departure arrives about 06:10, leaving roughly 10 minutes to reach the 06:20 train. That is probably unworkable unless the station is at the quay. If the first sailing moves to 05:30 or later, the train connection is lost, and that connection is the point of C.
   - Next steps: ask the harbour authority whether an exemption is realistic. If not, C needs redesigning around the 05:30 limit, and the 06:20 train may have to be given up for an alternative. Owen's crew and fuel results would also need rerunning, because they were built on the 05:15 start.

2. **The frequency needs checking against the arithmetic.** With one vessel and a 55-minute crossing, a round trip is at least 110 minutes plus turnarounds. That is about one departure every two hours from each port. "Near-hourly" only works if it means combined departures across both ends. Please confirm which one Pattern C means, because the board will read "hourly" as hourly from each port. I'm assuming the analysts meant combined, but the notes don't say.

3. **The demand data is probably out of date and biased.** The September 2024 survey was taken while the Corran Bridge was closed (March to November 2024). The road route round the estuary was cut, so the ferry would have carried traffic that normally drives. Since then the bridge has reopened and the new Inch Harbour park-and-ride has opened (January). Loadings from that survey are probably overstated, and the park-and-ride may change how people travel in ways the survey can't show. Both analysts relied on it, so their agreement is not independent confirmation. A fresh count or a recent sailing-load sample would be far better than adjusting the old one.

4. **The charter decision rests on conflicting reasons.** Priya rejected the Dunlin because its stern ramp doesn't fit the Vellmouth linkspan, which is a physical constraint. Owen says it's out only on cost and returns if the board finds money. Both can't be true. If Priya is right, money doesn't help unless the linkspan is modified or another vessel is found. If Owen is right, the board may be given a false choice. Please settle this before the board meets, because it shapes what you can offer them. A linkspan modification (cost and lead time) could be a board option in its own right.

## How sure can we be?

- Priya's 70% and Owen's 80% are each judged against different things. Priya is judging the pattern, while Owen is judging only crew and fuel. Neither covers the bylaw conflict, the frequency question or the survey bias.
- The two figures shouldn't be averaged or treated as agreement. They share the same data and the same flawed first-sailing time.
- My honest view is that the choice of Pattern C over the other two kept patterns is unproven. The other two may have none of these problems, so check whether either has a legal and workable first sailing.
- What does hold, subject to rechecking: a single-vessel service is feasible on crew hours within the limit, and fuel is a modest increase.
