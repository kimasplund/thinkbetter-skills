## Merged state of play

- **Problem:** The MV Tamsin retires in April. The MV Elder will be alone on Vellmouth–Inch Harbour (55 min each way).
- **Work done:** Priya considered seven patterns and kept three. Her preferred pattern is C, near-hourly service with a 05:15 first sailing from Vellmouth for the 06:20 mainland train. Owen stress-tested C on crew hours and fuel. It passed: a split shift keeps crew within the 12-hour duty limit, and fuel is up 4%.
- **Charter option:** The MV Dunlin is the only vessel available. It is currently treated as out.
- **Stated confidence:** Priya about 70%, Owen about 80%.

## Problems found when the two sets of notes are put side by side

1. **The 05:15 first sailing breaks the harbour bylaw.** Priya's own note says no vessel movements at Vellmouth between 23:00 and 05:30. A 05:15 departure is inside that ban. Owen's stress test covered crew and fuel only, so nobody has checked this. As written, Pattern C's headline feature is not allowed. Options are to move the first sailing to 05:30 or later, which probably misses the 06:20 train, or to seek a bylaw exemption from the harbour authority. Which of these is possible is unknown.
2. **The train connection is tight even if the bylaw is solved.** A 05:15 departure arrives at about 06:10. That leaves about 10 minutes to reach the 06:20 train. I'm assuming the station is at or beside Inch Harbour, but the notes don't say so. A 05:30 departure arrives at about 06:25, after the train.
3. **The two analysts disagree about why the Dunlin is out.** Priya says it is physically unusable because its stern ramp doesn't fit the Vellmouth linkspan. Owen says it is out only on cost, and that it comes back if the board finds money. Both can't be right. If Priya is right, extra money doesn't help, and Owen's "fallback" is not a fallback. This is easy to settle with the linkspan and ramp dimensions. Until it is settled, don't tell the board a second vessel is available if they pay.
4. **The passenger data is probably out of date and biased.** Both analysts rely on the September 2024 survey. The Corran Bridge, the road alternative round the estuary, was closed from March to November 2024. That means the survey was taken when islanders and visitors had little choice but the ferry. Since then the bridge has reopened, and the Inch Harbour park-and-ride opened in January. Ferry demand, peak timing and the share wanting early sailings may all differ from the survey. This affects the choice of pattern, not only the loadings.
5. **Owen's 80% is not independent confirmation.** He tested only crew and fuel. He did not test demand, the bylaw, or the train timing, and he uses the same survey as Priya. His result is that C is feasible on crew and fuel. It does not support a higher overall confidence.
6. **A point to check on "near-hourly".** With one vessel on a 55-minute crossing, a round trip takes about 110 minutes plus turnarounds. So each port gets a departure only about every two hours. Sailings are hourly only in the sense that they alternate between ends. If the board hears "hourly from Vellmouth", that would be wrong. The notes don't say which is meant, so please confirm what C actually offers at each end.

## How sure can we be

I would put real confidence in Pattern C at well below either analyst's figure, perhaps 30-40%, and I'd call that a rough judgement and not a measurement. The reasons are the bylaw conflict, the unverified connection to the train, and demand data from an abnormal period. Some things are firmer: C is feasible on crew hours and fuel, if Owen's split-shift numbers are right. The wider question of whether a three-pattern shortlist built on the old data still contains the best option is open. The two rejected patterns and the other four patterns that were dropped were presumably judged on the same survey.
