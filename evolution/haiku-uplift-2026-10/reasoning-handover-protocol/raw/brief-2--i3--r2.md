# State of play: Vellmouth–Inch Harbour timetable after the MV Tamsin retires

## The problem

The MV Tamsin retires in April, leaving the MV Elder as the only vessel on the Vellmouth–Inch Harbour crossing. Each crossing takes 55 minutes, and Vellmouth is the island side. The board decides in one week. The aim is a timetable that keeps the island connected, with the first sailing from Vellmouth getting islanders to the 06:20 mainland train.

## Bottom line

Pattern C, as the two analysts wrote it, cannot go to the board unchanged. Its first sailing from Vellmouth at 05:15 falls inside the harbour bylaw's closed period (no vessel movements at Vellmouth between 23:00 and 05:30). Neither analysis mentions this, and Priya's own notes list the bylaw. Pattern C needs a timing fix before it is credible, and that fix pulls against the 06:20 train connection.

## What each analyst found

**Priya**
- Considered seven timetable patterns and kept three. Pattern C is best: near-hourly service, first sailing from Vellmouth at 05:15.
- Rejected a charter of the MV Dunlin because its stern ramp does not fit the Vellmouth linkspan. This is a physical constraint, not only a cost issue.
- Loadings come from the September 2024 passenger survey.
- Her confidence in C: about 70%.

**Owen**
- Stress-tested Pattern C on crew hours and fuel. Both hold: crew stay inside the 12-hour duty limit with a split shift, and fuel is up 4% on today.
- Ruled out the charter because of cost. He says the Dunlin returns if the board finds the money.
- His confidence in C: about 80%.

## Problems the two analyses do not resolve

1. **The 05:15 first sailing breaks the bylaw.** The closed period runs to 05:30, so no departure from Vellmouth before then is allowed. The options are:
   - Move the first sailing to 05:30 or later. A 05:30 departure reaches Inch Harbour around 06:25, which misses the 06:20 train.
   - Keep 05:15 and get the bylaw changed or an exemption. This is unlikely to be quick and is not in either analysis.
   - Keep the train connection and accept that islanders cannot make it on the first sailing. This needs a decision, not more analysis.
   Owen's crew and fuel checks were done on the 05:15 timing, so they need rerunning once the start time changes.

2. **The Dunlin is not a free option.** Owen says the charter returns if money is found. Priya's finding is that the Dunlin's stern ramp does not fit the Vellmouth linkspan. Money does not fix that. Unless someone confirms the ramp can be modified or the linkspan is not the only berth, the charter stays out whatever the budget.

3. **The loadings may not describe the future.** The September 2024 survey ran while the Corran Bridge road route was closed (March to November 2024), so road-to-ferry demand that year may have been unusual. The council's park-and-ride at Inch Harbour opened in January and may draw car users away from the ferry. Neither effect has been checked.

4. **The fuel comparison has no clear baseline.** "Up 4% on today" compares a one-vessel plan with the current two-vessel service, or it uses a baseline that is not stated. The 4% figure is not yet meaningful until the baseline is defined.

5. **Crew hours depend on the split shift.** The split-shift plan keeps crew within 12 hours, but the timing of the shift against the closed period has not been checked.

## How sure we can be

Both analysts' confidence figures (70% and 80%) describe Pattern C before the bylaw problem was found. They should not be averaged or relied on as they stand.

- **Pattern C as written:** low confidence, around 25 to 35%. A hard constraint is violated.
- **Pattern C with the first sailing moved to 05:30 or later:** moderate confidence, around 50 to 60%. The main limits are the stale loadings, the unchecked crew and fuel figures for the new timing, and the unresolved connection to the 06:20 train.
- **Charter with the Dunlin:** not viable until the stern-ramp problem is solved, whatever the cost.

These are my estimates from the notes provided, not measured values.

## Open questions to settle before the board

1. Can the first sailing move to 05:30 or later, and is missing the 06:20 train acceptable, or is there another way to protect it?
2. Can the bylaw closed period be changed or exempted, and how long would that take?
3. Can the Dunlin's stern ramp be adapted for the Vellmouth linkspan, or is the linkspan the only berth?
4. Would a new passenger survey, or a check against current ticket data, give loadings that reflect today's road and park-and-ride conditions?
5. What is the fuel baseline, and does the 4% figure compare like with like?
6. Has Owen's crew and fuel analysis been rerun for the revised timing?

## Suggested next steps before the board

- Rerun Pattern C with the first sailing at 05:30 or later, and with the 06:20 train treated as a fixed constraint or as a trade-off.
- Confirm the Dunlin's ramp and linkspan position with the harbour authority.
- Check the loadings against current data, or label them clearly as 2024 figures in the board paper.
- Present the 05:30 issue to the board as a decision, because it is a trade-off between the train connection and the bylaw.
