# Handover: blossom-end rot in compartment 3, Lindqvist Glasshouses

For: Daniel Achterberg, head grower (starts Monday)
From: Owner

## Problem statement

Since mid-August, 6–9% of truss tomato fruit has shown blossom-end rot across 4 ha in three compartments. Nearly all of it is in compartment 3. Your job is to choose and implement a fix.

Constraints you must work within:
- **Foliar sprays are out.** Grenmark Foods' Clean Leaf programme (renewed July) bans all foliar applications after first fruit set.
- **Gas is limited.** The fixed-price contract covers 410,000 m³ to the end of March. 296,000 m³ is used, so about **114,000 m³** remains at contract rate. Beyond that, gas is billed at spot, roughly four times the contract rate.
- **Timing.** The crop runs until week 2. The new vent motors have a 10-week lead time.

## What we know

1. **Calcium supply in the solution is on target.** Feed solution lab tests in weeks 32, 34 and 36 all matched target. Leaf tissue calcium is normal. The problem is not the calcium in the feed tank.
2. **Compartment 3 is humid at night.** Night humidity runs 92–95%, against 85–88% in the other compartments. Its old vent motors only open to 40%. Our best explanation is low night-time transpiration. Calcium moves to fruit mainly with transpiration water, so fruit in a humid, still house can get too little calcium even when the solution is correct. This is the leading explanation, not a confirmed cause.
3. **Compartment 3 has higher drip EC.** Week 36 readings: compartment 1, 2.8; compartment 2, 3.0; compartment 3, 3.9. This is a separate finding, not yet explained. High root-zone salinity can reduce calcium uptake, so it may be part of the same problem, but we have not tested that link.

## What we have not done, and why

- **We have not raised calcium nitrate.** The feed supplier's rep has suggested this repeatedly. The tank is already on target, so more calcium would not fix the transport problem. It would also push EC higher in compartment 3, which is already the highest.

## Where to start

Focus on these, in roughly this order:

1. **Investigate compartment 3's drip EC.** Check whether the 3.9 reading is spread across the compartment or comes from a few lines or emitters. Check for blocked or uneven emitters and for drainage. Consider reducing EC in compartment 3 toward the other compartments' level.
2. **Improve night-time air movement in compartment 3 with the existing equipment.** Open the existing vents to their 40% maximum at night where weather allows, and use circulation fans if you have them. Night heating also helps dry the air, but it spends gas. Budget it carefully against the 114,000 m³ remaining.
3. **Order the compartment 3 vent motors now.** At €48,000 with a 10-week lead time, they will only arrive in time to matter if ordered immediately. Treat them as the permanent fix for the humidity cause.
4. **Monitor.** Track fruit for blossom-end rot per truss, compartment 3 night humidity, and drip EC each week. Fruit set in compartment 3 after the changes is the real test.

## Do not spend time on

- Raising calcium nitrate, or any calcium adjustment to the feed tank. The tank is already correct.
- Foliar calcium or any other foliar product. Clean Leaf forbids it.
- Repeating the leaf or feed calcium tests as the main line of work. They have already shown the supply is adequate.
- Heavy night heating in compartment 3 as a long-term plan. It will use up the contract gas quickly, and spot gas costs about four times as much.

## Open questions

- What is the cause of compartment 3's higher EC? Is it drip uniformity, emitter flow, or how the feed is distributed?
- How much does night ventilation at 40% actually change compartment 3's humidity?
- Does lowering EC in compartment 3 reduce rot on its own?

## Confidence

- Low night-time transpiration as the main cause in compartment 3: **moderate, capped at about 0.6.** It is the best fit with the evidence, but it is untested.
- The EC difference as a contributing factor: **low, capped at about 0.4.** It is unexplained.
- Calcium supply in the tank as the cause: **low, about 0.1.** Lab results point away from it.
