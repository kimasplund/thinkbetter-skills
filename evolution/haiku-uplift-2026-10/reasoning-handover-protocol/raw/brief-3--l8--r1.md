# Handover: blossom-end rot in compartment 3

For Daniel Achterberg, head grower from Monday.

## Where things stand

Blossom-end rot (BER) is running at 6–9% of fruit since mid-August, nearly all of it in compartment 3. The feed is not short of calcium: lab tests in weeks 32, 34 and 36 were on target, and leaf tissue calcium is normal. So the problem is getting calcium to the fruit, not supplying it to the root zone.

The working explanation is low night-time transpiration in compartment 3. Calcium moves to fruit mainly with water through the xylem, and fruit transpire very little, so the fruit depend on the plant's transpiration stream to bring calcium in. Night humidity in compartment 3 sits at 92–95%, against 85–88% elsewhere. Its vent motors only open to 40%, so it cannot vent enough to get moist night air out. Less water moving through the plant means less calcium reaching the fruit.

The drip EC readings from week 36 fit the same picture. Compartment 1 is 2.8, compartment 2 is 3.0, and compartment 3 is 3.9. Lower water uptake concentrates salts in the root zone, so compartment 3 is likely taking up less water relative to nutrients.

This is the best explanation we have, not a proven cause. Treat the steps below as a test.

## What not to spend time on

- **Raising calcium nitrate.** The feed is already on target, and adding calcium raises EC further in a compartment that is already at 3.9. The supplier rep's advice would likely make this worse. Do not do it.
- **Foliar calcium sprays of any kind.** Grenmark's Clean Leaf programme bans foliar applications after first fruit set. Do not use them.
- **Repeating the feed solution or leaf calcium tests.** They have already been done and came back normal. Only repeat them if something changes.
- **Re-investigating compartments 1 and 2.** They are not showing the problem.
- **Searching for a new cause before testing the transpiration theory.** Finish the cheap tests first.

## What to do now

1. **Get more night air out of compartment 3 with what is available.** Open the vents to their 40% limit on a night-time schedule, and run circulation fans at night to move humid air around the crop. Log the vent position and humidity each night so the effect can be measured.
2. **Bring compartment 3's EC down toward the others.** Reduce the feed concentration or increase the drip volume or drain in compartment 3 until EC is close to 3.0. This is cheap, reversible, and tests the EC link directly.
3. **Consider night heating to dry the air.** Heating pipes can lower night humidity, but they use gas. Only about 114,000 m³ remains under the fixed-price contract (410,000 minus 296,000) until the end of March, and anything above that costs about four times the contract rate. Estimate the gas use for the nights needed before you commit, and track it against that remaining allowance.
4. **Track the result weekly.** Count BER by truss in compartment 3 and compare it with the other compartments, along with night humidity and EC. Give the stop-gap steps two to three weeks before judging them.

## The vent motors

The new motors for compartment 3 cost €48,000 with a 10-week lead time. If ordered this week, they would arrive around late December, leaving roughly 6–8 weeks of crop before week 2. That is still worth something, but less than the full season. Get the order decision from the owner now, because the lead time runs out whether or not the decision is made. The owner should weigh the €48,000 against the fruit lost at 6–9% BER and the value of compartment 3's yield over the rest of this season and next.

## Assumptions

- Today is around week 41, so the crop runs about 10 more weeks to week 2.
- The EC readings in the brief were taken from the drip lines and reflect the root zone in each compartment.
- The gas figures in the brief are current, and no other gas use is already committed.
- Nothing has changed in compartment 3 since the August onset apart from what is described here.
