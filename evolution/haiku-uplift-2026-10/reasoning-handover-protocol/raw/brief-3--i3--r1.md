# Handover: blossom-end rot in compartment 3

For: Daniel Achterberg, head grower from Monday
From: Owner, Lindqvist Glasshouses

## The problem

Since mid-August, 6–9% of truss tomato fruit has shown blossom-end rot. Nearly all of it is in compartment 3. The crop runs to about week 2. Your job is to choose and implement a fix.

## What we know

- **Calcium supply is not the failure point.** Feed solution lab tests in weeks 32, 34 and 36 were on target every time, and leaf tissue calcium is normal. Adding more calcium nitrate to the feed would not address the problem, and our feed supplier's suggestion to do that is one we have not taken.
- **Compartment 3 is humid at night.** Night humidity there runs 92–95%, against 85–88% in the other compartments. Its vent motors only open to 40%. Our working explanation is low night-time transpiration: with little water moving through the plant, calcium is not carried to the fruit, even when the solution contains enough of it.
- **Compartment 3 drip EC is high.** Week 36 readings: compartment 1, 2.8; compartment 2, 3.0; compartment 3, 3.9. A high root-zone EC is a second possible contributor, because it can reduce water and calcium uptake. We have not confirmed this.

## Constraints you must work within

- **No foliar applications after first fruit set.** Our Grenmark Foods "Clean Leaf" contract forbids them. Do not spend time on foliar calcium, even though it is the obvious quick fix.
- **Gas budget.** The fixed-price contract covers 410,000 m³ to the end of March. We have used 296,000 m³, leaving about 114,000 m³. Gas above that is billed at spot, roughly four times the contract rate. Any heating-based fix needs to be costed against that remaining allowance first.
- **Vent motors.** New motors for compartment 3 are quoted at €48,000 with a 10-week lead time. Ordered today, they would arrive around late December, which leaves very little of the crop after installation. Treat them as a longer-term fix, not the first step.

## Where I would start

1. **Get compartment 3 venting at night now, using what we already have.** Open the existing vents manually or by hand-set timers as far as they will go, and run circulation fans if they are available. The aim is to bring night humidity closer to 85–88% and restore transpiration. Watch for cold-night damage and check the heating budget as you go.
2. **Check drip EC in compartment 3 and bring it closer to compartments 1 and 2.** Measure at several points in the drip line, not only at the feed tank, and reduce the EC of the solution sent to compartment 3 if the root-zone reading is high. Do not raise calcium to compensate.
3. **Track the result for two to three weeks.** Count new blossom-end rot per truss in compartment 3 against compartments 1 and 2, and record night humidity and EC every day so you can see whether the changes are working.
4. **Decide on the motors in early November.** If night venting alone clears the humidity problem, the €48,000 order may not be needed for this crop. If it does not, the order has to go in now to matter at all.

## What not to spend time on

- Raising calcium nitrate or changing the calcium content of the feed. The lab results rule this out as the first cause.
- Foliar calcium or any other foliar product after first fruit set.
- Re-running feed solution lab tests in the short term. Their results are already on target.
- Heating to fix humidity. Gas above the contract allowance is roughly four times more expensive, so it should be a last resort.

## Open questions

- Is the high EC in compartment 3 a cause or only a symptom of the humidity problem?
- How much of the remaining 114,000 m³ does the crop need through to week 2, and how much can be saved by better night venting?
- Do the old vent motors have a repair option that would get them past 40% for less than €48,000?

## Confidence

Moderate, around 60%. The humidity and transpiration explanation fits the pattern, which is concentrated in one compartment, and the calcium supply checks are clean. But we have not confirmed the mechanism directly, and the EC result could point to a separate root-zone problem. Treat the first two steps as tests, and let the three-week count decide what to do next.
