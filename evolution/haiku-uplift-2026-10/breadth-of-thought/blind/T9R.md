There are more realistic options than "buy the field or don't". The key point for council is that the field is about seven times bigger than you need, and two of your own assets (the reserved plots and Ferney Bank) may be worth more than anything the farmer is offering.

## How big the need is

- At 95 burials a year, 25 years needs roughly 2,400 graves. You have 360, so the shortfall is about 2,000.
- At typical cemetery layouts, that is around 2 to 3 hectares. This is my rough figure, and your bereavement officer should confirm it for your layout. It is not 14.
- The asking price works out at about £82,000 a hectare, which is well above what I would expect for plain farmland. That suggests the price includes hope of development. Ask for an independent valuation before anyone treats £1.15m as the real market price.
- Don't plan on cremation rising. Six years flat at 70% is the best evidence you have, so use a flat 95 a year as the base case.

A fallback sits behind these. Compulsory purchase of part of the field exists, but it is slow, adversarial and needs higher approval. Mention it only so councillors know it is there.

## Two things that change the picture

**The reserved plots.** Rights granted in the 1960s and 70s for 75 years expire between about 2035 and 2054. That is inside your 25-year window but after your 360 spaces run out around 2030. So they can supply roughly 380 more graves later, but they leave a gap of about five years. Voluntary surrender (writing to owners and heirs, perhaps with a refund) could bring some of that forward. Others will not be traceable or will want to use their plots, and you would need to keep those.

**Ferney Bank.** If the site survey and planning work out, 3 hectares you already own covers the whole 25-year need. It is the only route that avoids a large purchase and does not depend on another party's goodwill. It is also the one with the most unknowns, and it is quick to test.

## How the options combine

No single option is likely to cover the whole 25 years safely, so a package is more realistic. Two sensible ones:

- **Cheapest and lowest risk:** Option 7 or a fee change to bridge to about 2035, then Option 5 as grants expire, with Option 4 as the long-term site if it checks out. The remaining cost is modest and nothing is committed to a large purchase.
- **Most certain supply:** Option 1 with Option 2. It gives certainty and a single site, but only if you can recover a good part of the cost on the surplus. Otherwise the repayments land on residents.

My view is that the full-field purchase should only go to council as the last of three or four routes, not the first.

## What to find out before January

1. Whether the 360 figure includes or excludes the 380 reserved plots (I have assumed it excludes them), and whether the 95 burials include ashes interments.
2. The exact dates and wording of the reserved grants, and a legal view on reclaiming them.
3. Ferney Bank's distance from Sorrel Lane, its ground and drainage, any covenants or open-space status, and a rough layout cost.
4. Whether Ashby Combe would sell or share capacity, and why their take-up has been low.
5. An independent valuation of the field, and whether the farmer would accept a buyer for the surplus.
6. Local fee comparisons, so any fee change is grounded.
