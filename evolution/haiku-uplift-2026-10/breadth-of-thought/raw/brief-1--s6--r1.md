# Options for the Brannock Ridge water supply

The helicopter option cannot carry the hut through a dry spell at full occupancy, and the late-June overflow is water you currently lose. Both facts shape the list below. Here are five options worth putting to members, with the numbers behind them.

## The options

**1. Store the June surplus.** The spring overflows hard in late June, so the water a late-summer shortfall needs is already running past the hut. Your August shortfall lasted 19 days. At 55% occupancy (about 3.3 m³ a day) that is roughly 63 m³ to store, against the 12 m³ tank you have now, which holds about two days at full use. A larger covered tank at the spring, or a second tank filled from the overflow, is the direct fix. Cost is unknown, so get two quotes once the hydrologist has measured the June overflow volume. Weakness: it does nothing in a year when June is poor and the store never fills.

**2. Cut demand and shorten the late season.** This is cheap and can start this summer. From mid-August, when occupancy is already near 55%, the options are to close the showers, run composting or dry toilets for the late season, and end the season on 31 August rather than 20 September. The flush toilets and showers are probably the largest draws, but get a meter reading before deciding which to cut. Weakness: guests will object (the 2016 upgrade was a response to complaints), and this reduces the gap without closing it.

**3. Pipe water from the Grauberg spring.** The alp spring is 1.1 km away and 190 m lower, so water must be pumped up. Lifting 6 m³ a day through 190 m takes roughly 8 kWh a day with a typical pump, a rough estimate that needs checking. The alp's spring has never failed, which makes this the most reliable source on the table. It needs a written agreement with the farming family, a check of water rights, a route survey, and an allowance for their dairy demand. Pipe and pump costs are not known. Weakness: you depend on a neighbour's supply and goodwill.

**4. Borehole.** The quote is €140,000 with about a 60% chance of usable water. Reserves are €75,000, so the project needs €65,000 more from a loan, an appeal, or a levy. The real risk is that 40% of the time the club spends €140,000 and gets no water. A borehole is independent of the spring, which is its strength, but a dry hole cannot be recovered. Before committing, ask the geologist whether a smaller exploratory survey is possible.

**5. Helicopter top-up as a backstop.** At €1,100 for 0.8 m³ the cost is about €1,375 per m³. Full occupancy at 6 m³ a day would need roughly 7 to 8 rotations, around €8,000 a day, so the €75,000 reserve would last about nine days at full use. This works only as a short emergency measure, to keep the hut open while options 1 to 3 take effect. It depends on weather. Budget a sum for it, but do not rely on it as the plan.

## Comparison

| Option | Upfront cost | Running cost | Protects against late-summer failure | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Store June surplus | Unknown (quotes needed) | Low | Yes, if June overflow is large | Poor June year |
| 2. Cut demand, shorten season | Low | Lost guest income | Partly | Guest complaints |
| 3. Grauberg pipeline | Unknown (survey needed) | Pumping power | Yes, spring has never failed | Dependence on neighbour |
| 4. Borehole | €140,000 | Low | Yes, if the hole yields | 40% chance of no water |
| 5. Helicopter | About €1,375 per m³ | Very high | Only for a few days | Weather, cost |

## Set aside

- Relocating the hut is outside what the committee can decide in this cycle.
- Roof or snowmelt collection would add a small volume at this altitude, and the roof area is unknown. It works as an extra on option 1, not as a fix on its own.

## What I would put to the AGM

A sensible package is options 1 and 2 now, since they are cheap, fast, and use water you already have. Survey option 3 in parallel, because it is the likeliest long-term answer. Hold option 4 until a second geological view is in hand. Keep option 5 as a budgeted backstop.

If members prioritise cost, choose 1, 2 and 5. If they prioritise long-term independence from the spring, choose 3 or 4. If they want both, commission the pipeline survey and the borehole advice side by side and decide at next year's AGM.

## Assumptions and what would sharpen this

- I used your figure of 6 m³ a day at full occupancy and assumed 55% from mid-August.
- I have no quotes for tanks, pipes, or pumps, so those costs are open.
- The helicopter and borehole figures are as quoted.
- To firm up the choice, the most useful things to get are the June overflow volume, a meter reading by fixture, the Grauberg family's view, and whether the club can borrow the €65,000 gap.
