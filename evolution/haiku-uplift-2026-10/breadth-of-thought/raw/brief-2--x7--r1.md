Here are the realistic options I can see, roughly in the order I'd put them to council. Some figures are my own rough estimates, so please check them with your bereavement officer before January.

**The numbers first**

- You have 360 spaces and about 95 burials a year, so about 3.8 years of stock.
- Over 25 years you need roughly 2,400 spaces (95 x 25), so the gap is about 2,000 spaces, not 360.
- The 14 ha field is far bigger than that need. At a typical lawn-cemetery density it would hold many times the 2,400 you need, so buying all of it is paying for land you won't use for a long time.
- The 380 reserved graves in the east section are already part of the answer, and they are the cheapest source of space if you can use them.

**Options**

1. **Reclaim or reuse the reserved east-section graves.** This is probably the most important option. Check the deeds and the register for each of the 380 plots. Many of the 1960s and 70s rights will reach their 75-year end in the next 10 to 20 years, and once they lapse the plots can be reused under the council's powers. Before that, you can write to holders offering a refund or an exchange for a current grave, and ask whether they will surrender. Get legal advice on the exact powers under the Local Authorities' Cemeteries Order 1977 and the Burial Act 1857 before you promise anything.

2. **Use Ferney Bank for a new cemetery or a green/woodland burial ground.** At 3 ha it is much closer to the size you need than the field. It is already council-owned, so the cost is mainly the works: survey, drainage, groundwater checks, roads, fencing, and planning permission for change of use. A woodland or natural burial area can also sell well at a premium and suits the ground better. Get a site investigation and a rough cost before January.

3. **Buy a smaller part of the field, or lease or option it.** Ask the farmer whether he would sell part of the field, or grant a lease or option on a few hectares, even though he has said all 14 ha as one lot. Even a lease would stop the price rising while you plan. Don't assume he will say no until you have asked in writing.

4. **Ask Ashby Combe about a shared arrangement.** Their extension is three miles away and has had almost no burials since 2017, so it has spare capacity now. An inter-authority agreement could cover some of the gap for a few years. This is a fallback rather than a full answer, because residents and families will expect a local cemetery, and it has to be agreed with their council and checked for residency rules.

5. **Make the existing ground go further.**
   - Re-plan the north section. It is full but mostly long grass, and the Friends of Sorrel Lane already look after it, so ask whether any ground can be opened up again legally.
   - Tighten the layout in the active east section, or use multi-depth graves where the ground allows.
   - Keep the north section as it is and treat its reuse as a long-term option rather than a near-term fix.

6. **Adjust fees and cremation planning.** A £1,450 right for 75 years is cheap compared with the cost of new land. A fee review that covers the true cost of new space is needed in any case. Cremation has been about 70% for six years, so do not plan on a rise. Instead, plan the cremated-remains provision, such as a columbarium or a garden of remembrance, as a way of using less ground for each burial.

7. **Buy the whole field, but later or with a partner.** If the field is still the best long-term site, you could take an option now and buy when the money is in place. Possible sources are borrowing under the Prudential Code, selling surplus council land such as the Ferney Bank site, or a joint arrangement with a neighbouring authority. Council should not commit the £280,000 reserve to a £1.15m purchase without a plan for the rest.

**What I would take to council in January**

- A plan that combines options 1, 2 and 5 for the next five years, with option 4 as a short-term backstop.
- Option 3 pursued in writing now, so the field stays available without a commitment.
- A decision on option 7 deferred until the Ferney Bank costs and the reserved-grave position are clear.
- A timetable for a fee review.

**Assumptions I have made**

- The 95 burials a year and the 360 spaces are accurate for the current year.
- The reserved graves are all valid and documented.
- Ferney Bank can be used as cemetery land, subject to planning and ground conditions.

Please take legal advice on the reuse and reclaim powers, and on any shared arrangement with Ashby Combe, before you put any of this to council as settled.
