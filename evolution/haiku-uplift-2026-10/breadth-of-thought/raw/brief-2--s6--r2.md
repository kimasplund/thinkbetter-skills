The numbers come first. At about 95 burials a year, 25 years needs roughly 2,400 spaces. You have 360 free, so the gap is about 2,000. The 380 reserved but empty graves in the East section would bring you to about 740 spaces, roughly eight years, which still leaves a gap of about 1,600. Every option below should be judged against that gap, not only against "buy the field or don't."

## Realistic options

**1. Buy the whole 14-hectare field (£1.15m).**
This secures land for well beyond 25 years, and the farmer is willing to sell. But it is about £870,000 more than your £280,000 reserve, so it needs borrowing, and it buys far more land than the next 25 years need. You could buy it and license it back to the farmer for grazing or cropping until it is needed, which keeps the land productive and offsets the holding cost. This is a long-term decision that needs a loan business case.

**2. Recover capacity in the existing grounds.**
- *East section reserved plots.* The 380 empty plots were sold in the 1960s and 70s. If the rights run 75 years from purchase, they expire between roughly the mid-2030s and the early 2050s, which falls inside your window. A review could contact holders, offer buy-backs or swaps, and set a renewal policy. Some holders may have died or moved, and what the council can do with unclaimed rights is a legal question. Get advice before acting.
- *North section.* It has been full since 1888 and is mostly long grass. Whether old graves can be reused, and how, depends on the law and on the records. The Friends of Sorrel Lane are a stakeholder and possibly an ally. Check the legal and records position before ruling it out.

**3. Develop Ferney Bank.**
The closed 3-hectare pitch-and-putt is already council land, so there is no purchase cost, and it is far cheaper than the field. It needs a ground survey (drainage, water table, soil depth), planning consent for cemetery use, and the cost of laying out, fencing and access. Its location relative to where families live also matters. If the ground is suitable, this is probably the best value for money, and it works alongside option 2.

**4. Share capacity with Ashby Combe.**
Their extension has had hardly any burials since 2017, so the capacity exists three miles away. A formal agreement could let Wickham Lees residents use it, possibly at a different fee for non-residents. The drawbacks: residents may not want to travel, the other council sets the terms, and you do not control it over the long run. It works well as a stopgap or backstop, not as the whole plan.

**5. Lease land instead of buying.**
Renting a field or part of one for 25 years avoids the upfront cost, but rent is an ongoing cost and you still need planning and a suitable site. It is worth asking the farmer whether he would lease part of the field, even though he has said he will sell only the whole lot.

## Ways to slow the use of space

- **Ashes.** Ashes can go into an existing grave or a small memorial garden and take very little land. About 30% of funerals are burials, so this affects only the cremation side, but it still helps.
- **Grave depth and density.** Multi-depth or shallower-depth burial in new sections gets more burials per acre. It needs ground and legal checks.
- **Natural or woodland burial.** This is a different product that may attract demand, and it uses land differently. It needs its own setup and ongoing management.

## Ruled out

- **Relying on cremation rising.** It has been steady at about 70% for six years, so it is not a plan.
- **Raising grave fees to reduce demand.** It would hit affordability and does not create any space.

## What I would put to council in January

Use the cheapest and fastest sources first: a review of the East section reserved plots, and a legal check on reusing the North section. Assess Ferney Bank now, since it is already yours. Agree a stopgap with Ashby Combe. Ask the farmer about a lease, and get costings for the whole-field purchase with a licence back to him. Ask council for a decision on which surveys and legal reviews to commission, not a decision to buy the field.

## Assumptions to verify

- The 360 free spaces exclude the 380 reserved plots.
- The 75-year rights run from the date of purchase.
- Ferney Bank is not in a flood or conservation area.
- Ashby Combe would accept residents from outside its own area.
