I'm head of operations at Pellow Cycles. We build about 4,000 electric cargo bikes a year; 60% go to courier and delivery fleets on lease, the rest to retail.

Three weeks ago Velan Semiconductor end-of-lifed the VX-41, the microcontroller on our motor controller board. Last-time-buy orders close in ten weeks, and our board stock covers about seven months of production.

Our electronics team puts a controller redesign at 14 months: about 4 months to port the firmware to the Corwen M7, which is widely available; about 7 months to design a new sensor for hill-hold mode, which relies on a timer peripheral only the VX-41 has; then about 3 months of EMC certification. Telemetry shows hill-hold is engaged on about 6% of rides, almost all on retail bikes.

A last-time buy covering the full 14 months would cost about €1.9 million up front. We can free up around €700,000, and the bank has already refused to extend our facility.

Other things going on. Brisk Motion, an old competitor, went into administration in August, and the administrator auctions its parts stock next month; Brisk bought its controllers from the same contract manufacturer we use, Tarrow Electronics. Our trade-in scheme has left about 1,900 used bikes in the warehouse, mostly 2020–2023 models; we strip the batteries and sell the rest for scrap. Every model we've built since 2019 uses the same controller board.

The board meets in three weeks. I want to give them the full menu, not just "how much last-time buy can we afford". What are our options?
