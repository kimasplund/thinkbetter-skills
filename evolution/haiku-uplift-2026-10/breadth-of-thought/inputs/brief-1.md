I'm warden of the Brannock Ridge Hut, which the Ostkamm Alpine Club runs at 2,640 m: 48 beds, open 1 July to 20 September, reachable only on foot (about 3.5 hours) or by helicopter for supplies.

Our water comes from a spring 300 m above the hut, piped into a 12 m³ tank. At full occupancy we use about 6 m³ a day. Last August the spring stopped for 19 days. We closed for 11 of them, refunded bookings and cancelled two guided groups. It has dwindled in late summer in three of the last five years, and our hydrologist says that's the pattern from now on as the snowfield above us shrinks. In late June, by contrast, it runs so hard the overflow floods the path to the toilet block.

Other background. We replaced the old dry toilets with flush toilets and added two showers in 2016, after guest complaints. From mid-August occupancy drops to around 55% anyway, because the glacier route gets badly crevassed and the guiding companies stop booking it. The Grauberg alp, 1.1 km down the valley and 190 m lower, runs a dairy all summer; its spring has never failed and the family who farm it are friendly. Our solar array was upgraded last year and has plenty of spare capacity.

The committee has two proposals: drill a borehole by the hut (€140,000 quoted; the geologist gives about a 60% chance of usable water), or fly water in when we run short (one helicopter rotation carries 0.8 m³ and costs €1,100). We have €75,000 in reserves.

What are our options? The AGM is in November and I'd like to give members more than two choices.
