# J-B scores, brief-1

E3B: Catches the 125 L/guest anomaly, the half-empty timing, storage as a timing fix, and a quantified "drinking-and-cooking only" mode (about 3-4 rotations per outage) that beats closing. Asks whether the 60% means enough water. The 20-40 L hut benchmark is unsourced. "€10-15k is about 10-14 rotations" is slightly off. "Opening a week later in late June" makes no sense for a 1 July opening.

E7U: Strong diagnosis (125 L/guest, 55% timing, storage vs supply) with sound arithmetic, and it says costs are guesses. The reduced-service idea is not quantified. The table and the eight options run long. The benchmark is vague ("a small fraction") rather than invented.

N9U: Clean arithmetic ("reserves buy ~55 m³"), and the dairy-catchment water test is a good point. It misses the per-guest demand anomaly, and the demand cuts have no numbers. Concise and hedged.

M2Z: The core numbers are right. "Only 63-114 m³ ... tanks cost far less than a borehole" is asserted without support. The €15k helicopter budget is never reconciled with a 63 m³ gap. It states that showers and toilets use the most water as fact. It misses the per-guest anomaly.

U9G: Good diagnosis and packages, plus an honest assumptions line. The invented 15-30 L benchmark and the €30-80k storage figure drive its "fits your reserves" conclusion. Carrying water "by vehicle" ignores the fact that the hut is reachable only on foot.

A9H: Thorough. The 40-50 L target arithmetic is correct and consistent, including the tank lasting 5-9 days and a 25 m³ store. It flags the borehole drawing on the same snowfield. The 20-40 L benchmark is unsourced, and the reply is long, with an offer at the end.

N8L: The "cheap or shared" recommendation for Grauberg contradicts its own "unknown until quoted" table entry. It overstates closing from mid-August as "already close to what happens naturally". The three unknowns at the end are well chosen. It misses the per-guest anomaly.

S8O: The pump energy figure (~8 kWh) is well hedged, and the decision branches by member priority are useful. Calling the borehole "independent of the spring" ignores the shrinking-snowfield concern. Demand cuts are not quantified.
