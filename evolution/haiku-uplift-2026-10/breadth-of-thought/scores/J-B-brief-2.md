# J-B scores, brief-2

H5F: Clean and readable, and the arithmetic holds. It misses the reopenings question and the expiry timing of the reserved plots. It asks "whether any rights have already lapsed" when none from the 1960s and 70s can have. It counts the reserved plots in its starting numbers and then calls them a bonus. It doesn't ask why Ashby Combe is empty. The lead-time figures are stated without support.

A5D: Covers the right options and adds a sensible fee review, with a good assumptions list. It suggests selling Ferney Bank to fund the field, which contradicts its own main option. It says "once they lapse the plots can be reused" with more confidence than it should. "Woodland burial sells well at a premium" has no support. It doesn't ask why Ashby Combe is empty.

E1V: Strong. It gets the 2035-2054 expiry window right, notes that some burials go into existing graves, asks why Ashby Combe is empty, and flags hope value in the field price. It treats fee income as if it were net, and it states "many old graves have unused interments" as fact. It is long, but it lands a conditional recommendation.

M8H: Thorough. It flags whether the 360 includes reopenings, gets the expiry window right, asks the Ashby Combe "why", and suggests stopping advance reservations. It adds a 10-20% growth allowance the brief doesn't support. The household figures are invented but marked "if". It is long, and its recommendation is softer than E1V's.

P5P: Concise and well proportioned, with correct expiry dates and an honest assumptions list. It misses the reopenings question and doesn't ask why Ashby Combe is empty. It rules out a fee review rather quickly.

Y7B: Best insight. It puts timing first, audits the 360 against reopenings, notes that the north section's 75-year rights expired long ago, mentions a faculty for consecrated ground, asks why Ashby Combe is empty, tests cremation sensitivity with correct arithmetic, and points to the perpetual obligation. The text is dense and long. A few unsupported guesses appear (a 3-5 year lead time, a five-figure feasibility cost), but the legal assumptions are stated up front.

T9R: Very good. It spots that the reserved plots only free up from 2035, which leaves a gap of about five years after 2030. It reads Ashby Combe's low take-up as a demand signal and gives a clear view that the field comes last. It marks estimates as rough. One flaw: a "fee change" cannot bridge a capacity gap.

I0B: Mostly generic, with no capacity arithmetic for Ferney Bank or the field. It proposes natural burial in the full north section and talks of "lifting and re-using" graves loosely. It treats a closed course as having sporting value and asks whether rights "have lapsed" when none can have. It misses the expiry timing and the Ashby Combe signal.
