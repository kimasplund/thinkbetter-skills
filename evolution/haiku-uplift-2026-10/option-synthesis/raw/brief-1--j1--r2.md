My recommendation: don't haul all 600 to Dunmere Flats. Split the colonies. Send about 200 to Agnete's cellar, keep the rest outdoors in wraps, and use Rafe's offer only to cover whatever shortfall is left after the February 20 count. This beats both of your current options on expected profit and stays inside your $15,000 line.

**Why not the full Dunmere haul**

Hauling all 600 costs about $63 each up front ($30 haul, $8 rent, $25 feed), so roughly $37,800. Your line has about $15,000 spare, so you can't fund it without a bank increase, which you've already been refused. Its expected return is similar to the cellar option, and it carries more cash risk.

**Rough numbers (my assumptions, not guarantees)**

Expected graded colonies per colony sent, and the upfront cost per colony:

| Option | Survive | Grade | Graded per colony | Upfront per colony |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Outdoor wraps | 63% | ~69% (104 of 150 last spring) | ~0.44 | $3 |
| Dunmere Flats | 85% | 90% | ~0.77 | $63 |
| Agnete's cellar | 84% | 80% | ~0.67 | $18 |

Each graded colony is worth $190 to you. Each colony short of 420 costs you $40. So a graded colony is worth about $230 when you are below 420.

Expected results for the whole 600:

- **All outdoors, no broker:** about 262 graded, 158 short, so about $41,700 net after wraps and penalties.
- **Plan below, no broker:** about 309 graded, 111 short, so about $49,200 net. Upfront cash is about $4,800.
- **Plan below, with Rafe covering 80:** about 389 graded, 31 short, so about $55,900 net. Upfront cash is still about $4,800, because Rafe is paid only when the co-op pays you.

If it costs about $30 a colony like your haul, the 200 colonies cost about $9,600 with transport, which still fits your $15,000 line alongside about $1,200 of wraps.

**Reconsider Rafe**

Your reasoning on his offer leaves out one thing. A grading colony from him is paid $190 by the co-op and you pay him $150, so you keep $40. It also cuts your shortfall by one, which saves the $40 penalty. That is about $80 a colony when you are short. If you are short, his offer is profitable and needs no cash until the co-op pays. Only take the colonies you actually need, because if you aren't short, the extra colonies are worth nothing.

**Things to check**

- Does the co-op pay for graded colonies above 420, or cap it at 420? I assumed a cap.
- Does the $40 penalty apply to 420 minus the number graded? I assumed yes.
- Your 104 of 150 grading figure: I took it as a rate per colony sent. If it is a rate per survivor, the grading rate is higher and the plan does better.
- Winter losses are the biggest risk. Neighbours' 15% and Agnete's 16% may not hold in a bad winter. Your own losses have been 34% to 40%. Keep the expected-value view in mind, but also think about what a worse winter does to your grading count, and how much room you have for Rafe.
