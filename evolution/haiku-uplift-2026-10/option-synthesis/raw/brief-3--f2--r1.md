## Assumptions I'm working from

- The ferry run is about 2.5 days each way, which is how I read "five days per round trip". Velaro is outside the UK, so customs apply to every route.
- Your crates are made from untreated offcuts, so they carry no heat-treatment stamp.
- "Door to door" on both Quintell quotes includes the final delivery to the hotel. I have not confirmed that, and the bridge makes it doubtful (see below).
- The contractor's dates are firm: nothing before the 14th, and £1,200 a day from the 19th.

## Two problems that sit under every option

1. **Wooden packaging has to be heat-treated and stamped.** That line on Quintell's checklist is the international wood-packaging rule, and it applies to crates you built from offcuts. Customs can refuse or hold a container full of unstamped crates, and the usual remedy is to re-crate at the destination, which would cost you the delivery window. Ask Quintell today whether your crates can be accepted. If they can't, you have three ways out: rebuild with stamped timber, fumigate or heat-treat the crates through a certified treater, or switch to non-wood packing (board, foam and wrap on pallets that are themselves stamped or plastic). Any of these takes time out of your 3 March finish date, so start before you book.
2. **The 7.5-tonne bridge.** A 40 ft container lorry will be well over 7.5 tonnes. Nine tonnes of cargo plus a container and a tractor unit comes to roughly 15 tonnes or more. A quote that says "door to door" may really mean "to the port or yard on the near side of the bridge". The hotel needs a delivery plan with 7.5-tonne vehicles for the last leg, and you need to know who pays for it.

**Groupage loses on both cost and timing.** It costs about £840 more than the full container, and you share the schedule of every other load in the box. The container is only about 78% full, but you pay less for it than for the share.

**The van run is the dearest and the tightest.** Your load is 9 tonnes. Two 7.5-tonne vans can't carry that in one go, so four van-loads (about 2.25 tonnes and 13 m³ each) is the realistic plan, which is where the £9,600 comes from. If trip one reaches the hotel on the 14th, the vans are back home around the 16th or 17th, and trip two lands on the 19th or 20th: £1,200 to £2,400 in charges. You'd also lose two vans and four drivers out of a nine-person shop for ten days, and your crates would still have to be stamped. Joss is right that you'd control the timing and that 7.5-tonne vans are legal on the bridge. Those are the real advantages, and they don't cover a £5,000 gap.

**Cost with the extras.** The container at £4,150 plus a re-crate and a bridge relay is still probably under the £9,600. I can't price those two items from here; get quotes before you commit.

## What most threatens the container plan

- **Crates not accepted or not ready for the 12th (about 30%).** The re-crate would come on top of finishing the joinery by 3 March. Fallback: the 19 March sailing, which puts you on site 16 to 23 April and exposes you to roughly £1,200 to £6,000 in charges. Because of that, decide the crate question first and book the sailing second.
- **The bridge relay is not covered by the quote (about 20%).** It adds cost but doesn't threaten the dates, provided it's arranged in advance.
- **A port or customs hold (about 10%).** With the 12 March sailing you have about two days of slack before the 18th, and no slack on the 19 March sailing.

Overall, I'd put the chance of missing the 18 April deadline on the 12 March sailing at around 20%, mostly through the crate question. On groupage I'd say 60% or more, because most of its range falls after the 18th.

## The option I didn't pick

If we chose the container and by April that was the mistake, it would be because the crate rules or the bridge made the container slower than it looked, while Joss's vans arrived on time. The decision rule above answers that: if the container can't meet the dates with compliant packing, you fall back to the 19 March sailing or the shorter van relay. I'd keep the groupage booking only as a stopgap while you wait for answers, and cancel it as soon as the container is held.
