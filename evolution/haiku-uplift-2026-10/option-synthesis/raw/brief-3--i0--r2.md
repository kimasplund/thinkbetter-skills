**Recommendation: book the full 40 ft container on the 12 March sailing. Before you confirm, fix two problems that nobody has flagged yet: the crates and the bridge.**

**Why the container beats the other two**

- **Cost.** Groupage at £96/m³ is about £4,992 for 52 m³. The full container is £4,150 for 67 m³, so it's cheaper and you get 15 m³ spare. Check whether the groupage rate is charged on volume or on weight. At 9 t for 52 m³ it may be a different figure.
- **Timing.** Your window is 14 to 18 April, and you can't store anything before the 14th.
  - Groupage takes 5 to 7 weeks. If it leaves around 3 to 10 March, it lands between about 7 and 28 April. That could be a week early with nowhere to put it, or well past the 18th. Each day late costs £1,200, so a 10-day slip is £12,000.
  - The 5 March sailing (4 to 5 weeks) lands around 2 to 9 April, which is too early.
  - The 12 March sailing lands around 9 to 16 April, which is the best fit. The 19 March sailing lands around 16 to 23 April, so the late end is a real risk.
  - Ask Quintell for the transit time to the door and whether it includes customs clearance. Also ask what the holding or storage charge is if the container arrives a few days early. That cost is small compared with delay charges.
- **Joss's vans.** Four van-trips at about £2,400 each is £9,600, more than double the container. It also ties up ten days of travel and both vans, and it takes nine people's best drivers out of the workshop. The load is 9 t, so you'd need two trips anyway. A 7.5 t van carries only about 2.5 to 3 t of payload, so two vans can't take it in one go. Joss is right that vans give you control, but you'd pay a lot for it. Keep vans as a fallback, not the plan.

**Problem 1: the crates**

Quintell's checklist says wooden packaging must be heat-treated and stamped. Crates built from offcuts almost certainly aren't. That is a standard ISPM 15 requirement. Unmarked crates can mean the shipment is refused, held, or has to be re-packed at the destination, and that would wreck your April window. It probably applies to the vans on the ferry as well, so Joss's option doesn't avoid it. Your choices:

1. Build the crates from ISPM 15 stamped timber or sheet material, which a certified supplier can provide.
2. Buy stamped crates or pallets and use them for the base and structure.
3. Use non-wood packaging, such as cardboard, foam and blankets on stamped pallets.

Ask Quintell today whether the furniture itself needs any treatment or documents, since it is finished timber, and what exactly the destination inspects. You have until about 3 March, and the 12 March sailing leaves little slack, so decide the crate approach this week.

**Problem 2: the bridge**

The only bridge into the harbour quarter has a 7.5 t limit. A 40 ft container lorry carrying 9 t of cargo weighs far more than that, so it can't cross, and "door to door" probably doesn't hold as written. Ask Quintell in writing:

- Who delivers the last mile, and with what vehicles?
- Will they unload (devan) the container at a depot on the near side and shuttle the load over in 7.5 t or lighter vehicles?
- What does that cost, and does it go in the quote?

Ask the same question about groupage. If shuttling needs several trips, plan for it within the 14 to 18 April window and get it priced before you commit. Also tell the hotel's contractor about the limit and agree where they will receive the goods. The 4 or 5 trips for a shuttle may need a booked delivery slot.
