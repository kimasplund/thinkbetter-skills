The container is 67 m³ and your load is 52 m³, so it fits with room to spare. 9 tonnes is well within a 40 ft container's payload.

Joss is right that driving it yourselves gives you control. But the price of that control is about £5,000 extra, plus the lost production time. Most of the control is also available by choosing the right sailing and asking Quintell the right questions.

## Why 12 March

- **5 March** is too tight. You finish crating on 3 March, and the container needs loading, customs paperwork and port cut-off. It would also land as early as 2 April, which means about two weeks with nowhere to put it.
- **19 March** can land up to 23 April, which is 5 days late. At £1,200 a day that is about £6,000, more than the saving over any other choice.
- **12 March** lands 9-16 April. The late end is inside your window. The early end is up to 5 days before the 14th, so ask Quintell to hold the container at the port or depot and time the delivery for 14 April or later. Ask them to confirm the cost of that holding.

I'm assuming "4 to 5 weeks" is a typical transit time and not a guarantee. Ask Quintell how reliable it is, and whether the 12 March vessel has a history of delays.

## Two problems to fix before you book

**1. Your crates probably aren't allowed.** The checklist says wooden packaging must be heat-treated and stamped. That is the international standard (ISPM 15). Crates built from offcuts almost certainly aren't stamped, and customs can reject or fumigate unstamped wood on arrival. That would put your delivery date at risk. This applies however the load travels, so it affects Joss's plan too.

- Ask Quintell whether the rule applies to your destination and what proof they want.
- Rebuild the crates from stamped, heat-treated timber, or buy certified crates or pallets. Plywood and other manufactured board are often exempt from the rule, but confirm that with Quintell before relying on it.
- Allow time and budget for this. Crates are due by 3 March, so work out now how much you need and who can supply it. This is another reason to choose 12 March over 5 March.

**2. The 7.5-tonne bridge.** The only bridge into the harbour quarter has a 7.5-tonne limit. A 40 ft container on a lorry weighs far more than that, because the container, trailer and tractor unit are all on top of your 9 tonnes of cargo. So a "door to door" container delivery can't cross it as quoted.

- Ask Quintell today how the last leg is done. Most likely the container is unloaded at a depot outside the quarter and the crates go over the bridge in smaller vehicles, each weighing less than 7.5 tonnes fully loaded.
- Ask whether that transfer and the extra trips are included in the £4,150. If not, get a price. Even with that added it is unlikely to close the gap to the vans, but you need the number.
- The same problem affects groupage, so it doesn't change the ranking.
- Joss's vans are 7.5-tonne vehicles. Loaded, they would be at or over the limit on the bridge. Check their actual loaded weight and the exact wording of the restriction, including whether it is gross vehicle weight.
