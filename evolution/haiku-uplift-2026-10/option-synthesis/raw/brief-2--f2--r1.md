# Options: powering Thistledown

**Decide by:** 31 October · **Objective:** three nights of power for ~7,000 people with no loss of medical-tent power, no generator running within 150 m of the campsite between 23:00 and 07:00, and this year's hire plus fuel at or under £50,000 · **Horizon:** two weeks after the festival

**Assumptions I'm working from:**
- "Go halves" means a 50/50 split of the £38,000 quote, so your share is £19,000.
- The 150 m is measured from the campsite edge to the generator, and an engine that is switched off counts as "not running". Neither is confirmed; the council has to say so in writing.
- The overnight 45 kW includes the medical tent and covers the full 23:00-07:00 window (8 hours, 360 kWh).
- Gantry's "100 kVA" set delivers about 80 kW; a 200 kVA set about 160 kW.
- You gave no budget. I used last year's £41,860 (hire £25,200 plus fuel £16,660) plus about 20%.
- My prices for cable, a battery backup for the medical tent and a changeover panel are estimates, not quotes.
- The festival's 11 weeks run from today.

## The recommendation

**Run this year on diesel sets re-sited so that nothing runs within 150 m of the campsite overnight, with a battery backup on the medical tent. Take your half of the grid connection now, but plan it as next year's power, not this year's. Don't buy six hybrids.**

- The grid cannot help this year. The 14-week lead time is longer than the 11 weeks you have.
- It also won't deliver "no generators, ever". A 400 A three-phase supply is about 277 kVA, roughly 250 kW usable. Your peak is 520 kW. The grid would carry the campsite, the base load and about half the peak, and you'd still hire three or four sets for the stages. By my estimate that still cuts the annual bill by roughly £10-19k, so your £19k share could pay back in one to four years. I haven't seen the operator's standing charges or the on-site distribution cost, so confirm both before you sign.
- Last year's layout is not an option. The two sets at the campsite edge are inside the exclusion zone, so running them overnight breaches the licence. Switching them off would cut the medical tent's power.
- The hybrids fail on arithmetic, not on novelty. Six engines of about 80 kW give 480 kW against a 520 kW peak, so any engine trip at the evening peak means load-shedding. Eight hours at 45 kW needs about 360 kWh, which is four of the six battery packs at the campsite, leaving two sets for the stages. They would only break even on cost if the fuel saving is at least 32%, and the 55% is Gantry's own figure.
- Chance this misses the objective by the horizon: **~15%** for this year's power, because a single remote feed to the medical tent is the weak joint and the battery backup closes most of it. About **30%** that the grid is live and paying for itself by next year's festival, because connection dates and landowner terms slip. Reference class: events that moved generators after a noise condition. I have no cited dataset, so treat that as weakly sourced. The difference from yours is that you keep a known machine and change only where it stands.
- Compared in one context, not blind. I built this recommendation and favour it. Its final check ran in the same context as the analysis, because I can't start a separate one here, so it is weaker than an independent check.

## What the choice had to satisfy

Written down before any option below was considered. No hash or commit seals it, because there was nothing here to commit to; the text is the record.

- **Must pass (a violation removes the option):** no generator running within 150 m of the campsite from 23:00 to 07:00; ready and tested within 11 weeks; covers the 520 kW peak.
- **Must be at least as good as the current plan:** continuity of medical-tent power (scored 1/3/5/7: 3 = one supply path and one set, with a stated recovery time; 5 = two independent paths or a battery that carries the tent through a set failure; 7 = both); this year's hire plus fuel at or under £50,000.
- **Where options compete (1/3/5/7):** cost this year (7 = £3k or more below last year, 5 = within £3k, 3 = £3-7k above, 1 = more than £7k above); running cost over the ten-year lease; margin at the evening peak with one set down; what you learn whatever happens.
- **Adoption:** a challenger replaces the current plan only if it passes every must-pass item, matches it on both guards, leads by at least 3 value points, trails on no value dimension by more than 2, and its premortem leaves no unmitigated fatal flaw. Margins under 3 points are inside the noise and the answer is "inconclusive". The current plan as run last year breaches the licence, so it cannot be kept as it stands. The margin test therefore applies between the legal options.
- **Combinations:** adopted only after a test against each parent, and only if they beat both.

## How each finalist dies

### Current plan (the baseline)
**Strongest case:** Six sets with 960 kW against a 520 kW peak, one supplier you know, £41,860 all-in, and no new equipment.
**Killing modes:** The licence is enforced after last year's complaints (~40%, because the condition exists because of those complaints and the sets run all night inside the zone) · you comply by switching the campsite sets off at 23:00 and the medical tent goes dark (~20%, because that tent needs power all night and the only supply is in the zone) · a set fault or a diesel price rise hits the fixed budget (~10%, because the fleet is old routine with no new control).
**Chance it misses the objective:** ~70%. Reference class: events running unmodified after a new condition, weakly sourced; the disanalogy is that you could pre-empt a complaint by moving the sets.
**Most dangerous:** the second mode, because the first sign is a medical complaint at 02:00 and it can't be undone mid-event. **Hidden assumption:** the council won't enforce a condition it wrote last year. **Cheapest falsifier:** ask the licensing officer whether any exemption is available (no cost, 2-5 days); it is killed by any answer other than a written exemption.

### Re-sited diesels
**Strongest case:** The same machines and supplier, and the same arithmetic you already trust. Only the cable run and the medical backup are new. The 45 kW overnight load is about 70 A, which on a roughly 200 m run of 35 mm² cable is about a 4% voltage drop (my estimate; the electrician confirms).
**Its own arithmetic:** Cost is £45-48k with six sets (£41,860 plus roughly £3-6k for cable, panel and backup), or £41-44k if Gantry confirms five sets cover your zones (peak 520 kW against 800 kW, and 640 kW with one set down). Overnight the remote set runs at about 28% load, burning roughly 14-16 L an hour, about £560 over three nights.
**Killing modes:** One remote set or one cable feeds the medical tent and fails overnight (~20%, because there is a single supply path where there used to be two sets) · night noise complaints continue from the village or the neighbouring farms, because the licence distance is a proxy for the real cause (~15%, because the condition came from complaints, not from a measurement) · the cable, panel and backup cost more than my estimate and the late hire is hard to find (~10%, because it is 11 weeks out and a run of this length is unusual).
**Chance it misses the objective:** ~20%. Reference class: events that re-sited sets after a condition, weakly sourced; the disanalogy is that this adds a cable but no new technology.
**Most dangerous:** the single feed to the medical tent, because the first sign is a medical-tent alarm at night. **Hidden assumption:** the licence distance is measured as I have read it. **Cheapest falsifier:** a written reply from the council on the measurement point (no cost, 2-5 days); it is killed by a reply that measures to the licence boundary and not the campsite edge, if no route 150 m clear is available.

### Six hybrids
**Strongest case:** Your supplier is offering it, the fuel claim could save about £3,800 against last year (4,410 L, £7,497 of fuel, plus £30,600 of hire, £38,097 in all), and engines that stay off overnight may meet the licence by design.
**Its own arithmetic:** Six engines of about 80 kW give 480 kW against a 520 kW peak, so the batteries must supply 40 kW or more for hours with no engine in reserve. At 45 kW, 360 kWh needs four packs at the campsite once you allow usable capacity (about 20% margin), leaving two sets for the stages. The cost breaks even only at a 32% fuel saving (fuel at or under £11,260, 6,624 L). At a 20% saving it costs about £2,070 more than last year.
**Killing modes:** The campsite packs run flat before 07:00 and the engine either starts inside the zone or the medical tent goes dark (~30%, because the sizing margin is thin if the night load is 56 kW and not 45) · an engine trip at the evening peak forces load-shedding because there is no spare capacity (~20%, because the engines fall 40 kW short of peak and every set is needed) · the 55% saving does not appear (~15%, because the claim is from the vendor and your sets have a flat, high daytime load, not many idle hours).
**Chance it misses the objective:** ~50%. Reference class: first use of a supplier's new product at full scale, weakly sourced; the disanalogy is that Gantry is a known supplier.
**Most dangerous:** the first mode, because the first sign is a battery level dropping toward zero in the small hours, in the zone where an engine start is a breach. **Hidden assumption:** the vendor's 55% applies to your load profile. **Cheapest falsifier:** ask Gantry for metered fuel logs from an event with a similar profile (no cost, 2 days); it is killed if they can't supply them or if the logs show under 32%.

### Hybrids at the campsite plus diesels
**Strongest case:** The battery sits where the rule bites, four packs (480 kWh) carry a 360 kWh night, and three diesels (480 kW) feed the stages.
**Its own arithmetic:** Hire is 4 x £5,100 + 3 x £4,200 = £33,000. Fuel is probably £12-15k, so the total is about £46-48k, which is more than last year. With one stage diesel down, 320 kW of engines remain for stage and base load that is likely well above that.
**Killing modes:** An unfamiliar battery control system runs the medical tent's feed (~30%, because it is the first time you've used it, in the one place an outage matters most) · the cost exceeds the plan and the saving never shows (~20%, because the hire is higher than the diesel hire and the fuel claim is unproven) · the stage diesels have no spare at the evening peak (~10%, because three sets are tight against the load).
**Chance it misses the objective:** ~30%. Reference class: mixed fleets at a first event, weakly sourced; the disanalogy is that the night load is small and well known.
**Most dangerous:** the control system, because the first sign is an unexplained engine start at 03:00. **Hidden assumption:** an auto-starting engine is acceptable to the council. **Cheapest falsifier:** ask the council in writing whether battery mode with an engine locked out counts (no cost, 2-5 days); it is killed by a "no" or "only if it can't start".

### Grid, staged
**Strongest case:** Your landlord is paying half, you have ten years of lease, and at 400 A the supply carries your base and campsite load all year.
**Its own arithmetic:** Capacity is about 277 kVA, around 250 kW. Annual cost with three or four hired sets, a reduced fuel bill and grid energy is perhaps £23-32k against £41.9k, saving roughly £10-19k a year. That is a one to four year payback on £19k, before the on-site distribution and standing charges that I have not seen.
**Killing modes:** The connection slips past next year's festival (~30%, because the 14-week lead time is the operator's estimate and works on land often run late) · the supply turns out less useful than assumed, with capacity at half the peak and extra on-site works and metering (~20%, because the quote covers the connection, not the site's distribution) · once the grid is live you drop the generator contract and a feeder fault or an overload on a festival day leaves no backup (~10%, because success makes the site depend on one supply).
**Chance it misses the objective:** ~30%. Reference class: small commercial grid connections to rural sites, weakly sourced; the disanalogy is that your supply sits on a landowner's farm with a shared owner.
**Most dangerous:** the slip, because there is no warning until the works date moves. **Hidden assumption:** the quote's 400 A is enough for the intended use and the date holds. **Cheapest falsifier:** ask the operator for the supply voltage, capacity in kVA, the quote's expiry, and what the price excludes (no cost, 2-5 days); it is killed if capacity is under about 250 kVA or the delivery date is later than next spring.

**What the whole field assumes:** that the council reads the 150 m condition the way you do (the measurement point, and an engine that is off). If it doesn't, the siting for the diesels and the hybrids' appeal both change. This is untested, so it is the first thing to settle.

## The decision rule

**IF** the council confirms in writing by 24 October that the 150 m runs from the campsite edge, **AND** a cable route 150 m or more clear is surveyed with under 5% voltage drop, **THEN** book the re-sited diesels with the medical backup and changeover, ask Gantry whether five sets cover the zones, and order the cable now. **ELSE** (the council measures differently, or no route fits) ask Gantry in writing for four hybrids at the campsite with an engine lock-out and 8-hour autonomy at 56 kW. Cut the night load (showers closed, LED lighting on timers) to bring the battery need down.

**Grid:** **IF** the operator confirms at least ~270 kVA, the quote's expiry and what it excludes, **AND** Gwen Aske signs heads of terms covering who owns the supply, who pays for the on-site works, and what happens to the share if the lease ends, **THEN** accept your £19,000 share before the quote expires. **ELSE** hold and ask for an extension of the quote. Don't pay a deposit before both answers are in; the cheap checks come first.

**Cheapest decisive experiment:** Three emails this week to the council, the network operator and Gantry (metered fuel logs). They cost nothing and take 2-5 days. Any of these flips the call: the council measures to the licence boundary; the operator's capacity is under 250 kVA; or the logs show under 32% saving, which closes the hybrid question for good.

**Runner-up:** Hybrids at the campsite plus diesels; switch if the council wants no generator on site at all overnight, no clear route exists, or Gantry's logs show a saving of 35% or more.

**If passing over the runner-up was the mistake:** By the horizon, the re-sited sets drew night complaints from the village, because the cause was never the distance to the campsite. Battery mode would have run silently. That resolves into the ELSE branch for next year (a hybrid night block); this year's call stands, since the complaint would have come at an event with no better tested alternative.
