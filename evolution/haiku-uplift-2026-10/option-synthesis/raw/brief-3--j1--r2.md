**Recommendation: book the full 40 ft container on the 12 March sailing.**

Here is how the three options compare for your dates.

**1. Groupage (£96/m³, 5 to 7 weeks)** is about £4,990 for 52 m³ and is the most expensive of the freight options. It is also the least reliable for your window. Departing in the first week of March and taking 5 to 7 weeks lands anywhere from roughly 9 April to 23 April. That misses your 14 to 18 April window at both ends, and each day late costs £1,200. Groupage also depends on sharing a container with other loads, so your date is less certain.

**2. Full 40 ft container (£4,150, 4 to 5 weeks)** is the cheapest and fastest option. It also carries your load with room to spare, since it holds 67 m³ and you need about 52 m³ crated. The sailing dates work out as follows:
- 5 March sailing: arrives about 2 to 9 April. Too early, and you have nowhere to store it before the 14th.
- 12 March sailing: arrives about 9 to 16 April. This overlaps your window best, so it is the one to book.
- 19 March sailing: arrives about 16 to 23 April. Risks being late.

Ask Quintell to confirm the sailing and to hold the container at port or in their yard so it doesn't have to reach the site before 14 April. Then agree a delivery slot inside 14 to 18 April.

**3. Joss's own vans (about £9,600 in total)** costs more than twice the container. Four van trips at £2,400 each comes to £9,600, and each round trip takes 5 days, so it is slow and ties up two drivers. Two 7.5-tonne box vans also cannot each carry about 4.5 tonnes of crated furniture safely, because their payload is well below that once the van itself is counted. Loading them near their 7.5-tonne gross limit also leaves no margin. Joss is right that you keep control, but the cost and the load risk are high for what you gain.

**Things to settle this week, whichever option you pick:**
- **Heat treatment and stamping.** Wooden packaging must be heat-treated and stamped to the ISPM 15 standard. Offcut crates built in-house will not meet this unless the timber is treated and stamped before you crate. Confirm a treatment supplier and their lead time now, because it has to be done before the 3 March crating deadline.
- **The 7.5-tonne bridge.** This limit applies to whatever vehicle makes the last leg into the harbour quarter, not just your vans. A 40 ft container on a truck will be far over the limit. Plan for transfer at a staging point outside the quarter onto smaller vehicles under 7.5 tonnes gross, and ask Quintell and the hotel's contractor to confirm the route and who pays for the transfer.
- **Customs paperwork.** Ask Quintell which of the checklist items are their responsibility and what the import duties and VAT will add to the quoted prices.

**Assumptions I have made:** I have taken Quintell's transit times as estimates, not guarantees. I have assumed the 52 m³ and 9 tonnes are the crated figures. I have not been able to check the bridge's actual route or Velaro's port arrangements.
