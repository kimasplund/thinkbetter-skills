**Short answer:** Don't go all-in on Dunmere, because you can't pay for it. Split the herd. Put as many colonies as the cellar will take into the cellar, send a smaller block to Dunmere, and leave the rest outdoors. Take Rafe's offer as a backstop, because your objection to it doesn't hold up. Before you commit, check three things (listed at the end).

**Why not everything to Dunmere**

- The haul, rent and feed come to $63 a colony. For 600 colonies that is about $37,800 up front, and you have $15,000 of credit that the bank won't raise.
- With that cash you could send about 238 colonies and have nothing left. That also leaves no money for wraps on the rest, and no cushion.

**What each option does for you**

I assumed about 65% of outdoor colonies grade. Your 150 southern colonies graded 69%, and 63% survive a typical winter, so 65% is a middling guess.

| Option | Up-front cost/colony | Expected graded |
|---|---|---|
| Outdoors in wraps | $3 | about 65% |
| Agnete's cellar | $18 | about 80% |
| Dunmere Flats | $63 | about 90% |

- The cellar is the best value. Each extra graded colony costs about $100 at Dunmere compared with outdoors, but only about $100 for the cellar's improvement too, and the cellar needs a third of the cash. Compare cost per colony moved, not just grading rates.
- Outdoors alone leaves you short. 600 × 65% is about 390, so you'd miss 420 by roughly 30. That is a $40 penalty each, plus lost sales.
- You don't need 90% on every colony. You need 420 graded, and the contract probably pays for no more than 420. I'm assuming that, so check it.

**Suggested plan (about $12,000 up front, leaving about $3,000 spare)**

- About 200 colonies to the cellar: $3,600. Expect about 160 graded.
- About 120 colonies to Dunmere: $7,560. Expect about 108 graded.
- The other 280 stay outdoors in wraps: $840. Expect about 182 graded.
- Expected total is about 450 graded, against 420 needed. At $190 each, 420 colonies bring in $79,800.
- If winter is worse than average, you have about $3,000 of credit to move more colonies to Dunmere by late October. Don't spend it all now. Winter losses cluster, because a hard winter hits every outdoor colony at once, so keep the buffer.

**Rafe's offer: reconsider it**

You said you won't pay $150 for a colony you get $190 for. But that is a $40 profit on each one. It also costs you nothing up front, since you pay him when the co-op pays you.
- Without Rafe, a missing colony costs you the $40 shortfall charge.
- With Rafe, you pay $150 and collect $190. That is +$40 instead of -$40, an $80 improvement per colony.
- Up to 80 colonies at $150 means up to $12,000 owed at the end of March, paid out of that month's co-op payment. That is a reason to ask about his terms, but it needs no cash now.
- It's cheap insurance against a bad winter. It also lets you hold back some of the $63-a-colony Dunmere spend.
- Ask Rafe three things. Does he charge anything if you don't use the colonies? Will they pass the co-op's 8-frame grading? Can he deliver by February 20?

**Check before you commit**

1. **Cellar capacity.** "Holds about 500" may include Agnete's own 300, which would leave room for only about 200 of yours. That is why I planned 200. If more space is free, shift Dunmere colonies to the cellar. It is cheaper and nearly as good.
2. **One cellar season of data.** Agnete's 80% grading comes from four years of one operator's colonies. Ask about ventilation, temperature and humidity, and whether she'd let you look, since a cellar that is a few degrees too warm can ruin colonies.
3. **Contract terms.** Confirm that the 420 is a cap, and that the February 20 pickup means your colonies must be out of the cellar and healthy by then. Also confirm that the $40 charge is per colony short and not a flat penalty.

If the cellar takes fewer than 200 colonies, or the grading rate for your outdoor colonies is nearer 60%, then raise the Dunmere block and lean on Rafe for the gap. Either way, avoid winter outdoors for the whole herd. Three winters of 34 to 40% losses make that your riskiest choice.
