**Short answer:** For this year, don't switch everything to hybrids and don't wait for the grid. Keep most of the diesel fleet, fix the campsite overnight problem specifically, and commit to the grid for future years. The licence condition is the real constraint, and neither the hybrid pitch nor the grid offer addresses it on its own.

**1. The grid can't be ready this year, and it wouldn't cover your peak anyway**
- Eleven weeks out against a 14-week lead time means it misses this year, even if you sign tomorrow.
- A 400 A three-phase supply is roughly 270-290 kVA, or about 250 kW usable. Your peak is about 520 kW, so even in future years it covers around half the peak. You'd still need generators or batteries for the rest, unless the operator will quote a bigger connection.
- It is still probably worth doing. Your half is £19,000, which is under half of one year's current generator hire and fuel (about £41,900). That's a strong case over a ten-year lease. It would also cleanly solve the overnight campsite load, which is the hard part of the licence condition.
- Ask the operator what a larger connection would cost, and whether the connection can be energised in time for next year's build. Get the cost split with Gwen in writing (who owns the asset, what happens if the lease ends).

**2. Hybrids: the saving is small, and the claim needs checking**
- Current cost: 6 x £4,200 = £25,200 hire, plus 9,800 L x £1.70 = £16,660 fuel, about £41,860.
- Six hybrids: 6 x £5,100 = £30,600, plus fuel at 45% of today's (if the 55% cut is right) of about £7,500, about £38,100.
- That saves roughly £3,700, and only if Gantry's figure holds on your load profile. Treat it as unproven. Ask for their fuel-saving basis, a written guarantee or a fuel-linked price, and references from a festival of similar size.
- Capacity is tighter than it looks. A 100 kVA set is about 80 kW continuous, so six give about 480 kW on engines alone, below your 520 kW peak. You'd depend on the batteries at peak, with no spare unit. Your current fleet has plenty of headroom.
- So all-hybrid is paying more for less margin and a small, uncertain saving.

**3. The overnight condition is the main problem**
No generator within 150 m of the campsite may run from 23:00 to 07:00. The campsite draws about 45 kW overnight, including medical, which can't drop out. There are three ways to handle it.

- **Option A: move the two campsite generators more than 150 m away and run cable to the campsite.** This is likely the cheapest. It costs distribution cable, a distribution board, and fitting. Voltage drop over a few hundred metres at 45 kW is manageable with correctly sized cable. Check that the new position is acceptable to the council (measured from the campsite boundary or from the nearest tent?), and that it isn't near a neighbouring property.
- **Option B: battery-only overnight at the campsite.** 45 kW x 8 h = 360 kWh. Each hybrid has 120 kWh, and you can't use all of it, so you need about 4 units (about 480 kWh nominal), not 2. That's about £20,400 of hire against £8,400 for two diesels. The batteries must also be fully charged by 23:00 and can't recharge until 07:00. A cold or high-demand night leaves no margin. It's workable but expensive and has less margin.
- **Option C: a mix.** Put the campsite on Option A, and add one hybrid or a standalone battery or UPS just for the medical tent. That gives the contractor uninterrupted power through any generator trip or changeover, and it works whatever happens to the main supply.

My pick is A plus a dedicated battery or UPS for medical (C). It meets the condition, gives medical genuine redundancy, and costs far less than moving the whole site to hybrids.

**4. Suggested plan for this year**
1. Keep the arena on diesel: four 200 kVA sets, plus a spare if the budget allows. Check that none of them is within 150 m of the campsite, or that they're off at 23:00 with the arena load shed.
2. Relocate the two campsite sets, or replace them with Option B/C, and get the council to confirm in writing how they measure the 150 m and whether battery-only operation counts as not running.
3. Ask Gantry to supply one or two hybrids as a trial rather than a full switch. This lets you collect real fuel data for next year without risking the site.
4. Confirm the 520 kW peak figure and the overnight load with your own metering from last year. Everything above depends on them.
5. Sign the grid offer with Gwen now, and ask for a larger-capacity quote, so next year you can shrink the fleet to a peak top-up.

**Assumptions:** I've assumed the arena and stage generators are already more than 150 m from the campsite, that the 45 kW overnight figure includes the medical tent, and that about 80% of battery capacity is usable. Please check these, since they change the sizing in Option B. The decision that can't wait is the campsite overnight arrangement, so settle that with Gantry and the council first, and the rest of the generator order by the end of the month.
