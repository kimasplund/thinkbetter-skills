## How each finalist dies
### Stay outdoors in wraps
**Strongest case:** $1,800 total, no cash risk, no logistics, and you know how your yards behave. Your best recent winter (34% loss) still leaves 396 alive.
**Its own arithmetic:** 600 at your average loss is about 378 alive and about 330 graded, 90 short of 420. Even at 34% loss with every survivor grading, 396 is below 420.
**Killing modes:** Another 35-40% winter leaves you near 330 graded (~40%, because that is the average of three straight winters) · Survivors alive but under 8 frames on Feb 20 because late winter is slow (~20%, because a hard valley winter delays build-up; first sign is small clusters at a late-January check) · A cold snap or closed road around the Feb 20 pickup (~5%, because it is a single day on which everything depends).
**Chance it misses the objective:** ~90%, reference class your own last three winters; disanalogy, I do not know whether mite control or colony strength was the same in those years.
**Most dangerous:** the second mode, because nothing shows until late winter and nothing can be fixed after Feb 1. **Hidden assumption:** that the loss is mainly cold exposure rather than the colonies' health going in. **Cheapest falsifier:** an alcohol wash on 20 colonies this week, about $0-$50 and one day; most samples heavily infested means the loss is not about the place.

### Dunmere for as many as cash allows
**Strongest case:** what half the valley does, and the neighbours report the lowest losses available, with feeding included.
**Its own arithmetic:** the money buys about 185 places ($11,655, plus $1,245 to keep 415 outdoors). At the neighbours' 90%, that is 167 + 228 = about 395 graded. That is below 420 even before any shortfall. At 80% it is 376 and the net ($56.8k) is slightly under staying outdoors ($57.3k).
**Killing modes:** The 90% grading is not real (~30%, because 15% losses cannot coexist with 90% graded, and your own 104 of 150 was 69%; first sign is a neighbour who cannot show you grading tallies) · The line empties before March, or the return haul is priced above $30 (~20%, because you are spending $12,900 of $15,000 with nothing quoted for the trip home; first sign is a missing return quote) · A crowded yard spreads disease (~10%, because half the valley using one place concentrates pests; this is the way success harms it; first sign is a neighbour's December losses).
**Chance it misses the objective:** ~90%, reference class the neighbours' self-reports plus your own 2026 south shipment; disanalogy, theirs are survivors who chose to talk and mine was a smaller contract.
**Most dangerous:** the cash mode: it is irreversible once the haul is paid. **Hidden assumption:** that Dunmere's grading holds for your colonies. **Cheapest falsifier:** ask three neighbours for their actual graded tallies against colonies hauled and the return-haul price in writing; $0, two days; if their graded share is under 80% or the quote is over $30, the option is gone.

### Cellar for the confirmed spare places
**Strongest case:** $18 a colony, 40 km away, four years at 16% loss, and 80% grading. You could use the whole spare space for $3,600.
**Its own arithmetic:** 200 at 75% (I cut Agnete's 80% for the larger load) gives 150, plus 400 outdoors at 55% gives 220, so 370 graded and 50 short. Best case is about 400, still under 420, so this option alone does not reach the objective.
**Killing modes:** There is not 200 of spare space (~30%, because Agnete's 300 may already take most of the owner's "about 500"; first sign is no written headcount by Oct 20) · Crowding at 500 total, which has never been tested above her 300, brings heat, CO2 or damp (~20%, because bees in a cellar need air exchange and a lightly loaded one hides the problem; first sign is cellar temperature drifting from Agnete's range) · A single site: the owner, power, or a flood decides the whole group (~10%, because you do not control the critical dependency; first sign is the owner changing the terms).
**Chance it misses the objective:** ~90% alone, reference class one operator over four years; disanalogy, her colonies and routine, not yours, and her 300 against your up to 200 more.
**Most dangerous:** the crowding mode: nobody sees inside until it is too late and the colonies are already inside. **Hidden assumption:** the 16% is the cellar's effect, not Agnete's beekeeping. **Cheapest falsifier:** visit this week and ask what she does besides the cellar (mite treatment, autumn weights) and what her losses were before; add a look at the temperature logs. $0, one day; if she has no records, or her pre-cellar losses were also low, the cellar is not what is protecting her.

### Broker cover only
**Strongest case:** no cash up front, $40 profit per colony, and it plugs exactly the gap that remains.
**Its own arithmetic:** 330 own + 80 = 410, 10 short even with every colony delivered.
**Killing modes:** The co-op will not accept outside colonies, or they fail the grade (~30%, because provenance and health rules are common and "meet the grade" is the broker's claim; first sign is the co-op's answer) · He cannot find 80 after the same winter (~20%, because "up to" is not a commitment and his suppliers face the same losses as you; first sign is no named source by Nov 15) · Your own colonies do worse than 55% (~15%, because a worse winter eats the cover; first sign is January dead-outs).
**Chance it misses the objective:** ~70%, reference class your own shortfalls plus his "up to 80"; disanalogy, no history with this broker.
**Most dangerous:** he fails in the same winter you do, and it shows only in January. **Hidden assumption:** that the co-op counts his colonies. **Cheapest falsifier:** a one-line question to the co-op this week; $0, 1-2 days; "no" kills it.

**What the whole field assumes:** that the losses come from where the colonies winter rather than from the state they enter winter in. If most colonies are mite-heavy or weak going in, a cellar and Dunmere carry that problem inside with them. Test this first: the mite wash costs almost nothing and comes before the haul.
