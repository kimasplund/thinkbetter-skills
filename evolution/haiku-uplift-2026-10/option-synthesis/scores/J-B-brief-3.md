O0M: Concise and correct on the crates, the bridge and holding the container until 14 April. But it attacks a straw man: it says the vans can't carry "about 4.5 tonnes each", when the brief's plan is two round trips at about 2.25 t per load. It misses that the van timing doesn't fit the window.
W8D: The deepest content of the set: the van schedule has no slack, operator permissions, plywood exemption, the 5 March sailing is infeasible after 3 March crating, a haulier alternative, and bracing wood. It is buried under pre-registration, adoption rules, a scoring table, "not blind" disclaimers and a calibration ritual. Many probabilities are invented, though flagged as weakly sourced.

E4Y: It catches that the second van trip lands on 19 or 20 April, and it proposes a near-side van relay for the bridge. Leakage ("compared in one context, not blind", "One ask"). Several of its percentages are unflagged guesses.
X0O: Solid and specific. It asks whether groupage is charged on volume or weight, and whether the finished furniture itself needs treatment. Its van payload reasoning is correct, and its questions to Quintell are good. It doesn't analyse whether the van timing fits the window.
N6S: Tight, and it adds a useful container fit and door-height check. It contradicts itself on holding: the container could be up to 5 days early, but it asks for "up to two days". It also misses that the vans' second trip would be late.
Z2Y: A good idea to use the vans as the shuttle over the bridge for the last leg, and it checks crate volume. It assumes the quotes are all-in including customs, which conflicts with its own bridge point. It invents "outside UK and EU". The "Committed/declined" label is leakage.
A0O: It misses the bridge for the container entirely ("the one real risk: early arrival"), and wrongly says a 10–14 March groupage departure reliably lands in the window. Its first step is to book before asking anything.
