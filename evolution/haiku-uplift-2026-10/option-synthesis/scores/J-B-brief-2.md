Notes (J-B, brief-2)

- K3Y: Catches the main traps. The grid misses this year and covers only about 250 kW. Six hybrid engines give only about 480 kW sustained, below the peak. The night load needs about 4 packs, not 2. It also asks whether battery-only running counts as running. Cheapest fix (relocate plus a UPS for medical) is well argued and sized. Small leap: "likely cheapest" for relocation is not priced.
- G1S: Right on lead time, grid capacity and that 2 packs are short at night. Says "pays back in well under a year" even though its own capacity point means the grid replaces only part of the spend. "Mixed fleet" has no unit counts, and it never checks the hybrids' sustained engine capacity.
- Y5H: Good break-even (32%), and a good check of the 55% claim (720 kWh lasts about 1.4 h at peak). Asks the council question. Misses the shortfall in sustained engine kW. "Three or four packs" leaves no margin at three. Has some padding (the site-move lease check).
- X3A: Concrete plan with costs: 4 hybrids at the campsite, 4 diesels on the main site. But it says six hybrids are "far more capacity than the 520 kW peak needs", which gets sustained capacity backwards. "Many stages stop around midnight" is unsupported.
- O8G: Recommends five hybrids, taking the 900 kW combined figure at face value. Engines alone give about 400 kW against a 520 kW peak, and it doesn't flag that, so the core advice is risky. The fallback and the Gantry conditions help. It leaks method ("one pass, not blind", tripwires).
- X6H: A sound pick (relocate everything, plus 2 hybrids on trial), but the decision rules, tripwires, "reverse autopsy" and closing "the ask" are scaffolding. The trial's net gain is overstated (about £1.2k net, not £2-3k).
- J1S: The overnight load-cut idea and the council "running" question are real insights. But it is buried under scoring tables, adoption rules and probabilities with invented reference classes, and it invents a £52k ceiling. Far too long for the task.
- F2L: The most complete content. It covers the hybrid engine shortfall, break-even, voltage drop, the grid's excluded distribution costs and landowner terms. But it comes with heavy method machinery, made-up failure percentages and pseudo-scored totals. A production manager would have to dig for the answer.
