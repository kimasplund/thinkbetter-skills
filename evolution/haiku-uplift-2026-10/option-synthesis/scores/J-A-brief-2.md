# J-A scores, brief-2

Notes:
- F2L: The strongest content. It catches the grid lead time, that 400 A is about 277 kVA against a 520 kW peak, that the hybrids' engines give 480 kW against that peak, that the night needs 360 kWh, and that hybrids break even at 32%. It also makes the Gwen heads-of-terms point. But it is buried under scoring schemes, adoption rules, "not blind" and "no hash seals it" commentary, and invented percentage probabilities. Far too long for the task.
- J1S: A careful night-energy analysis, the council's reading of "running", the load-cut lever, a yard test and a clean IF/ELSE. It misses that the hybrid engines fall short of the peak, and its plan rests on an unmeasured 20-24 kW night load. Heavy machinery, and like F2L too much ceremony.
- X6H: Sensible plan (relocate, trial two hybrids), but the reasoning is shaky. It says one 120 kWh pack lasts 2.5 h, "so it still needs an engine", which ignores using several packs. The 60% fuel tripwire would almost certainly trip. "Stop all engines" as a complaint response would cut power. Some leakage from "reverse autopsy" and "The ask".
- O8G: Recommends five hybrids. That gives about 400 kW of engine against a 520 kW peak, with the campsite packs also needed overnight. The capacity risk is not reconciled, and the fuel savings for five units are guessed. It does have a sensible fallback and council and UPS steps. The "one pass, not blind" remark is a trace of its method.
- X3A: Correct battery sizing (about 96 kWh usable, four packs) and a note that packs can't recharge in the silent window. But it never asks the council whether battery-mode hybrids count as running. Eight units cost more hire, and it never compares that with relocation. "Far more capacity" overstates the hybrids. It reads cleanly.
- Y5H: Gets the break-even and grid capacity right, but contradicts itself. The headline says diesel, while the plan puts hybrids at the campsite edge. The plan stays thin on specifics.
- G1S: "Pays back in well under a year" is wrong for a supply that covers about half the peak. It sites the hybrids more than 150 m away, which makes them pointless for the noise rule. The plan is muddled.
- K3Y: The best balance. It catches that six hybrid engines give 480 kW below the peak with no spare, the 360 kWh / four-unit night, the cost of the relocation against the battery route, a medical UPS, both council questions, ownership terms with Gwen, a larger grid quote and metering checks. Proportionate, and it reads as plain content.
