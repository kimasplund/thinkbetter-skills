# Pack notes: option-synthesis

**Brief 1 (commercial beekeeping, overwintering for a pollination contract).** The incumbent, wintering outdoors, looks cheap but fails the contract on its own numbers: about 260 graded colonies against 420, so penalties plus forgone revenue make it the worst-paying option (k1). The salient favourite, moving everything south, needs about $37,800 before the co-op pays, against a $15,000 line the bank won't raise (k2). The cellar looks like a 500-place fallback, but Agnete's 300 colonies already occupy it, which only shows once the reader tries to use it (k3). The broker offer is framed by the user as a thin margin, but against the $40 penalty it is an $80 swing per colony with no cash needed before March (k4). A feasible winner exists: about 200 in the cellar, about 150-170 south, the rest outdoors, plus the broker, which clears 420 within the cash line. k3 and k4 are the ones a quick reply is most likely to miss. The two framings keep every fact and number of this brief, so the same keys apply.

**Brief 2 (festival temporary power).** The diesel incumbent is dominated by the hybrid sets once fuel is counted; the user's per-unit price worry points the wrong way (k1). The grid favourite cannot arrive in time: 14 weeks against 11 (k2). The decisive constraint is split across the brief. The campsite generators sit at the campsite edge, the medical tent needs power all night, and the licence condition at the end bans generators within 150 m from 23:00 to 07:00 (k3). Even after switching to hybrids, two campsite packs hold 240 kWh against a 360 kWh night, so the obvious swap fails at about 04:20 (k4). k3 and k4 are the hard ones: k3 needs three separated facts joined, and k4 only appears after the reader has already chosen hybrids.

**Brief 3 (export freight for a hotel fit-out).** The forwarder's provisional groupage booking is dominated by a full container that is cheaper, faster and handled less (k1). The salient pressure is the delay charge, which pushes toward speed, but the window has a floor: the first sailing arrives before the floors are sealed, so the winner must time the sailing or hold near the site (k2). The 7.5-tonne bridge limit, presented as a note for the drivers, stops a container truck reaching the hotel and forces a transload (k3). The checklist line on heat-treated, stamped wooden packaging, presented as routine paperwork, conflicts with the workshop's habit of building crates from offcuts, and it binds every option including Joss's vans (k4). k2 and k4 are the most likely misses. k3 tends to surface only after the container has been picked over groupage.
