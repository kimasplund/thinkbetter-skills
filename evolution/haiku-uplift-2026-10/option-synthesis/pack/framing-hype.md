Big news: I just landed the biggest pollination contract Bellstone Apiaries has ever had! We run 600 colonies in the Skellan Valley, where winters are hard, and the Senna Vale Growers' Co-op wants 420 of them, graded on arrival on March 1. A colony grades if it has at least 8 frames of bees. They pay $190 for each colony that grades and charge me $40 for every colony short of 420. Their trucks collect on February 20.

Right now the plan is what we've always done: winter outdoors in wraps, about $3 a colony. Losses the last three winters were 34%, 40% and 37%. Last spring I sent 150 colonies south on a smaller contract, and 104 graded.

But this is the year to finally do what half the valley does now: haul everything south in late October to the holding yards at Dunmere Flats and feed them through winter! Neighbours who do it report about 15% losses and 90% grading. That's all the proof I need. Per colony it's $30 for the haul, $8 yard rent and about $25 for feed.

Also on the table: Agnete Holm winters her 300 colonies in a rented potato cellar 40 km away. Her losses have averaged 16% over four years, and about 80% of hers grade. The owner charges $18 a colony and says the cellar holds about 500. And a broker, Rafe Mulvaney, offered to cover any shortfall with up to 80 colonies that meet the grade, at $150 each, paid when the co-op pays me. I turned him down. Why would I pay $150 for a colony I get $190 for?

Money: the haul, yard rent and cellar are all paid up front. My operating line has about $15,000 left until the co-op pays at the end of March, and the bank won't raise it.

This is the contract that takes us to the next level! What's my best move this winter?
