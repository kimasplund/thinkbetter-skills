**Short answer:** Don't go all-in on Dunmere, because you can't pay for it. Put as many colonies as the cellar will take into it, keep the rest outdoors, and take Rafe's offer as a backstop. Turning Rafe down is the one decision I'd reverse.

**Why Rafe's offer is better than it looks**

You compared his $150 with the $190 the co-op pays. The right comparison is with what a missing colony costs you. Every colony short of 420 loses the $190 and also costs you a $40 penalty, so a short colony is a $230 swing. If Rafe fills a gap, you pay $150, collect $190 and avoid the $40. That nets you +$80 per colony compared with being short.

It also costs nothing up front. You pay him when the co-op pays you, so it doesn't touch your $15,000. And you only pay for colonies you would otherwise be short. At most it covers 80, which is a real limit but a useful one.

**Why not Dunmere for everything**

- It costs $63 a colony ($30 + $8 + $25). For 600 colonies that is $37,800 up front, against $15,000 of credit. The most you could send is about 220, and that leaves you nothing for anything else.
- The gain is smaller than it looks. Outdoor colonies are grading about 65% (the three winters' losses were 34 to 40%, and 104 of 150 graded last spring). Dunmere is about 90%. That is about 25 more graded colonies per 100, for about $60 more per colony.
- A colony you get by moving up from short is worth $230, or about $57 of extra expected value per colony sent to Dunmere. That is roughly break-even, and it ties up cash you can't spare.
- Once Rafe's colonies are available, each Dunmere colony replaces about 0.25 of a colony you could buy from him for $150. That's worth about $37 and costs $60, so Dunmere loses.

**Why the cellar is the best value**

- It costs $18 a colony, about $15 more than wintering outdoors.
- It lifts grading from about 65% to about 80%. That is 15 more graded colonies per 100, worth roughly $34 each at $230, and roughly $22 each even if the alternative is Rafe at $150.
- It is the only option where the gain clearly beats the extra cost.

**The plan**

1. Put about 200 colonies in the cellar. That is $3,600, and it should produce about 160 that grade. Confirm the capacity first. "Holds about 500" may include Agnete's 300, which would leave only about 200 spare. If more room is free, use it, up to your cash limit.
2. Winter the other 400 outdoors in wraps, about $1,200. Expect about 260 to grade.
3. That gives about 420 graded, which is right at your target. Up-front cash is about $4,800, leaving roughly $10,000 of your line in reserve.
4. Call Rafe back. Ask for a written agreement for up to 80 colonies that meet the grade (at least 8 frames), delivered in time for the February 20 trucks, at $150, payable when the co-op pays you. Ask what happens if he can't supply them.
5. Check your colonies in mid-January and early February. If the count is badly short, you will know early enough to use Rafe's colonies.

**What can go wrong**

- Outdoor losses of 40% would give about 360 graded colonies against 420. That is 60 short, which Rafe's 80 would cover.
- Rafe is the weak link. If he doesn't deliver, each missing colony costs you $230. If you want insurance against that, the spare reserve could send some colonies to Dunmere instead of keeping them outdoors. Each one moved improves your odds but costs about $60 more. With about $10,000 spare, you could move up to about 150 colonies and still have some cash left. I'd do that only if you don't trust his supply. Dunmere's haul is in late October, so decide soon.
- The cellar's 80% figure comes from Agnete's colonies. Your own may differ. Her four-year losses of 16% are encouraging, and the cellar is only 40 km away. Ask her how she manages humidity and ventilation, and visit before committing.

**Assumptions I made**

- Colonies beyond the 420 you deliver earn nothing.
- The 65% outdoor grading rate comes from your recent losses and last spring's 104 of 150.
- Dunmere's $63 and the cellar's $18 are all paid up front, and those are the only up-front costs.
- Your $15,000 has to cover other costs too, so don't spend all of it on wintering.
