My recommendation: keep diesel hire for this year's event, add a hybrid mix only if Gantry can prove the fuel saving in writing, and sign for the grid now for 2027 onward. The grid cannot arrive in time for this festival.

**The numbers (last year's figures, my assumptions noted)**

- Current diesel plan: 6 x £4,200 hire = £25,200. Fuel: 9,800 L x £1.70 = £16,660. Total about £41,860.
- Hybrid plan as quoted: 6 x £5,100 = £30,600 hire. Hybrids cost £5,400 more in hire.
- Fuel break-even: hybrids only pay off if they cut fuel by at least about 32% (£5,400 / £16,660). Gantry claims 55%, which would give fuel of about £7,500 and a total of about £38,100, roughly £3,800 cheaper than diesel.
- The 55% figure is a vendor claim. It depends on how often the battery can carry the load. Your peak is 520 kW, and six packs hold 720 kWh in total. At full peak the batteries would last only about 1.4 hours, so the engines would run for much of the event. Ask Gantry for their load model using your real profile, and get the 55% figure guaranteed or priced as a contract term.

**The grid**

- The quoted 400 A three-phase supply is roughly 277 kVA, or about 250 kW usable. That is below your 520 kW peak, so the grid alone cannot run the site. It can cover the overnight base load (about 45 kW) and much of the daytime load.
- Your share of £38,000 is about £19,000. Spread over a ten-year lease, that is about £1,900 a year, which is cheap against £40k-plus of annual power costs.
- A 14-week lead time from 11 weeks out means the connection would arrive about three weeks after the festival. Order it now for 2027, and plan this year on generation.
- Check that the lease, the landowner agreement and the network operator's quote all still apply if the festival moves sites.

**The campsite and the council condition**

- The condition limits generators within 150 m of the campsite between 23:00 and 07:00. The two units at the campsite edge are the problem.
- Overnight the campsite needs about 45 kW for roughly 8 hours, which is about 360 kWh. Two hybrid packs hold only 240 kWh, which is not enough. Three or four packs charged during the day could cover the night with the engine off, which may satisfy the condition.
- The medical tent needs uninterrupted power. A battery-backed supply with a diesel fallback further from the campsite is the safest arrangement for it.
- Confirm with the council whether a battery running with its engine off counts as a generator under the condition, and whether a hybrid's engine may start if the battery runs low overnight. Get the answer in writing before you commit.

**Suggested plan for this year**

1. Keep the four main-site units as diesel 200 kVA for peak load, or hybrids if Gantry proves the fuel saving.
2. Put battery-backed hybrids at the campsite edge, sized to run the night with the engine off, and confirm this with the council.
3. Ask Gantry for the load model, a fuel guarantee, and the council position in writing. Decide by the end of the month.
4. Sign the grid connection now for 2027.

**Assumptions I made**

- Last year's 9,800 L and 520 kW peak are typical, and diesel prices are unchanged.
- The 400 A figure is the supply rating, and the usable capacity is roughly 250 kW.
- Hire rates are fixed for the full event, and delivery and fuel handling are included in the quotes.

**Questions to answer before the decision**

- Can Gantry guarantee the 55% fuel saving, or give a price for that guarantee?
- Are the two campsite-edge units able to be moved further from the campsite?
