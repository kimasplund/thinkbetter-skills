**Why this beats the other two options**

- **Cost.** 52 m³ on groupage at £96/m³ comes to about £4,990. The 40 ft container is £4,150 for the whole box, and your 52 m³ fits inside its 67 m³. The vans come to about £9,600 (four van-trips at £2,400).
- **Time.** Groupage takes 5–7 weeks from a consolidation that leaves after you finish on 3 March. The worst case lands around 28 April, ten days past the 18 April window, which costs £1,200 a day. The container takes 4–5 weeks. The 12 March sailing should arrive roughly 9–16 April, inside your window if delivery is set for the 14th.
- **Why not the other sailings.** The 5 March sailing costs the same, but it arrives 2–9 April, before the floors are sealed, so you would pay to store it for up to two weeks. The 19 March sailing arrives 16–23 April, and at the 5-week end it misses the 18th.
- **The vans.** They give the most control, but they cost more than twice the container, take ten working days of driving across the water and back, and carry the bridge risk below. Keep them for the last leg, not the main carriage.

**Check these before you book**

1. **Wood treatment.** This is the biggest risk, and it comes from your own offcut crates. Wooden packaging going abroad must be heat-treated and stamped, as Quintell's checklist says. Offcuts are almost certainly not certified. Either use a certified heat-treatment service or buy treated timber, and confirm the lead time fits before 3 March. Crates without the stamp can be refused or held at the border, and that would blow the window. Ask Quintell which treater they use.
2. **The bridge.** The 7.5-tonne limit applies to every vehicle going into the harbour quarter, not only your vans. A 40 ft container lorry will almost certainly not get through. Ask Quintell in writing how they will deliver: either to a transfer point this side of the bridge, or on a vehicle under the limit. If they transfer, your two 7.5-tonne vans become the last leg, which is the one useful job for Joss's plan. Check the load against the vans' payload and make sure the drivers have the bridge warning.
3. **Delivery date and storage.** Ask Quintell to deliver on 14 April, or to hold the container at a secure site until then, and get the storage price in writing in case it arrives early.
4. **Customs.** Velaro is across the water, so expect customs paperwork on top of the heat-treatment line. Send the checklist to the hotel's contractor and to a customs broker, and make sure the invoice and packing list match the crate count.
5. **Crate size.** Check that the crates are still around 52 m³ once built. If they come out bigger, the 67 m³ container may not hold them, and you would need a second box, which changes the price.
