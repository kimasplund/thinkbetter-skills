**Recommendation: book the full 40 ft container on the 12 March sailing (£4,150).**

It is the cheapest option that lands inside your 14–18 April window with a usable margin, and it is the easiest to control.

**Why not groupage**
- Cost: 52 m³ × £96 = £4,992, which is £842 more than the container.
- Timing: transit is 5–7 weeks and the consolidations leave weekly. Leaving on 3 March gives an arrival anywhere from about 7 April to 21 April. Early arrival means nowhere to put the load, and late arrival costs £1,200 a day. Only a departure around 10–14 March reliably lands in the window, and the spread of transit times means even that is not guaranteed.
- Your load shares a container with other people's freight, so your dates depend on theirs.

**Why the 12 March container**
- The container has 67 m³ of space and your load is 52 m³ and 9 t, so it fits comfortably.
- Sailing 12 March with 4–5 weeks transit gives arrival of 9–16 April. Almost all of that range is inside the window.
- The 5 March sailing arrives 2–9 April, which is too early because the floors are being sealed until the 14th.
- The 19 March sailing arrives 16–23 April. At 5 weeks, that is up to 5 days late, or £6,000 in delay charges.

**The one real risk: early arrival (9–13 April)**
Ask Quintell now whether the container can be held at the port or terminal and delivered to site on 14 April, and what that storage costs. Get this in writing before you book. Your goods are finished by 3 March, so you have about a week of buffer before the 12 March sailing.

**Joss's option (own vans)**
- Cost: 2 trips × 2 vans × £2,400 = £9,600, which is more than twice the container price.
- Time: 10 days of driving for the two round trips, plus crossings and driver hours. This is a planning and safety load on a nine-person firm during the busiest month.
- Weight: a 7.5 t box van has only a few tonnes of real payload after its own weight, so 9 t across two vans is tight. Check each van's actual payload before counting on this.
- Bridge: a 7.5 t van is at the limit of the harbour bridge. Confirm with the contractor or the local authority that it is permitted and whether any restriction applies to your vehicles.
- Control: you can keep control with the container too. Ask Quintell for a booking reference, sailing confirmation and vessel tracking, and set a delivery date with them.

**Things that apply whichever option you choose**
- Wood packaging: your own crates made from offcuts will need ISPM 15 treatment and stamping, as Quintell's checklist says. Offcuts that have not been heat-treated and stamped will not be accepted at the border. Confirm with the kiln or supplier how long this takes, and book it now so it is done before 3 March.
- Customs paperwork: start on Quintell's checklist now, so the documents are ready when the container is loaded.
