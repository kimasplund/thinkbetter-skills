**Ship it as a full 40 ft container on the 12 March sailing.** Groupage is the most expensive and least predictable option for a load this size, and the vans cost more than twice as much as the container for a control benefit you can get more cheaply.

**The numbers (door to door, before the issues below)**
- Full 40 ft container: £4,150 for 67 m³, 4–5 weeks. Your 52 m³ fills about 78% of it.
- Groupage: 52 m³ × £96 = £4,992, 5–7 weeks, on a weekly consolidation. That is £842 dearer than the container, and its arrival spreads over about two weeks against a five-day window.
- Vans: 2 trips × 2 vans × £2,400 = £9,600, about ten days of driving for your crew. That is £5,450 more than the container.

**Which sailing**
- **5 March:** arrives roughly 2–9 April, 5–12 days before the floors open. Only works if you can hold the load for up to two weeks.
- **12 March:** arrives roughly 9–16 April. Most of that range is a few days early, so you need a holding place for a day or two. The cost of holding is far below the delay charges.
- **19 March:** arrives roughly 16–23 April. Each day after the 18th costs £1,200. Averaged across the range, that is roughly £2,000–2,500 in delay charges.

12 March keeps the cost predictable. These ranges assume transit is spread evenly across the quoted weeks. That is my assumption, not Quintell's.

**Three things to settle before booking, whichever option you take**
1. **Heat treatment.** Crates built from offcuts will not be accepted as they are. Wooden packaging needs heat treatment and an IPPC stamp. Send the offcuts to an accredited treater, or buy certified crating, and confirm the lead time now. Crates must be finished by 3 March, so this is the real deadline.
2. **The bridge.** A 40 ft container on a lorry is far over 7.5 t. Ask Quintell to deliver to a yard outside the harbour quarter and transfer to a vehicle under the limit, or to name a route that avoids the bridge. Get the answer in writing, because it may change the price.
3. **Fit.** Check the tallest wardrobe and reception pieces against the container's internal height (about 2.39 m standard, 2.69 m high-cube) and its door opening (about 2.3 m high). A 78% fill leaves little room for awkward crates.

**Why not the vans**
Joss is right that the vans give the most control over arrival day. But they cost £5,450 more than the container, take two drivers away for about ten days, and at 7.5 t they sit exactly on the bridge limit with no margin for load. The vans are the fallback if Quintell cannot confirm the 12 March date, a bridge route, or a holding arrangement.

**What to do this week**
- Ask Quintell for three things: the 12 March booking with an ETA commitment, the bridge transfer plan, and the charge for holding the container for up to two days early.
- Book a heat treater and confirm the stamp turnaround.
- Stuff the container 4–9 March, leaving time for customs papers before sailing.

**Decision rule**
- IF Quintell confirms the 12 March sailing, the bridge transfer and a holding arrangement, THEN book the container for 12 March.
- IF the 12 March slot is full, or the bridge plan cannot work for a container, THEN either book the 19 March sailing and tell the contractor now that arrival is 16–23 April, or take the vans and accept £9,600.
- Do not choose groupage. It costs more, arrives less predictably, and at 52 m³ it is too large to be the cheap option.

**Assumptions**
- The sailing and delivery dates are next March and April.
- The 52 m³ and 9 t are the crated volume and weight.
- Velaro requires customs entry. The same paperwork applies to every option, so it does not change the choice.
- Van payload and loading plan are not checked here.
