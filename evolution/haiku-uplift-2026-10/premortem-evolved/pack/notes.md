# Pack notes

**Brief 1 (pottery studio second site).** The user's "comfortably in the black" line is the main trap. It sets $13,600 per 8-week course against $9,400 per month, so the space loses money even when full (k1, hard). The kiln figures are spread across one bullet, and they only show a shortfall once a reply counts two firings per piece and adds the members' work moved over from the dying kiln. Full enrollment then causes the backlog (k2, hard, a case where success is the cause of failure). The gas meter's 10–14 week lead time from June 2 runs past both the August 4 start and the end of the rent-free period, and with the old kiln not being replaced there may be no firing at all (k3, medium, a dated-constraint audit). The 140-name waitlist is the most prominent evidence of demand, but it comes from a free fair booth and shows no willingness to pay (k4, easier). The hype and skeptical framings keep every fact and number, and the skeptical register adds no doubt aimed at any specific key.

**Brief 2 (nonprofit tutoring expansion grant).** The grant looks like secure money. Because it pays quarterly in arrears with a 45-day lag, $150k of reserves at $50k a month runs out at the end of January, about six weeks before the first payment (k1, hard, needs dates turned into cash arithmetic). The success definition fails on the organization's own data: a full year yields about 12 points, yet the new schools must reach 15 after about 13 weeks (k2, hard, the decisive fact sits in the last bullet). The principals' enthusiasm points the wrong way. The binding approval is a district MOU that took five months last time, against about three months available, and it also gates the student data the year-2 metric needs (k3, medium, a test of commitment grade and lead time). The tutor pipeline must grow 2.5x from a single university source whose spring term starts after launch (k4, medium).

**Brief 3 (chili crisp entering a grocery chain).** The retail deal reads as a win. Funding 25% off the $8.99 shelf price costs $2.25 a jar, more than the roughly $2.00 contribution, so every promoted jar loses money (k1, hard, needs the shelf price, not the wholesale price). If the buyer's velocity estimate holds, the deductions (slotting $36k plus promo billbacks) and net-60 terms mean little cash arrives before midsummer, while a second minimum run comes due around late May (k2, hard, success causes the cash crunch). If velocity falls short, the 5,000-jar per-flavor minimum outlasts the 6-month shelf life and expired stock is charged back (k3, hard, the downside half of the same trap). The trial batch's 2–3 weeks were never added to the schedule, so the March 1 date slips (k4, medium, found by summing stated lead times).
