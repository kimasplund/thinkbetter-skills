- W9S: The most complete analysis. Its decision rule and walk-away points are strong. But it is extremely long, full of machinery (commitment scale, I×F, tiers, calibration), and pads the scenarios with invented figures.
- J7A: Puts the MOU first and calls the Jan 12 date the fatal flaw, not the bar. Arithmetic error: it says the deficit peaks near $100k, but the brief's numbers give about $75k. Heavy machinery, including scaffolding lines like "If the most-likely and most-dangerous were the same: they are not".

Calls the year-two trigger probably unreachable and gives concrete renegotiation options (proportional release, full-year trigger). Practical pre-vote steps. A few generic lines ("funders often prefer...", "typically leave after one semester") are stated without support.
- M5G: Ranks the year-two bar first, which is correct. Cash math is right, and it notes the recurring gap and that idle coordinators make the cash problem worse. Asks whether the MOU can be amended. Recommends "accept with changes" and puts the downside question squarely to the board. The "other points" list pads it slightly.
- P6X: Misranks the central issue. It gives the bar only about 10%, says there is "no single fatal flaw", and its rebuild does not try to renegotiate the bar. Internal contradiction: it assumes the 70 tutors are unavailable in January, then launches on them. Its "8 to 10 points" from a quarter of the dose is unsupported, and the referral-overflow mode is invented. Heavy machinery.
