Notes (J-A, practitioner view):

- H8Z: Very strong substance. It finds the kiln as the binding constraint (about 105 finished pieces a week, so about 18 beginners at most), the reduction-vs-electric glaze shift for members, the deposit and member-consent gates, and the landlord letter needed for the utility application. But it is buried under I×F tables, failure-share percentages, tier labels and an adversary kill chain. The invented "~85%" and the "2-3x lead time" rule are thinly grounded.
- Z8T: Concise and correct on unit economics, the meter date, two-firing capacity and the Christmas cash gap ($34k vs $47k). The steps are sensible but have few thresholds. It honestly asks for the missing loan and registration data.
- Q6F: Covers the main issues, with the heavy method scaffolding. The two-cohort cost comparison is muddled. It invents details ("one afternoon", "you are the only person driving"). It ranks kiln capacity as second-order even though the plan's own numbers make it certain.
- E6P: Correct and readable. Good non-obvious points: ask whether the utility needs a signed lease, and the waitlist fills one cycle, not a year. It hedges the capacity math with a "fired once" case that a potter wouldn't need. Next steps have no thresholds.
- S6K: The best balance. Correct per-month math, the rent-free period ending before the kiln, members left without firing, capacity framed as finished pieces (33-71% over), a deposit test with a threshold, and a clear ordered fix list. The conversion range is asserted as "typical" without a source.
- E3E: The deepest analysis. Kiln saturation at 15-22 beginners, gas cycle-time risk, unpriced fit-out, owner bandwidth, permitted use, and a cash walk to Christmas that checks out. Very long, with heavy machinery leakage (commitment grades, I×F, tie-break logic).
- J4O: Clear and actionable. Rent starts before the kiln, a pilot at the existing studio, a stop rule, and a practical doubt about 70 pieces in 24 cu ft. The "probably underpriced" and conversion claims are lightly supported.
- N3Y: Method-heavy with real errors. It says five cohorts at $340 "clear $9,400", which they don't (about $9,200). Its timeline has two courses run by October. A "2-3x rule" stretches the meter date to March. It misreads students as attending four evenings a week. Several checklist and decision dates fall after the June 2 signing, and the decision rule requires a meter date the quote makes impossible.
