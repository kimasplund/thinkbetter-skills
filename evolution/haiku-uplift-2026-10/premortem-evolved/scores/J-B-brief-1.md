Notes (J-B, skeptical reviewer):

- N3Y: Gets the per-course vs per-month error ($7,370) and the double-firing kiln math right. But it says five cohorts at $340 "clear $9,400", which is wrong (about $9,200). It calls an uncited "2–3× rule" for lead times a rule. Its decision rule lets you sign without fixing the economics it names as the fatal flaw. Heavy ceremony throughout (I×F, falsifiers, calibration review).
- J4O: Covers the timeline, unit economics, kiln shortfall, waitlist and guarantee clearly, then gives ordered fixes, a cheap pilot and a stop rule. The "typical conversion 10–20%" figure is unsourced, though hedged. Well sized.
- E3E: The deepest analysis. Arithmetic checks out (cash level on Sept 30, a $12k shortfall at full fill, the kiln saturating at 15–22 beginners, the waitlist as a one-time pool). It flags its own estimates as unverified. Buried under heavy machinery: commitment grades, tie-breaks, I×F, an overall 90% figure. Far longer than a gut check needs.
- S6K: Correct per-cycle loss, a $430 break-even price and 105 finished pieces a week. The "5–15% typical conversion" is invented. "8–10 months" of cushion slightly misstates $22k/$2k. Concise and usable.
- E6P: Sound, and honest about whether pieces are fired once or twice ("check which your numbers use"). Notes that the waitlist fills one cycle, not the year. Gives a $434 break-even, a pilot and a firing fallback, and states its assumptions plainly. The best balance of insight and calibration.
- Q6F: Correct math, including the roughly $13k gap to Christmas, plus the kiln shortfall and member-firing risk. Its fixes are vaguer: it doesn't land on a break-even price and says "cut the fixed cost or loan". It ends by asking for data, which is reasonable.
- Z8T: Assumes the year is 2027. The two-cohort rebuild worsens the monthly gap, and its cost-benefit line is a non sequitur. It names the "binding constraint" as the cushion vs the guarantee, which is muddled. The $800/month consumables figure is invented. Heavy machinery.
- H8Z: Strong, non-obvious points: the kiln as the binding constraint (about 18 students' worth of capacity) and the change in glaze colour moving from electric to gas reduction firing. Its arithmetic checks out. It also has a garbled tripwire, an overall 85% figure, commitment-grade jargon and very heavy ceremony.
