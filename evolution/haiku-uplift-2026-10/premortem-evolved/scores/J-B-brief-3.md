Notes (J-B, brief-3)

H5A: Best coverage. It catches the trial-batch slip, the second-run cash wall (about $93k needed against $60k), payback after September and the velocity risk, and it proposes a cheap 4-store shelf trial to test velocity. The arithmetic checks out. It is buried under heavy scaffolding (Autopsy, I×F tables, prevent/limit labels, a kill chain, calibration rows), and its narratives add invented details (UPC reprint, 3% leakage), though these are partly labelled as weakly sourced.
S1A: The cash trough (-$42k), the expiry figure at half velocity (~3,800 jars) and the mid-October payback are all correct. It ranks the March 1 slip low and adds a tripwire for a warehouse date it has already shown to be impossible. Machinery leaks throughout ("commitment grades: accept/comply", I×F tables).
J6H: The richest correct analysis: the free fill equals two weeks of sales, the price jumps 33% when the promo ends, production should run no earlier than 3 weeks before delivery, and the base business needs ring-fencing. It counts the fill at forgone wholesale, which slightly overstates the September deficit. It is honest about having no category data, but it is very long and full of method labels.
F6A: Clear and readable, with the correct timeline and the correct September loss. It misses the second production run that 22,500 jars sold would require, so its "low point March-May" and "first payment May or June" understate the cash problem.
W6P: Catches the second-run cash need and gives an actionable one-flavor rebuild. It states the farmers-market price as "$6-8" with no basis in the brief. It ranks a stockout at 5% as "rare" even though its own #1 assumes the first run sells out by mid-June. Its breakeven leaves out the free-fill cost. Heavy method scaffolding.
Y6H: Plain and well proportioned. All the core math is right: the timeline, a reorder needed by early May, $90-100k peak need, and roughly break-even to negative by September. It gives a breakeven-velocity sensitivity and ordered counters (start the trial batches now, phase the rollout, cap credits), and it flags the buyer's 6/week as optimistic without inventing data.
G9S: Readable, with good counters, but it never mentions the reorder and so misses the second-run cash crunch. It calls September profitability "plausible on an accrual basis", which its own figures contradict. Its 3/week year-one estimate leaves out the expiry credits it raises later.
S6T: Concise and correct: about $51k of upfront give-ups, roughly 30 weeks to recover them, a reorder needed by early May, a $35-40k gap, and about -$6k by September, with a clean revenue/cost check. Its counters are prioritized, and the "survive half forecast and 90-day pay" rule is a useful test. Its advice on timing the trial batch is a little weaker than Y6H's.
