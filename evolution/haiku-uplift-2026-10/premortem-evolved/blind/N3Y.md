# Saltmarsh second space: how it fails before Christmas

**Horizon:** December 25 · **Success was defined as:** the new space covers its own costs by Christmas without pulling money from the original studio.

**Note on the percentages:** shares of failure, meaning "if this dies, this is what killed it." They are not the chance the plan fails overall. They sum to 100.

**Assumptions I'm working from:**
- Dates are as you wrote them: lease signed June 2, rent-free through August 1, first courses the week of August 4, kiln meter applied for the same day.
- Each course is 8 weeks, and cohorts run back to back with no gap. Four cohorts of 10 is the ceiling, because there are four weekday evening slots.
- The $9,400 a month covers everything you listed. Clay, glaze, kiln shelves, gas hookup, and kiln repairs are not in that list.
- Every piece needs two firings, a bisque and a glaze.
- I could not check any dated source, so no outside statistics appear below.

## The Autopsy

**The strongest case for this plan:** You already have 30 paying members who buy wheel time and firing, so you know people pay you for this. You have 140 names from a free booth, which is evidence of interest even if not of payment. The room is leased with two rent-free months to fit it out. Four cohorts at $340 is $13,600 per course, and a 24 cu ft gas kiln is a standard way to get production firing. A reasonable advocate would say the expansion adds revenue from a known product, using space you can afford to fit out before rent starts.

Six ways this died, ranked by probability.

### 1. The schedule cannot reach breakeven even when every seat is full
**Probability:** ~40% (the cap for a single mode). The plan's own numbers fail its own target, so this is close to arithmetic rather than a risk. Impact is high and the feasibility is the highest in the report.
**Cause of death:** The plan compares a course to a month. $13,600 is one 8-week course. Converted to a month, 40 full seats earn about $7,370 ($13,600 × 52/12 ÷ 8). Against $9,400 a month in costs, the new space is about $2,000 short every month even at full occupancy with no gaps. Breakeven needs about 51 paying students per 8-week course ($9,400 × 8/4.33 ÷ $340), and the schedule holds 40.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (August): Four cohorts start the week of August 4 and fill. Rent of $4,100 has begun, plus the kiln loan and staffing. The first course brings in $13,600, which is less than its share of the costs.
- Month 2 (September): The second course runs at full capacity. The monthly gap of about $2,000 is now visible in the bank balance, but it looks like a timing problem, so no one changes the schedule.
- Month 3 (October): Two more courses have run. The gap is about $6,000 and grows every month. Money has to come from the original studio's account to cover it, which breaks the Christmas definition of success.
- Month 4 (November): The $22,000 cushion is down to roughly $12,000 once the cumulative shortfall and kiln costs are counted, and the owner has begun drawing on the original studio's income.
- Month 5 (December): At Christmas the new space is still uncovered by about $10,000 in cumulative terms, the success test fails, and the owner is making the decision you are making now, under worse conditions.
**The assumption that allowed it:** That a full course is the same thing as a full month of income, so the four-cohort plan looks profitable on its face.
**First warning sign:** The first course's receipts on the week of August 4, compared against that month's $9,400 in costs. The gap is visible before the second course starts.

### 2. The kiln slips past August 4, the members leak, and the gap starts the cash clock
This is the compound failure. Mode 1 is the arithmetic gap, and this mode is the one most likely to make it bite before anyone notices.
**Probability:** ~20% — the gas utility's 10–14 week quote is a lead time that runs past two weeks, and utility approvals tend to run late. Under the 2–3× rule for lead times, the meter could land between about October 20 and March 23, not August 11–September 8.
**Cause of death:** The gas kiln can't fire anything until the meter is live. The earliest it could be live is August 11, a week after the first cohort starts, and the 10–14 weeks are a quote, not a date. Meanwhile the original studio's electric kiln is dying and you have said you won't replace it. Members who pay monthly for firing lose that service. Some leave for a studio that still fires. Your original studio's income drops at the same time the new space has no firing and a full-rent lease.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (June): You sign June 2 and apply for the meter the same day. The electric kiln is still running but getting unreliable.
- Month 2 (July): The rent-free period ends August 1. The meter has no installation date yet. You tell members firing is coming.
- Month 3 (August): The first cohort starts August 4 on the wheel, with no kiln to fire the bisque. Students' first pieces sit on shelves. The electric kiln fails, and members' work is driven across town to a kiln that can't take it yet.
- Month 4 (September): The meter is still not live. Members who can't get work fired start leaving the original studio. Rent of $4,100 is running with no firing revenue.
- Month 5 (October): Monthly income at the original studio has dropped, and the new space's cash shortfall from mode 1 starts to pull on it.
**The assumption that allowed it:** That the utility's 10–14 weeks is a firm schedule, and that firing can wait while the room is already paid for.
**First warning sign:** No written meter date by July 1, and any member asking when their work will be fired. Both show up about five weeks before the first cohort starts.

### 3. The costs that aren't on the list
**Probability:** ~15% — the $9,400 line names rent, the kiln loan, insurance, utilities, and an instructor, but nothing for clay, glaze, kiln shelves and furniture, the gas hookup fee, kiln repair, or the kiln's installation and venting. Each is small, but together they add a recurring amount to a gap already about $2,000 a month.
**Cause of death:** The monthly gap in mode 1 is larger than the number you're working from, because the consumables and installation are missing. Clay and glaze scale with pieces made: 60 members' pieces and up to 120 students' pieces a week. The gas for three firings a week is a variable cost that isn't on the list.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1: The first clay and glaze orders come in above the budget. The owner assumes they'll come out of the utilities line.
- Month 2: Gas bills arrive for the kiln's first firings, and the utility line is already committed to the original studio's invoices.
- Month 3: The installation and venting bills arrive after the kiln has been set in place, and the loan is paid while the kiln is still not firing.
**The assumption that allowed it:** That the $9,400 is complete, because it was written as a list of named lines.
**First warning sign:** The first month's invoice total against the list, roughly in September.

### 4. The waitlist doesn't turn into 40 paid seats for the first course
**Probability:** ~10% — the 140 names are interest from a free booth, not payment. The first course needs 40 paid seats from them, which is about 29% of the list, and the second and third courses need the same again. Lower-priced free sign-ups tend to convert less than a full list suggests, but I can't source that rate here.
**Cause of death:** The waitlist was built at a free fair booth, not from paid deposits. At $340, a name must decide to spend that much on an 8-week beginner class, for four evenings a week, and at a new location. A partial first course cuts revenue directly, and the schedule has no slack to make up for it.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (July): Half the list replies. Many names are for different evenings than the four cohorts you've set.
- Month 2 (August): The first course starts with 25–30 paid students. Two cohorts are short and the instructor is paid anyway.
- Month 3 (September): The second course has to be filled from the same list, and the list is now smaller.
**The assumption that allowed it:** That 140 names on a sign-up sheet means 140 possible students at $340 each.
**First warning sign:** Paid deposits against the list by July 21. Fewer than 15 paid by July 1 means the list is not a demand signal.

### 5. The schedule works, and the kiln can't keep up
**Probability:** ~10% — a success mode, and the one that bites only if mode 4 does not happen first.
**Cause of death:** Three firings a week at about 70 pieces each is roughly 210 piece-firings. Members put through about 60 pieces a week, and 40 students making 2–3 pieces a week adds 80–120 more. That is 140–180 pieces a week, and each needs two firings, so 280–360 piece-firings a week against a capacity of 210. Even if each piece is counted once, the kiln is at 67–86% with no slack for a failed load or a cycle that runs long. A two-day cycle at three loads a week is already close to the limit.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1: The first cohort's pieces start arriving, and the bisque shelves fill in the first week.
- Month 2: Members' work and students' bisque queue behind each other. Two loads are late in a week, and the glaze schedule slips.
- Month 3: Students' pieces come back late or unglazed, and a few students stop coming, so the course's paid seats drop.
**The assumption that allowed it:** That the kiln's throughput is measured by load count, not by piece firings, so 210 looked like room.
**First warning sign:** The first week in which a bisque load is late, which in the plan is the first week of September.

### 6. The lease turns a business miss into personal debt
**Probability:** ~5% — the lease does not cause the business to fail, but it converts any miss into personal exposure. The guarantee is a fixed cost that does not depend on the schedule working.
**Cause of death:** Rent of $4,100 begins around August 2 and runs for 60 months. That is about $246,000 over the term, and you are personally liable for it under the guarantee. The rent starts before the kiln can be live, and it keeps running after the first course, the second, and every course that fails to fill.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (June): The lease is signed with a personal guarantee and no exit clause.
- Month 2 (August): Rent of $4,100 starts while the kiln is not yet firing.
- Months 3–5: The cash shortfall takes the guarantee from a contingency to something you have to pay out of your own savings.
**The assumption that allowed it:** That the lease can be left if the business doesn't work.
**First warning sign:** Cash balance on the first day rent is due, after the first course's receipts.

**Considered and cut:** Meter delay as a standalone mode (folded into #2), member drift at the original studio (folded into #2), instructor no-show (folded into #3, since the instructor is in the cost line).
**Downgraded — guarded for now:**

| Mode | Guard that holds it down | Signal that re-upgrades it |
|---|---|---|
| Instructor not available for four evenings | The instructor is already a line in the $9,400 | Instructor has not confirmed four evening slots in writing by June 15 |

**Most likely:** #1 — highest feasibility among the high scorers, because the failure is already written into your numbers.

**Most dangerous:** #2 — the warning arrives on August 4, after the June 2 signature, which is the point of no return. Nothing about the kiln is visible until the first cohort is already paying rent, so it is silent until it's too late to change the lease. It is the same mechanism as #1, but it makes #1 bite earlier. Because #2 is the one you can least recover from, it is the one that matters most.

**The hidden assumption:** Modes 1, 4, and 5 all depend on the same belief: that four 10-seat cohorts, filled from the waitlist, are the steady-state business. Mode 2 depends on the kiln being ready on schedule, which is a separate belief. The root shared by the most modes is that the 40-seat schedule is the revenue engine. Two modes that both die unless that holds are stronger evidence than either's probability alone.

**Fatal flaw:** Yes. The core comparison is wrong. $13,600 is one course, $9,400 is one month, and at full occupancy the new space earns about $7,370 a month. No fill rate that four 10-seat cohorts can produce covers the costs. The binding constraint is the schedule, not the demand.

## The Rebuild

**The revised plan:** Don't sign June 2 as written. Sign only once the lease has no personal guarantee (or a capped one), has a break clause, and the kiln has a written meter date. Reprice or add capacity so 40 seats cover costs: at $340, you need five cohorts (50 seats) to clear $9,400, or about $440 per seat at four cohorts. Keep the original studio's members firing through a separate arrangement that doesn't depend on the new kiln. Collect paid deposits before the first course starts. Keep the $22,000 cushion for the shortfall you can already see.

**What changed and why:**

| Change | Failure mode it closes | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| Lease: remove the personal guarantee or cap it, add a break clause at 24 months. Decision date: before June 2. | #6, and the exposure in #2 | The landlord may refuse, and you may lose this space. |
| Written meter date from the utility. Decision date: July 1, before the first cohort. | #2 | If there's no date, the start moves, and the rent-free months run out first. |
| Member firing arrangement outside the new kiln. Decision date: July 1. | #2 (members leaving the original studio) | Outside firing costs per load; order of magnitude is small against the monthly subscriptions at risk. |
| Five cohorts or a price of about $440 at four cohorts. Launch-blocking. Decision date: June 1. | #1 | Five cohorts need a fifth evening slot; a higher price may cut demand (#4). |
| Itemized cost list including clay, glaze, shelves, gas, installation, and repairs. Fast-Follow. Decision date: June 1. | #3 | About a day of work. |
| 30 paid deposits before the first course. Launch-blocking. Decision date: July 21. | #4 | The first course may be delayed if deposits are slow. |
| Enrollment capped at 30 until the kiln is proven. Track, with tripwires below. | #5 | Fewer seats in the early courses. |

Most-dangerous mode #2 is labeled **prevent**: the meter date and the member firing arrangement close the entry path. Mode #6 is labeled **limit**: the guarantee is the containment, because the likelihood of a miss can't be moved by the lease alone.

**Pre-launch checklist:**
1. **Meter date.** Check the utility's written confirmation of an installation date. Walk away if there is no date by July 1, or the date is later than September 30. Falsifier: the utility's letter says "applied, typically 10–14 weeks" with no date.
2. **Paid deposits.** Check the sign-up list against paid deposits by July 21. Walk away if fewer than 15 are paid by July 1. Falsifier for mode #4: fewer than 15 paid deposits by July 1.
3. **Full-occupancy math.** Rebuild the monthly costs from the itemized list. Walk away if 40 full seats at the price you choose don't cover the corrected monthly total.
4. **Lease terms.** Check that the guarantee is removed or capped and that a break clause exists. Walk away if the 5-year guarantee stands with no exit.
5. **Member firing.** Confirm in writing the arrangement for members' work during the kiln gap. Walk away if the electric kiln can fail with no firing plan.

**Decision rule:** IF the utility confirms a written meter date no later than August 4, AND the lease has no personal guarantee (or a capped one) with a break clause, AND 30 or more paid deposits are in hand by July 21, THEN sign the lease and start course one. ELSE delay signing and start courses only after the meter date.

**If the decision rule lands on delay, reduce, or don't — the reverse autopsy:** The horizon passed. We didn't sign until the meter was confirmed, and it turned out to be a mistake.
- The landlord leased the space to someone else in July, and the second location never opened.
- The 140 names found another studio's 8-week course and stayed there.
- Members' firing was arranged without the new kiln, so the gap no longer mattered, but the new space's revenue was never built.

**What tips the balance:** A written meter date by August 4, and 30 or more paid deposits by July 21, would reverse the call toward signing with the guarantee removed. Without the lease change, the caution stands.

**Consciously accepted (at most one):** Some pieces wait up to two weeks for firing if the meter lands late but before August 31. The priced cost is a few delayed pieces and some student patience, and it is accepted because the alternative is refusing the course start.

**How the rebuilt plan dies:** The new weakest joint is the price or the fifth cohort. At $440 or with five cohorts, demand has to stretch to a larger list than the one you have. The fix depends on mode #4 being solved first, so it is not strictly dominant.

**Residual risk:** The meter date can still slip after it is confirmed. The waitlist may convert below 30 even with deposits. Both are residual, and both remain tripwires.

## The Adversary

**Playing:** The nearest other studio in Corvel Bay that teaches 8-week beginner courses. They gain most from the waitlist and from your members if firing fails. They can't sustain a low price for long without giving up their own margin, and they can't take your members' firing contracts without firing for them. Their best alternative is to keep their current courses and ignore you, which costs them nothing but the members.

**The kill chain:**
1. **Probe (June 15–July 10):** They learn about the new space from the Harbor Days sign-up sheet or from public permit records, and they count your 140 names.
2. **Position (July 13–31):** They offer the same waitlist a "founding" rate for courses starting before August 4.
3. **Strike (August 4–September 8):** The meter is late. They offer to fire members' work at their kiln for free for six weeks.
4. **Entrench (September–December):** They keep the members who switched and hire the instructor you had lined up.

**Detection opportunities:**
- Move 1: Your waitlist names mention a competing course, or a new studio shows up in the Harbor Days follow-up. Visible by July 10.
- Move 2: Waitlist members reply asking whether the price is "matched." Visible by July 31.
- Move 3: Members ask whether their work can be fired elsewhere. Visible in the first week of August.
- Move 4: Members cancel the monthly subscription at the original studio. Visible in September, in the monthly count.

**The move you'd never see coming:** They hire your part-time instructor for their own evening course, so you have four cohorts and nobody to teach them. The instructor line is already in your costs, which is why nobody checks it.

**What this means you should change:**
- Get a firing arrangement for members that doesn't depend on the new kiln, as in the rebuild.
- Lock the instructor's four evenings in writing before you sign.
- Set your own founding rate and publish it before a competitor does.

**Kill condition:** The meter has no written date by August 4 AND fewer than 30 students have paid for course one. Either alone is a warning. Together they mean stop: don't start course one, and ask the landlord now about the exit terms.

**What this analysis probably missed:** The owner's own time. This frame treats the business as money and firing, so it under-weights the fact that you would run the original studio, four evening cohorts, and a kiln load schedule with the same person. Nothing in the plan prices that, and it is the category most likely to be the real cause of a late and silent failure.
