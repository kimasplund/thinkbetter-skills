**Short answer: not as written.** The product is clearly wanted and the unit margin is real, but the terms plus your timeline and cash would very likely leave you out of money around April or May. They also would not get you to "profitable by September." Fixable, but you'd need to renegotiate before December 15.

Assumptions: Calloway invoices when you ship to their warehouse, the opening fill is free, and the 25% promo is funded by you. Your message doesn't say whether the 25% is off the $8.99 shelf price or the $5.40 wholesale price, so I show both. The numbers are rough.

## The unit economics

- Per jar: $5.40 wholesale minus $3.10 co-pack minus $0.30 freight leaves **$2.00 gross margin** (37%). That's before returns, and before the cost of your own time managing a bigger operation.
- 48 stores x 3 flavors = 144 store-flavors. At the buyer's 6 jars per store-flavor per week that's **864 jars/week, about $4,670/week in sales and $1,730/week in margin, roughly $7,500/month**.

## One-time costs Calloway is asking you to fund

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Slotting (144 x $250) | **$36,000** |
| Free fill (1,728 jars) at your landed cost of $3.40 | ~$5,900 (about $9,300 of foregone wholesale revenue) |
| Launch promo, 8 weeks, ~6,900 jars | **$9,300** if 25% of wholesale, **$15,600** if 25% of shelf price |
| **Total** | **~$51k to $57k** |

At $7,500/month of margin, that's 7 to 8 months of Calloway margin spent before you see a dollar of profit, and that assumes velocity holds. During the promo itself, if the discount is off the shelf price ($2.25/jar), you lose money on every jar sold.

Break-even check: to recover slotting plus promo within 12 months you need about 3.2 jars/store/flavor/week. At 6/week you'd clear it, but "expects about 6" is the buyer's forecast, not a commitment. That's 18 jars per store per week of one specialty condiment, which sounds optimistic to me. At 3/week you take about 14 months to break even, and at 2/week you never do in year one. Ask Calloway for sell-through data on comparable local or specialty products. Also ask whether slotting gets refunded if a flavor is delisted. It shouldn't be a pure sunk cost.

## The timeline doesn't work

- Order the first week of January (~Jan 5).
- Trial batch sign-off adds 2 to 3 weeks, so production is scheduled around Jan 19 to 26.
- Plus the 6-week lead time gives finished goods around **Mar 2 to 9**.
- Freight, warehouse receiving and distribution to stores add more time.

Your "late February" delivery only works if the trial batch is free of delay, and trial batches often need a second iteration. Realistic in-store date: mid to late March, or later. Missing a March 1 contractual date with a new chain is a bad first impression and may carry penalties. Fix: ask Halvard to run the trial batches **now** (October/November), so sign-off is done before you sign with Calloway. Then the lead time is a clean 6 weeks, and you can order in early December or earlier.

## The cash doesn't work

You have $60k of liquidity ($35k savings plus $25k credit line).

- January: 50% deposit on 15,000 jars (5,000 x 3 flavors x $3.10 = $46,500) is **$23,250**.
- Late Feb/March: balance of **$23,250** plus freight **$4,500**.
- That's **~$51,000 out before Calloway pays a cent**, leaving about $9k.
- Calloway collections come late. Net-60 means about 2 months after invoice. Slotting plus promo deductions of roughly $45k to $52k come out of invoices that run about $4,670/week, so the first 10 or so weeks of invoices net to roughly zero. Meaningful cash arrives around **mid-summer at best**.
- Your 15,000 jars last only about 15 weeks (the 1,728 free-fill jars come first, then 864/week). That's roughly mid-June, so you must place order #2 around early May given the 6-week lead time. That's another ~$23k deposit when you have about $9k left, and nothing collected yet.

So by about May you'd have committed ~$74k against $60k, and that's before rent, your own pay, interest on the credit line, and the existing $9k/month business needing working capital for market inventory. Any slip, such as late delivery or slower payment, forces you to miss a payment to Halvard or pull down the credit line entirely.

Add the risks that make this worse:
- **Returns and expiry.** Expired or damaged product is credited back, with no stated cap. With a 6-month shelf life, a slow store can eat a sizeable share. 15,000 jars sitting in a warehouse plus store shelves is a lot of the clock. Ask whether Calloway has a minimum remaining shelf-life rule on receipt (many do, often 60%+), which would shrink your usable window.
- **Minimums.** 5,000 jars per flavor is about 17 weeks of supply per flavor at forecast and far longer if a flavor underperforms. A weak flavor ties up $15k+ of cash.
- **Concentration.** Calloway would be roughly 70% of revenue, so one buyer change or delisting is existential.
- **Capacity and quality.** You're moving production out of your own hands. Taste and consistency from the co-packer are your brand. The trial batch is the only protection you have.

## "Biggest account and profitable by September"

- **Biggest account:** yes. About $20k/month versus $9k/month today.
- **Profitable by September:** only on a monthly run-rate basis, and only if velocity holds at ~6/week. Cumulatively, from March to September (~30 weeks) you'd earn about $52k of margin against $45k to $52k of slotting and promo, so roughly **break-even to slightly negative**, before spoilage credits. If velocity is 4/week you're clearly negative through September.

## What I'd do

1. **Don't sign Dec 15 on these terms.** Counter with these changes, in rough order of importance.
2. **Phase the rollout.** Start with 12 to 16 stores, or all 3 flavors in fewer stores, with expansion triggered by hitting sell-through targets. That cuts slotting and the first order by about two-thirds and gives you real velocity data.
3. **Slotting:** reduce it, or convert it to a refundable/pro-rated credit. Spread it over 12 months of invoices rather than taking it up front, or trade it for a smaller free fill. Get it written down that it's not repeated if you reorder.
4. **Promo:** define it as 25% of wholesale, cap the dollar amount, and tie it to an agreed volume.
5. **Payment:** ask for net-30, or at least don't let deductions arrive before the product has sold.
6. **Returns:** cap expired/damaged credits (for example 2% of sales), and require them to rotate stock.
7. **Start date:** move to April 1 or later, or make it "30 days after our trial batch is approved."
8. **Co-packer:** push Halvard on the 5,000-jar minimum (try 2,500 per flavor, or a 2-flavor launch), and on payment terms (deposit 30% with the balance net-15 or so). Ask whether trial-batch lead time can overlap with your order. Ask what the price breaks look like.
9. **Funding:** if you want the full 48 stores, you need more capital, such as a larger line, purchase-order financing, or a small raise from friends or family. Model roughly $90k to $100k of peak need, not $60k.
10. **Before signing:** build a simple week-by-week cash model through October with scenarios at 6, 4 and 2 jars/week, and walk away if the 2/week case sinks you.

The opportunity is real, and a smaller, staged version of this deal could be very good for you. A 48-store, three-flavor launch on these terms bets your entire cash position on an unproven forecast.
