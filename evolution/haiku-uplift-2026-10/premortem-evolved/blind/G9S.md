Short answer: the demand is real, but this deal as written is not a good one for you. The economics work only if Calloway's velocity holds, and the structure, timeline and cash position leave very little room for anything to go wrong. Here is the breakdown, with my assumptions listed at the end.

**The unit economics**

- Margin per jar: $5.40 wholesale minus $3.10 production minus $0.30 freight = $2.00 (about 37%).
- Placements: 48 stores x 3 flavors = 144 store-flavor placements.
- At the buyer's 6 jars per placement per week, that is 864 jars a week, or about 3,700 a month, worth about $20k a month at wholesale. Your current total is about $9k a month, so Calloway alone would more than double the business.

Year one at 6/week:
- About 44,900 jars sold, gross margin about $90k.
- Less slotting $36,000 (144 x $250).
- Less the free opening cases, roughly $5,900 at landed cost (1,728 jars).
- Less the 25% promo billback for 8 weeks, roughly $9k to $16k depending on whether the 25% is taken off wholesale or shelf price.
- Result: about $32k to $39k before your own overhead.

Year one at 3/week (half the buyer's estimate):
- Roughly break-even, possibly a loss of about $5k.

So the deal is worth doing only if the velocity is close to 6/week.

**The timeline does not support March 1**

- Order in the first week of January, plus the 6-week lead time, puts finished goods around February 16.
- The trial batch adds 2 to 3 weeks, which moves finished goods to roughly March 2 to March 9.
- Freight and store distribution come after that.
- Your plan says delivery to the warehouse in late February, which seems to leave out the trial batch. As written, the March 1 date is already at risk before anything goes wrong.

**The cash position is the real constraint**

- Production for the 5,000-per-flavor minimum is 15,000 jars x $3.10 = $46,500. You pay $23,250 in January and $23,250 on delivery. Freight adds about $4,500. That is about $51,000 out before Calloway pays you anything, and the trial batch cost is not included.
- You have $60k including the line of credit, which leaves roughly $9k for everything else, including your existing operations and interest on the line.
- Calloway pays on Net-60 from invoice. Slotting ($36k) and promo billbacks come out of your invoices first. At roughly $4,700 a week of invoicing, slotting alone absorbs about eight weeks of invoices. Depending on how invoices are cut, your first meaningful cash from Calloway is likely May at the earliest and more plausibly June or July.
- That means you are likely cash-negative through the spring even if the velocity is right. The goal of being profitable by September is plausible on an accrual basis at 6/week, but unlikely on a cash basis before summer.

**Other risks**

- The 6/week figure is the buyer's expectation, not a commitment. Ask whether there is any reorder obligation and what the delisting threshold is.
- Expiry at low velocity: at 3/week, a 5,000-jar batch takes about 35 weeks to sell through, longer than the 6-month shelf life. You could owe credits on roughly 1,250 jars per flavor, around $20k at wholesale.
- Retailer onboarding costs and requirements (UPC/GS1 codes, EDI or invoicing setup, certificate of liability insurance, possibly a food-safety audit) cost money and time that are not in your plan.
- Moving all production to Halvard means your existing channels (farmers markets, online) also need to work at their cost structure. Check that.
- Halvard's 50% deposit is at risk if they miss the date or the trial batch fails. Confirm their capacity, insurance, recall responsibility and that the 6-month shelf life is validated.

**What I would do before December 15**

1. Ask Halvard for a trial batch now, in November or early December, rather than after the order. Get a written date and cost. This is the single biggest schedule fix.
2. Ask Calloway for a launch window that depends on delivery, for example April 1 or a staggered start, rather than a fixed March 1.
3. Propose a staggered launch: one flavor first, or a pilot in 12 to 24 stores. One flavor at 5,000 jars is about $17k all-in (production plus freight), instead of about $51k for all three. Negotiate a lower first-run minimum if Halvard allows it.
4. Negotiate slotting: spread it over 3 to 6 months, pay it per flavor as each one launches, or reduce it.
5. Cap the promo billback in dollars, and state whether it is calculated on wholesale or shelf price.
6. Require a minimum remaining shelf life at delivery (for example, three months), and cap the expiry and damage credits.
7. Agree a 8 to 12 week velocity review with clear delisting terms.
8. Ask for Net-30, or an early-payment option.
9. Check whether your line of credit can be drawn for this purpose. Consider invoice factoring or purchase-order financing for the Calloway receivables. Factoring typically advances 80 to 90% of an invoice for a fee.
10. Build a month-by-month cash-flow model from December through September with three velocity cases (3, 4.5 and 6 per week) and both promo bases. Keep a cash reserve of at least $15k beyond the production outlay.

**Bottom line**

The opportunity is strong if Calloway's velocity holds, and it could roughly double the business. As proposed, though, it asks you to put almost all your liquidity into a 3-flavor, 48-store launch on a date your own trial-batch timing rules out, with no cash coming in until mid-year. Sign only after a staggered start, a realistic launch date and tighter slotting, promo and return terms are agreed in writing.

- Slotting is a one-time fee per store per flavor.
- The 25% promo is billed back on either wholesale or shelf price (I gave both).
- The $3.10 Halvard price is all-in for the jar.
- Freight is one delivery of 15,000 jars.
- The trial batch cost is not included, because you did not give it.
- Invoices are issued on shipment to the warehouse.
- Your current business's costs are not included in the cash model.
