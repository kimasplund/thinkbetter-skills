Short answer: the Calloway account is real money, but the deal as written is not safe to sign on December 15. The main problems are the March 1 date, which the production timeline can't meet, and the cash: you front roughly $51k before the first Calloway payment arrives, and your margin per jar is thin.

## The numbers (my assumptions in brackets)

Volume: 48 stores x 3 flavors = 144 store-flavor pairs. At the buyer's 6 jars per week, that is 864 jars/week, or about 44,900 jars a year. At $5.40 wholesale, that is about $243k a year gross, more than double your current ~$9k/month. [Assumes the buyer's 6/week holds.]

Unit economics per jar:
- Revenue $5.40
- Landed cost $3.10 production + $0.30 freight = $3.40
- Gross margin about $2.00 (37%), before your own overhead, labels, insurance, and so on. [Assumes $3.10 includes packaging. Confirm this.]

One-time costs in the first year:
- Slotting: $250 x 144 = $36,000, deducted from invoices.
- Free opening cases: 1,728 jars x $3.40 = about $5,900 of product given away.
- 25% launch promo for 8 weeks: 6,912 jars x $1.35 (25% of wholesale) = about $9,300. If the 25% is taken on the $8.99 shelf price instead ($2.25/jar), it is about $15,600.

Position at September (about 26 weeks after launch), if sell-through is 6/week:
- Gross margin on about 22,500 jars sold: about $44,900
- Less slotting, free cases, and promo: about $51,200
- Net: roughly -$6,000 before any overhead. On the shelf-price promo basis, it is closer to -$12,000.

So on these assumptions, the account is not profitable by September. It becomes profitable from the second half of the year onward, if the velocity holds and slotting does not recur as a charge. Ask whether slotting is one-time or annual.

## Timing: March 1 is not achievable

- Trial batch: 2-3 weeks, done before scheduling.
- Production: 6 weeks from order to finished goods.
- Total: 8-9 weeks from order to finished goods.

Ordering in the first week of January gives finished goods around March 2-9, not late February. To have product at the warehouse by about February 20, you would need to order around December 19-26, which is right after your December 15 signing, over the holidays, with no slack. The safer path is to negotiate a later launch (April is realistic) or a staggered store rollout.

## Cash: the real constraint

Your $60k ($35k savings + $25k line) against the first order:
- 50% deposit in January: $23,250
- Balance on delivery in late February: $23,250
- Freight, roughly $4,500 for 15,000 jars at $0.30
- Total before the first sale: about $51,000

That leaves about $9k for the trial batch, labels, packaging, insurance, the Calloway vendor onboarding costs, and your existing farmers market and online business.

Cash comes back late. Net-60 means the first meaningful payment is around May or June. Slotting and promo billbacks are netted out of those invoices, so the first check may be much smaller than the invoice. Your low point is likely March-May, and you could be drawing the line of credit while still short. Any shortfall (a late delivery, a short shipment, a delayed receipt) could breach the line.

Also, the 5,000-jar minimum is per flavor, so you are committing about 15,000 jars. At 3 jars/week instead of 6, the stock lasts about 7 months, which is longer than the 6-month shelf life. Expired product is credited back to Calloway, so you would carry that loss.

## What I would negotiate before signing

1. Launch date: move it to April or later, or stagger the 48 stores over several weeks.
2. Pilot: start with about 10-12 stores and one flavor for 8-12 weeks to prove sell-through before committing to all 144 pairs.
3. Slotting: reduce it, make it one-time, or split it into installments. Calloway should share the risk.
4. Promo: have Calloway fund part of the 25%, or cap the billback. Confirm the basis (wholesale or shelf price).
5. Expiry: limit the credit to product that expires in-store, or require a sell-through floor, and cap it.
6. Payment: net-45 or net-30 with an early-pay discount, or a deposit from them on the first order.
7. Free cases: confirm they count toward the 5,000-jar minimum and are not extra.
8. Chargebacks: ask for their late and short-shipment fines and OTIF penalties in writing.

## Things to confirm with Halvard before you order

- Whether the $3.10 includes packaging, labels, and a food-safety plan.
- Whether the deposit is refundable if the trial fails or Calloway cancels.
- The cost of the trial batch and whether it counts toward the minimum.
- Freight terms: whether the $0.30 is included or quoted separately.
- Their FDA facility registration and recall procedures. Calloway will require this, and you will likely need product liability insurance with Calloway named as an additional insured.

## Bottom line

This could be a good account for your business, but the terms front-load cost and cash ahead of your revenue, and the date doesn't work with Halvard's lead time. I would not sign on December 15 as written. Ask for a later start or a pilot, and get Halvard's trial and production timeline in writing before you commit.
