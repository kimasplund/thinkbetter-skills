Here are the holes I see, roughly in order of how likely they are to break the January 12 start. I've assumed the 70 current tutors stay available, the $50k/month is all-in incremental cost, and the grant pays only on the quarterly schedule you described. Please check those.

**1. The MOU will not be signed by January 12.**
Your current MOU took about five months. You have about three months from today (October 13) to January 12, and the district office has not started on this one. Knowing the people now helps, but it does not change how long their review takes. Tutoring at the 10 new schools needs school space and student data access, so no MOU means no tutoring there. Also ask whether it will start processing before the board vote, which should already have happened on October 8. If the MOU is not signed, the January 12 date is not realistic.

**2. The principals' enthusiasm is not the approval you need.**
Ten principals saying they are excited is good, but the approval that matters is the district office's. Principals can also be replaced or may not have the authority to approve outside programs. Get written sign-off from the district office, not from the principals.

**3. The tutor pipeline does not line up with the start date.**
- Harmon's spring semester starts January 20, eight days after your January 12 launch. Your largest tutor source is not available until after the start date.
- You need about 175 new tutors, plus background checks and training, between now and January 12. The recruiting and training window is about eight weeks.
- Your existing 70 tutors run about 2.3 kids each. The 400 new kids need about 175 tutors at that ratio. That number is consistent, but it is the hardest part of the plan.
- Winter break and the gap between Harmon's fall and spring terms may also thin out your current tutors. Check this.

**4. The cash runs out before the reimbursement arrives.**
- Coordinators start November 1, so costs begin at $50k/month. November through January is $150k, which equals all of your unrestricted reserves.
- Arbelle pays quarterly in arrears. The November–January quarter closes January 31. The expense report is usually paid about 45 days after close, so around March 17.
- By mid-March you will have spent November, December, January and February, which is about $200k. That leaves a gap of roughly $50k before the first payment arrives, and you have no reserves left to cover it. Your existing four-school operations, training costs and background checks also draw on the same cash.
- Reserves at zero is a serious risk for a nonprofit. A late expense report, a rejected line item, or a slow approval would leave you unable to pay coordinators or tutors.

**5. The year-2 bar is harder than it looks.**
- Your current 12-point result comes from a full October–April year.
- The new schools would get roughly January 12 to mid-April, about 13 weeks, with new coordinators, new tutors and new site processes.

- Also check the timing. The benchmark is mid-April, but the money is released "next summer," so year 2 spending may start before the money arrives. Confirm this against the grant terms.

**6. The success definition is too narrow.**
"All 14 schools running in January" measures launch, not quality. It says nothing about tutor safety, attendance, or whether kids are actually getting twice-weekly sessions. It also does not define what happens if the year-2 gate is missed.
