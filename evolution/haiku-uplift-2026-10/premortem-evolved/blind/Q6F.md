# Second space across town: failure analysis before the June 2 signing

**Horizon:** December 25, 2027 · **Success was defined as:** the new space covers its own costs by Christmas without drawing money from the original studio.

**Note on the percentages:** shares of failure, meaning "if this dies, this is what killed it." They sum to 100. They are not the odds the plan misses overall.

**Assumptions I'm working from:**
- Your dates are the next occurrence on the calendar: lease June 2, 2027; first courses the week of August 4, 2027; target Christmas 2027. Today is October 2026, so the lease is still ahead of you.
- The $9,400 a month includes rent, the kiln loan payment, insurance, utilities and the part-time instructor.
- Course fees are paid at enrollment. The 140 waitlist names are sign-ups from a free booth, not paid seats.
- Every piece is fired twice, once for bisque and once for glaze.

Real capital and a five-year personal guarantee are at stake, so this is a full review.

## The Autopsy

**The strongest case for this plan:** Thirty members already pay monthly for wheel time and firing, so you have a paying base that already wants this. A booth at a public fair produced 140 sign-ups in one afternoon, which is a live demand signal. The two rent-free months give real room for fit-out. Eight-week beginner courses are a clear, sellable product that feeds members, and a second room adds capacity without touching the original studio's floor. An informed advocate would say the risk is execution, not demand. Every mode below has to beat that case.

**The plan's own arithmetic misses the target.** Full-room course revenue is $13,600 per 8-week course, about 1.85 months. That is roughly $7,400 a month at 100% fill with no gap between courses, against $9,400 of fixed costs. Even the best case runs about $2,000 a month short. At half fill, course income falls to about $3,700 a month, and the $22,000 cushion is gone by late November. This is mode 1, and it is a top-two failure by arithmetic, not by risk.

Six ways this died, ranked by probability.

### 1. The courses cannot cover $9,400 a month, even when full
**Probability:** ~40%. The plan's own numbers miss its target at every fill rate short of a gapless, full calendar, so the arithmetic itself is the cause of death.
**Cause of death:** $340 a seat on 40 seats yields about $7,400 a month of course income against about $9,400 of fixed costs. Covering one 8-week course's share of fixed costs takes roughly $17,300, which is about $430 a student for a full room.
**How it unfolded:**
- August 2027: four cohorts start August 4 and fill. $13,600 in fees looks like the business working.
- September 2027: the first course ends around September 29 with no revenue planned for the gap. Rent, loan and instructor keep running through it.
- October 2027: the second course fills at about half, roughly $6,800 of income. The forecast was built on the first course, so the gap is not visible yet.
- Late October 2027: the cash balance drops under $12,000 while the next course is still forecast as full.
- Late November 2027: the cushion is gone. Christmas is reached only with money from the original studio, which is the outcome you ruled out.

**The assumption that allowed it:** that one full course's fees are steady monthly income. They are one course's worth, paid in one block.
**First warning sign:** cash under $12,000 by September 30, 2027, with the second course still forecast full.
**Reference class:** second locations of single-owner service businesses. Judgment call, no sourced base rate. These usually fail on cash timing and pricing per seat, not on demand. **Disanalogy:** you already have a paying member base and a waitlist, which pushes the demand side up. Nothing in the plan moves the per-seat price, which pushes this number up, not down.

### 2. The kiln is not live when the courses start, and the first students' work stalls
**Probability:** ~20%. The utility's 10–14 weeks is an estimate, not a contract. Lead times over two weeks should be treated as 2–3 times the stated timeline until evidence says otherwise, and at 2x the meter lands around October.
**Cause of death:** no firing for the first weeks of the courses, while the original electric kiln is dying and has no replacement.
**How it unfolded:**
- June 2, 2027: lease signed, meter application filed the same day.
- August 4: courses start. Beginners throw pieces they cannot fire. Member work goes to the dying electric kiln, which fails in August.
- August–September: members' firing stops. Beginners' bisque piles up unfinished, and first-course students ask for refunds around week four.
- October 2027: the meter is upgraded and firing resumes, but there is a backlog and two cohorts' worth of unhappy customers.
- December 2027: second-course enrollment is down because word has spread. This interacts with mode 3 (see the compound scenario below).

**The assumption that allowed it:** that the utility's stated date is the real date, and that the old kiln survives until the new one is live.
**First warning sign:** no written meter date from the utility by July 15, 2027.
**Reference class:** equipment that depends on a utility connection. Public lead-time estimates for utility work commonly slip (weakly sourced; no dated source checked). **Disanalogy:** you applied on the day of signing, which is the earliest possible date, so you are already at the optimistic end.

**Compound scenario (modes 2 and 3):** the kiln stalls the first cohort for six to eight weeks. Refund requests and word-of-mouth cut the conversion of the 140 names into second-course seats. A thinner second course widens the gap in mode 1 until the cushion is gone by November, not December.

### 3. The 140 names are not 40 paid seats
**Probability:** ~15%. A free sign-up at a booth is a weak signal. Filling the first run needs 29% of the names to pay.
**Cause of death:** conversion lands nearer 15–20 paid seats. Cohorts run at half, and mode 1 gets worse.
**How it unfolded:** the first cohorts open at a partial room, the owner keeps all four cohorts on the calendar, and the fixed costs stay the same.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that a name on a sign-up sheet is a buyer.
**First warning sign:** fewer than 20 paid deposits by May 14, 2027.

### 4. Firing capacity: a full calendar outruns one kiln
**Probability:** ~10%. The arithmetic is certain once the room is full and the kiln is live, but it only bites after modes 1–3 hold, so it is second-order.
**Cause of death:** three loads a week at about 70 pieces gives about 210 firings. Demand is 60 member pieces plus 40 students at 2–3 pieces each, so 140–180 pieces a week, each fired twice, which is 280–360 firings. That is 25–40% short every week, and the backlog grows by roughly 70–150 pieces a week. You also drive the member work across town three times a week, and you are the only person doing it. A full course is the thing that breaks the system.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that three firings a week is enough. It is not once bisque and glaze are both counted.
**First warning sign:** unfired backlog above 140 pieces on any Friday after the kiln goes live.

### 5. The lease outlives the business, and the guarantee makes the loss personal
**Probability:** ~10%. The space has to close first, which needs one of the modes above. When it does fire, it is the largest and least reversible loss.
**Cause of death:** a closure around month four or five leaves roughly $230,000 of remaining rent under your personal guarantee (60 months at $4,100 is $246,000 in total). Your $22,000 cushion covers about 2.3 months of full fixed costs with zero income.
**How it unfolded:** the lease is live from June 2. By November, closure is the only exit. The landlord holds the guarantee, the savings are gone, and the original studio's cash is drawn in to cover the gap.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that the space can be closed without the guarantee coming due.
**First warning sign:** the guarantee is live on signing. The first in-flight sign is October 2027 income under $5,000.

### 6. Unpriced costs (the omission)
**Probability:** ~5%. Each item is small. Together they erode the margin the plan is already short of.
**Cause of death:** the plan prices none of the following. Clay and glaze for 40+ beginners each week. Kiln shelves and kiln wash. Gas and venting, plus whether the new room has a gas line and a vent at all. Fuel and time for three member-work runs across town each week. Breakage and replacement for members' pieces. Instructor pay for cohorts that don't run.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that $9,400 covers everything, when it covers only the lines listed.
**First warning sign:** unbudgeted monthly costs above $800 by October 31, 2027.

**Considered and cut:** owner bandwidth across two sites (folded into #4).

**Tie-break for #4 and #5 (both ~10%):** #4 ranks above #5 because its trigger, a full calendar on a live kiln, is on the schedule. #5 needs the business to fail first, and it is ranked by impact in the verdict instead.

**Downgraded — guarded for now:**

| Mode | Guard that holds it down | Signal that re-upgrades it |
|---|---|---|
| Rent drain before first revenue | Two rent-free months (June–July) | Fit-out not finished by July 31, 2027 |
| Instructor drops out | Part-time instructor already in the fixed costs | No signed instructor contract by June 30, 2027 |

**Most dangerous:** #5. It is signed on June 2, before any trading evidence exists. Its first warning sign arrives at the same moment as the point of no return, and what it costs is a personal debt that outlasts the business. **The hidden assumption:** that the 40 seats fill on schedule and stay full. Modes 1, 3, 4 and 5 all die unless that holds, and mode 2 depends on paying students getting their work fired.

**Fatal flaw:** the plan's own arithmetic does not reach the success definition, even at full fill. The binding constraint is the $22,000 cushion set against the personal guarantee.

**The most-certain assumption, attacked first:** "comfortably in the black." On your numbers it is not. That claim is mode 1, and it survives only if the price per seat changes.

## The Rebuild

**The revised plan:** do not sign on June 2 as written. Hold the lease until a written meter date exists, at least 20 seats are paid, and the monthly P&L closes at the price you actually charge. Then sign with a guarantee cap or a break clause. Start with two cohorts (20 seats) until the kiln is live and the backlog clears. Price the course to break-even, about $430 a student for a full room, or cut the fixed costs by about $2,000 a month, for example by paying the instructor per cohort. Budget consumables and transport before signing.

**What changed and why:**

| Change | Failure mode it closes | What it costs | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hold lease signing to the decision rule below | 1, 5 | Up to eight weeks of the rent-free clock and some of the summer window | Launch-Blocking, decide by May 14, 2027 |
| Written meter date, or contracted firing slots, before the first course | 2, 4 | The first course may move to October | Launch-Blocking, by July 15, 2027 |
| Guarantee cap or break clause at month 12 | 5 | The landlord may refuse, in which case you don't sign | Launch-Blocking, in writing before June 2, 2027 |
| Price to break-even or cut fixed costs by ~$2,000 a month | 1 | A higher price may cut enrollment; a per-cohort instructor may not accept | Launch-Blocking, by May 14, 2027 |
| Start with two cohorts until the backlog clears | 2, 3, 4 | Lower early income, about $6,800 per course | Fast-follow |
| Budget consumables, transport and venting | 6 | About $800 a month | Fast-follow |
| A second firing source (commercial firing service) once the room is full | 4 | Per-piece fees | Track |

The two-cohort start costs less than the mode it closes: about $2,000 a month of shortfall, against the ~$6,800 course income you give up by running half the room in the first month. Acceptable in this case.

**Labels for the most dangerous mode:** #5 is **limit**. The likelihood cannot be moved by you, so the response is the guarantee cap or break clause, plus the decision rule that keeps you from signing until one exists. #1 is **prevent**, by not signing until break-even closes.

**Pre-launch checklist:**
1. **Written meter date.** Ask the utility for a date in writing after your application. Walk away if there is no written date, or if it is after August 4, 2027, with no firing alternative. **Falsifier:** a date after August 4 proves mode 2's timing is real.
2. **Paid seats.** Count payments, not names, in the booking system on May 14. Walk away with fewer than 20 paid. **Falsifier:** under 20 paid falsifies the demand assumption behind modes 1 and 3.
3. **Break-even on paper.** Write a one-page monthly P&L with 20 seats and 40 seats, checking whether the $9,400 includes the loan payment and the instructor. Walk away if break-even at $340 needs more than 40 students. **Falsifier:** this shows mode 1 is real rather than a guess.
4. **Firing source for the gap.** Get a quote from a commercial firing service for about 70 piece-loads a week for August through December. Walk away if there is no quote for that gap.

**Decision rule:** IF by May 14, 2027 there are at least 20 paid seats, AND the utility's written meter date is on or before August 4 (or firing slots are contracted), AND the monthly P&L closes at the price you charge, THEN sign June 2 for two cohorts with a guarantee cap or break clause. ELSE delay the lease eight weeks and re-run the numbers, or don't sign.

**If the decision rule lands on delay, the reverse autopsy:** The horizon passed and we didn't sign, and it was a mistake.
- The booth names converted once they were asked to pay, and the summer window closed on a cohort we could have filled.
- The delay used up the rent-free months that were meant to cover fit-out, so the rent started on an empty room.
- The kiln's written date came in October anyway, so the wait bought nothing that the decision rule could not have bought in May.

**What tips the balance:** a written meter date by July 15 plus 20 paid seats by May 14 reverses the delay and lets you sign. Without both, the caution stands.

**Consciously accepted:** none.

**How the rebuilt plan dies:** the new weakest joint is the price. If $430 a student halves enrollment, the two-cohort start fails at the same place, with less income. The contracted firing source may also cost more than the margin it protects. The fix is not strictly dominant.

**Residual risk:** the kiln slips past its written date, the landlord refuses the guarantee cap, and you are the only person carrying two sites' worth of work.

## The Adversary

**Playing:** a rival studio, or the community college's continuing-education program, offering beginner wheel courses in Corvel Bay. They gain most from your members and your waitlist. They can undercut you without risk and can match a price quickly. They cannot match your member community. Their best alternative is to wait for you to run out of cash.

**The kill chain:**
- **July 2027 (probe):** they post a "founding student" beginner offer for August, under $300 a seat.
- **August 4, 2027 (position):** they email your fair sign-up list with the same offer.
- **September 2027 (strike):** they offer your part-time instructor a higher hourly rate for their own cohorts.
- **October–December 2027 (entrench):** they launch a monthly pass that undercuts your per-course price.

**Detection opportunities:**
- July: the rival's listing or ad. Check their site and channels by July 15.
- August: your waitlist emails get a second offer, or paid seats are below 30 on August 4.
- September: the instructor's availability changes. Check by September 1.
- October: second-course enrollment below half the room.

**The move you'd never see coming:** the rival offers your 30 members free firing at their kiln. Your members' work is what the success definition depends on, and that is the one thing this plan has no answer for.

**What this means you should change:** hold your price rather than discounting. Get the instructor on a 12-month commitment before August. Keep member firing reliable before you open the second room.
