Here are the holes I see, roughly in order of how likely they are to sink the plan. I've assumed the October 8 vote is still ahead of you and that the Arbelle grant terms are as you described them. If either is off, tell me and I'll adjust.

**1. The district MOU is the critical path, and your timeline doesn't allow for it.**
Your last MOU took about five months. From an October 8 yes, five months lands in mid-March, after your January 12 start. Knowing the district people now helps, but it doesn't change the fact that no school space or student data can be used until the MOU is signed, and it covers ten new schools, not four. The principals' enthusiasm doesn't count here, because the district office has to approve. Before the board votes, find out what the district needs to see, whether they'll give a written timeline, and whether they will sign off on the MOU for all ten schools at once or only in phases. Don't hire coordinators until that answer is in hand.

**2. The tutor pipeline doesn't match the start date.**
You need about 175 new tutors, and nearly all of your current pool is Harmon students earning practicum credit. Harmon's spring semester starts January 20, which is eight days after your launch. Students can't be recruited, cleared, and trained for a January 12 start unless they're already enrolled and available, and most of the new pool won't be enrolled until the 20th. Also, your current ~70 tutors are mostly fall-semester students. Some will drop out at finals and not return in spring, so your actual headcount in January may be well under 70. Plan for attrition, and find out how many tutors you can really count on in each month.

**3. The cash math leaves no margin.**
Coordinators start November 1 at about $50k a month, so you spend about $225k by mid-March. Arbelle's Nov–Jan expense report closes January 31, and payment is 45 days after that, around March 17. That leaves you about $75k short at the moment of the first payment, against $150k in reserves. The gap doesn't close after that: you're always about six weeks of spending behind the reimbursements. This assumes that every dollar is reimbursable, that the four current schools are funded from somewhere else, and that there are no training, recruiting, or tutor-stipend costs before November. Each of those makes the gap worse. You'll want a line of credit or a bridge loan of at least $100k to $150k, a request to Arbelle for quarterly advances or monthly billing, or a slower ramp-up.

**4. The outcome target is harder than your current results, and it's measured on less exposure.**
Your current schools get a full October–April year and beat comparison students by about 12 points. The new schools get roughly 13 weeks, from January 12 to mid-April, with new coordinators and untrained tutors. Asking them for 15 points in that window means beating your proven result with a shorter dose and a weaker setup. You also need a comparison group at the new schools, which likely requires the same data-sharing approval from the MOU. Ask Arbelle whether the benchmark could be measured at the end of a full year or compared to a baseline, or whether a 12-point result would still release the money.

**5. The second year is all-or-nothing, and the plan has no fallback.**
If the target is missed, $600k of year-two funding disappears after you've already hired, trained, and placed people at ten new schools. Write down what you would do in that case: how coordinator and tutor commitments end, what the district agreement says about exiting, and who pays for wind-down. Also note that the April results and the summer release leave a gap during which costs keep running without a confirmed funder.

**6. The success definition measures the wrong things.**
"All 14 schools running in January" can be met by starting badly. It says nothing about tutor coverage, attendance, or whether kids are reading better. And the second-year release is tied to a single benchmark in one month. Define interim milestones: MOU signed by a set date, a minimum tutor count by launch, weekly attendance, and a mid-year benchmark check. Those would let you see trouble before the money decision.

**What I'd suggest taking to the board:**
- Accept the grant, but make the January 12 date for all ten schools conditional on a signed MOU. Otherwise, phase the rollout, with four to six schools starting in February if the MOU and tutors are ready, and the rest in the fall.
- Hire coordinators only after the MOU is signed, and tie their start dates to that.
- Secure bridge financing before the first expense is incurred, and ask Arbelle for an advance or monthly billing.
- Reopen the outcome terms, or at minimum set up a documented plan for the case where the target is missed.

The most urgent of these is the district MOU, because every other date depends on it.
