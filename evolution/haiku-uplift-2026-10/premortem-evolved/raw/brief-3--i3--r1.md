# Calloway deal: where it breaks

**Short answer:** Calloway is a real opportunity, but the deal as written can't be funded until Calloway starts paying you, and the March 1 date isn't reachable on your own timeline. Don't sign on Dec 15 as written. Counter with one flavor first.

**Assumptions I'm working from**
- The 25% promo is billed as 25% of your wholesale price ($1.35 a jar). If it's 25% off the $8.99 shelf price, it's $2.25 a jar, and the cash gap grows by about $5k.
- Freight ($0.30 a jar) is paid at delivery.
- The 6-week lead time starts at order, and the trial batch adds 2–3 weeks on top.
- Every Halvard run is the 5,000-jar minimum per flavor, so 15,000 jars.
- Calloway sells at 6 jars per store per flavor per week from the first week of sales, across all 48 stores.
- You have $60k available (savings plus the full credit line), no personal guarantee on the line, and your current markets keep running.

The strongest case for the deal: one listing roughly doubles your revenue. At 6 jars per store per flavor per week, that's about $20k a month wholesale against your $9k total today. After production and freight, each jar earns $2.00 (37%). A buyer putting all three flavors into 48 stores at once is rare for a brand your size, and the kitchen and recipes already work.

Five ways this dies, ranked by probability.

## The Autopsy

### 1. You run out of cash before Calloway's money arrives
**Probability:** ~40%. Your own numbers put $51k out before the first Calloway dollar, against $60k available.
**Cause of death:** Production is $23,250 deposit in early January, then $23,250 balance plus $4,500 freight at delivery. That leaves about $9k of headroom. Calloway then nets its deductions against your first invoices: $36,000 in slotting plus roughly $7,000 in promo chargebacks. Net cash from Calloway doesn't arrive until about late July. The delivery slips into March, so sales start later while the promo still runs and bills. The first run also sells out in mid-June, so a reorder has to be placed around early May and needs another $51k before any Calloway cash shows up.
**How it unfolded:**
- Jan 5: $23k deposit leaves about $37k in the bank. Halvard's trial-batch sign-off is still pending.
- Feb: $27k balance and freight due at delivery. Delivery lands in mid-March, not late February. Cash on hand is about $9k.
- Mar–Apr: sales start mid-March at 864 jars a week. Invoices net about $3,500 a week after promo chargebacks, and all of it goes to the $36k slotting deduction.
- May: the reorder needs a $23k deposit. The only money left is the credit line, which is now carrying the first run.
- June: stock runs out around mid-June. The reorder is still in production.
- July: Calloway's first net payment arrives, after the reorder is already committed on the line.

**The assumption that allowed it:** that Calloway's money arrives like a normal net-60 invoice, not after $43k of deductions come off the top.
**First warning sign:** Feb 15. Cash on hand is about $9k against $27k due, and the delivery date has moved.

**Downgraded and considered:** the Halvard trial slip folds into #1.

### 2. Sales run below 6 a week, and the 15,000-jar run sits and expires
**Probability:** ~30%. Six jars per store per flavor per week is the buyer's expectation, not a volume commitment, and the terms have no volume clause.
**Cause of death:** At 3 jars per store per flavor per week, the run sells about 9,500 jars by August and leaves roughly 3,800 jars to expire. Calloway credits expired product back at $5.40 a jar, which is about $20k in credits on cash you've already spent. A buyer who sees slow sell-through can also cut the SKU at its first review, and the leftover stock is then yours.
**Reference class:** new products in regional grocery chains. I can't source a base rate for this, so treat the share as a judgment call. Disanalogy: your market sales show that people buy the product, but at $6–8 in a farmers market, not at $8.99 on a chain shelf next to brands with marketing. That pushes the number up.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that 6 a week holds from the first week and stays steady for six months.
**First warning sign:** April 12. Store-level sell-through in weeks 4–6 is under 4 jars per store per flavor per week.

**Considered and cut:** Calloway delisting after its first review (folded into #2).

### 3. Breakeven lands on your September goal with no margin
**Probability:** ~15%. Paid-out fees don't come back until late summer even if everything goes to plan.
**Cause of death:** The $2.00 margin has to clear $43k of launch fees (slotting plus promo). That's about 21,500 paid jars, or roughly 25 weeks at 864 a week, plus the two free-fill weeks. Breakeven lands around late August to early September. At 80% of target velocity it moves to mid-October. Your September goal depends on full velocity and an on-time start, and this number leaves out your time, the credit line interest, and the kitchen.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that $2.00 a jar is the real margin, with no labor, interest, or chargeback disputes.
**First warning sign:** July 31. Cumulative contribution after fees is below $36k.

### 4. The operating load on two people is unpriced
**Probability:** ~10%. A mundane base-rate killer that nobody budgets.
**Cause of death:** 48 stores, three flavors, weekly restocking, reconciling chargebacks against invoices, handling expiry claims, managing the Halvard reorder, and still running the farmers markets and online orders. None of it is priced or staffed. At this scale it's plausibly 10 or more hours a week, and it's split between you and Ines.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that the Calloway account is a line item on the existing schedule.
**First warning sign:** March 31. Reorder timing is slipping, or chargebacks aren't reconciled by the first invoice run.

### 5. Success outruns the reorder and the shelf goes empty in June
**Probability:** ~5%. This needs the velocity from #2 to hold, so it's rare on these terms, but it's damaging.
**Cause of death:** If sales hit 864 a week, the 15,000-jar run empties in mid-June. A reorder on the normal schedule arrives in mid-July, leaving about four weeks of empty shelves inside the buyer's review window.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that the reorder can be placed and paid for in the time the first run takes to sell.
**First warning sign:** May 15. Less than three weeks of cover on hand.

**Considered and cut:** a Halvard quality failure is handled as a downgraded mode below. The promo base error folds into #1 as a sensitivity.

**Downgraded — guarded for now:**

| Halvard quality or shelf-life failure | Halvard's stated 6-month shelf life and your sign-off on the trial batch | Trial batch fails sign-off, or a finished run is off-spec |

**The stated timeline is out of reach on its own numbers.** Order Jan 5, plus 6 weeks, puts finished goods around Feb 16. Add the 2–3 week trial batch and you get Mar 2–9, before freight. A late-February delivery and a March 1 in-store date don't fit.

**Most likely:** #1. It has the highest feasibility among the high scorers, because the arithmetic is fixed by the terms you were offered.

**Most dangerous:** #1 as well. The $23k deposit is paid on Jan 5, the first warning sign shows up on Feb 15, and by then the balance and freight are due. Recovery means a credit line you've already drawn.

**The hidden assumption:** that Calloway's money arrives on net-60 at full velocity from March. #1, #2, and #3 all die unless the cash timing and the 6-a-week rate hold together.

**Fatal flaw:** As written, the deal can't be funded to the point where Calloway pays you. The binding constraint is the $60k, not the margin. The 15,000-jar minimum makes that worse, and the timeline pushes the first delivery into March.

## The Rebuild
**The revised plan:** Launch one flavor, your strongest seller, to all 48 stores. Order a 5,000-jar Halvard run ($17,000 all-in at $3.40 a jar). Slotting for one flavor is $12,000 (48 stores × $250). Move the in-store date to mid-April, or to March 15 if Halvard gives you a written trial-batch date by Dec 31. Cap the promo at 4 weeks. Limit expiry credits to damaged or recalled product, and get Calloway's 12-week velocity review in writing, as a review and not a delist. Add flavors two and three after sell-through proves the rate. The one-flavor version needs about $17k in production cash, which leaves about $43k of headroom before Calloway's first net payment.

**What changed and why:**

The largest cost is the deferred slotting. It's less than the $36k exposure you'd take on with all three flavors. #1 is labeled **prevent** (one flavor closes the entry path). #2 is labeled **limit** (the likelihood can't move, so the reorder rule caps the damage).

**Pre-launch checklist:**
1. **Halvard written quote.** Check the quote for $3.10 a jar, the 5,000-jar one-flavor minimum, the 50/50 terms, and a dated trial batch. Walk away if no trial date is committed before the Jan 5 order. Falsifier: no trial date by Dec 31 means the March 1 date is already dead.
2. **Calloway written terms.** Check net-30, the promo base, expiry limits, and the 12-week review. Walk away if expiry credits are uncapped and there's no review clause. Falsifier: Calloway refuses a written review, which means they plan to delist by default.
3. **Liquidity.** Check that at least $35k is in savings on Jan 5 without touching the credit line. Walk away if it's below that.

**Decision rule:** IF Calloway agrees in writing to a one-flavor start, net-30 on first invoices, a capped promo base, and a 12-week velocity review, AND Halvard gives a dated trial batch and a $3.10 quote, AND $35k is on hand on Jan 5, THEN sign Dec 15 and place the Halvard order Jan 5. ELSE counter with the one-flavor, April-start terms, or decline. The trial batch and four weeks of sell-through come before any second-flavor order.

**If the decision rule lands on delay, reduce, or don't — the reverse autopsy:** The window closed and we passed. Calloway put a regional competitor in the March reset, and we'd said no to 48 stores. Or we signed all three flavors on paper, the cash gap showed up in February, and we spent the credit line before the first payment.

**What tips the balance:** Calloway's written acceptance of the one-flavor start with the velocity review. Without it, the caution stands.

**Consciously accepted:** A possible June stockout if sales beat 6 a week. Priced at about $17k of cash for an early reorder, which the Jan 5 cash cushion covers.

**How the rebuilt plan dies:** The weakest joint is that Calloway may refuse a one-flavor listing, since buyers often want the full set. Then the rebuilt plan doesn't launch at all. The one-flavor fix is strictly better on cash, but not on the buyer's terms.

**Residual risk:** Velocity itself, Calloway's category reset after the first review, and the labor load.

## The Adversary
**Playing:** Calloway's buyer. They gain the most: 48 stores of shelf space, with the slotting, promo, and expiry costs carried by you. Their risk is low, and their best alternative is another regional chili crisp brand that accepts their terms.

**The kill chain:**
- Dec 15: they set the terms, including slotting netted against invoices, and you sign the full set.
- Jan–Feb: the trial batch and delivery slip, and your deposits are already paid.
- Mar 1–Apr 26: the promo runs and gets billed, and the deductions come off invoices before any cash.
- May–June: the buyer's first sell-through review decides whether to keep the SKU and whether to take back expired product.

**Detection opportunities:** The Halvard trial date (Dec 31), the cash balance (Feb 15), store-level sell-through (Apr 12), the promo chargeback rate (Apr 26), and the first review notice (June).

**The move you'd never see coming:** A category reset in about June. Calloway swaps your SKU for a new national brand, and you're left with stock you can't sell, which then expires into credits.

**What this means you should change:** Sign for one flavor. Put the promo base and expiry terms in writing. Don't let slotting net against the first invoice without a schedule.
