Short answer: the case does not hold as written. The oven may still be worth buying, but on corrected numbers it misses the four-month payback unless the proofer can also be expanded. Here is Petra's reasoning, point by point.

**1. Capacity (the main problem)**
The proofing cabinet holds 60 loaves per batch, so the D9 cannot be fed 72 loaves per load. Dough goes straight from proofer to oven, so the proofer caps each load at 60. Realistic output is 60 × 9 = 540 loaves a day, up 108 from 432, not up 216 to 648. The 150 turned-away loaves would not all be covered. This assumes the proofer stays as it is; a second cabinet or a larger one would change the numbers.

**2. Revenue is not profit**
€6.00 includes 10% VAT, so the shop keeps €5.45 net. Ingredients and packaging are €1.70, leaving about €3.75 contribution per loaf. Petra's €900 a day is gross revenue on 150 loaves and ignores both VAT and ingredient cost.

**3. Energy goes up, not down**
Per loaf, energy does fall: €4.00 ÷ 48 = 8.3 cents now, and €5.20 ÷ 72 = 7.2 cents on the D9. But the bill rises. Nine loads at €5.20 is €46.80 a day against €36.00 today, so energy costs about €10.80 a day more. The "€7 a day saving" is the wrong sign for the total bill.

**4. Interest is overstated**
A standard monthly repayment loan at 6% over five years on €54,000 costs about €1,044 a month, €62,640 in total, with about €8,640 of interest. The €16,200 figure applies simple interest to the full principal for five years, which a standard loan does not do. The true total cost is €62,640, not €70,200.

**5. Payback, recalculated**
Net extra profit at 540 loaves a day is about 108 × €3.75 = €405, less €10.80 energy, so about €395 a day.
- Payback on the €54,000 price: about 137 trading days, roughly 23 weeks or 5.3 months at six days a week.
- Payback on total repayments (€62,640): about 159 trading days, roughly 27 weeks.

Four months at six days a week is about 104 trading days. Both figures fail the board's test.

If the proofer could feed a full 72 loaves per load and the 216-loaf increase were real, net profit would be about 216 × €3.75 − €10.80 ≈ €800 a day. Payback would then be about 68 trading days on the price, or about 78 on total repayments, roughly 11 to 13 weeks. That would pass. So the decision turns on the proofer, not the oven.
