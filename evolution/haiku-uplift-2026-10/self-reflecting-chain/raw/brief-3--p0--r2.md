Dara's conclusion does not hold. Her figures put Lantern's effect at about 9 points per pupil and the 30% expansion at 2.7 points on the district average. Working through her numbers step by step, the effect is closer to 1 to 2.5 points per pupil, and expanding to the bottom 30% would lift the district average by roughly 0.2 to 0.7 points, short of the 2-point goal.

**Step 1: Her September and June averages cover different pupils (point 1)**

- Lantern September average of 82 covers all 180 pupils. Her June average of 94 covers only the 150 who finished.
- The 30 leavers had a September average of 74. Removing them raises the September average of the 150 finishers.
- Finishers' September total: (82 x 180) - (74 x 30) = 14,760 - 2,220 = 12,540, so the average is 83.6.
- Like-for-like finisher gain: 94 - 83.6 = **10.4**, not 12.

**Step 2: The comparison group is wrong (point 1)**

- Her "everyone else" gain of 3 is the wrong benchmark. Last year, pupils in the bottom 15% in September gained 8 points by June without Lantern. Those pupils start low, so they tend to rise anyway (regression to the mean).
- The right question is how much more Lantern pupils gained than similar pupils would have: 10.4 - 8 = **about 2.4 points per pupil**, not 9.

**Step 3: The 30 leavers make the result uncertain**

The leavers' June scores are not given, so bound the all-180 result:
- If leavers stayed at 74: all-180 June average = (94 x 150 + 74 x 30) / 180 = 90.7, a gain of 8.7, or +0.7 over the benchmark.
- If leavers gained the benchmark 8 (to 82): all-180 June average = 92.0, a gain of 10, or +2.0 over the benchmark.
- So the per-pupil effect is roughly +0.7 to +2.4, depending on the leavers and the basis used.

**Step 4: The gap argument (point 2)**

- Dara's arithmetic is right on her numbers: (26 - 17) / 26 = 35%.
- Corrected, the September gap is 108 - 83.6 = 24.4 and the June gap is 17, a narrowing of 7.4 (about 30%).
- Last year the gap narrowed by 5 points with no Lantern (8 - 3). Lantern accounts for about 2.4 points of narrowing, not 9. The gap is also not the board's goal measure.

**Step 5: The harder Form B (point 3)**

- Scores are stated to be on the same equated scale, which should already adjust for form difficulty.
- If equating is imperfect, a harder form would understate June scores, so Dara's direction is possible. But nothing given shows equating was imperfect, so "the true gain is larger than 12" is an unsupported assumption, and the corrected gain is 10.4 or less.

**Step 6: The district-average arithmetic (point 4)**

- Her multiplications are correct, but her inputs are not.
- Corrected, 15% coverage x about 2.4 points = about 0.36 on the district average (range about 0.1 to 0.4).
- At 30% coverage the same effect gives about 0.7 (range about 0.2 to 0.7). Her 2.7 came from doubling the coverage of a 9-point effect.
- Reaching 2 points at 30% coverage would require about 6.7 points per pupil, roughly 3 times what the data show.
- The bottom 30% are less extreme than the bottom 15%, so their effect may be smaller still. Assuming the effect scales linearly is a further assumption.

**Caveats and assumptions**

- I assumed the "everyone else" figures (108 to 111) are correct and cover the 1,020 non-Lantern pupils.
- I assumed last year's bottom-15% gain of 8 is a fair benchmark. It comes from one earlier cohort, so it is an estimate, not a fixed fact.
- The goal wording is unclear. If "average" means the cohort's June average, a natural gain of about 3 to 4 points per year may already meet the goal without Lantern. This needs clarifying before the vote.
- Dara's analysis is within-year growth. It does not show how next year's average compares with this year's.

**What I would ask Dara for before Monday**

1. Compare Lantern finishers with the benchmark, or report all 180 with the leavers' June scores or bounds.
2. Confirm the equating of Form B and state whether any adjustment was made.
3. Use a stronger comparison: pupils just above the 15% cutoff in September, who were not enrolled. If enrolment strictly followed the cutoff, the difference at the cutoff is a fairer estimate of Lantern's effect than the "everyone else" group.
4. Define the goal precisely: the June average on the same scale, or the year-on-year change in the district average.

On the current evidence, the committee should not expect Lantern expansion alone to reach the 2-point goal.
