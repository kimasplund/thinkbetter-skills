**Assumptions:** the silicone sealing on 8-12 March is the last wet trade. I count days the way Owen did, so the day after the trade ends is day 1. The freezer can be loaded and emptied on weekends. There are no public holidays between now and the opening.

**Step 1: the 30-day climate clock is counted from the wrong date.**
- The policy counts from the end of the last wet trade, not from when the HVAC stabilised. The silicone seals finish on Friday 12 March.
- Day 1 is 13 March and day 30 is 11 April. Objects can enter from Monday 12 April.
- Owen's date of 31 March is 12 days too early.

**Step 2: freezing is fine, but his arithmetic is wrong.**
- Slots are the limit. The 8 oversized crates take 16 slots and the 52 normal crates take 52, so 68 slots are needed. The freezer holds 12, so 60 crates is not 5 loads. It is at least 6 loads, since 68 / 12 = 5.7.
- Run back to back from Monday 15 March, the loads come out on 18, 21, 24, 27 and 30 March and 2 April. This needs someone to handle the freezer on weekends.
- The last crates finish their 24-hour sealed rest on Saturday 3 April.
- This is still well before 12 April, so freezing is not on the critical path. It does fail his own "5 loads" claim, and it leaves about a week of buffer, not zero.

**Step 3: mounting dates count weekends as working days.**
- 126 objects at 9 a day is 14 working days. The team works Monday to Friday, so this is not 14 calendar days.
- Starting Monday 12 April, the 14 working days run to Thursday 29 April. Owen's 13 April is wrong.
- Even from his own 31 March start, the end date would be Monday 19 April, not 13 April.

**Step 4: the 48-hour closed period was left out.**
- Each case stays closed for 48 hours after its last object. If the last object goes in on Thursday 29 April, the earliest final check is Saturday 1 May.
- That is two days after the 29 April sign-off deadline. It is also the opening day itself.
- Owen's 14 April check ignores the 48 hours, and his "two weeks of slack" is really about minus two days.

**What would fix it**
To get the final check on or before Thursday 29 April, the last object must be mounted by Tuesday 27 April. That means finishing 126 objects in the 12 weekdays from 12 to 27 April, which is 10.5 a day, not 9. These options would do it:
1. Work two weekend days, for example 17-18 April.
2. Add a mounter or extend hours so the team reaches about 11 objects a day.
3. Mount in case order so earlier cases finish their 48 hours sooner. This doesn't fix the last case, but it lets the conservator check cases as they close.
4. Ask the conservator whether sign-off could be split by case, or moved to the morning of 1 May. That is a risky fallback.

Don't try to shorten the 30-day clock. It is a conservation policy, and the only way to start sooner is a formal exception from the conservator. Also, any slip in the fitters' dates or the HVAC readings moves everything one for one. With zero buffer after the fixes, even a one-day slip puts the sign-off in doubt.
