Dara's conclusion does not hold. Expanding Lantern to the bottom 30% is unlikely to reach the 2-point goal on this evidence. Here is the check, step by step.

**Step 1: Dara's September and June figures cover different groups.**
The 180 pupils averaged 82 in September. Thirty of them left, with a September average of 74. That leaves 150 finishers whose September average must be about 83.6 ((180 × 82 − 30 × 74) / 150). Their June average was 94, so the like-for-like gain for finishers is about 10.4 points, not 12. The 12-point figure compares the finishers' June scores with all 180 pupils' September scores, which mixes two groups.

**Step 2: The 3-point baseline is the wrong comparison.**
Dara subtracts the gain of pupils outside Lantern (3 points). But Lantern pupils were chosen for scoring in the bottom 15%, and last year's bottom 15% gained 8 points by June without Lantern. Low scorers tend to rise on a retest, so the right baseline for Lantern pupils is about 8, not 3. Using last year's figure:
- Finishers: 10.4 − 8 ≈ 2.4 points per pupil (or 12 − 8 = 4 if you accept Dara's gain figure).
- Dara's 9-point estimate is therefore overstated by roughly two to four times.

**Step 3: The Form B point is not established.**
The facts say June scores are on the same equated scale as September. If the equating works, a harder form should not bias the comparison, so the claim that the true gain is larger than 12 is unsupported. It would only hold if the equating understates June scores, and nothing in the facts shows that. Treat it as an open question to check with the test provider, not as a correction.

**Step 4: The gap comparison overstates the effect.**
The gap is 26 points in September (82 vs 108) and 17 in June (94 vs 111), which is a 35% narrowing. But the September gap for finishers is about 24 (83.6 vs 108), and the June gap is 17, so the like-for-like narrowing is about 30%. The narrowing is also partly the regression effect from Step 2, so it does not show that Lantern caused it.

**Step 5: The district-level arithmetic does not reach the goal.**
Dara's 1.35-point lift uses the 9-point estimate, and the 2.7 figure assumes the effect doubles with enrolment. Using the corrected per-pupil effect of about 2.4 (and 4 as an optimistic upper case):
- Current programme (15% of pupils): about 0.36 points (up to 0.6).
- Expanded to bottom 30%: about 0.72 points (up to 1.2).

Both are below the 2-point goal. The doubling in Dara's summary also assumes that pupils ranked 15–30% respond as well as the bottom 15%, and that the 20% attrition rate does not change. Neither is shown in the facts.

**What this means for Monday's vote**
- The evidence supports a modest positive effect of Lantern, not a 2-point lift from expansion.
- Before voting, ask Dara for a comparison with a matched group of similar pupils who were not enrolled, and for the finishers-only September and June figures.
- Confirm the Form B equating with the test provider.
- If the goal is a 2-point rise in the district average, expansion alone is unlikely to achieve it on these numbers. The board should look at other measures or set a more modest target.
