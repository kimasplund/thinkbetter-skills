Dara's arithmetic is mostly right, but her reasoning does not support the claim that Lantern will beat the 2-point goal. Here is the check, point by point.

**Point 1: the 12-point gain and the 9-point effect**

- The June average of 94 covers only the 150 pupils who finished. The September average of 82 covers all 180. The 30 who left had a September average of 74, so the 150 who finished averaged (180 × 82 − 30 × 74) ÷ 150 = (14,760 − 2,220) ÷ 150 = 83.6 in September. Like for like, the finishers gained 94 − 83.6 = 10.4 points, not 12.
- Comparing Lantern pupils with "everyone else" assumes the bottom 15% would have gained the same 3 points as everyone else. Last year's figures show they would not: without Lantern, the bottom 15% gained 8 points, because pupils who score lowest tend to rise toward the average. The right comparison is therefore 10.4 − 8 = 2.4 points per finisher, not 9. Even on Dara's own numbers, 12 − 8 = 4 points.

**Point 2: the gap narrowing**

- The arithmetic is right: 26 to 17 is a 9-point narrowing, about 35%. But last year the gap narrowed by 5 points with no programme at all (8 − 3). Net of that, Dara's figures show about 4 points attributable to Lantern, and the matched figures about 2.4.

**Point 3: Form B**

- June scores are reported on the equated scale, which is meant to make Form B comparable with September. Equating should already remove the difficulty difference, so it is not a reason to expect the true gain to be larger. The point would matter only if equating were imperfect, and then the direction of the error is unknown. Ask the test provider how well Form B was equated before treating this as a correction.

**Point 4: the district lift and the expansion**

- 15% of pupils × 9 points = 1.35 points. That multiplication is valid only if 9 is the true effect. At the corrected 2.4 to 4 points per pupil, Lantern lifts the district average by about 0.4 to 0.6 points, not 1.35.
- Doubling coverage to 30% does not simply double the lift. It assumes the extra pupils (ranks 15 to 30%, who are less far behind) respond as strongly as the lowest 15%. It also ignores that 30 of 180 pupils (17%) left the programme, a rate that may rise at scale. At 2.4 to 4 points per pupil, the bottom 30% would lift the average by roughly 0.7 to 1.2 points. Reaching 2 points would need an effect of about 6.7 points per pupil across the bottom 30%. Dara's 2.7 relies on the uncorrected 9.

**What this means for Monday's vote**

- On the evidence supplied, the 2-point goal is not met under any estimate here, even at 30% coverage. The data do support saying that Lantern helps its pupils, by a modest amount. They do not support saying it will move the district average by 2 points.
- Before the board relies on Dara's figures, ask for:
  - June scores for the 30 pupils who left, or a note explaining why they are missing;
  - a comparison group of similar pupils not in Lantern this year, to replace last year's cohort as the baseline;
  - the Form B equating report.

**Assumptions**

- The leavers' September average of 74 is correct, and the 82 for all 180 pupils is the average of those same 180 pupils' September scores.
- "Everyone else" is the same group in September (108) and June (111), and last year's figures are comparable with this year's.
- The 2.4 to 4 point figures are estimates. They assume the 30 pupils who left would have gained about as much as the finishers, which the data cannot confirm.
