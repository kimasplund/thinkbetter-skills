I checked Dara's four steps. The arithmetic in each step is mostly correct, but the comparisons behind it are not, and once they are fixed the case for Lantern is much weaker than the summary suggests.

**Step 1: the 12-point gain and the 9-point effect**

- The 12 points compares two different groups. The September average of 82 covers all 180 pupils who enrolled. The June average of 94 covers only the 150 who finished. The 30 who left had a September average of 74, so the September average for the 150 finishers is (180 x 82 - 30 x 74) / 150 = 83.6. Like-for-like, the finishers gained 94 - 83.6 = 10.4 points, not 12.
- The 9 points subtracts the gain of everyone else (3 points). That is the wrong comparison. Pupils in the bottom 15% tend to gain more than other pupils even without any programme. Last year the bottom 15% gained 8 points with no Lantern, so that is the right baseline.
- Using the right baseline, the estimated effect per Lantern pupil is about 10.4 - 8 = 2.4 points. Dara's 9 is too high by a wide margin. Even the uncorrected 12 - 8 = 4 is well below 9.

**Step 2: the gap narrowing from 26 to 17**

- The arithmetic is right: 26 to 17 is a 9-point reduction, or about 35%.
- The 26 mixes the 150 finishers with the rest of the cohort, so the September gap is really 108 - 83.6 = 24.4 when comparing like with like.
- The gap also narrowed last year without Lantern, from the bottom 15% gaining 8 and everyone else gaining 3. That is a 5-point narrowing with no programme. Subtracting it, the Lantern-related narrowing is about 7.4 - 5 = 2.4 points, which matches step 1. A 35% reduction is not evidence of a large effect.

**Step 3: "the true gain is larger than 12 because June used Form B"**

- This only holds if the equating between the two forms failed. If the equating works, the scores are comparable and there is no adjustment to make.
- If Form B really is harder and the equating did not fully correct for it, then June scores are understated for everyone, not just Lantern pupils. The true gain could therefore be larger, but the size of the adjustment is unknown.
- It is also unclear which form was used last year for the 8-point figure. If last year's test was a different form, the comparison with this year's 8-point baseline is distorted in the same way.
- Point 3 is an unquantified assumption. It should not be used to support the conclusion.

**Step 4: the 1.35 and 2.7 district-average figures**

- The arithmetic is right given Dara's inputs: 0.15 x 9 = 1.35, and 0.30 x 9 = 2.7.
- With the corrected effect of about 2.4 points per pupil, the 15% programme lifts the district average by about 0.15 x 2.4 = 0.36 points. Expanding to the bottom 30% gives about 0.3 x 2.4 = 0.72 points. Neither reaches the board's goal of 2 points.
- The 30% figure also assumes the effect carries over to pupils who scored higher in September, and that it does not fall as the programme grows. Neither assumption is tested by the data.
- To reach 2 points with 30% coverage, each enrolled pupil would need to gain about 6.7 points. The data suggests about 2.4.

**Other limits on the evidence**

- Last year's comparison is a single year, so the 8-point baseline could be noisy.
- The 30 who left (17% of enrolled pupils) may differ from those who stayed in ways that affect the June figures. The summary does not say why they left.
- The 2.4 estimate is still an observational comparison with a historical group, not a randomised comparison.

**Conclusion**

Dara's summary overstates Lantern's effect. The most defensible estimate is roughly 2 to 2.5 points per enrolled pupil, which is about 0.4 points on the district average at current coverage. That falls well short of the 2-point goal, even at 30% coverage. Before Monday's vote, I would ask Dara to:

1. Report the finishers' September average (83.6) alongside the June average (94).
2. Use last year's bottom-15% gain (8 points) as the baseline, not everyone else's gain (3 points).
3. Say whether the Form B equating was checked, and which form was used last year.
4. Report the outcomes for the 30 pupils who left, or at least their June results if they were tested.

Assumptions I made: the 111 June average for everyone else covers all 1,020 pupils outside Lantern, and the 108 September average is for the same group. The 82 September average for all 180 Lantern pupils is taken as given, since the summary does not show the calculation.
