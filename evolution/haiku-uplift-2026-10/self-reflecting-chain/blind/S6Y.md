Short answer: Dara's arithmetic is mostly right, but the reasoning does not hold up. Lantern's effect looks like roughly 2 to 4 points per pupil, not 9. Expanding it to the bottom 30% would add well under 1 point to the district average, nowhere near the 2-point goal. I would not vote on the basis of this summary.

I went through the four steps in order, because each one feeds the next.

**Step 1: "Lantern added about 9 points per pupil" (12 minus 3)**

This step has two problems.

*The 12-point gain mixes two different groups.* The 82 in September covers all 180 pupils. The 94 in June covers only the 150 who finished. The 30 who left averaged 74 in September, so the 150 finishers averaged (180 x 82 - 30 x 74) / 150 = 83.6 in September. Their actual gain is 94 - 83.6 = **10.4 points**, not 12. Dara's figure is inflated because the weakest pupils dropped out of the June numbers. We also have no June data for the 30 leavers, so we can't say what Lantern did for the full 180.

*The wrong comparison group.* Lantern pupils were chosen because they scored lowest in September. Low scorers on a single test tend to bounce back somewhat on retest, partly through chance and partly through ordinary growth. Last year, with no Lantern, the bottom 15% gained 8 points while everyone else gained 3. The fair baseline for Lantern pupils is therefore about 8, not 3.

| Measure | Gain | Baseline gain | Lantern effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dara's figure (12, all 180 in September) | 12 | 8 | 4 |
| Corrected for leavers (finishers only) | 10.4 | 8 | 2.4 |
| Dara's claim | 12 | 3 | 9 |

So the defensible range is about **2.4 to 4 points**. This rests on a single prior year as the baseline, so there is real uncertainty either side. Even so, 9 is not supported.

**Step 2: "The gap narrowed from 26 to 17, a 35% reduction"**

The arithmetic is correct: 108 - 82 = 26, 111 - 94 = 17, and 9/26 is about 35%. But the same baseline problem applies. Without Lantern, last year's pattern (8 against 3) would have narrowed the gap by about 5 points anyway, from 26 to roughly 21. Only the remaining part of the narrowing can plausibly be credited to Lantern, and the 94 still excludes the leavers. I would drop the "35% reduction" headline or present it with that caveat.

**Step 3: "Form B was harder, so the true gain is, if anything, larger"**

This does not follow. The June scores are already on the same equated scale as September, and the purpose of equating is to adjust for form difficulty. Adjusting again would double count. Form B also applied to every pupil, so it does not affect the comparison between Lantern pupils and the rest. The honest position is that the equating should have handled this. The only open question is how well the equating works at the low end of the scale, and the data here can't answer that. The claim should not be used to push the estimate upward.

**Step 4: "15% x 9 = 1.35, and doubling to 30% gives 2.7, beating the goal"**

The multiplication is right, but it uses the inflated 9, and the extrapolation adds further problems.

- With a realistic effect of 2.4 to 4 points, the current programme lifts the district average by only about 0.4 to 0.6 points (0.15 x 2.4 = 0.36, 0.15 x 4 = 0.6).
- Doubling coverage does not safely double the effect. The extra pupils (roughly the 15th to 30th percentile) score higher and are less far behind. They are likely to benefit differently, probably less, and have less room to gain. Even if their effect matched the current group's, 30% coverage gives about 0.7 to 1.2 points.
- To reach +2 on the district average from the bottom 30% alone, those pupils would need to gain about 6.7 points above their normal growth. That is well beyond anything the data shows.
- About 1 in 6 pupils (30 of 180) left the programme. Any expansion plan needs to allow for that.

I'm assuming the board's goal means 2 points above what the district would reach anyway. If it means a 2-point rise in the raw average, the picture is different, because natural growth already contributes. On last year's pattern that was about 3.75 points (0.15 x 8 + 0.85 x 3). In that case the goal may be met regardless of Lantern, and the average would be a poor test of whether the programme works.

**Overall**

| Dara's claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Lantern adds about 9 points per pupil | Not supported. About 2.4 to 4 points. |
| Gap narrowed 35% | Arithmetic correct, but mostly not attributable to Lantern. |
| Harder form means the true gain is larger | Not supported. Scores are already equated. |
| Expansion reaches 2.7, beating the goal | Not supported. Likely 0.7 to 1.2 at best. |

**Before Monday**

1. Ask Dara for the June scores of the 30 who left, or at least for confirmation that they were tested. This gives the effect across all 180, not only the finishers.
2. Ask for the baseline, meaning last year's 8-point gain for the bottom 15%, and whether it is stable across years or based on a single cohort.
3. Ask whether Lantern pupils were compared with similarly low-scoring pupils who weren't enrolled, if any exist. That is a much better comparison than "everyone else."
4. Ask why 30 pupils left and whether the programme can retain more of them.

**Fair to Lantern.** Even on the corrected numbers, Lantern may well be doing some good. A gain of 2.4 to 4 points over baseline is plausible and worth having. The issue is the size of the claim. If the board votes to expand, it should do so as a modest improvement for the pupils involved, not as the route to the district's 2-point goal. That goal probably needs other measures as well.
