**Short answer: no, the case doesn't hold up as written.** Corrected, the D9 pays back in about 5.3 months (about 6 months if you count loan interest). That misses the four-month rule. There is a fix that may get it under, covered at the end.

Here are Petra's steps in order.

**Step 1: capacity, 648 loaves a day, up 216. Wrong.**
The D9 takes 72 loaves per load, but the proofing cabinet holds only 60 and proofs in the same 40 minutes. Dough goes straight from proofer to oven, so you can't load more than 60 per bake. The cabinet was fine for the old 48-loaf oven. It becomes the bottleneck with the D9.
- Real output: 60 × 9 = 540 loaves a day, up 108 (not 216).
- The 150 loaves a day of turned-away demand would only be partly met. About 42 a day would still go unserved.

**Step 2: "150 extra loaves at €6.00 is €900 a day of profit". Wrong in two ways.**
- Volume: it's 108 loaves, not 150 (see step 1).
- €6.00 is revenue, not profit. It includes 10% VAT, which isn't yours to keep: €6.00 ÷ 1.10 = €5.45 net. Ingredients and packaging take another €1.70. The contribution is about **€3.75 a loaf**.
- 108 × €3.75 = about **€405 a day**, not €900.

**Step 3: energy "saving" of about €7 a day. Wrong in direction.**
Her per-loaf figures are right when the oven is full: €5.20 per load (€4.00 + 30%) ÷ 72 = 7.2 cents, against €4.00 ÷ 48 = 8.3 cents. But two things undo the saving.
- At 60 loaves per load, the D9 costs €5.20 ÷ 60 = 8.7 cents a loaf, which is worse than today's 8.3.
- More to the point, the saving is a per-loaf comparison, and you aren't baking the same loaves. Total energy goes up by €1.20 a load × 9 loads = **€10.80 a day extra cost**. It is a cost to subtract, not a €7 gain to add.

**Corrected daily gain:** €405 − €10.80 = about **€395 a trading day**, against her €907.

**Step 4: financing cost of €16,200. Overstated.**
6% × €54,000 × 5 treats the whole balance as outstanding for all five years. On a standard monthly repayment loan the balance falls each month. At 6% over 60 months the payment is about €1,044 a month, total repaid about €62,600, so interest is about **€8,600**, not €16,200. Her total of €70,200 is too high by about €7,600.

**Step 5: payback of 77 days, about 13 weeks. Wrong.**
The four-month window is about 104 trading days at six days a week.

| Basis | Cost | Daily gain | Trading days | Months |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Purchase price only | €54,000 | €395 | about 137 | about 5.3 |
| Including loan interest | about €62,600 | €395 | about 159 | about 6.1 |

Both fail four months. Petra's 77 days came from three overstatements that happened to be in her favour: the volume, the profit per loaf, and the energy. The financing error was the one that went against her.
