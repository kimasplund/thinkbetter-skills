I run operations for Hollin Lane Bakery, a co-op with three shops supplied by one central bakehouse. Our head baker, Petra, wants to replace the deck oven and has written the payback case below. The board only approves unbudgeted equipment that pays back within four months, and I'm presenting on Thursday. Can you go through her reasoning step by step and tell me whether it holds up?

Background: the current oven bakes 48 loaves per load, 40 minutes per bake, 9 loads a day, six days a week. Last spring we added a second rack to the proofing cabinet, so it now holds 60 loaves and proofs in the same 40 minutes, which lets dough go straight from proofer to oven. A loaf sells for €6.00 on the shelf, including 10% VAT, and ingredients plus packaging come to €1.70 a loaf. Energy runs about €4.00 per load.

Petra's case:

1. The new Tarrow D9 oven (€54,000) takes 72 loaves per load with the same 40-minute bake. At 9 loads that's 648 loaves a day, up 216, which comfortably covers the roughly 150 loaves of demand we turn away each day.
2. 150 extra loaves at €6.00 is €900 a day of extra profit.
3. The D9 uses 30% more energy per load, but it's more efficient per loaf: 7.2 cents versus 8.3 cents now. Saving 1.1 cents on each of 648 loaves is about €7 a day.
4. We'd finance it over five years at 6% on a standard monthly repayment loan, so interest is 6% × €54,000 × 5 = €16,200, for a total cost of €70,200.
5. €70,200 ÷ €907 a day = 77 trading days, about 13 weeks. Comfortably inside four months.
