I chair the education committee for the Brindlemoor school district. Our analyst, Dara, sent the summary below ahead of Monday's vote on expanding Lantern, our Year-4 reading tutoring programme. The board's goal is to raise the district's Year-4 reading average by 2 points next year. Can you check Dara's reasoning step by step?

Facts:

- All 1,200 Year-4 pupils sat the district reading test in September. The 180 lowest scorers, the bottom 15%, were enrolled in Lantern.
- 30 of those 180 left Lantern during the year (moved schools or withdrew). Their September average was 74.
- Dara's September figures include all 180 Lantern pupils; the June figures cover the 150 who finished.
- June scores are reported on the same equated scale as September's, although June used Form B of the test, which has harder questions.
- Last year, before Lantern existed, the pupils in the bottom 15% in September scored on average 8 points higher by June; everyone else gained 3.

Dara's summary:

1. Lantern pupils averaged 82 in September and 94 in June, a 12-point gain. Everyone else went from 108 to 111, a gain of 3. So Lantern added about 9 points per pupil.
2. The gap between Lantern pupils and everyone else narrowed from 26 points to 17, a 35% reduction.
3. Because June used the harder Form B, the true gain is, if anything, larger than 12.
4. Lantern covers 15% of pupils, so its 9 points lift the district average by about 1.35. Expanding it to the bottom 30% doubles that to 2.7, beating the 2-point goal.
