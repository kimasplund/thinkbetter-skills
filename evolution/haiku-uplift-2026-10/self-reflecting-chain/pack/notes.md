# Notes on planted keys

**Brief 1 (bakery oven payback).** Petra's five-step payback chain has a sound skeleton: the load counts, per-loaf energy arithmetic and the final division are all computed correctly from her inputs. Four of the steps are still invalid. First, the oven is not the constraint. The 60-loaf proofing cabinet, mentioned only in the background, caps each load at 60, so capacity rises by 108 and not 216 (k1). Second, revenue is treated as profit, and the price still includes VAT (k2). That is the obvious one. Third, a correct per-loaf efficiency figure is used to claim a total saving when the number of loads is unchanged and each load costs more (k3). Fourth, the interest is computed as simple interest on the full principal for a repaying loan, which overstates it (k4). This error points toward caution, so readers hunting for optimism skip it. Fixing only k2 leaves payback close to the four-month line. Fixing everything gives about six months, which clearly fails the board's rule.

**Brief 2 (museum gallery installation schedule).** Owen's plan is a dependent sequence: climate stability, then freezer treatment, then mounting, then the final check. Each step feeds the next. The stability clock is started at the HVAC date, but the policy counts from the last wet trade, and the silicone case seals finish on 12 March (k1). That fact sits next to the HVAC date and reads as background. Mounting is counted in calendar days rather than working days (k2). The freezer step ignores the oversized crates that take two slots (k3). That error is real but stops mattering once k1 moves the entry date, and a strong reply should notice this. The final check ignores the 48-hour closed-case rule (k4). Only after k1 and k2 are corrected does the chain show mounting ending on 29 April and the sign-off being missed.

**Brief 3 (school tutoring programme evaluation).** Dara's arithmetic is right throughout: the gains and the 35% gap reduction are computed correctly. The inferences are not. The comparison against non-selected pupils ignores regression to the mean, and last year's +8 rebound for the bottom 15% is the real benchmark (k1). The September baseline includes 30 lower-scoring leavers who are missing from June, which inflates the gain (k2). This only shows up if the reader combines the leaver average with the 180/150 note. The claim that a harder June form understates the gain is wrong because the scores are equated (k3). It sounds cautious, which makes it easy to accept. Finally, the 9-point effect is carried over to a different population when the programme is doubled to the bottom 30% (k4). Corrected, the effect is about 2-3 points per pupil and the district lift is about 0.3 points, nowhere near the 2-point goal.
