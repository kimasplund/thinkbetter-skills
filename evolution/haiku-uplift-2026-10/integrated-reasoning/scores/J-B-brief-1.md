# J-B scores, brief-1

Notes:
- V7Y: Correct diagnosis, with useful checks on the all-in cost and the threshold. The crazing mechanism is vague ("shrinks or expands at a different rate"). It never notes that remakes eat capacity. Calling A "the most expensive of the three that needs all partners" is muddled, since C costs more.
- D9K: Sound and well hedged. It names alternative May changes, works out the ~170 remakes a week, makes the "more capacity at 9% multiplies remakes" point, and compares the clay saving with the remake cost. The glaze-fix direction is correct.
- N1E: Insightful on capacity (7% of output), moisture expansion and the governance optics of B. But it states that a "higher-expanding clay under the same glaze would explain the crazing", which is the wrong direction (a higher-expansion body puts the glaze in compression). Long.
- R7U: Strong all round. The mechanism is correct, the capacity loss is quantified, it suggests testing reduction demand via a studio gas kiln, says what would change its view, and lists clear assumptions. "2% for years" slightly overstates the brief.
- X3B: Very actionable: test in both kilns, check whether the current controller can already slow the cooling, and specific glaze levers. Overconfident that a Friday expiry "is a sales tactic", and it guesses C's capacity is "likely similar".
- A6T: Internally inconsistent. It says A is "the only option that needs all three partners" (C does too) and recommends B "for capacity" (B adds none). The diagnosis is fine.
- S3N: Compact and well calibrated (70%, flagged as inference). Testing in both kilns is good. It never quantifies the capacity lost to remakes. The quartz/feldspar remark is plausible but generic.
- C5D: Its fallback "if you must decide by Friday, pick B" misreads the brief, since only A's quote expires and B adds no capacity. Thinner on fixes. The table is mostly "not stated".
- G9H: Diagnosis right, but it lands on "if forced by Friday, buy B within Ines's authority". Nothing forces that, and it edges towards using the threshold. Some filler ("useful information about how much they want the sale").
- K0J: Thorough: alternative causes (detergent, glaze thickness), the moisture-expansion mechanism, and what would change its confidence. Long. "A quote deadline is a seller's pressure tactic" is overconfident, and its assumption about old-clay availability contradicts itself.
- V7X: Good on catching the defect in-house with boil tests, on the glaze raw-material batch as an alternative cause, and on installed cost versus the threshold. But it states the mechanism backwards (a body expanding more puts the glaze in tension), and asserts that C adds no capacity.
- I7B: Recommends buying A by Friday despite its own diagnosis and the 6-week lead time. It ignores that remakes consume ~7% of capacity. One sentence about B is garbled. It assumes quotes are all-in while also flagging B's installed cost.
- R7B: Good: capacity cost quantified, a fix-first order, and governance on B. It overclaims that fixing crazing is "likely worth more extra output than any of the kilns", when A adds ~30% against ~7% recovered.
- D9Q: Best decision framing. It notes that crazing inflates the capacity number used to size the kiln, includes an explicit "what would change my view" section, and gives correct glaze-fix chemistry. Minor overconfidence ("you'd have the unanimous approval").
- O1J: Practical on landed cost, gas/flue work and the remake arithmetic. But it recommends committing to A by Friday while also saying to test first. "Room for a higher margin of error on loads" is filler.
- Y0L: Adds the under-firing/porosity and firing-check angle, which is a good subtle point. Correct glaze levers and well-handled deadline logic. Small unsupported claims: the test kiln has "new elements", and a bigger chamber makes cooling uneven.
