My recommendation is not to sign any of the three kiln quotes by Friday. The timing points at the clay body, not the kiln, and buying equipment won't fix the crazing if that's the cause. Here's the reasoning.

**What the evidence says**

- Rejects went from about 2% to 9% starting in May. You switched clay body suppliers in April. That's a one-month gap, and the change came right before the problem.
- Your 14-year-old kiln has new elements (January), and it was fine for years before the switch.
- Your two-year-old test kiln shows about 8% crazing since May. It's a different kiln, so the kiln is unlikely to be the common cause. The common factor across both kilns is the new clay body with the same satin white glaze.
- Crazing is usually a glaze-to-body fit problem: the clay shrinks or expands at a different rate from the glaze, and the glaze cracks over time. Your glaze hasn't changed, but a different body with different shrinkage can produce exactly this pattern. The fact that it shows up after dishwashers fits that too, since repeated thermal and moisture cycling exposes a poor fit.

I can't confirm this from what you've told me. But it's a much more likely explanation than "the kiln is wrong," and it's cheap to test.

**How each kiln fits the actual problems**

You have two separate problems: capacity (you're turning away accounts) and crazing (9% rejects). Only one of these is a kiln question.

- **Kiln A (€18,400, 30% larger, 6-week lead time):** Solves capacity. It doesn't address crazing. The six-week lead time means it won't help until mid-to-late November at the earliest, and it's the most expensive of the three that needs all partners' agreement.
- **Kiln B (€14,900, same size, programmable controlled cooling):** Controlled cooling can reduce thermal stress in the glaze, so it has some plausible relevance to crazing. But it doesn't add capacity, and it's unlikely to fix a body-glaze mismatch on its own.
- **Kiln C (€22,100, gas, reduction firing):** Reduction firing would change how your glazes develop and would most likely require different glaze recipes. It doesn't address capacity or crazing in your current oxidation-fired satin white. The two restaurant requests are a sales and product-range question, not a reason to buy this kiln now.

**Who decides**

- Kiln B is €14,900, which is only €100 under the €15,000 threshold. If shipping, VAT treatment, installation or accessories push the total over €15,000, it needs all three partners. Check the all-in cost before treating it as Ines's call alone.
- Kiln A and Kiln C are both above the threshold, so they need all three partners regardless.

**What I'd do before Friday**

1. Ask the Kiln A supplier to extend the quote by 2–3 weeks. Vendors often will, and you lose nothing by asking. Note that a six-week lead time means the kiln arrives too late to matter for this month's crazing anyway.
2. Test the body. Fire a set of pieces on the old clay body and the new one, using the same glaze and the same kiln, and run them through a few dishwasher cycles or a boil-and-cool test. This is the test that will tell you whether the body is the problem. Your test kiln is a good place to do it.
3. Ask the new clay body supplier for its thermal expansion or shrinkage data, and compare it with the old body. A significant mismatch with a satin white glaze would confirm the diagnosis.
4. Check whether a glaze adjustment or a slightly different firing schedule (slower cooling) reduces crazing on the new body. You may be able to fix this without buying anything.
5. If the body test points at the clay, consider going back to the old body or finding a third supplier with a body that suits your glaze. That's far cheaper than any kiln.

**Recommendation for Ines and Tomasz**

Don't commit to any kiln by Friday. Ask for a short extension on Kiln A, and run the body test in the next two to three weeks. If the test confirms the body is causing the crazing, the crazing problem is solved for much less than €14,900, and the capacity problem can be decided separately on its own merits. If you do decide to buy for capacity, Kiln A is the one that matches that need, but it should be a separate decision from the crazing fix.
