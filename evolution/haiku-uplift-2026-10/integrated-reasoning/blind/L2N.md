**Getting Marguerite and Dov to agree**

- **Show them the calendar first.** Much of the argument is about whether the other person's idea is feasible. Put the dates on one page. It shows the saison can only be draft, the amber needs filing this week, and nothing conflicts. This changes the argument from "which one" to "how do we sequence both."
- **Agree on what the festival is for.** Ask each of them to write down what a good festival looks like (sales, press, repeat customers, brand recognition). They will probably overlap more than they expect, and the differences are the real issue to resolve.
- **Take Dov's risk concern seriously.** A smoked plum saison is a polarising flavour. Limiting it to draft limits the downside: no cans unsold, no label cost, and a fast read on customer reaction. That is a real answer to his objection, not a concession.
- **Give Marguerite a real win.** She gets her debut, on the biggest weekend, with a clear plan to can it afterwards if it sells. Ask her to pick the volume and serving style.
- **Give Dov real ownership.** The amber relaunch is his project and has the tighter deadline, so he should file the label this week. Ask him to lead the festival presentation of both beers.
- **Make them co-owners of one plan.** Have them present the combined plan together to the team, so the team stops seeing two sides.
- **Agree the success measures in advance.** For example: litres of saison poured, reaction at the tent, amber can sales against last year. Then the post-festival debrief is about evidence, not opinion.
- **Talk to each of them separately first.** Ask what they're afraid of losing. Marguerite may worry her craft is being sidelined, and Dov may worry the brand is being put at risk. Naming that usually lowers the temperature.
- **You make the call and say so.** If they still can't agree by a set date (I'd say this week, because of the label deadline), decide, explain the reasoning openly, and thank both.
