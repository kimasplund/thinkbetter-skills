Short answer: neither side is fully right, and the engine is probably not broken in the way either of them thinks. The overbooking factor is set for winter no-show behaviour and is being applied to summer weekends. That mismatch explains why the problem appears only on Fridays and Saturdays.

**The arithmetic**

- Deck capacity is 62 car-equivalents.
- With a factor of 0.06, the engine sells up to floor(62 × 1.06) = floor(65.72) = **65** car-equivalents.
- Winter (7.5% no-shows): 65 × 0.925 ≈ 60.1 expected to turn up. That fits on 62, so overbooking worked and nobody was left on the pier. Odile's "it has always worked" is true for that season.
- Summer weekends (1.8% no-shows): 65 × 0.982 ≈ 63.8 expected to turn up. That is about two over the deck. Under simple assumptions (no-shows independent, booking units sold equal units boarding), a full weekend sailing sold to 65 overflows most of the time, roughly 85-90%.
- Any sale of 62 or fewer cannot overflow, because at most 62 people can show up. The overflow risk comes entirely from the margin above 62.

So the engine is doing what it was told. IT is right that the mechanics work. Odile is right that the factor is the cause, but wrong that it has always worked: it only worked in the season the factor was measured for, or in a season with high no-shows.

**Why only Friday and Saturday**

Those are the sailings that now sell close to the 65 limit. The dropped sailings will have pushed demand onto the remaining ones, so more of them sell out. I have not seen the sailing-by-sailing data, so treat this as a likely explanation to check, not a confirmed one.

**Second contributor: vehicle weights**

The engine must count car-equivalents (car 1, van 1.5, campervan or trailer 2), both for the sold total and for the 62 limit. If it counts bookings as vehicles, or if the default type "car" is left on campervans, the sold total undercounts the real deck load. That makes overflow more likely than the factor alone suggests. Your deckhands' complaint may be pointing at the same fault, so I would not treat it as separate. Check it, but I cannot confirm it from the brief.

**What I would do before the August bank holiday**

1. Confirm the sold total is in car-equivalents, and that the weights are applied at booking time.
2. Set the factor to 0 for Friday and Saturday sailings, which caps sales at 62. The cost is about one or two empty places per sailing at 1.8% no-shows. The benefit is no stranded passengers, which the council and reviews are already punishing.
3. If you want some overbooking back, set it per season, from measured summer weekend no-shows, not the 2023 winter figure. Even at 63 sold, the expected shows are about 61.9, so there is still roughly a 25-30% chance of one overflow. Only use that after you have a few weeks of summer data, and keep a rebooking plan for the rare overflow.
4. Keep the 6% number out of the bank-holiday weekend unless you have checked it against the summer no-show data.

**Assumptions and gaps**

- I have not seen the engine code, the booking data, or the no-show records. The figures above are estimates from the numbers in your message.
- I do not know how many weekend sailings were full, so I cannot check whether the 11 incidents are the whole picture.
- If the no-show rate is measured by bookings rather than car-equivalents, the numbers shift a little, but the conclusion holds.

To settle IT versus Odile quickly: pull the 11 failed sailings and, for each, compare sold car-equivalents, actual boarded car-equivalents, and no-shows. If the sold total is near 65 and the no-shows are near 1-2, the factor is the cause. If the sold total is well below 65 but the boarded total is still over 62, the vehicle weights are the cause.
