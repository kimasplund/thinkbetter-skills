Here's the timing first, because it decides which options are real.

**What fits in 49 days**

- **A canned plum saison for the festival is not possible.** From FV2 the brew would start on day 23, and the saison needs 27 days (brew, 10 days primary, 16 days conditioning, one day to package). It would be packaged on day 50, one day after the festival. Even if it were ready, the registry needs packaged product and a lab-measured ABV, then takes 25 business days. Cans would not be approved in time.
- **A draft-only saison from FV4 is possible.** FV4 is free now. Brewed today, the beer would be ready to keg around day 27, well before the festival. Ten hectolitres is about double what you poured on draft last year over the weekend, so the volume is there. The catch is that FV4 is your pilot tank, so this uses it up for the next four weeks. I'm assuming draft doesn't need registry approval, since the rule you described covers can labels. Confirm that with the registry before you pour anything.
- **A relabelled or renamed amber is possible, but only if the application goes in today.** Submitted today (13 October), 25 business days lands around 17 November. Can printing takes five days, so the cans would be ready around 24 November. The festival is day 49, which is about 1 December. That leaves roughly a week of slack. Each day you wait eats into it. You need the final ABV, which you already have for the amber since its ABV hasn't changed.

**Creative options**

1. **Festival draft-only saison.** Brew the plum saison on FV4 now as a one-off tap at the festival, with a plum-and-harvest pour. Keep it off the cans entirely and tell people it's "only on tap at Vellmoor." This gives Marguerite her debut without any label approval.
2. **Harvest Amber, new art, same beer.** Dov's route. Rename or reskin it now, and make the new can artwork the festival's visual identity: a harvest-season palette, a festival-date stamp, a "Vellmoor edition" band.
3. **Both at once, different channels.** Cans stay as the amber (relabelled), and the saison is draft-only. Each person gets their idea on stage, and nothing competes for the same shelf.
4. **Plum-and-amber tasting flight.** Pour a small flight of the amber and the saison side by side at the tent. It's cheap, and it turns the disagreement into a visible comparison for customers.
5. **Merch and tent extras.** Festival-only merch tied to whichever beer you pick, such as a glass or a tote with the harvest art.
6. **Post-festival saison launch.** Brew the saison properly after the festival, get it through approval, and launch it in cans in the new year. That turns "too risky" into a planned release.

**Getting Marguerite and Dov to agree**

- **Take the decision out of the argument by putting the timeline in front of both of them.** Show the 27-day saison clock and the 25-business-day approval clock together. Most of the "which beer" fight is really about what can physically happen by 1 December, and the calendar answers part of it.
- **Split the decision rather than choosing a winner.** Marguerite owns the draft saison on FV4. Dov owns the amber relabel and the festival identity. Each gets a clear win, and each has a stake in the festival going well.
- **Set a same-day deadline for the amber application.** The registry clock forces a yes or no on the rename within the week. Let Dov make that call with the registry timeline in view, and agree that the saison draft goes ahead the same day.
- **Ask each of them what would make them say yes.** Ask Marguerite what would make a draft-only saison feel like a proper debut, and ask Dov what would make the amber relabel feel like a real event rather than a rename. Their answers often overlap more than the original argument suggests.
- **Run a short joint tasting.** Pour the amber and the saison side by side. Do this once before anyone commits to anything, and let the team taste rather than argue.
- **Give the decision a clear owner and a date.** You make the call, announce it to the whole team once, and name the next review point. Two weeks of circling is what's costing you the team's trust, more than either option.
