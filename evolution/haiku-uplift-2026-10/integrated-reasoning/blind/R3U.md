Here is how the timelines work out, then some festival ideas and a way to bring Marguerite and Dov together.

**The timeline decides most of this**

- **Smoked plum saison, draft only, brewed today in FV4.** A brew today gives 10 days primary, 16 days conditioning and 1 day to keg, so it is ready on day 27. The festival is on day 49, which leaves about three weeks of slack. FV4 holds 10 hl, which is enough for a festival draft run. The cost is that FV4 is taken for the saison, so pilot batches wait.
- **Saison in cans for the festival: not possible.** A can label needs Provincial Beverage Registry approval, which takes 25 business days (about 35 calendar days). Even if you submitted the moment the beer was packaged on day 27, approval would land around day 62, after the festival. The application also needs the final lab ABV, which you won't have until packaging. The registry only handles packaged product, so draft is the only way the saison can pour at the festival.
- **FV2 is too late for the saison.** It frees up on day 23, so a brew there would be packaged around day 50, one day after the festival.
- **Renaming Harvest Amber with new can art: possible but tight.** The amber's ABV is unchanged, so you can likely use the existing lab figure, assuming you have a recent lab-measured result on file. If you submit the application today, 25 business days puts approval around day 35. Five days of can printing then puts the new cans around day 40, which leaves a small margin before day 49. Any slip in approval or printing means the old cans would be the ones on sale. Confirm with the registry whether a name-only change needs the same approval as new artwork.
- **Draft amber under any name** does not go through the registry, since it isn't packaged. Check that with the registry as well.

**A plan that gives each of them a win**

1. Brew Marguerite's saison today in FV4 for a draft-only festival debut, and present it as a limited tap exclusive.
2. Submit Dov's amber rename and new can art to the registry today, using the amber's existing ABV. Print the cans as soon as approval comes through.
3. Set a checkpoint on day 35 (registry approval). If approval hasn't arrived, the festival cans stay as they are and the rename moves to the next event.

This works if the brew slot, the FV4 booking and the registry submission all happen today. If any of those slip, drop one of the two projects.

- A "smoke and plum" tap takeover, with the saison as the only draft on one line and a short tasting-note card on the bar.
- A flight: a small taster of the saison alongside the amber, served as a "old meets new" pairing.
- A plum-and-smoke food pairing with a local smokehouse or bakery.
- A "Harvest Amber, reimagined" can-art contest with festival-goers voting on the design, as a promo for the rename.
- A pop-up merch angle: a limited-run tote or glass for the saison draft, sold at the merch tent.
- A brewer-led tasting talk where Marguerite explains the smoked malt and Dov runs the storytelling around it.
- A loyalty stamp card: visit the tap line for the saison and the amber to get a discount on cans.
- A small-batch bottle or keg-only release of the saison, sold out at the event.

**Getting Marguerite and Dov to agree**

- Reframe it as one festival with two products, not one winner. The saison gets the tap line, the amber gets the cans. Each person owns the thing they care about.
- Put the timeline on the table. Once the registry math and the FV2 date are visible, the question stops being "whose idea is better" and becomes "what can we actually ship by day 49." That takes the heat out of it.
- Agree on the decision criteria before arguing about options: cost, risk, brand fit and the festival audience. Score each option together.
- Run a small test. Marguerite can pour a sample of the saison for Dov, and Dov can show Marguerite the can mock-up next to the amber. Tasting and seeing the artwork usually moves things faster than debate.
- Give each of them a clear role in the festival so neither one feels overruled.
- Agree in advance on how you will decide if you still disagree, and make the decision yourself on a set date if needed.
