Here is how the timing and the options look, followed by some ways to bring Marguerite and Dov together.

**The timeline decides a lot**

Today is day 0 and the festival is day 49.

- **Smoked plum saison in FV2:** FV2 frees up on day 23. Brew day 23, primary to day 33, conditioning to day 49, packaging on day 50. That is one day too late, and a saison usually benefits from its full conditioning. Cutting conditioning short to make the date is a quality risk.
- **Smoked plum saison in FV4:** FV4 is free now. Brew day 0, primary to day 10, conditioning to day 26, kegged on day 27. That fits comfortably for draft. A 10 hl batch is about double last year's draft volume, so it should cover the weekend if you pour carefully. The trade-off is that you give up a pilot-sized slot and need the brewhouse for a day.
- **Saison in cans:** Not possible for the festival. The registry needs the lab-measured ABV on packaged product, so the earliest application is around day 27. Twenty-five business days later is about day 62, after the festival. Draft is the only realistic saison format for this event, unless you can get a provisional or expedited review, which I can't confirm from here.
- **Harvest Amber rename and new cans:** This works. The ABV is already known and the amber needs no new recipe. If you submit by about 16 October, approval lands around 17 November (25 business days, ignoring holidays). Printing five days later is about 24 November, which leaves roughly a week before 1 December. The margin is thin. A rejection or request for changes would put the cans at risk, so submit this week.

**Creative options**

1. **Draft-only saison, amber in cans (the hybrid).** Marguerite's smoked plum saison goes on tap from FV4 as the festival draft exclusive. Harvest Amber gets Dov's rename and new artwork for the merch tent cans. Each person gets their launch, and neither product competes with the other's format.
2. **Plum saison as a "festival tap takeover."** Pour it at a dedicated tap with its own branded tent sign, and sell 200 ml tasting pours to build a waitlist for next year's cans.
3. **Harvest Amber reinvented as a seasonal name, not a new recipe.** Keep the recipe and ABV unchanged, and use the rename to tell a harvest story (the Vellmoor Amber). This keeps Dov's registry route simple.
4. **Smoked plum and amber side-by-side tasting flight.** Offer a flight of the amber and the saison, so customers compare them. It costs little and makes the saison a talking point rather than a gamble.
5. **Merch-tent exclusives that don't need registry approval.** Glassware, tote bags, a festival-only tasting card, or a collectible pour-token system. These can be ready quickly and give the tent a reason to queue.
6. **Pilot-to-cans roadmap.** Use the festival as a public pilot. Collect pour data and feedback, then decide on cans for next year with a full registry timeline.

**Getting Marguerite and Dov to agree**

- **Split the event rather than choosing a winner.** Option 1 gives each of them a real launch. That is the core of an agreement that doesn't feel like a loss.
- **Set a decision date.** The amber registry submission is the hard deadline. Ask them to agree on the split by 16 October, so the paperwork can start.
- **Let the timeline make the call.** Walk through the day-by-day schedule together. Most of the disagreement may be about what is possible, not what they want.
- **Agree on success criteria before the festival.** For example, draft pours, customer feedback scores, and sell-through of cans. Both can judge the result against measures they agreed on in advance.
- **Give each a clear owner role.** Marguerite owns the saison from brew to tap. Dov owns the amber rename, label submission and merch tent. Each has real authority in their lane.
- **Run a small blind tasting.** Put the saison and a sample of the amber in front of a few regular customers or staff. Let the result inform the next step, not the opinion of whoever argues loudest.
