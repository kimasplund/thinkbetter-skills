**Short answer:** Buy Kiln A if all three partners can sign off by Friday. It is the only option that adds capacity, and capacity is what is costing you accounts. But don't buy any kiln expecting it to fix the crazing. The evidence points to the clay body change, not the kiln.

**Why the crazing probably isn't the kiln**

- The timing lines up with the clay body switch in April, not with anything about the kiln. Rejects were about 2% last year and jumped to 9% from May, the first month after the new body was in use.
- Your two-year-old test kiln is also showing about 8% crazing since May. It is a different kiln from your main one, so the kiln is unlikely to be the common cause. The one thing both kilns share is the new clay body.
- Your glaze recipe hasn't changed. Crazing is usually a mismatch between the glaze and the body. A body that is cheaper but has a different thermal expansion will craze under a glaze that used to fit it, and dishwasher cycles bring this out after a few weeks.
- Controlled cooling (B) can reduce some stresses, but it won't reliably fix a glaze-body mismatch. Reduction firing (C) changes the glaze chemistry and surface, and it doesn't address the mismatch either.

These are my inferences from what you've told me, not tested results. The cheapest way to check is to fire a test batch of the new body and the old body side by side in the test kiln, with the same satin white glaze, and run them through a dishwasher cycle. You could also ask the clay supplier for the body's expansion data, or go back to the old body for the next few weeks to see whether rejects fall.
