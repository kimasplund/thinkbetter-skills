The festival is 49 days out, on 1 December. Today is Tuesday 13 October. Both of your people can get something they want, because their ideas take different routes. The catch is that both routes have to start today.

Two assumptions sit under this. First, the 25 business days are counted without holidays. Second, the 1 December date is right. If either is off, the table moves.

## What I would do

Run both ideas, because they don't compete for the same product.

1. Brew Marguerite's smoked plum saison in FV4 this week, as a draft-only festival exclusive. Kegs should not need a registry approval, since the registry only handles packaged product, but confirm that with the registry before you rely on it.
2. File Dov's amber change today, so it is in the registry queue while the saison is fermenting. Ask the registry whether an art-only change (no rename) follows the same 25-day route. If it does, that is a compromise you can offer Dov.
3. Make the decision today. Both deadlines fall today, so waiting another round costs one of the two ideas.

## Creative options

Draft and bar (no registry route needed, subject to the confirmation above):
- Smoked plum saison, draft-only festival exclusive from FV4. Expect about 10 hl, which is about double last year's draft volume.
- Plum-steeped Harvest Amber: if you have finished amber on hand, rack a keg and add smoked plum at the keg. No new brew, no fermenter, and the amber's ABV stays about the same.
- Amber and plum shandy, plus a non-alcoholic plum tonic for drivers and kids.
- A festival flight of three 100 ml pours, for example saison, amber and one other beer, served with a food-vendor pairing sheet.
- A live smoked-plum demo at the tent, which sells the saison without a label.
- Hand-pumped or cask-style Harvest Amber at the tent, if you have the equipment.

Merchandise (no registry route needed):
- A festival-only glass or tap handle with a new design.
- Tote bags or tees with the new can artwork.
- A festival bundle of glassware and a can, if the existing can label is unchanged.

Cans (registry route):
- Dov's rename of Harvest Amber, with new can art. Submit today.
- A compromise: keep the Harvest Amber name and change only the art. Same registry route and timeline, but much less brand risk.

## Getting Marguerite and Dov to agree

- Give each a clear win. Marguerite gets the saison on tap at the festival. Dov gets a new can for the amber, either renamed or re-skinned.
- Separate the two decisions. The saison is a brewing decision, and the amber is a packaging and brand decision. The only shared resources are the brewhouse day and staff time, so say that openly.
- Test before you scale. Agree in advance on what success means for the saison, such as keg sell-through by the end of the festival. If it sells out, Dov has data to back a can version next year, when the registry lead time can be planned properly.
- Ask Dov what exactly he fears: unsold cans, confusing a loyal name, or losing the festival revenue. Each fear has a different fix, and naming it often ends the circular argument.
- Set a deadline. If they can't agree in a single meeting today, you should decide, in writing, with a short reason. Then tell them both which of the two gets FV4 and the registry filing.

## Gaps in the brief

- The time to fill and pack cans after printing is not in the brief. It eats into the amber route's one-week slack.
- I assumed the FV4 batch yields about 10 hl after losses, and that the 20 hl brewhouse can run a half-size batch without trouble.
- I assumed the amber has a current lab ABV on file, since the registry needs one.
