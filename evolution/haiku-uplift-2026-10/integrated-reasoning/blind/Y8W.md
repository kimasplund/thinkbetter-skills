Here is how I see the timeline, the options, and a way to get Marguerite and Dov aligned. The festival is 49 days away, so the calendar does most of the deciding.

## What the timeline allows

**Smoked plum saison**
- On FV4 (10 hl, free now): brew today, 10 days primary, 16 days conditioning, then package. That's ready around day 27-28, well before the festival.
- On FV2 (free in 23 days): brew on day 23, then 10 + 16 days puts packaging on day 49 or 50. That's the festival day or one day after, with no slack. Don't plan on FV2 for this.
- Cans of the saison won't make the festival. The registry needs the final lab ABV, which you can only get once the beer is finished (around day 27). Then 25 business days (about 5 weeks) takes you to roughly day 62, after the festival.
- Draft is the realistic route. Your brief says the registry only deals with packaged product, so a draft-only saison shouldn't need approval. Confirm that with the registry before you pour it.
- FV4 is only 10 hl. Last year's whole draft pour was about 5 hl, so a 10 hl batch is enough for one festival weekend if it's a featured tap, not the whole bar.

**Renaming Harvest Amber**
- Draft: you can rename it on tap signage and in the festival program right now, with no registry step. The label rule applies only to cans.
- Cans: the new name and artwork need registry approval. If you submit today, 25 business days puts approval around day 35, and 5 days of printing puts cans in hand around day 40. That fits, but with about 9 days of slack. A rejection or a request for more information would miss the festival. Submit immediately if you go this way.
- The amber's ABV hasn't changed in years, so the lab data you already have may cover the application. Ask the registry whether an existing measurement is accepted.

## Creative options for the festival

1. **Plum saison as a draft-only "first pour"** on FV4. Make it a single, named tap with a plum garnish and a short story about the recipe. This is Marguerite's debut with the lowest regulatory risk.
2. **A "Harvest Flight"** of three 100 ml pours: Harvest Amber, the plum saison, and one more. People get to compare, and it doesn't force a choice between the two recipes.
3. **Tasting vote at the tent.** Pour the saison and the amber side by side and let attendees vote on a name for the saison or for the renamed amber. Use the result to decide which goes to cans in the spring.
4. **Smoke demo.** Smoke plums or a small food item in front of the tap. Smell carries well at festivals, and it tells people what the beer is about.
5. **Local plum partner.** Pair with an orchard or market grower who gets a logo on the tap badge and a sample pack in return.
6. **Merch tent limited run.** Sell a numbered festival glass or poster featuring the saison concept, with the cans of current products at the same table.
7. **Renamed amber on draft now, cans later.** If Dov's rename works on draft first, the festival tests the name with real drinkers before the can run.

## Getting Marguerite and Dov to agree

- **Put the calendar on the table.** The dates are fixed. Show both of them the FV4 and FV2 timelines and the registry clock. Much of the argument is about whether the saison is possible at all. The answer is that it's possible on draft and not in cans, and that the amber rename is possible but tight.
- **Give each of them a real win.** Marguerite gets the saison on tap at the festival. Dov gets the new name on the amber and the festival campaign. Neither has to lose for the other to win.
- **Separate the two decisions.** The saison is a draft pilot on a 10 hl tank, and it's cheap to try. The rename is cheap on tap and only costs a registry fee and printing if it fails. Both risks are small and reversible, which makes it easier to say yes to both.
- **Set a decision date.** The registry submission for a can rename has to go in within days, so the decision on that needs to happen this week. Tell them that now. Ask both of them to make a short case by a set time, then decide.
- **Make it a shared launch.** Ask Marguerite to present the saison and Dov to present the festival plan in the same meeting, and have them pitch as one team. People tend to stop picking sides once they're presenting the same event.
- **Keep the final call with you.** If they still disagree after the facts are on the table, you make the call and say so. Two weeks of circling is worse for the team than a decision that's imperfect but clear.
