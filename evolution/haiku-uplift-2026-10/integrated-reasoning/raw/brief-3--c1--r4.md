## Creative options

Ordered roughly from most to least conservative.

1. **Both, split by format.** The saison goes on draft only, as a festival exclusive from FV4. The renamed Harvest Amber is the can in the merch tent. Marguerite gets her debut and Dov gets his launch, and neither one is risked on the other. This is my recommendation.
2. **Saison as a "tap takeover" event.**
   - Pour it in 2-3 serves, such as a half pour or a flight with a smoked-plum garnish.
   - Add a "first pour" moment at a fixed time on Saturday.
   - Make it a festival-only keg, with a sign saying "never canned, maybe never again" to build scarcity.
3. **Saison as a vote.** Pour it next to the amber and let festival-goers vote with tokens. Use the result to decide whether the saison gets a canned release and a label application after the festival. The label application can start the moment the lab ABV exists, so you could submit it during the festival.
4. **Pre-sell the can.** The saison can goes on sale in the winter. At the festival you take pre-orders, a mailing-list signup or a "reserve your first case" card. Dov gets a campaign and Marguerite gets a launch with a lead list.
5. **Amber with a twist in the same keg run.** Rename and re-art the amber, then do a small special keg of it, such as a smoked-plum-infused or oak-aged amber. That is a hybrid that keeps both ideas alive. Treat it as a stretch idea: infusing the amber would change the recipe and possibly the ABV, so check it doesn't trigger a new lab ABV and application.
6. **Pilot first, then decide.** Brew the saison in FV4 anyway and keg it. If it tastes great at day 28-34, push it as the star. If it doesn't, you have a normal festival and the renamed amber still carries the weekend. The downside is small because FV4 is the pilot fermenter.
7. **Collaboration story.** Have Marguerite and Dov co-write the tap-handle and festival tent story: "the brewer's smoked plum saison, the marketer's Harvest Amber." Turn the disagreement into the narrative, but only if they both want that. If it reads as forced, skip it.
8. **Fallback if the can art slips.** If the amber artwork isn't finalized in time, keep the current can and run the rename on tap handles, signage and tent banners only. Check with the registry whether non-can materials need approval.

## Getting Marguerite and Dov to agree

The fight is over a choice that doesn't actually exist, so the first job is to change the question.

- **Show them the calendar first, in one page.** Put the two timelines side by side. Seeing that both are possible usually removes most of the heat. Do it with both in the room, not one at a time.
- **Split the fight into two decisions.**
  - Decision A is what goes in kegs. Marguerite owns this and Dov is consulted.
  - Decision B is what goes on cans and in the merch tent. Dov owns this and Marguerite is consulted.
  - Each owner has a real veto in their own area and an advisory voice in the other.
- **Give Dov a risk guard-rail he can accept.** His worry is that a new recipe is risky. Answer it with a go/no-go taste panel on day ~25, with named tasters, a pass bar agreed in advance (for example 7 out of 10 panelists), and a fallback of pouring only the amber if it fails. Pre-agreeing the bar matters more than the bar itself.
- **Give Marguerite a visible win.** The saison is on the festival's biggest stage and she picks the tasting notes and pour sizes. Her fear is probably being told her work is "too risky," so credit the experiment.
- **Name the shared enemy.** The enemy is the two clocks, the registry and the single free fermenter, not each other. Ask each of them to say what they would need to hear to feel good about the other's plan. Questions like that cool a standoff faster than arguments.
- **Set a decision deadline, and own it.** Pick a date within the next 2-3 days, because the amber application has to go in by about day 7-9. You make the call if they haven't converged. Say so up front, so neither feels the decision was sprung on them.
- **Watch the "picking sides" problem separately.** Talk to the people who've started to pick sides and say plainly that the answer is both. A short all-hands message from you can end the split faster than anything the two of them say.
