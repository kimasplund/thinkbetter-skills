Key facts behind this:

- The saison is 27 days from brew day to keg: 1 brew day, 10 days primary, 16 days conditioning, 1 day to keg. To keg by Nov 30, the brew has to start by about Nov 3.
- FV4 is free now but holds only 10 hl. Brew a half-size batch to fit it. That is about twice last year's draft pour, so spare beer can go on other taps.
- The registry approves packaged labels only. A draft-only saison should not need approval, but confirm this with the registry before you pour it.
- A can rename or any new can label needs approval first. The application needs a current lab-measured ABV, which should be quick to get for the amber.

## Creative options for the festival

1. **Draft-only saison debut.** Brew it in FV4 this week and make it a tent exclusive. Keep the batch to the festival and a few taps after.
2. **Harvest Amber rename on draft, with new can art later.** Put the new name on the tap handles, banners and menu now. Only the cans wait for the registry, so the festival brand change doesn't depend on approval.
3. **Both at once.** The saison and the amber refresh don't compete for the same product, so they can share the festival. The saison is the draft headline, and the amber's new cans sell at the merch tent.
4. **Plum-infused amber on draft instead of a saison.** A draft variant of the amber uses the same FV4 slot and timing, so it is a straight swap with the saison.
5. **Saison and amber tasting flight.** Serve small pours side by side with smoked food pairings, and let attendees compare the two.
6. **Festival merch with the new art.** Shirts, glasses and stickers carry Dov's artwork. Merch doesn't need a can label approval, though confirm that too.
7. **Vote for next year's can.** Have attendees vote on the saison against the amber. The winner gets the full registry process in the new year, when there is time for it.

## Getting Marguerite and Dov to agree

- **Put the calendar on the table.** Both ideas fit if they are decided this week. The real deadlines (brew by about Nov 3, submit the label by about Oct 14) are facts, not preferences, and they take the personal edge out of the debate.
- **Give each of them a piece they own.** Marguerite owns the saison and its tasting notes. Dov owns the amber rename, the artwork and the festival branding.
- **Cap the risk.** Dov's worry is a new product confusing the brand. A draft-only, half-size saison limits the downside, and the festival data (pour counts, feedback cards) decides whether it goes into cans next year.
- **Decide as owner, with a date.** Tell both of them you will decide by a set day, then decide. A decision made on time is easier to accept than a long standoff.
- **Talk to each one separately first.** Ask each what they need to feel the plan is safe, then bring both into one meeting with the timeline printed out.
