I'm one of three partners at Fenwick Clayworks, and I'll be honest: I'm not sure a comparison like this will settle anything. Every kiln vendor says theirs is the best, and I've been burned by sales pitches before. Still, here's the situation.

We make stoneware tableware for about 40 restaurants, roughly 2,400 pieces a week, and we're at capacity. We've turned down two new accounts this year because we couldn't fit the work in.

The problem is crazing. Rejects ran at about 2% last year, and since May they've been 9%. Most of it isn't caught at the kiln. Restaurants report it two or three weeks after delivery, once the pieces have been through their dishwashers, and we remake them for free.

Our main electric kiln is 14 years old (we replaced its elements in January), so we've collected three replacement quotes. I doubt any of them is as good as it looks on paper:

- Kiln A: €18,400, 30% larger chamber, six-week lead time. The quote expires this Friday.
- Kiln B: €14,900, same size as ours, with programmable controlled cooling.
- Kiln C: €22,100, gas-fired, can do reduction firing.

Ines, who runs production, wants B. Tomasz, who handles sales, wants C because two restaurants have asked about reduction glazes. I lean towards A for the space, though I'm not confident about it. Our partnership agreement says equipment over €15,000 needs all three partners to agree. Anything below that is the production lead's call.

Some background: in April we switched clay body suppliers after our old one raised prices. The new stoneware body is cheaper, and our glaze recipes haven't changed; it's the same satin white we've used for six years. We also fire some loads in a small two-year-old test kiln, and its crazing rate since May has been about 8%.

If you can, compare the three kilns and tell me which one to buy before the quote runs out on Friday. I need something I can defend to Ines and Tomasz, who will both pick holes in it.
