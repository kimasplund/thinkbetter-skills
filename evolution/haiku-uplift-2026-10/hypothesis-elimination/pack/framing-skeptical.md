I'm honestly not sure this is a real problem, and our chemistry lead, Ansel Morrow, thinks I'm chasing noise. I manage the core lab at Kestrel Valley Health: two hospitals and five outpatient clinics. Since about September 8, outpatient potassium results above 5.5 mmol/L have gone from about 1.5% of outpatient samples to 6%. Doctors are recalling patients. Two were sent to the ED, and both had normal potassium on repeat. Inpatient and ED flag rates haven't moved, which is partly why Ansel shrugs at it.

Where the high outpatient results came from, September 8–30: Ridgeway 44%, Millbrook 39%, Harbour 5%, the other two clinics 12%. Ridgeway and Millbrook together send about 30% of outpatient volume.

What changed:
- August 1: network-wide cold-hold policy. Any specimen waiting more than 30 minutes for transport goes in the clinic fridge. That was a month before any of this, so I doubt it matters.
- September 1: Ridgeway hired a new phlebotomist, Tomas, who prefers 23-gauge butterfly needles. Ridgeway's nurse manager has noticed more difficult draws since. Yes, that's one person's impression, but I trust her read.
- September 4: new potassium reagent lot on Analyzer A. QC mean moved up 0.08 mmol/L, still inside limits. I know that's within limits, but I don't love a lot that moved at all. Analyzer A runs about 60% of our volume, and 57% of the flagged samples ran on it.
- September 7: outpatient courier runs moved to a contractor. Ridgeway and Millbrook went from our own driver's 90-minute loop to one pickup a day at 3:30 pm. Harbour is on the same contractor but spins its samples on site before pickup. Samples have always waited somewhere, so I'm not sure this is anything.

Other numbers: hemolysis index is normal on 93% of the flagged samples, though the index isn't perfect. Calcium and sodium on them look normal. At Ridgeway and Millbrook, 11% of samples drawn before 11 am flag high, against about 1% of those drawn after 1 pm.

My director wants to quarantine the Analyzer A lot, and Ridgeway wants Tomas retrained. What's actually going on, and what should I bring to Friday's quality meeting? Be brutal.
