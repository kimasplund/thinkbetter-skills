I'm finance director at Hollin & Pike, a 22-store home and garden chain. Our Q3 stock counts put shrink at 3.1% of sales in our six northern stores, against 1.3–1.5% in every other store and in those same six last year.

What changed this year:
- May: self-checkout went into nine stores, the six northern ones plus three in the south. The three southern ones are at 1.4%.
- July 1: we switched security contractors at eight stores, five of them among the northern six.
- July 8: the northern six moved bagged landscape lines (compost, bark, gravel; 20–40 kg bags) off our distribution centre and onto direct delivery from a regional supplier, Greenfold. The garden centre team at Store 14 loves the change: Greenfold pallets come five layers of ten bags and fit through the side gate.
- August: police circulated a bulletin about an organised group stealing power tools from DIY stores across the north.

Where the extra shrink sits across the six: bagged landscape 68%, power tools 7%, paint 6%, everything else 19%. Power-tool stock is within 2% of book at four of the six stores.

Receiving works the same everywhere: staff scan the pallet label and the system books the pallet quantity from the item master. IT has confirmed nobody has edited item master data this year, so bagged lines are still set at 60 per pallet, as they always have been.

Our head of loss prevention wants to pull self-checkout from the six stores, and the COO wants the old security contractor back. Each costs about £90k. What's actually driving this, and what should we check before we spend anything?
