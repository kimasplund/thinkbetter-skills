I run the claims contact centre at Bramblecote Mutual, a regional home and motor insurer with about 140 agents. Average handle time sat at 6.1 minutes all summer. From October 3 it jumped to 7.9 and has stayed there for three weeks. We now miss our answer-time target every afternoon, and the operations board wants a fix.

What changed around then:
- September 26: 18 new agents finished training and went live. New starters usually run about a minute slower than tenured staff at first.
- October 2: CRM release 14.1. Agents complained screens were slow to load. IT pushed a patch on October 9 and says load times are back to normal.
- October 3: IVR refresh. Shorter menu tree, a new voice, and we dropped the date-of-birth and postcode prompts because the vendor showed that's where most callers hung up. IVR abandonment fell from 9% to 4%.
- Late September: the Harnley floods. Flood claims are complicated, and they're about 11% of October calls.

What the data shows:
- AHT rose by about the same amount, 1.7 to 1.9 minutes, for tenured and new agents alike.
- AHT for October 10–24 matches October 3–9.
- All of the increase is talk time. Hold and after-call work are flat.
- Motor-only calls, which have nothing to do with the floods, rose by the same amount.
- QA listened to 40 calls and says the extra time is mostly in the first couple of minutes, before the caller gets to the claim.

Our verification rules haven't changed: every caller passes ID checks before we discuss a policy.

My workforce manager wants 12 temps, and IT wants to roll back the CRM. Which is right, and what's actually going on?
