The most likely cause is a counting error created by the July 8 change to Greenfold deliveries, not theft. Neither self-checkout nor the contractor change explains the pattern. I would not spend the £180k until the checks below are done, which should take a few days.

**Why the Greenfold change fits**

- Greenfold pallets arrive as five layers of ten bags, so 50 bags per pallet.
- The item master sets bagged lines at 60 per pallet. Receiving scans the pallet label and books 60 from the master, not the count on the pallet.
- Each Greenfold pallet therefore puts about 10 bags into the system that never arrived. That is roughly one in six booked bags on those lines.
- The overstatement shows up at count time as shrink. It affects only the six northern stores, because only they moved to Greenfold. It appears in bagged landscape, which is 68% of the extra shrink.
- IT's finding that the item master is unchanged is consistent with this. The 60 figure was correct for distribution-centre pallets and is wrong for Greenfold's.

**Why the other candidates fit less well**

- Self-checkout: the three southern stores with self-checkout are at 1.4%, the same as the stores without it. Self-checkout alone does not raise shrink here.
- Security contractor: this changed at five of the northern six, but power-tool stock is within 2% of book at four of the six, and power tools are only 7% of the excess. The contractor is not where the loss sits.
- Police bulletin: it is about power tools, which are 7% of the excess.
- Paint (6%) and other lines (19%) are worth a look afterwards, but they do not explain most of the gap.

**What to check first (cheap, and each one could rule this out)**

1. Count the bags on a sample of Greenfold pallets at Store 14 and at two or three other northern stores. If the count is 50 and the system says 60, the cause is confirmed.
2. Compare the quantities on Greenfold delivery notes with the receiving records since July 8.
3. Check when the gap starts. If the bagged-line shortfall begins on or about July 8, that supports this explanation. If it started earlier or is spread evenly through the quarter, look elsewhere.
4. Cycle-count bagged SKUs at the northern stores against book. The gap should be about 10 bags per Greenfold pallet received.
5. Check whether the same bagged lines reaching those stores from the distribution centre (if any) match book. They should, and that would be a useful control.
6. Compare bagged-line shrink at the five northern stores with the contractor change against the one without it. If the contractor is irrelevant, the two groups should look the same.

These checks assume Greenfold bags carry the same item numbers as the distribution-centre bags. If they use separate item numbers, check those as well.

**If the count is confirmed**

Correct the pallet quantity for the Greenfold items (50, or a supplier-specific figure), adjust stock on hand for the excess already booked, and measure shrink again. If the northern rate drops to about the baseline, the self-checkout and contractor decisions can wait. Any remaining excess in power tools or paint can then be assessed on its own.
