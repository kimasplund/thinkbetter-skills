**Short answer:** Don't spend the £180k yet. The most likely driver is a receiving and booking error on the Greenfold bagged landscape lines. It is probably not theft, and neither the self-checkout nor the contractor explains the pattern.

**The likely cause: a pack-size mismatch**

- Your system books 60 bags per pallet from the item master, and nobody has edited it.
- Greenfold pallets are five layers of ten bags, which is 50 bags.
- If the northern stores scan a Greenfold pallet label, each pallet books 10 bags that never arrived. Those bags are on the books but not on the shelf, and they show up as shrink at the next stock count.
- The timing fits. It started on July 8, in exactly the six stores that moved to Greenfold, and last year those stores were normal.
- The size fits. Bagged landscape is 68% of the excess shrink, and it is the only category with a specific mechanism that creates phantom stock.
- Store 14's team likes the new pallets, so nobody there has a reason to flag the 50 versus 60 difference.

**Why the other explanations fit worse**

- **Self-checkout:** it went into nine stores, and the three southern ones are at 1.4%, which is normal. Self-checkout also wouldn't concentrate losses in 20–40 kg bags.
- **Security contractor:** it changed at eight stores, and five of them are northern. That leaves three non-northern switched stores, which should be at normal shrink if the contractor were the cause. Also, one northern store kept its old contractor. If it shows the same elevated shrink, the contractor is ruled out.
- **Organised power-tool theft:** tools are only 7% of the excess. Four of the six stores are within 2% of book on tools, so the gang theory explains little. Check the other two stores individually, because there may be a small real theft problem there.

**What to check, cheapest first (days, not pounds)**

1. **Count a Greenfold pallet.** At Store 14 or any northern store, count bags per pallet on a few deliveries. If it is 50 and the system booked 60, that is most of the answer.
2. **Reconcile the numbers.** Take the pallets received since July 8 at the six stores and multiply by 10 bags. Price it at cost and compare it with the extra shrink in the landscape categories. If it is close, the mechanism is confirmed. If it explains only part, look for a second cause.
3. **Check the invoices.** Are you being billed for 50 or for 60 per pallet? If Greenfold bills 60 but ships 50, you can claim money back. If it bills 50, you are only losing book value.
4. **Check whether the pallet counts differ by supplier.** Compare landscape shrink on distribution-centre-supplied lines (if any remain) with the Greenfold lines within the same store.
5. **Test the contractor with the control store.** Compare the one northern store that kept its old contractor, and the three southern switched stores, with the rest.
6. **Look at the two stores where tools are off book.** Check CCTV and incident logs there, and check whether the police bulletin matches those sites.
7. **Look for other direct-delivery lines.** Anything else that moved from the distribution centre to direct supplier delivery has the same risk, which could explain part of the 19% "everything else" and the paint.

**What to do**

- Hold both the self-checkout removal and the contractor change until steps 1–2 are done. They take a few days.
- If confirmed, fix it at the source: set up a Greenfold-specific pack size of 50 in the item master (or a supplier-specific receiving quantity), or receive by actual count. Then do a one-off stock adjustment and recount.
- Most of this is probably an accounting adjustment rather than real loss. Part of the 3.1% would then clear once the books are corrected.
- Add a receiving check. Spot-count the bags on any direct-delivery pallet, and flag any supplier whose pack size differs from the master.

**Assumptions:** I haven't seen the pallet data or invoices. If the count shows 60 bags per pallet, this explanation fails and the contractor and tool theft move up the list. But the 50 versus 60 gap is specific, cheap to test, and matches the timing, location and category of the shrink.
