The most likely driver is the July 8 switch of bagged landscape lines to Greenfold, not self-checkout or the security contractor. Bagged lines account for 68% of the extra shrink, and the timing and location fit.

The mechanism looks like a receiving error. Your system books each pallet at 60 bags, taken from the item master. A Greenfold pallet is five layers of ten bags, which is 50 bags. So every Greenfold pallet is booked with 10 bags that never arrived. That creates phantom stock, which then shows up as shrink at stock count. Ten bags out of 60 is about 17% overstatement on each bagged receipt. Nothing in the item master has changed, which is why IT's confirmation doesn't rule this out. The 60 figure was right for the distribution centre pallets. It is wrong for Greenfold pallets.

The other drivers fit poorly:
- Self-checkout: the three southern self-checkout stores are at 1.4%, so the technology alone does not produce the spike. Power tools are also within 2% of book at four of the six northern stores, so self-checkout is not showing up in the stock records.
- Security contractor: it changed at three stores outside the northern six, and those show no spike. Power tools are the only category a theft ring would plausibly hit, and they make up only 7% of the extra shrink.
- Police bulletin: this is about power tools, which again are mostly at book.

Before spending £180k, check these things, which are cheap:
1. Count a sample of Greenfold pallets at receiving, say 10 to 20 pallets across the six stores. If they hold 50 bags, the item master is overstating each pallet by 10.
2. Check the Greenfold delivery notes and invoices for the bag count per pallet, to rule out a supplier short-delivery as well.
3. Compare bagged-line book stock against physical counts at one northern store. A gap of roughly 10 bags per Greenfold pallet received would confirm the receiving error.
4. Fix the pallet quantity for Greenfold bagged lines in the item master, or set up a separate item record for the direct-delivery supplier, then re-count one store to see whether its shrink drops toward 1.4%.
5. Check whether any bagged-line shrink appears at stores still on DC delivery. If it does not, that supports the receiving explanation.
6. Keep the power-tool and security work going, but as a targeted review of the two northern stores where stock is not within 2% of book, not as a reason to pull self-checkout or reinstate the contractor.

Assumptions: I have not seen the Greenfold delivery paperwork, and I am inferring from the figures you gave. If step 1 shows pallets are holding 60 bags, the explanation is wrong and I would look at the supplier and at theft of bagged lines in more detail.
