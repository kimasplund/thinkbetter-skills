**Short answer: don't spend the £90k on either option yet. The most likely driver is a receiving mismatch on the Greenfold pallets. It looks like a booking error, not theft.**

**The key clue**

Greenfold pallets are five layers of ten bags, so 50 bags. Your item master books 60 per pallet, and nobody has edited it. Every Greenfold pallet scanned in therefore books 10 bags that never arrived. Your DC pallets really were 60, so this never showed up before July 8.

- Bagged landscape is 68% of the excess shrink, and it is the only category whose supply route changed in the six stores.
- The timing fits. The route changed on 8 July, which is in Q3.
- It explains why the stores differ. Only the northern six moved to Greenfold.
- It is large. If a store takes one pallet of a line, the book overstates stock by 20% of that pallet, and that flows straight into shrink at the next count.

**Why the other explanations fit worse**

- **Self-checkout:** it has been in nine stores since May. The three southern stores with it are at 1.4%, which is normal. Self-checkout alone doesn't produce the northern gap. Pulling it would cost £90k and probably fix nothing.
- **Security contractor:** the switch covered eight stores, five of them northern. The other three are presumably outside the northern six and look normal, and the one northern store that kept its old contractor should be checked. Contractor changes also don't explain why 68% of the excess is in heavy bagged goods. Nobody walks out with 20–40 kg bags in volume at that scale.
- **Organised power-tool theft:** power tools are only 7% of the excess, and four of the six stores are within 2% of book. This may be real but small at one or two stores. It is not the main story.
- **Paint (6%) and other (19%):** these could be a mix of ordinary noise and some genuine theft. I wouldn't read much into them yet. Check them again after the pallet issue is corrected.

**What to check before spending anything (cheap, a week or less)**

1. **Pallet arithmetic.** Take Greenfold deliveries since 8 July for each northern store: pallets received × 60 booked, against pallets × 50 actual. Compare the gap in bags, and in £ at cost, with the bagged-landscape shrink for the quarter. If they roughly match, that's the cause.
2. **Physical spot check.** At Store 14 and one other store, count the bags on two or three incoming Greenfold pallets. Confirm 50, and check whether any are mixed or part pallets.
3. **Split by supplier.** Look at shrink on bagged lines by source (DC vs Greenfold) and by week. It should step up in the week of 8 July and be concentrated in SKUs now supplied by Greenfold.
4. **Control comparison.** Check for any bagged-landscape SKUs in the northern stores that still come from the DC. They should show normal shrink.
5. **Check for the same problem elsewhere.** Is anyone else booking from the item master per pallet where the pallet configuration differs, for example other direct-delivery suppliers?
6. **Residual theft check.** After removing the booking gap, re-run the northern shrink. If it's back near 1.3–1.5%, you're done. If something is left, look at the power-tool stores with a gap and at paint, and then at the security and self-checkout data. A leftover of about 1 point would be where it's worth asking about the contractor.

**What to do**

- Stop the bleeding: change receiving for Greenfold lines to book the actual quantity (a supplier-specific pack/pallet quantity of 50, or scan or count by bag). Ask Greenfold for an ASN or delivery note with quantities.
- Correct the stock book for the affected lines in the six stores. This is an adjustment, not a loss. Restating it will lower the reported shrink for Q3.
- Hold the self-checkout and contractor decisions until the residual is known. Ask for evidence on each (incident data, CCTV, exception reports) before committing £90k.
- For the power tools, pass the police bulletin to the stores with the gap and tighten controls there. That's much cheaper than a chain-wide change.

**Assumptions:** I'm assuming all Greenfold pallets are 50 bags regardless of line (check bark, compost and gravel separately, as bag sizes differ), that the DC was the only source before 8 July, and that the count dates align with when the pallets were booked.

If step 1 shows the gap, the real cost is a one-line item master fix and a restatement, not £90k.
