The data points away from theft and away from self-checkout. The most likely driver is the July 8 change to direct delivery of bagged landscape lines, combined with a stock-book setting that no longer matches what arrives on the pallets.

**Why the explanations rank this way**

| Candidate | What the data shows | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Bagged-line receiving error (Greenfold pallets) | Started July 8, only at the six northern stores, and bagged landscape is 68% of the extra shrink | Strongest fit |
| Organised power-tool theft | Power tools are only 7% of the extra shrink, and four of six stores are within 2% of book | Unlikely as the main cause |
| Self-checkout | Three southern stores also have self-checkout and sit at 1.4%, and the northern shrink is concentrated in bagged stock, not in checkout-prone lines | Unlikely |
| Security contractor change | Changed at eight stores, five of them northern, so it cannot explain the six-store pattern on its own; no effect is visible in the other stores that changed | Unlikely |
| Pre-existing northern problem | The same six stores were at normal levels last year | Ruled out |

**The likely mechanism**

Receiving books the pallet quantity from the item master, and bagged lines are still set at 60 bags per pallet. Greenfold pallets are five layers of ten, which is 50 bags. Every Greenfold pallet therefore books 10 bags more than physically arrived. Those bags never existed on the shelf, so they show up as missing stock at the count, and they land almost entirely in bagged landscape lines. The arithmetic is consistent with the reported gap: a 1.7-point rise in shrink on sales, with roughly two-thirds of it in bagged lines, is what a systematic overbooking of this kind would produce. Item master data being unedited is consistent with this, because the setting was correct for the old supplier's pallets and is wrong for Greenfold's.

Note that the mismatch can look like theft from the count side, which is why the power-tool story is attractive, but the power-tool shortfall is small and mostly absent.

**What to check before spending the £180k**

1. Count a Greenfold pallet on arrival and compare it with the system quantity. If it shows 50 bags booked as 60, the mechanism is confirmed.
2. Pull the receiving records since July 8 for bagged lines at the six northern stores and compare booked quantities against Greenfold's delivery notes.
3. Do a blind recount of bagged landscape at two or three northern stores. If the shortfall matches about 10 bags per pallet received, the problem is in the books, not the shelves.
4. Check whether the other northern stores that do not receive Greenfold direct deliveries show the same shrink. They should not, if this is right.
5. Once confirmed, correct the item master for Greenfold bagged lines (or set per-supplier pallet quantities), write off the phantom stock once, and re-run the Q4 counts.

**What to do with the two proposals**

Hold both. Removing self-checkout from six stores and bringing back the old contractor would cost about £180k together and are unlikely to move the number, because the evidence does not point at either. If the recount in steps 1 to 3 does not explain the gap, reconsider the power-tool theft angle first, since the police bulletin is a real signal, and only then look at the checkout and security options, using the three southern self-checkout stores as the comparison.

**Confidence:** high that the receiving quantity is the main driver, pending the pallet count in step 1. Low confidence in anything beyond that until the recount is done.
