**Short answer:** most of this is probably not theft. It looks like a receiving error that creates phantom stock on paper. Don't spend the £90k on either option yet.

**The likely cause**

Greenfold pallets are five layers of ten bags, so 50 bags. Your item master books every bagged pallet at 60, and IT has confirmed it was never changed. Each Greenfold pallet scanned in therefore adds 10 bags to the books that were never on the shelf. Your DC pallets presumably really were 60, so the old setup was correct and the new one isn't.

That fits the numbers well:
- 68% of the excess is in bagged landscape, the only category whose supply route changed on July 8.
- It hits exactly the six stores moved to Greenfold.
- The error is about 17% of every pallet's quantity (10 of 60 booked), applied mechanically on every delivery. Bagged lines are heavy and high-volume, so this can account for a large share of a 1.6-point gap on its own.
- "Nobody edited the item master" is the problem, not reassurance. The master stayed at 60 while the physical pallet changed.

**Why the expensive explanations fit worse**

| Explanation | What argues against it |
|---|---|
| Self-checkout | The three southern stores with self-checkout since May are at 1.4%, which is normal. It also wouldn't concentrate losses in 20–40 kg bags. |
| New security contractor | Eight stores switched and only five are northern. The other three are presumably at normal shrink, so confirm that. The July 1 switch also doesn't explain why one category dominates. |
| Organised power-tool theft | Tools are only 7% of the excess, and four of six stores are within 2% of book. The bulletin is real but explains a small slice at most. |
| Paint and "everything else" (25%) | This is unexplained. It may be normal noise, or a smaller second issue such as contractor coverage. Handle it separately and after the pallet issue is fixed. |

**What to check first (days, nearly free)**

1. **Physically count a Greenfold pallet at Store 14 and one other northern store.** Confirm 50 bags, not 60. Check that every landscape SKU is 50 and not, say, 40 for the heaviest bags.
2. **Compare Greenfold delivery notes and invoices with what the system booked.** If you were invoiced for 50 per pallet and booked 60, that confirms it and gives you the exact size of the error.
3. **Rebuild expected shrink.** Take Greenfold pallets received since July 8 multiplied by 10 bags multiplied by unit cost. Compare that with the 68% slice, which is about 1.2 points of sales. If it covers most of it, you have your answer. If it covers little, reopen the question.
4. **Check the timeline.** Landscape variance should start in July, not May. Look at it by week and by store, with the smallest Greenfold volume showing the smallest gap.
5. **Check whether any northern store still gets landscape from the DC.** Its landscape shrink should be normal.
6. **Check the three non-northern contractor stores and the three southern self-checkout stores for landscape and tool variance.** They should look normal.

**Likely actions once confirmed**

- Set Greenfold items at the actual pallet quantity. Give them their own item records or supplier-specific pack sizes, so DC and Greenfold packs can't be confused.
- Where possible, receive by counted or invoiced quantity rather than by pallet label for direct-delivery lines.
- Adjust the Q3 book stock. Much of the 3.1% is a bookkeeping loss rather than missing product, so report it as a correction and not as theft. Check whether the stock write-off and any supplier claims were handled correctly.
- Review other direct-delivery suppliers for the same pack-size mismatch.

**On the two requests**

Hold both until the Greenfold count is done. If the pallets are 50 and the variance reconciles, neither is needed to explain the 3.1%. Keep a smaller watch on the residual: after correcting the pallet quantity, if the northern six are still above 1.5%, that remainder is where the contractor and tool-theft questions belong. That is a much smaller problem than the £180k combined you've been asked to approve.

**Confidence:** fairly high, around 80%, pending the physical pallet count. That count is the single test that will confirm or kill it.
