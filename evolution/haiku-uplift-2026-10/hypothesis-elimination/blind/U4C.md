**Short answer:** the most likely driver is a receiving-data error on the Greenfold pallets, not theft. I wouldn't spend the £90k on either option yet.

**The Greenfold pallets**

Your receiving process books whatever the item master says a pallet holds, and the item master says 60 bags. Greenfold pallets are five layers of ten, so 50 bags. Every Greenfold pallet scanned in since July 8 books 10 bags that never arrived. Those bags then show up as missing at the next stock count. Nobody edited the item master, so nothing looks wrong in IT's audit. The change in supplier and pallet configuration is what broke the assumption.

The size of the effect fits. Your excess shrink in the six stores is about 1.7 points of sales (3.1% against roughly 1.4%). Bagged landscape is 68% of that, or about 1.15 points. A 10-in-60 overbooking is a 20% overstatement of what actually arrived, and that applies only to the Greenfold stock. Heavy bagged lines with high unit values and big volumes in Q3 could easily account for that much. I'd confirm the arithmetic with real receipt volumes rather than rely on my estimate.

**Why the other explanations fit worse**

| Explanation | Evidence against it |
|---|---|
| Self-checkout | Three southern stores have had it since May and sit at 1.4%. The six northern stores were at normal levels in the earlier months of the same period, if you have monthly data (check). Self-checkout losses also would not concentrate 68% in 20–40 kg bags. |
| New security contractor | Three of the eight switched stores are outside the north and show normal shrink. It also doesn't explain the product mix. Thieves don't favour 40 kg bags of gravel. |
| Organised power-tool theft | Power tools are only 7% of the excess, and four of the six stores are within 2% of book on tools. |
| Paint and "everything else" (25% of the excess) | Not explained by the above. This may be ordinary noise, or a smaller second issue. Leave it until the landscape effect is removed and you can see what remains. |

I can't rule out genuine theft of landscape bags, which is easy to do at a side gate. But theft would not produce a loss that is this concentrated in one product family and one delivery route, starting on the date the route changed.

**What to check before spending anything**

1. **Count a Greenfold pallet.** Walk to Store 14's receiving area and count the bags on the next delivered pallet. If it's 50, the cause is found. This takes ten minutes and costs nothing.
2. **Compare receipts with delivery notes.** For July onward, compare units booked at the six stores with units on Greenfold's despatch notes and invoices. The gap should be about 10 per pallet.
3. **Check the timeline.** Pull weekly or monthly shrink on bagged landscape for the six stores. The step up should start the week of July 8, not May or July 1.
4. **Look for a control.** Find a northern store with the old contractor, or any store still receiving landscape lines from the distribution centre. If shrink there is normal, that supports the cause above and removes the contractor from suspicion.
5. **Check what Greenfold invoices.** If you are paying for 60 per pallet and receiving 50, you have a supplier billing problem as well as a stock-record problem. If you pay for 50, the book value is overstated but cash is fine.
6. **Review the other direct deliveries.** Check any other lines that moved to supplier delivery for the same mismatch.

**What this means for the decisions**

- Don't pull self-checkout from the six stores. The southern stores show it isn't the cause.
- Don't bring back the old security contractor on this evidence.
- If the pallet count confirms 50 per pallet, fix the item master or receive by actual count. Then adjust the inventory for the bags that were never there, so the shrink figure is restated rather than written off as loss. The restatement may also change the Q3 numbers you report.
- Keep an eye on power tools given the police bulletin, but the stock counts don't show a problem yet.

Once the landscape effect is stripped out, see what's left in the northern six. If shrink is still above the 1.3–1.5% range, that's the point to look at security or self-checkout in more detail, with a much smaller number to explain.
