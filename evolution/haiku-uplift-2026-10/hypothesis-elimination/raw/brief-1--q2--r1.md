The most likely explanation is pre-analytical: the blood is changing in the tube between the draw and the centrifuge, not the patients or the analyzer. The clearest sign is that the excess is concentrated where specimens sit longest, and the two ED patients who were sent in had normal potassium on repeat. This pattern is typical of pseudohyperkalemia, where potassium leaks out of red cells in unspun whole blood, especially when it is cold. I can't confirm this from the summary alone, so treat it as a strong hypothesis to test.

**Why the cold-hold and courier changes fit best**

- The 1 August cold-hold policy puts any specimen waiting more than 30 minutes in the clinic fridge. Refrigerated whole blood is a known cause of raised potassium.
- On 7 September, Ridgeway and Millbrook moved from your driver's 90-minute loop to one pickup a day at 3:30 pm. Specimens now wait hours, so many more of them end up in the fridge for a long time.
- Harbour is on the same contractor but spins its samples on site before pickup. Its rate is 5%, which fits: centrifuging early removes the problem even with a daily pickup.
- Ridgeway and Millbrook are 30% of outpatient volume but produced 83% of the high results (44% plus 39%). Those are the two sites the courier change hit.
- Morning draws sit longest before the 3:30 pm pickup. That fits 11% high before 11 am against about 1% after 1 pm.
- The timing is roughly consistent: the courier change came three days before the spike began. The August policy came five weeks earlier, so it may have been partly masked until the longer waits began.

**What doesn't fit the other explanations**

That is not over-representation, so the lot does not explain a jump to 6%. Quarantining it would also not fix the cause and would delay other testing.

Retraining him would be a weak response to this data. Also, the first high results appear after the 7 September courier change, not on his hire date.
- Hemolysis, calcium and sodium all look normal. That also argues against a sample integrity problem beyond the fridge and transit delay.
