I'm the asset manager for Ellery Crossing, a 140,000 sq ft neighbourhood shopping centre owned by Ashby Ridge Property Trust. Our anchor, Harrowfield Market, has 38,000 sq ft on a lease that expires next December. Sales there have slid for three years, and Harrowfield says it will renew only at 40% less rent.

I'd rather let Harrowfield go. Ironvale Fitness wants 22,000 sq ft of the box for a 24-hour gym and Sorrelwood Health wants the other 16,000 for an urgent-care and imaging clinic. Both have signed letters of intent, and together they'd pay about 30% more than Harrowfield pays today. They'll need more parking than a grocery store, but we have 610 stalls and the lot is rarely half full.

Some background. When we re-leased the centre in 2015, the leasing team filled our 26 inline shops in under a year by offering the usual grocery co-tenancy protections. Harrowfield also owns the fuel station on the pad at the front of the lot; it bought the pad outright in 2009, and the station shares our entrances and parking under the reciprocal easement recorded with that sale. Their loyalty card takes 10 cents a litre off fuel for every $100 spent in-store. Our mortgage is with Marchbank Life and runs to 2029; any lease over 15,000 sq ft needs their sign-off, which has always been routine.

The nice part is that we don't need anything from Harrowfield; their lease simply runs out. The investment committee meets November 20 and wants a recommendation, and I also need the inline tenants and the new operators on board. What should I recommend, and how do I bring everyone along?
