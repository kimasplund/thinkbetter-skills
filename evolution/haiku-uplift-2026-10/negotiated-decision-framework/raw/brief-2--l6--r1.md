**1. The east lateral closure is the weakest part of the draft.**
- The orchard growers are concentrated on the east lateral, so an August 1 shutoff lands hardest on the people who want priority, and it lands right in the post-harvest window they say matters most for next year's fruit buds.

Closing the lateral early may cut that seepage and could breach the agreement or put the payment at risk. Confirm the terms with counsel before you count that money as safe. - The 9% conveyance saving is real, but it has to be weighed against the recharge loss and the orchard impact. Get a number for the recharge effect before adopting the closure.

**2. Voting and conflicts need checking.**
- Any departure from equal shares needs a vote weighted by acreage owned, not acreage farmed. The Bennings sold their land to Quillon Land Partners and lease it back. Confirm who holds the voting acreage for those parcels. Quillon, not Neil Benning, may hold the votes, and that changes the math on any priority plan.

- A seven-member all-grower board is a conflict-of-interest risk on any allocation plan. Consider having the board adopt the framework and have district counsel review the voting and recusal rules.

**3. A structure both groups can accept.**
- Keep equal shares of 55% as the baseline. This honors the bylaws and gives the row-crop growers what they were promised.
- Add a defined orchard protection, for example a fixed post-harvest reserve or a tree-maintenance allocation for permanent crops, with a clear acreage cap and a clear trigger. Make it a formula, not a discretionary grant, so it is easy to defend and easy to verify.
- Fund the protection through the trading market rather than by taking water from row crops outright. Orchards could buy post-harvest water from row-crop growers who are fallowing, which turns the fallowing into a paid benefit for the row-crop side. Set a trading window and a price-reporting rule so the market is visible.
- If the lateral must close, phase it or move the closure date, and compensate the affected orchards through the reserve. Do not shut the lateral on August 1 without checking the Dunmere agreement and the orchard impact first.
- Hold a ratification vote with the acreage weighting disclosed and the conflicts recorded. Plans that change allocation rules tend to hold when the people who lose something see the rule was applied transparently.
