You can probably get to ten votes, but not with the plan as you've framed it. Three things in your own message could stop the vote or the sale after it passes. Your plan has to deal with those first, and the persuasion work comes second.

## Problems to fix before you count votes

**1. The Halloran gift may bind the sale.** The plaque reads "for the education of the children of Brennock". If the 1958 deed or gift instrument carries a restriction, a reverter or a charitable-purpose condition, then selling to a developer may need a court or the state attorney general's approval, or it may be barred outright. Vellacott's lawyers will find this in title review. Opponents will find it sooner, and a trustee who is nervous about liability only needs this one issue to vote no.
- Have school counsel pull the deed and the gift records now, before any more public steps.
- If there is a restriction, the plan should say how you will honour its purpose, not work around it. Some options are a named Halloran lower school on the south campus, a scholarship fund seeded from the proceeds for Brennock children, or a community-access arrangement. Check any of these with counsel first.
- Make sure the Vellacott letter of intent is conditional on clear title and any needed approvals. If it isn't, renegotiate that before February 12.

**2. The Ardelle Foundation has $800k left to pay on a north-campus program.** Read the pledge agreement. If the money is tied to that program or site, closing the north campus may let Ardelle stop paying or ask for a refund. Their view also matters beyond this pledge, because they are your biggest donor signal.
- Call them before the board vote and before it leaks. Ask whether the grant can move to a south-campus outdoor program. Treat the $800k as at risk in your budget until you have that in writing.
- You could offer the foundation a named role in the new building, with outdoor learning space in the design.

**3. The Sycamore Lane conditions are not just history.** Your capacity figure is 700, but you've told me the enrolment ceiling exists and not what it is. Check the actual number. Moving 230 more students takes the south campus to 640, which may be well under the ceiling or may not be. Also check the following:
- A new $8M building probably needs its own planning-board approval, and the neighbours will be present. The 2004 conditions are the starting point for that conversation: traffic marshals, the 9pm cutoff, and drop-off for 230 more children, many of them younger and arriving in cars.
- The neighbours are a decision-maker you haven't listed. Approach them early with a traffic and drop-off plan, and don't wait for them to find out through a notice. If they organise against you, the building timeline slips, and your financial case depends on that building.

## The sequencing problem

The board votes February 12. Contracts go out January 10 and are due March 1. So families will decide on re-enrolment while the closure is still open, and trustees will vote before you know how many families are staying.

Your budget assumes 92% return. At roughly $30k of revenue per student, each point is about six students and about $190k a year. If lower-school families react to a closure announcement by leaving, losing just a few of the 230 students wipes out a large share of the savings. In the worst case, you close a campus and the deficit is still there.

You have two ways to handle this:
- **Move the vote or split it.** Hold a first vote in February on a decision to proceed, conditional on clear title, Ardelle's response and planning feasibility, with a binding vote on the sale after March 1 re-enrolment data comes in. Vellacott may accept an extension of the LOI. Ask them.
- **Or keep February 12 and give the board explicit tripwires.** For example, if re-enrolment is below a stated threshold on March 1, the board reopens the decision before closing on the sale. Trustees can approve more easily when there is an exit.

Either way, tell families early and plainly, before January 10, what is being proposed and what is not decided yet. Families who hear it from the school keep their trust far longer than families who hear it from other parents.

## What each group needs

**Trustees (you need 10 of 15).** Count now. Do individual conversations with every trustee in the next three weeks and sort them into yes, leaning yes, undecided and no. Don't count on the vote going your way until you know which trustees would be moved by which issue. For skeptics, the strongest case rests on:
- The status quo is not safe. An 11% decline over five years with a $1.1M deficit is a path toward larger cuts, and the figure to show them is what the school looks like in 2030 if nothing changes.
- The sale money funds the building, and the remaining roughly $5.5M gives a cushion. Be exact about what it can and cannot be spent on, and have a board-approved rule on whether any of it goes to operating costs.
- Alternatives were looked at. Prepare a short comparison showing what you considered (a partial lease, selling part of the six acres, a phased move, a different buyer, raising tuition or fundraising more). Trustees vote more willingly for something when they can see the alternatives were tested and why they lost. A skeptic who asks "did we try X?" should have an answer ready.

**Lower-school parents.** Their two concerns are the commute and little kids sharing space with teenagers, and both can be answered with design, not just reassurance.
- Build the lower school as its own wing or building with a separate entrance, drop-off, playground and schedule, and a defined rule for when younger and older students mix. Show them the plan.
- Involve a parent group in the design brief, with real input on drop-off, bus routes and aftercare. Consider a shuttle or bus service for the first two years, since the nine kilometres is the practical obstacle for many families.
- Keep what they love about north. The outdoor learning program should have a south-campus home that is as good, with the same teachers.
- Offer families a way to commit with less risk, for example a guarantee that the building and program will be as promised or a tuition-lock for the first year.

**Teachers and staff.** Job worry is the thing that will spread fastest.
- Say as early as you can what the staffing plan is. If you can commit that no teaching role is cut because of the move (relying on attrition and redeployment), say so. If you can't, say what the process will be, how much notice, and what severance or placement help is offered. Vagueness will do more damage than bad news.
- Bring a few lower-school teachers into the design of the new building and the transition team. They will be believed by their colleagues in ways you will not.

**Vellacott.** They want the site and have signed an LOI, so you have some leverage. Use it to get a longer closing window, conditions that protect you on title and approvals, and possibly a leaseback or phased handover so north can run until fall 2028 without a gap.

## What the plan should contain

1. **Legal and approvals track (now):** deed review, Ardelle agreement review, Sycamore Lane conditions and ceiling, planning-board path, and LOI conditions.
2. **Board track (now to February 12):** one-on-one trustee conversations, an alternatives paper, a three-scenario financial model (92%, 85% and 80% re-enrolment) and a decision on whether to hold a conditional vote.
3. **Community track (before January 10):** a clear announcement, listening sessions at both campuses, a parent design group, a staff Q&A with written answers, and a plain account of what is decided and what is not.
4. **Neighbour track:** early contact with Sycamore Lane residents and a traffic and drop-off proposal.
5. **Donor track:** a conversation with Ardelle, and other major donors before they hear it elsewhere.
6. **Commitments package:** staffing approach, the lower-school design principles, transport support for the first years, and a plan to honour the Halloran purpose.
7. **Tripwires and review points:** the March 1 re-enrolment number, the date title and approvals are confirmed, and a board review before closing.

## How I'd read your position

You have a real financial case, and the capacity is there. What you don't yet have is proof that the sale is legally clean, that the money you're counting on will stay, and that families will stay through the change. Settle those three, and the vote becomes much easier to win and the plan much easier to carry out. If counsel finds a binding restriction in the Halloran gift, or Ardelle says the pledge ends with the north campus, you should find that out before February 12 and not after.

Where I have assumed things, such as the per-student revenue and the ceiling, please check them against your actual figures.
