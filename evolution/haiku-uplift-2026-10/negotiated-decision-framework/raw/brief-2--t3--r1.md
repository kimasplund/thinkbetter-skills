## Three things to check before you bring a plan to the board

1. **Who holds the acreage.** Any departure from equal shares needs a member vote weighted by acreage owned, not acreage farmed. The Bennings sold their land to Quillon last year and lease it back, so Quillon may own much of the acreage that votes. Neil Benning may not be the person whose votes count. Pull the current ownership records before you count votes. Also check whether Neil has a conflict of interest on a plan that affects land his company's lease depends on.

2. **The east lateral closure and Dunmere.** Closing the east lateral on August 1 saves about 9% in conveyance losses, but the same seepage recharges Dunmere's wells and funds the $40,000 a year. Cutting seepage early could breach or strain the 1997 agreement, and it would hit the orchards on the east lateral right when they need water. Check the agreement's terms and talk to Dunmere before you commit to the date.

3. **Whether the bylaws and the basin authority permit what you want.** Check whether the board can adopt trading rules and closure timing on its own, and whether the basin authority's 55% allocation can be delivered on a timing schedule instead of a flat cut.

## A plan that gives each group something real

**Keep equal base shares at 55%.** This matches the bylaws, so the row-crop growers and Orrin have no legal objection, and it avoids a weighted member vote.

**Make trading the main tool for orchard priority.** Open trading lets growers who fallow, such as Orrin, sell water they won't use to orchards that need it. Set up a simple district clearinghouse with a posted price range and a deadline for trades, so trades can happen before the post-harvest window. This turns Orrin's fallowing into a benefit for the orchards without changing anyone's entitlement.

**Protect a post-harvest window through scheduling, not allocation.** Ask the district operator to plan deliveries so east-lateral orchards get guaranteed water after harvest. This needs no change to shares and no vote, and it can be written into the plan as an operational rule.

**Move the east lateral closure off August 1, or make it conditional.** Options: close it after the orchard window ends, or close only the unlined segments with the highest losses. Tie any earlier closure to Dunmere's agreement, and if the 9% saving is needed, pair it with a plan that replaces the seepage for Dunmere's wells.

**Give the orchard growers a seat in the plan.** Brief them before March 18 and ask what post-harvest volume they need. Their agreement on the timing and the scheduling rule is more valuable than a larger share.

## How this should land

- **Orchard growers:** likely to accept and possibly endorse, since they get a protected post-harvest supply and a trading route.
- **Row-crop growers and Orrin:** likely to accept, since equal shares and open trading stay intact.
- **Neil Benning and Quillon:** depends on the ownership check. If Quillon holds the acreage, get its position in writing before the vote.
- **Dunmere:** needs to be consulted before the closure date is set.
