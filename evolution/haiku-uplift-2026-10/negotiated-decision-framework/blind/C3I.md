## Recommendation

Go ahead with planning to replace Harrowfield, but do not commit to it before the investment committee on November 20. Ask the committee for approval to pursue the Ironvale and Sorrelwood deals on a conditional basis, not a final decision. The case is strong on rent, but three things could make it much weaker than it looks: the fuel station easement, the inline co-tenancy protections, and the Marchbank consent.

## Why the numbers need a second look

- The 30% uplift is measured against what Harrowfield pays today. The real comparison is Harrowfield's renewal offer, which is 40% less than today. Against that offer, the two operators pay roughly 2.2 times as much (1.3 / 0.6). That gap is real, but it should be tested against the costs of the switch: downtime, free rent, tenant improvements to split one 38,000 sq ft box into two, and leasing commissions.
- Letters of intent are not leases. Ask how binding they are, what conditions they carry (permits, financing, parking studies, the easement), and when they expire.
- Check whether the 2015 rent roll and the new operators' rents are on comparable terms, including escalations and any percentage rent.

## Issues to resolve before November 20

1. **Fuel station and reciprocal easement (highest risk).** Harrowfield owns the pad, and the easement covers shared entrances and parking. Get counsel to read it for: any consent right over changes to parking, access, signage, or site layout; any operating covenant or use restriction that binds the centre if the anchor leaves; and any termination or relocation rights. A 24-hour gym and an urgent-care clinic may change the parking and traffic pattern the easement was written around. The fuel station also depends on the loyalty tie-in to the store. If Harrowfield stays on the pad after its lease ends, the fuel customers will keep using your entrances, and you may need a parking or cost-sharing agreement with them.
2. **Inline co-tenancy protections.** The 2015 leases were filled on grocery co-tenancy terms. If the anchor goes dark, or is replaced by a gym and clinic that do not meet the grocery definition, some or all of the 26 inline tenants may get rent reductions, kick-out rights, or both. Pull every inline lease, list the exact trigger language and the remedy, and model the rent loss if the tenants use those rights. This is the most likely source of hidden cost.
3. **Marchbank Life consent.** Both new leases exceed the 15,000 sq ft threshold, and the 16,000 sq ft clinic is only just over it. The consent has been routine before, but this is a change of use and a new tenant mix on a centre with a 2029 loan. Brief Marchbank early, before the committee, and ask whether the loan covenants (such as DSCR or occupancy tests) hold during the vacancy and the fit-out period.
4. **Parking.** The 610 stalls look ample, but the gym's 24-hour use and the clinic's appointment peaks are different from grocery trips. Commission a parking count at the peak hours of each new use, and add the fuel station's customers to the count.
5. **Downtime.** Harrowfield's lease ends next December. Work out the rent gap from the month the anchor goes dark until the new leases pay rent. Include the fit-out period for the gym and clinic.

## Plan for the November 20 committee

Present a recommendation with three options, each with its own numbers:
- **A. Let Harrowfield go and sign Ironvale and Sorrelwood**, conditional on the easement review, the co-tenancy analysis, and Marchbank consent.
- **B. Renew Harrowfield at a middle rent** (for example, 15 to 25 percent below today), which keeps the anchor and the fuel station's traffic flows.
- **C. Short-term extension with Harrowfield** (one to two years) while the new deals are finalised, which buys time without locking in the old terms.

Ask the committee to approve spending on counsel and a parking study now, and to authorise negotiations with the two operators toward leases that start after Harrowfield's lease ends.

## Bringing the others along

**Inline tenants (26 shops).** Talk to them before the anchor announcement, not after. Explain the plan, what the new operators will bring (24-hour gym, medical clinic, both likely to drive daytime and evening traffic), and the timing. Give them the co-tenancy analysis in summary, and be clear about any rights they have. Ask for their views on the new mix and on any changes to hours or signage. Offer a written update at each milestone.

**Ironvale and Sorrelwood.** Agree on a timeline for leases, fit-out, and opening. Ask them to share their parking needs and hours. Make their deals conditional on the same items as the committee plan, so no one is surprised later.

**Harrowfield.** Keep the conversation open. Their renewal offer is a negotiating position, and they may move. Be clear that you are planning for the end of the lease, and ask whether they want to discuss a shorter term or an exit with a fuel-station agreement. Do not let them learn about the new operators from a tenant.

**Fuel station.** Talk to whoever runs it, since they depend on the shared site and the loyalty tie-in. Agree in writing on access, parking, and signage before any change is announced.
