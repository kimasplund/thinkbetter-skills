The financial case is real, but four facts in your message could each stop the plan or sink the vote. I'd resolve them before you count trustees, because several could change what you are asking the board to approve.

## Four issues to settle first

**1. The Halloran gift.** "For the education of the children of Brennock" is on a plaque, and it may also be in the deed or gift instrument. Don't assume it is only sentiment.
- Have counsel read the original deed and any gift agreement now. Check for a restriction, a reverter or a right of enforcement. Also check whether the state attorney general must be notified or must approve a sale of charitable property.
- If a restriction exists, the path is usually court approval (cy pres or deviation) or a negotiated release with the heirs or the AG. That takes months and is public.
- Check whether Vellacott's letter of intent is contingent on clear title and on any approvals. If it is, a title problem could kill the $13.5M after trustees have voted.
- Reach out to the Halloran family early and personally, before they hear it from a neighbour or a reporter. Offer something real, such as a named space at the new building, a commitment to educational use of part of the land, or a conservation easement on some acreage. Treat their view as information, not as a hurdle.

**2. The Ardelle Foundation pledge.** $800k is still to come, and it is tied to a north-campus outdoor learning program. Closing the north campus may breach the grant terms or end the foundation's willingness to pay.
- Read the pledge agreement and call the foundation's program officer before the vote.
- Ask for a written agreement to redirect the remaining funds to an outdoor learning program on the south campus. Pay attention to whether the foundation prefers a restructured grant to a returned one.
- Until you have that, treat the $800k as at risk in your model.

**3. The Sycamore Lane conditions.** You describe the enrolment ceiling as something "we've never come close to". Consolidation takes you to 640 students on that site, plus any growth. Find the number in the planning approval.
- If the ceiling is below 640, or below 700, you need a variance or a modified approval. That means a public hearing with the same neighbours who fought the gym.
- A new $8M building is a separate approval, and its traffic, noise and construction impact will be argued against the existing conditions (marshals, the 9pm limit).
- Your consultant's 700-student figure is about physical capacity, not what is legally permitted. Ask for a land-use lawyer's opinion on both.
- Meet the neighbours before you file anything. They will be easier to work with if they hear a traffic plan and a construction plan from you first.

**4. The calendar doesn't work.** Re-enrolment contracts go out January 10, but the board doesn't vote until February 12, and families must commit by March 1. Families would be signing without knowing whether their child's campus will exist, and a 92% return assumption depends on that.
- Either announce the intended direction before the January 10 mailing, or send the contracts with clear, honest language about the pending decision. Don't mail contracts that quietly assume the outcome.
- Consider a short contingent window, such as a deposit that is refundable for a set time after the vote. Families who are undecided may then stay in rather than leave early.
- Run the budget at 92%, 88% and 84% return. North-campus families are the group most likely to leave, and 230 students is 36% of enrolment. If 15% of them go, that is about 35 students, roughly $1M in tuition at a $19M budget with 640 students (about $30k a head), which could wipe out much of the $1.1M deficit improvement. Use your real tuition and aid numbers.

## Winning the vote

You need 10 of 15, so you need to win about two-thirds of the room. The most useful approach is to learn what each trustee needs and then design the plan around it.
- **Count privately now.** Meet each trustee one on one. Sort them into supporters, persuadable and opposed. For the persuadable ones, ask what would make them vote yes. Then ask what would make them vote no, and what they could accept if it happened.
- **Find the real objections.** Likely ones are the Halloran legacy, fear of losing north families, financial risk, and reputation. Each needs a different answer. Legacy concerns need the legal and family work above. Risk concerns need numbers.
- **Show the alternatives.** Trustees will ask why closure rather than other options. Bring at least these, costed: (a) close north and sell, (b) keep north and cut costs, (c) sell part of the north site and keep a smaller early-years campus, (d) lease the north site rather than sell, (e) partner or merge. Having honestly looked at the others is what lets a trustee say yes with confidence.
- **Ask what the board will accept as proof.** For example: a clean title opinion, a signed Ardelle redirect, and a zoning path before the final vote. Making these conditions of closing gives hesitant trustees a way to support a decision that is not yet irreversible.
- **Consider a two-step vote.** Step one, on February 12, approves the direction and authorises negotiation, with the sale conditional on the items above. Step two, later, approves the closing. This makes ten votes easier and reduces your risk if title or zoning goes wrong.
- **Don't surprise anyone.** A trustee who first hears a problem in the meeting will vote no. Send the board the legal findings, the financial scenarios and the alternatives at least two weeks ahead.
- **Be honest about the $13.5M.** An LOI is not a contract. Ask Vellacott about its due-diligence period, financing, rezoning contingencies and walk-away rights. A buyer who needs rezoning for housing faces its own public process, which may give the neighbours a second fight.

## Keeping parents and staff

**Lower-school parents** (commute and little children with teenagers)
- Take both concerns seriously and build real answers into the design. Don't present them as a communications problem.
- Separate the lower school physically: its own entrance, drop-off, playground and schedule where possible, with limited shared spaces and supervised mixing. The $8M building makes this feasible, so show them floor plans and let a parent group review them.
- Offer transport. A bus or shuttle, extended care, and staggered start times can address the nine-kilometre commute.
- Form a parent design committee with real influence over the lower-school building and the transition, and tell them what they can and can't decide.
- Offer tuition protection or a transition guarantee, such as a fee freeze for the first year, for families who stay.

**Staff** (job fears)
- Say early what you do and don't know. If you can honestly commit that no one will lose their job because of the consolidation, say so. If you can't, give the process and timeline for any reductions.
- Offer defined options: relocation, role changes, priority hiring for new positions, retention bonuses through the transition, and severance and support for anyone affected.
- Involve teachers in designing the lower school and the transition. They know what will work.
- Brief staff before parents and before the press whenever possible. They should not read it in a newsletter.

**Everyone**
- Hold a first announcement meeting for each group, then town halls, Q&A, and a single page of facts that you update regularly.
- Give the reasons plainly: the deficit, enrolment, what happens if nothing changes, and what you considered. People accept bad news better when they can see the logic and have been heard.
- Tell north families what will be preserved. Examples include the outdoor learning program (relocated), the traditions of the lower school, and a commemoration of the Halloran gift.

## What the plan should include

1. **Legal workstream**: deed and gift review, AG or court path, title opinion, LOI terms, and a Halloran family engagement plan.
2. **Land-use workstream**: the actual ceiling and conditions, permit modification, neighbour outreach, traffic and construction plans, and a timeline for hearings.
3. **Donor workstream**: Ardelle redirect in writing, plus a check on other restricted gifts tied to north.
4. **Financial model**: scenarios at different return rates, one-time costs (building overruns, moving, severance), sale proceeds net of fees and taxes, and the effect on the operating deficit. An $8M building from $13.5M leaves $5.5M. Say what that is for: reserves, endowment, or deficit support. It should not cover recurring losses for long.
5. **Enrolment and marketing plan**: re-enrolment timing, retention tactics for north families, and a plan to attract new students.
6. **Program and facility design**: lower-school separation, safeguarding, and transport.
7. **People plan**: staffing, support, communications, and a timeline.
8. **Governance plan**: trustee one-on-ones, alternatives paper, a two-step vote, and decision criteria agreed ahead of time.
9. **Risk register and triggers**: for example, if the title fails, if the permit is denied, or if re-enrolment falls below a set level by March 1, what happens next. Decide the fallback now.
10. **Timeline**: work backwards from fall 2028. A new building plus permits and possible litigation can easily take longer than you expect. Check that the building could really be ready by then, and what you would do if it isn't, such as temporary classrooms.
