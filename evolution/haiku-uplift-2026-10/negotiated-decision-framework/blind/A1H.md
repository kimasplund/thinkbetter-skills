Winning the vote and keeping families and staff with you are linked. If the board votes on a plan that parents and teachers already trust, the vote is easier. If it votes on a sale the community sees as a done deal, you will fight every step afterward. Right now the timeline works against you: re-enrolment contracts go out January 10 and are due March 1, but the vote is February 12. Families would be asked to recommit before they know whether the north campus is closing. Fix that first.

**Check these before the vote**

1. **The plaque.** The 1958 gift was made "for the education of the children of Brennock." The deed may contain a restriction or reversion clause. Have counsel read the deed and any gift documents this month. If the Halloran family or their heirs could contest the sale, the opposition gets a strong argument, and a $13.5M letter of intent may not give clean title. Approach the Halloran family or their trustees early, before the community raises it for you.

2. **The Ardelle pledge.** Confirm whether the remaining $800k is restricted to the north-campus outdoor learning program. If it is, closing north may forfeit it or require the foundation's consent. Ask Ardelle in writing.

3. **The sale terms.** Find out what the letter of intent makes conditional (zoning, due diligence, financing, closing date). Check whether the buyer's timeline fits fall 2028 and what it means for moving costs.

4. **The numbers.** The deficit is $1.1M on a $19M budget. Sale proceeds minus the $8M building leave about $5.5M, but that is one-time money. It does not close an operating deficit unless consolidation cuts recurring costs. Build a five-year model that includes staff reductions, north-campus operating costs saved, tuition lost from families who leave, and the 8% re-enrolment shortfall the budget does not assume. Show the board what happens if re-enrolment comes in at 85% or 88%.

5. **The south campus and the 2004 conditions.** The facilities consultant says the campus can hold 700, but the planning approval also set traffic marshals, a 9pm event cutoff, and an enrolment ceiling. Adding 230 lower-school students and their families will change the traffic picture. Ask the planning office whether the existing conditions need an amendment and what a new permit would require. The Sycamore Lane neighbours will object first, so talk to them before a motion reaches the board.

6. **Does the $8M building need to happen now?** Ask whether the south campus can take K–5 in existing or modular space for the first years. That would reduce how much of the sale depends on a construction commitment.

**Building the vote**

Ten of fifteen is a high bar, so count trustees now. Mark each one as yes, no, or undecided, and write down what each undecided trustee needs to see: the deed answer, the Ardelle answer, the numbers, the neighbour plan, or the family and staff plan. Individual meetings before February 12 will do more than a contested floor debate.

Consider a conditional motion. The board authorizes negotiating the sale and the south-campus plan now, and final approval of the sale depends on:
- clean title and clearance of the 1958 gift terms,
- the Ardelle Foundation's written position on the pledge,
- confirmation or amendment of the south-campus planning conditions,
- re-enrolment at or above a stated threshold by a stated date.

Conditional approval is easier for undecided trustees to support, and it protects the school if one of these issues turns up late.

**Keeping parents and staff**

- **Tell families before January 10.** Explain the options, the reasons, and the date of the decision. Either send the re-enrolment letter with that context or hold it until the board has decided. Do not ask families to recommit to a campus that may close without telling them.
- **Lower-school parents.** The commute and the age mix are real concerns. Offer concrete answers: separate arrival and dismissal zones, a dedicated lower-school wing with its own entrance and hours, transport options from north-side neighbourhoods, and a transition plan with named contacts. A parent working group with real influence over those details will carry more weight than reassurance.
- **Teachers and staff.** Be specific. Say which roles are protected, which are at risk, and how severance, redeployment, and first claim on south-campus posts will work. Put it in writing before the vote. Uncertainty about jobs is what turns staff into opponents.
- **The history.** Some families will feel the gift is being broken. Raise it yourself. Acknowledge the promise made in 1958 and explain how the plan honours its purpose, if it does. If it does not, say so and explain the trade-off.

**Alternatives to put on the table**

- Phased consolidation: move K–2 first, then reassess in a year.
- Sell the north site but lease back part of it for the lower school for two or three years.
- Cut costs on both campuses first and show the board what that achieves.

Testing these alternatives makes the consolidation case stronger, because the board can see the other options were considered and why they fall short.

**What the plan should include**

- Written answers on the deed and the Ardelle pledge, before the vote.
- A five-year financial model with a re-enrolment sensitivity range.
- A traffic, planning, and neighbour plan for the south campus.
- Dated transition commitments for families and staff.
- A conditional motion with stated conditions.
- A timeline: January 10 family communication, February 12 vote, March 1 re-enrolment deadline, with a decision date families can see.

**Assumptions**

I do not know the deed's wording, the letter of intent's conditions, or the names and positions of the trustees. If the deed has a reversion clause, the plan changes substantially, so check that first.
