Short version: before you plan to win the vote, check four things in your own brief that could stop the plan after the board approves it. Each one can be settled before February 12, and each also changes how you should handle trustees, parents and staff. I'm assuming you're not a lawyer and that no counsel has reviewed these yet.

## Four issues to resolve before the vote

**The Halloran gift.** "For the education of the children of Brennock" on a plaque may be only sentiment. It may also be a deed restriction, a reverter clause or a charitable trust condition. If it is binding, selling to a developer could need a court's cy-près approval or the state attorney general's sign-off, and the Halloran heirs could object. Vellacott will find this in title work whatever you do.
- Get a title and gift-instrument review from counsel now.
- If there is a restriction, find out what a compliant sale looks like. That might be proceeds restricted to education, a court petition, or a negotiated release with the family.
- Make the LOI explicitly contingent on clear title and any needed approvals.
- Do not tell trustees or families the sale is "clean" until counsel says so.

**The Ardelle pledge.** The $800k still to come was pledged for the north-campus outdoor learning program. If the north campus closes, the foundation may be entitled to stop paying, and may ask for money back. Your budget probably counts on this money.
- Read the pledge agreement.
- Talk to the foundation before the vote, not after. Ask whether they would redirect the grant to a relocated program on the south campus.
- In your financial model, treat the $800k as at risk until they confirm in writing. Show trustees the downside case.

**The Sycamore Lane conditions.** You say the enrolment ceiling has "never come close." That is true at 410 students on that site. Consolidation puts all 640 there, and more if you grow. The facilities consultant says the campus can hold 700, but a physical capacity figure is not the same as the legal ceiling in the 2004 approval.
- Find the exact ceiling number and wording. If it is below 640, or below your growth plan, you need a planning-board modification.
- A new $8M building almost certainly needs approvals anyway. That reopens the whole 2004 fight, with neighbours who already hold conditions on traffic and evening events.
- Doubling the number of students at drop-off makes the traffic marshal condition a real operating problem. Have a traffic plan before you meet the neighbours.
- Budget time and cost for the approval process, and treat approval as a risk to the fall 2028 date.

**The numbers.** The sale price, the $8M building and "breathing room" need a fuller model before the vote.
- Consolidation savings: which operating costs actually go away (a campus, some staff, buses) and which do not.
- Transition costs: construction overruns, temporary space during the build, severance, transport, legal and approval costs.
- Cash timing: when the $13.5M arrives versus when you pay for the building, and what happens if the sale closes but approvals stall.
- Enrolment loss from the change itself. Your budget assumes 92% re-enrolment. Closing a campus usually drives some departures, and the lower-school families are the likely ones. Run 88% and 85% cases and show what each does to the deficit.
- Whether the $1.1M deficit is structural. If enrolment keeps falling, consolidation helps but doesn't finish the job.

## The calendar problem

Your dates are in an awkward order.
- January 10: re-enrolment contracts go out.
- February 12: the board votes.
- March 1: contracts are due.

Families will be deciding whether to re-enrol before the board has decided, and possibly while rumours run. Parents of north-campus children will be the most uncertain, and a family that leaves in the gap costs you tuition you assumed you had. Options:
- Announce the proposal and its timeline to all families before the contracts go out, so nobody feels ambushed.
- Add a short, clear note to the contract packet saying a decision is pending and when it will be made.
- Consider a re-enrolment window that extends past February 12, or a no-penalty withdrawal option for a few weeks after the vote. That costs some certainty but may keep families who would otherwise leave.
- Track deposits weekly from January 10 and agree in advance what number triggers a rethink.

Also consider whether a fall 2028 date gives you any slack. If title, planning approval and construction all have to finish first, one more year of overlap may be safer than a closure that happens before the new building does.

## How to win the vote, and what that should mean

You need 10 of 15, which is two-thirds. That means you need broad conviction, not a bare majority, and a split board will lose you the community anyway.

- **Count trustees now.** Talk to each one individually before any meeting. Sort them into supporters, persuadable and opposed, and find out what each one cares about (fiduciary risk, legacy, the Halloran link, local ties, lower-school families).
- **Don't present one option.** Show trustees two or three alternatives, with the same level of financial detail for each:
  1. Close north and consolidate (your plan).
  2. Keep both campuses and cut costs. Include a realistic estimate of what that does to the deficit.
  3. Lease, not sell, the north site, or sell part of it and keep the outdoor program.
  4. Phase the move, for example moving some grades first.

  If your plan still wins on a fair comparison, you will have the evidence you need. If it doesn't, you want to know before February 12.
- **Bring the risks yourself.** Bring the deed question, the Ardelle pledge and the planning conditions to the board with your proposed handling of each. A trustee who learns about them from someone else will lose trust in the whole proposal.
- **Use a committee.** Have a small group of trustees (including one sceptic) test the model and the legal advice, and report to the full board.
- **Offer conditional approval.** Trustees who are uneasy may support a resolution that approves the sale "subject to" title clearance, planning approval, Ardelle confirmation and a minimum re-enrolment figure. That gets you the 10 votes without asking anyone to ignore the risks, and it protects the school if a condition fails.
- **Check conflicts and procedure.** Make sure no trustee has a link to Vellacott, and follow your bylaws on notice, quorum and recusal. A vote that can be challenged is worth little.

## Keeping parents and staff with you

**Lower-school parents.** They have two specific worries, commute and young children sharing a campus with teenagers. Answer with concrete plans, not reassurance.
- Safety and separation: a separate lower-school building (which your $8M would fund), its own entrance, drop-off and play areas, separate timings for breaks and pick-up, and supervision rules. Show the plan, and let a parents' group review it.
- Commute: bus routes or a subsidy, extended before-care and after-care, staggered start times. Be honest about what the extra nine kilometres means for a five-year-old.
- Involve them: invite a group of north-campus parents into the design of the lower school and the transition. A parent who helped design something is less likely to leave over it.
- Keep what they value: the outdoor learning program, traditions, and the north-campus community identity, moved or recreated at the south campus.

**Staff.** Job fear is the main thing to address, and rumours will move faster than you do.
- Tell staff early and in person, before the news reaches the press or parents.
- Say what you know and what you don't. If there will be fewer positions, say so with a rough number and a timeline. If you can guarantee that most roles move, say that.
- Commit to a clear process for roles, with notice, severance, and help finding other posts where positions end.
- Prioritise redeployment, and consider a hiring freeze now so you can absorb departures through attrition.
- Give lower-school teachers a real part in designing the new building and schedule.

**The neighbours and the wider town.** Meet Sycamore Lane residents early, before any filing, with a traffic plan and a clear picture of what stays the same. Be ready for local press and for questions about what Vellacott will build on a site given "for the education of the children of Brennock." A public commitment about the legacy, such as a plaque, a naming, a community use or a portion of the proceeds for education, will matter to the town and to the Halloran family.

## What the plan should include

1. **Decision memo for the board**: options compared, the full financial model with downside cases, risk register, and the conditions for approval.
2. **Legal workstream**: Halloran title and gift review, any court or attorney-general process, LOI contingencies, and Ardelle agreement review.
3. **Planning workstream**: the exact 2004 conditions, a modification application if needed, a traffic plan, and approvals for the new building.
4. **Finance workstream**: sale proceeds and timing, building cost with contingency, bridge financing if the cash doesn't line up, and the Ardelle outcome.
5. **Enrolment plan**: weekly tracking from January 10, scenario triggers, retention outreach to north-campus families, and a decision rule for what you do if re-enrolment falls below 92%.
6. **Communications plan**: who hears what and when (trustees, then staff, then parents, then neighbours and press), with a single FAQ and a named contact.
7. **People plan**: transition support, redeployment, severance terms and a timeline for staff decisions.
8. **Transition plan for students**: the lower-school building and separation design, transport, and a staged move.
9. **Fallback options**: what you do if title, planning or Ardelle falls through, for example delaying a year, a lease instead of a sale, or a smaller version of the plan.

## A rough timeline

- **Now to mid-November**: counsel reviews the gift and LOI, read the Ardelle pledge and the 2004 conditions, call the foundation, build the downside financial models.
- **Mid-November to December**: individual trustee conversations, a small trustee review group, early briefings for staff leaders, and a first parent information session.
- **Before January 10**: announce the proposal, the process and the decision date to all families and staff. Send contracts with a clear note and any flexibility on withdrawal.
- **January**: parent and staff listening sessions, neighbour meetings, publish answers to common questions, and watch re-enrolment weekly.
- **Early February**: final board package with options, risks and conditions. Circulate it well ahead of the meeting.
- **February 12**: vote on a resolution with conditions.
- **After**: complete the legal and planning steps, report to families before March 1, and keep the community updated through construction.

## The main point

Your plan may well be the right one, and the financial case for acting is real. But on what you've told me, the sale depends on a gift restriction you haven't checked, a pledge that may lapse, and an enrolment ceiling you haven't read against 640 students, and your re-enrolment dates run ahead of your vote. If you settle those first and put the facts and alternatives in front of trustees, you are far more likely to get ten votes, and much less likely to see the plan stall after you win them.
