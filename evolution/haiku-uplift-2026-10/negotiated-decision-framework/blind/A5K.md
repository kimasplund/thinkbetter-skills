Winning this vote depends less on persuading people than on fixing three problems in the current timeline before February 12. Some of them could sink the sale regardless of how the trustees vote.

## Problems to resolve before the vote

**1. The Halloran gift may restrict the sale.** The plaque says the land was given "for the education of the children of Brennock." That may be a plain statement of purpose, or it may be a deed condition, such as a reverter clause that returns the land to the family if it stops being used for education. Get the original deed and the 1958 gift instrument, and have counsel confirm in writing whether you can sell and what the Halloran family or their heirs could do about it. Vellacott's letter of intent will probably have a title or due-diligence contingency, so this has to be settled first.

**2. The Ardelle money may not follow the north campus.** The $2M pledge is for the north-campus outdoor learning program. Check whether the grant agreement names that site, requires the program to stay there, or allows reallocation. Ask the Foundation directly what happens to the remaining $800k if the campus closes. Don't count it in the plan until you have an answer.

**3. The re-enrolment timing works against you.** Contracts go out January 10 and are due March 1, but the board votes February 12. Families will be asked to recommit to a school whose future isn't decided yet, and the vote will happen before you know whether 92% of families return. Either move the vote until after March 1, or give families the decision (or a clear "we are deciding between X and Y by date Z") before they sign. Otherwise the 92% assumption is unreliable, and so is the vote's stated basis.

## Check the numbers the case rests on

- **Capacity.** The consultant says 700, but the 2004 planning approval carried an enrolment ceiling. Find that number. If it is below 700, the case changes. Also confirm the traffic marshal and 9pm event conditions still hold for 640 students plus the lower school's drop-off and pick-up pattern. Do the traffic count before the vote, not after.
- **The deficit.** The $1.1M operating deficit is a structural problem. Selling the north campus pays for an $8M building, but it doesn't close the gap unless the consolidated school spends less or enrols more. Show a five-year operating model with and without the sale, including the new building's debt service, operating costs, and the one-time costs of the move (transport, severance, transition). Remember the sale proceeds are $13.5M minus the $8M building, and the rest may be needed for debt, reserves, or endowment rules.
- **Enrolment.** An 11% decline over five years needs an explanation. If the commute and the mixed-age campus are the main reasons families leave or hesitate, consolidation may make the decline worse. Get an independent enrolment projection, not just the internal one.
- **Vote count.** Ten of fifteen is two-thirds. Count the votes: list each trustee, their position, what they need, and who has a conflict. Only then decide whether the vote can pass.

## What the plan should include

1. **Hold the vote on a credible date.** Move it after March 1 if you can, or set it as a decision on a plan that will be published before it. Don't go to the vote with the Halloran and Ardelle questions open.
2. **Build the alternatives into the same document.** Trustees will vote more confidently if they see the sale compared with the real options: cost reductions, tuition and aid changes, enrolment marketing, leasing part of the north site, a shared-use or partner arrangement, or a phased consolidation. Show what each one does to the deficit and to enrolment.
3. **Engage the planning board early.** Ask whether consolidating 640 students onto the south campus needs an amendment to the 2004 approval. Sycamore Lane neighbours fought the gym, so they will probably notice. A conversation before the vote is better than a complaint after it.
4. **Make commitments to lower-school families concrete.** Offer a transport plan (buses, a drop-off schedule), a separate lower-school area or schedule, and a clear timeline. Name what changes for K-5 children day to day, and what safeguards the school will keep. Listening sessions help, but only if feedback changes something.
5. **Treat staff differently from parents.** Teachers worried about jobs need a direct answer: how many positions are affected, what retraining or placement is available, and what the consolidated staffing model is. Hold them to the same timeline as families, and don't leave them to hear about it second-hand.
6. **Disclose conflicts and the Vellacott offer's terms.** Confirm that no trustee has a relationship with Vellacott Homes, and publish the LOI's key conditions (price, contingencies, timing, any development rights).

## Assumptions

I don't know your state's rules on nonprofit property sales or charitable trusts, the exact wording of the Halloran deed, the Ardelle grant terms, the 2004 approval conditions, or the Vellacott LOI. The points above assume those documents may contain restrictions that aren't in your brief. Have a lawyer who knows independent school and charitable property review them. If the answer is that the sale is clean, the plan above still makes a stronger case. If it isn't, you need to know that before February 12.
