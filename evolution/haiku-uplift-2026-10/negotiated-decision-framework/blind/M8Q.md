The plan can work, but not on the current timeline or on the assumption that space is the only obstacle. Before you try to win the February 12 vote, three things need checking, because each could sink the sale or the enrolment plan.

**1. Check the north campus title before anything else.**
The plaque says the land was given "for the education of the children of Brennock." That may be only a sentiment, or it may be a deed restriction or reverter clause. If it is a restriction, selling the site for a non-educational purpose could be challenged, or the Halloran family's heirs could have a claim. Get a title search and a lawyer's written opinion on the gift terms before the board votes. Also check whether the Vellacott offer is conditional on clean title, and whether the Ardelle Foundation's grant agreement ties the $800k balance to the north-campus outdoor program. If it does, closing the campus may cost you that money.

**2. Confirm the south campus can legally take the load.**
The consultant's figure of 700 students is a physical capacity estimate, not a permitted one. The 2004 approval set an enrolment ceiling, and you have never approached it, but the ceiling is the number that counts. Consolidating means roughly 870 students (230 + 410 + 230 growth-free), before any new lower-school building. Read the 2004 conditions and the current zoning. You will probably need a planning amendment, and the Sycamore Lane neighbours will know the history. Assume they will object, and ask for a traffic study and drop-off plan that covers the added lower-school traffic before you go public. A $8M building may also need its own approval.

**3. Re-examine the financial case.**
- The $13.5M is a letter of intent, not a closing. Ask for the purchase terms, the due-diligence period, and the deposit.
- Net proceeds after the costs of closing, moving, and the $8M building need to be in a budget table. Show the board what the deficit looks like with and without the sale, and what the endowment or reserves look like over five years.
- The $1.1M deficit is about 5.8% of budget. Ask whether cost cuts, a fundraising target, or a tuition change could close it without a sale. The board should see the no-sale alternative as a real option, not a straw man.
- Your budget assumes 92% re-enrolment. Every 1% drop costs about $190k in tuition if tuition averages roughly $19M divided by 640 students, about $30k each, and a 92% rate means about 51 families leaving. Run a scenario at 85% and 80%, because a consolidation announcement will likely cut retention.

**4. Sequence the decision around the re-enrolment deadline.**
Contracts go out January 10 and are due March 1, but the vote is February 12. You will be voting before you know your retention. Announcing a closure while families are deciding whether to re-enrol risks losing exactly the families you need. Options:
- Move the vote to after March 1, if the Vellacott LOI and the Ardelle grant allow it.
- Or hold the vote on February 12 but make the sale conditional on the results of the planning amendment and on re-enrolment at or above a set threshold.
- Or announce the consultation now and the decision later, without naming a closure date until the numbers are in.

**5. Count the votes now.**
Ten of fifteen trustees is two-thirds, which may be a bylaw requirement for selling real property. Check the bylaws, then do a one-on-one canvass of every trustee. Put the trustees' concerns (title, neighbours, staff, the Ardelle money) in a written list and answer each one in writing before February 12. Get the Halloran family's view, if they are reachable, through the lawyer rather than the press.

**6. Keep parents and staff with you.**
- Lower-school parents: name a specific commute plan (shuttles, a drop-off window, a sibling policy), not a general promise. Offer a family forum and publish the planning timeline.
- Concerns about little children on a campus with teenagers: propose a physical separation plan (separate entrances, schedules, a lower-school courtyard, a supervised transition) and show what it looks like. Ask the consultant to certify it.
- Teachers: state in writing what happens to jobs, with a transition plan, retraining, and a hiring commitment if the sale closes. Vague reassurance will not hold. Offer a staff working group that can see the budget.
- Do not hide the financial problem. Enrolment is down 11% and the deficit is real. Parents and staff accept a hard decision they understand more easily than one they suspect is being hidden.

**7. What the plan should include.**
1. A one-page decision memo with the deficit, the sale proceeds, and the no-sale alternative.
2. A title and legal opinion on the Halloran gift, plus the Ardelle grant terms.
3. A planning and traffic assessment for the south campus at projected enrolment.
4. A retention scenario analysis at 92%, 85%, and 80%.
5. A transition and staffing plan, and a commute plan for lower-school families.
6. A timeline that puts the vote after you know the retention outcome, or makes the sale conditional on it.
7. A trustee briefing and a parent and staff consultation calendar, starting now.

**Assumptions I made:** I have not seen the bylaws, the 2004 approval conditions, the Vellacott LOI, the Ardelle agreement, or the Halloran deed. The advice above depends on those documents. If the deed has no restriction and the Ardelle money is not tied to the campus, the sale case is considerably stronger. If either one is tied, the plan needs a different structure before the vote.
