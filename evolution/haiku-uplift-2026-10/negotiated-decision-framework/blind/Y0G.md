## Recommendation

Take the Ironvale and Sorrelwood deal to the November 20 committee as the preferred direction, but ask for approval conditional on three things being checked first: the inline co-tenancy terms, the reciprocal easement, and Marchbank's sign-off. Don't recommend letting Harrowfield go until those are clear, because the easement and the inline leases may make the exit more expensive than the rent gain suggests.

## Who is involved and what each one needs

| Party | What they want | What could stop them | What would bring them in |
|-------|----------------|----------------------|--------------------------|
| Ashby Ridge Property Trust (owner) | Higher, durable income from the anchor box | Downtime between leases, co-tenancy rent cuts, costly easement disputes | A clear timeline, the rent uplift modelled net of downtime and any inline rent relief |
| Marchbank Life (lender) | Covenant protection and a stable centre through 2029 | A large lease change they hear about late | Early briefing, with both new leases packaged for their sign-off (each is over 15,000 sq ft) |

| Ironvale Fitness (22,000 sq ft) | A ready, well-located box with workable parking and hours | Delays in delivery, or conditions on access and parking | Firm dates, a clear parking plan, and a signed lease |
| Sorrelwood Health (16,000 sq ft) | Space for an urgent-care and imaging clinic | Parking and access for patients, fit-out timing | Same as Ironvale, plus confirmation that access routes work for clinical traffic |

## What to check before November 20

1. **Inline co-tenancy clauses.** The 2015 leases were built on the grocery protections, so pull every one of the 26 leases and list what each says happens if the anchor closes or goes dark. This is the most likely place for the deal to lose money, and it decides how much rent relief, if any, to budget.
2. **The reciprocal easement recorded with the 2009 pad sale.** Confirm what it says about shared entrances, parking ratios, maintenance cost-sharing, and any consent needed to change access or parking. The fuel station shares your lot, so a gym and clinic may change the parking picture for it, and that could give Harrowfield leverage.
3. **Fuel station and loyalty traffic.** Harrowfield's loyalty card discounts fuel for in-store spend. Once Harrowfield leaves, that stream of cars stops, and the fuel operator's position on the pad may change. Ask the fuel operator directly what they plan to do.
4. **Parking demand.** Gym and clinic peaks (early mornings, evenings, clinic hours) differ from a grocery pattern, so test the 610 stalls against the combined peak, including the fuel station's use, rather than against average occupancy.
5. **Marchbank.** Ask for an early read on what sign-off they will need, so the two leases are not held up in December.

## How to bring everyone along

- **Harrowfield first, and privately.** They hold the easement and the fuel station, so the approach should be made before anyone else hears. Offer the 40% reduced renewal as a real option, and ask whether a shorter term, or a deal that trades their fuel-station access for a clear parking plan, would suit them better. A quiet, documented exit with a fair timeline lowers the risk of them contesting the easement.
- **Marchbank in parallel.** Brief them with the numbers and the co-tenancy analysis before the committee meets, so their sign-off is a formality, not a surprise.
- **Inline tenants next.** Tell them what is happening, what replaces the grocery draw (a 24-hour gym and a clinic bring different daytime and evening traffic), and what their rent position will be. Where a co-tenancy clause would be triggered, offer a negotiated waiver or a temporary rent step in exchange for a term extension. Tenants who feel consulted are more likely to stay.
- **New operators as a package.** Ironvale and Sorrelwood both need to see the same parking plan, access routes and delivery dates. Ask each to give a commitment on hours and parking so neither can use the other's concerns to push for better terms.
- **Committee on November 20.** Present a conditional recommendation: approve the new-operator deal in principle, with the remaining checks and sign-offs listed as conditions, and a fallback of renewing Harrowfield at the reduced rent if the co-tenancy or easement findings make the exit uneconomic.

## Key assumptions

- The inline leases contain grocery co-tenancy protections of the usual kind. The brief does not say what they contain, so this needs to be confirmed.
- The reciprocal easement gives Harrowfield's fuel station rights to the shared entrances and parking. The exact terms are unknown.
- The 30% rent uplift is gross and does not yet deduct downtime, fit-out incentives, or any inline rent relief.
- Harrowfield's 40% reduction is an offer for renewal, and it is not yet clear whether it can be used as a bargaining position.
