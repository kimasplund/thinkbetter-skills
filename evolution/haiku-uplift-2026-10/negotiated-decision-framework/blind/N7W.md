## Recommendation

Take the Ironvale and Sorrelwood plan to the November 20 committee, but ask for approval subject to conditions, not an unconditional decision to let Harrowfield go. The economics are attractive: about 30% more rent than Harrowfield pays today, from two tenants with signed letters of intent, in a centre where you have 610 stalls and rarely more than half are in use. The risk is not the plan itself. It is that you have not yet checked three things that the note treats as settled: Harrowfield's rights under the reciprocal easement, the grocery co-tenancy protections in the 26 inline leases, and the timing of the gap between Harrowfield leaving and the new operators opening.

Two points in the brief worry me most:

1. **Harrowfield has more leverage than "their lease simply runs out" suggests.** They own the fuel pad, and the easement shares your entrances and parking with it. Their loyalty card drives fuel sales and in-store spend, so they will feel the loss and may contest changes to access or parking. Before November 20, someone needs to read the easement for any consent, access, parking, relocation, or termination terms, and for anything that restricts changes to the parking field or entrances. The new operators' plans depend on that.

2. **Inline co-tenancy exposure.** Grocery co-tenancy clauses commonly let inline tenants pay reduced rent, or leave, if an anchor closes. Those clauses are the largest cash risk in this plan. Pull the 26 inline leases and quantify what happens to rent and termination rights if Harrowfield goes in December. That number belongs in the committee paper.

## Timing

If "next December" means December 2026, the lease ends roughly six to eight weeks after the committee meets. Gym and clinic fit-outs usually take several months, and the new leases will need permits. Unless the new operators can open the day Harrowfield leaves, there will be a dark period in a 38,000 sq ft box and the inline tenants will feel it. If the lease actually ends in December 2027, the timing is much easier, and the rest of this answer still applies.

## What to put in front of the committee

- The base case: Harrowfield not renewed, box split 22,000 / 16,000 sq ft, with the 30% rent uplift.
- Conditions to clear before the leases are signed:
  - Easement review completed, with any consent, access, or parking rights mapped against the new uses.
  - Co-tenancy exposure quantified and a plan agreed with the inline tenants (see below).
  - Marchbank consent in principle. Both leases exceed the 15,000 sq ft threshold, so the sign-off is required for each. It has been routine before, but ask now, not after signing.
  - Operators' leases conditioned on a delivery date and on the permits and zoning for a 24-hour gym and a medical clinic at this location.
- A fallback if the timing gap is real: offer Harrowfield a short bridge renewal at their proposed 40% lower rent, or a smaller space in the centre, so that you are not forced into a dark box. Pay for the bridge only if it buys enough time to keep the new leases on schedule.
- A decision date for the Harrowfield conversation, so the committee knows when the conditions must be cleared or the plan is reconsidered.

## Bringing everyone along

**Harrowfield first, and early.** Do not let them learn of the decision from the inline tenants or the press. Tell them directly, with the dates, and make it clear you want an orderly exit. Their fuel station is a real asset to them. Ask what they need from the easement to keep the station running after they leave. Offering to settle the easement terms on fair terms costs far less than a dispute over parking and access. Their renewal offer at 40% less is a starting point, not the end of the negotiation.

**Inline tenants next, before anyone else hears.** Their main concern will be footfall and whether their co-tenancy rights hold. Explain that the new uses bring different traffic: a 24-hour gym brings steady evening and early-morning visits, and a clinic brings weekday daytime visits. Neither is a grocery trip, but both are regular customers. Offer each co-tenancy holder a specific plan, such as a marketing contribution, a timeline with dates, or a temporary rent concession, in exchange for a written waiver for the transition period. Tenants who see a plan are far more likely to waive than tenants who hear about rent cuts later.

**Operators.** Sorrelwood and Ironvale have signed letters of intent, not leases. Use that to set conditions: delivery dates, permit responsibilities, hours, signage, and parking allocation. Share the easement findings with them once you have them, because their parking use is the thing most likely to cause friction with Harrowfield and with your inline tenants. Ask each of them for a commitment on hours that fits the centre's shared parking.

**Marchbank.** Brief them before the committee so the consent is a formality, not a surprise.

## Assumptions

- "Next December" means the upcoming December, but the timing answer changes if it does not.
- The inline leases contain grocery co-tenancy protections of the usual kind. I have not read them.
- The reciprocal easement gives Harrowfield rights over shared entrances and parking that could affect the new uses. I have not read it.
- The 30% uplift is measured on the combined rent of both new operators compared with what Harrowfield pays today, and is net of any incentives or fit-out contributions.
