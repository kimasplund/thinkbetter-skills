The plan can likely pass without changing equal shares, which is the part that makes the vote winnable. The change is in how the orchards get their post-harvest water, and in the east lateral date.

**What the two camps actually need**

- Row-crop growers need the bylaw promise kept: equal entitlement, no acreage-weighted rule that favors the big farms.
- Orchard growers need water in the window after harvest, when fruit buds for next year are set. Your August 1 closure lands right on that window, so it is the single most likely point of the orchards' objection.
- Orrin Castellane is expecting to fallow about half his hay. That means he will have unused entitlement this season, and open trading is worth real money to him. He has a reason to want water to move, and to sell it.

**Proposed plan**

1. Keep equal shares at 55% of normal entitlement for every farm. This keeps the plan inside the bylaws and avoids a member vote weighted by acreage.
2. Make trading the route to orchard priority rather than a change to shares. Any grower who fallows land can sell that entitlement to orchards, at a price they agree on, with delivery scheduled for the post-harvest window. Put the rules in writing: a minimum notice period, a price-reporting requirement, and a rule that water sold cannot be resold.
3. Move the east lateral closure from August 1 to after the post-harvest window, for example October 1, or at least after the orchards' last irrigation for bud set. You lose part of the roughly 9% conveyance saving for those weeks. Ask the engineer whether a partial closure or a shorter closure gets most of the saving without cutting orchard deliveries. If the saving is worth more than the orchard risk, say so in the vote; the board should not hide that trade.
4. Give the orchards a written guarantee on the post-harvest delivery, backed by the trading rule above, so they have something firmer than goodwill.
