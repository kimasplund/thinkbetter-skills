Notes (J-B, brief-3)

- P4Y: Catches co-tenancy, the easement, the loyalty-fuel link, lender covenants, renewal-option risk, exclusives and a hybrid option. Sequencing is sound. It states the easement is "perpetual" as fact, which the brief does not say.
- N7W: Good flag on the "next December" timing ambiguity and the dark-box risk. It accepts the parking claim with little scrutiny, misses exclusives and the hybrid, and assumes the 30% uplift is net of incentives (an optimistic assumption, though flagged).
- Y0G: Covers the core risks, but the stakeholder want/block/bring-in table exposes method scaffolding. "Offer the 40% reduced renewal as a real option" means handing Harrowfield its own ask. Thin on timing and fallbacks.
- C3I: The 1.3/0.6 = 2.2x comparison is correct and useful, and it raises DSCR and occupancy covenants. The advice to hand inline tenants a summary of their own co-tenancy rights, with no waiver trade, is strategically naive, and the 2015 rent-roll comparison point is irrelevant.
- W8N: Solid on the REA checklist and on the impact of 24-hour operation. "Do not give notice" fits a lease that simply expires poorly. The claim that six months of reduced inline rent "can wipe out much of" the difference is unquantified. Fallbacks are thin.
- J6J: Sharp decision rule (inline exposure vs about 0.7x current rent), a smaller-grocer hybrid, and flags for imaging obligations and health exclusives. It muddles the lender threshold ("exceed 15,000 sq ft in aggregate", when each lease exceeds it on its own). The interests section and the 60%/85% figures read as method residue and invented precision.
- R7A: Strong ranking with co-tenancy first. The threshold "must beat renewal at 60% of current rent", the hybrid and a co-tenancy-qualifying food replacement are good subtle points, and it adds a refinancing note and an ordered work plan. It asserts without support that the new tenants are "healthier" than the grocer.
- Y7R: The most complete and accurate reply. It checks renewal options, gives a correct parking ratio of 4.4/1000 against medical norms, says two sign-offs are needed, covers exclusives, credit and security, LOI extension with confidentiality, and gives a clear sequence and fallbacks. It is long, and the holdover point is slightly loose.
