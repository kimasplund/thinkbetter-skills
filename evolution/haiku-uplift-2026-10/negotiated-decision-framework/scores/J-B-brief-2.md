# J-B, brief-2

E5N: Catches the Quillon ownership point, the Dunmere risk and the August 1 timing. Treats Orrin's fallowing only as a supply benefit and never flags his conflict over trading prices. Keeps trading at open prices with only a clearinghouse. The "how this should land" predictions are stated without support, and the stakeholder table includes some filler rows.

G5M: Very thorough. It puts the August 1 closure first and handles Orrin's conflict tactfully. It also explains why acreage-weighted ratification favors large owners over the many small orchards. It offers timing flexibility and a reserve funded by willing sellers, and it gives a counter-case (a large orchard volume would change the deal). Calibration is good. It runs long but the length is mostly substantive.

C4G: Concise and accurate. It notes that the "budget is fine" conclusion depends on the Dunmere terms. It frames Orrin's fallowing as a market effect rather than a conflict. It proposes an orchard reserve and then a ratification vote without working through the weighted-vote math, which is a real gap.

D1J: The most complete and actionable. It covers the closure timing, the Dunmere reading, Orrin's conflict, Quillon, the board's makeup and a mid-season review. Its "what could still go wrong" section names what would change the plan and how to test fallowed supply early. Calling Neil a likely seller is an unflagged assumption, and the idea of using the $40k is risky although hedged.

V2A: Lean and mostly sound. It misstates the row-crop interest as opposing "an acreage-weighted rule that favors big farms." It leaves trading unpriced and unregulated, so it never addresses the orchards' weak bargaining position. Its assumptions are stated well.

Y3M: Strong. It proposes timing banking, a guarded exchange, a small hardship reserve, disclosure from Orrin and Quillon's vote. It makes the subtle point that the board should not engineer around a vote members expect to have. It turns the closure question into an acre-foot and dollar comparison. Its assumptions are explicit.

C6Y: Covers the main issues and adds creative Dunmere options. The claim about row-crop midsummer demand is unflagged. The process advice contradicts itself ("one package with two or three options").

H0A: Misquotes the brief ("most" of the orchards are on the lateral, where the brief says "many"). It recommends a reserved orchard block paid for by a larger row-crop cut, after conceding that this is a departure needing a weighted vote. It never addresses the fact that orchards would likely lose that vote. It also funds the block from conveyance savings that its own advice to delay the closure would reduce. Its checks (engineer, extension agent, title records) are good.
