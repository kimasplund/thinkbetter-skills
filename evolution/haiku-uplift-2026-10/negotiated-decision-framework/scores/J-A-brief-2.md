- H0A: Catches the lateral/bud-set clash, Dunmere, Quillon, and Orrin. It also makes two subtle points: the closure itself may count as a departure from equal shares, and fallowed acres may be treated differently under the 55% formula. But it proposes a reserved orchard block that would need the weighted vote, and it never notes the orchards would likely lose that vote. The recommendation stays tentative.
- C6Y: Solid coverage, with timing-banking, guardrails, a fallowing program, and metering for compliance. Ends with "two or three options" instead of landing one, and doesn't see that the acreage-owned vote works against the orchards.
- Y3M: Strong and practical. It turns the closure date into acre-feet and dollars, gives a timing split, an open exchange, and a check on Quillon's own interests. It also makes the sharp point "don't engineer around a vote members expect." Small inconsistency: a 3-5% hardship reserve is presented as staying within equal shares.
- V2A: Concise and clear, with good assumptions (basin approval, how sold water counts). Gives little real handling of Orrin's conflict. The opening line about row-crop growers fearing an acreage-weighted rule "that favors the big farms" misreads who that vote favors.
- D1J: Very actionable. It sees the orchards would probably lose the weighted vote, raises Quillon, Orrin, and board composition, and covers what could still go wrong and the odds of adoption. It is long, and calling Neil a likely seller is speculative.
- C4G: Covers the main issues and notes "budget is fine" depends on Dunmere. But its answer is a formula-based orchard reserve plus a ratification vote, with no look at whether that vote is winnable. Generic in places.
- G5M: The most complete. It treats the weighted vote as the real power map (orchards are many farms on few acres), and its package-not-menu reasoning is tied to the facts. It handles Orrin with care, with a disclosure practice for every board member. It is calibrated about the post-harvest volume and what would change the plan. Long, and uses mild positions-vs-interests framing.
- E5N: The stakeholder table and the "how this should land" predictions read as method scaffolding. The Dunmere row says the town would accept a cut in seepage without replacement, which is backwards. It predicts orchard acceptance with too much confidence.
