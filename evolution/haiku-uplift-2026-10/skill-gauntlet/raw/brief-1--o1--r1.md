I'd hold off on telling the clinics it goes live Monday. v2.4 doesn't clear everything, and one issue is a patient-safety problem.

**1. Dosing accuracy: v2.4 fails your own rule.**
Your rule was "no worse than v2.3 on dosing accuracy." The pharmacy tech's line-by-line check is the more reliable measure here, because it compared every output against the source notes. It found two v2.4 tables that turn a twice-daily drug into once daily, and none in v2.3. That makes v2.4 worse on dosing. Both versions scoring 5.0 only shows that the judges didn't catch the errors when reading for overall quality. Being clinicians doesn't make them better at spotting a transcription slip in a table than a line-by-line check is. Under-dosing a twice-daily drug is also exactly the kind of error that harms animals. Two errors in 24 outputs is about 8%, and the sample is small, so the true rate could be higher or lower. I'd treat it as a real defect until Dr. Achterberg has found and fixed the cause. The new medication-schedule table is the likely source, so that is where to look.

**2. The clarity bar was changed after the results.**
The 1.0 threshold was set on September 2, before you ran anything. v2.4 came in at +0.8, which missed it. On September 10, the day after the results, the bar became 0.5, and v2.4 now passes. The reasoning may well be right. A bar carried over from a 10-point scale could be too high for a 7-point one. But the change came after you saw the numbers, and it happens to be the change that flips the outcome. Anyone reviewing this later will reasonably doubt it. The honest way to report it is "missed the pre-registered bar, and we later revised it." If you want a clean answer, rerun the clarity comparison against a bar you set in advance.

**3. The judging wasn't fully blind to the author.**
Dr. Achterberg wrote v2.4 and was one of three judges. Even with A/B labels, she may recognise her own style, and she has an obvious stake in the result. Her scores should be excluded, or at least shown separately. If the +0.8 holds with only Callum and Bea scoring, that is stronger evidence. If it doesn't, you have your answer.

**4. The carprofen check points the other way.**
The two-sentence v2.4 answer versus the full discharge sheet from v2.3 is a sign that the two versions behave differently on an off-format question. It is only one question, so I wouldn't read much into it. If owners or staff might ask follow-ups like this, it's worth checking that v2.4 still gives complete, safe guidance when it needs to, including the missed-dose advice its own worked example covers.

**What I'd do**
- Don't announce Monday. Keep v2.3 live.
- Have Dr. Achterberg find out why the twice-daily doses were flattened, fix it, and have the pharmacy tech re-check a fresh set of cases. A reasonable bar is zero dosing errors, since any error here is one an owner could act on.
- Re-score clarity without the author as a judge. State the threshold before you run it.
- Report the September 10 change openly in whatever you send to leadership.
- If you want something for the clinics now, you could say "v2.4 is in final checks, with a decision expected in [date]". That is accurate and leaves you free to ship once the issue is fixed.

I don't know how long a fix and re-check would take. If it's quick, you may only be a week or so behind. v2.4 may well turn out to be the better version, but the evidence so far doesn't show that yet.
