# J-A scores, brief-2

Notes:
- D3Y: It gets the works-versus-concerts point right. It argues that a split season fails, then recommends a living-composer piece in most concerts, which is a mixed programme. It anchors classics on Fridays, where sales are already healthy, and does not target Saturdays.
- E6C: Lean and usable. It asks the council sharp questions (does Unbound count, arrangements, programmed or performed) and plans on the strict reading. Its claim that "the approach still holds" if the council counts differently is too strong.
- Y7Y: Strong. It caps Unbound's scale at about 1,000 seats, adds a 22-25% margin, flags rights fees for film scores, sets a review point, and has the board compare all three plans on the same measures.
- N0J: Concrete slot rules with a 60% Saturday review threshold. It splits the retention risk into artistic ambition versus pay, which is a good point. Two or three commissions (EUR 70-105k) is heavy but flagged for co-funding. Somewhat long.
- H3D: The most decision-ready. It separates "accessible music sells" from "contemporary concert music sells", notes that classics on Saturdays are untested, gives three specific conditions that would change the view, and names the cheapest checks (council definition, per-concert sales history by repertoire, talking to principals). It calls EUR 70k a "small fraction of budget" without knowing the budget.
- O7B: Says the problem is on Saturdays, then argues for classics on Fridays, which does not follow. It claims Unbound "addresses the single-ticket decline directly", which overclaims for a 180-seat room. The assumptions are clearly stated.
- S4K: Good insight that both sell-outs worked because the audience knew what it was getting. It has a solid risk list and a plain confidence statement. It is long and header-heavy, and one line on the grant risk is garbled.
- N9R: Concise, with assumptions listed. It asserts "demand is strongest where familiar and new sit next to each other" without support, and anchors classics on Fridays rather than on the weak Saturday slot. The heading "residual tensions" is a slight trace of method.
