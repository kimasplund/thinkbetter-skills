# J-B scores, brief-2

N9R: It correctly reads the grant as counting works rather than concerts and lists sensible assumptions. But it puts the classics on Fridays, which are already 81% full, so it does not target the weak Saturday slot. It also states without evidence that "the big Romantic concertos are what subscribers buy". It never asks whether the film concert or Unbound counts toward the grant.

S4K: Strong. It reads the grant by works, works out the commission cost (€385k if every quota work were commissioned) and recommends existing repertoire instead. It puts recognisable titles on Saturdays, asks the council whether film and Unbound count, and gives a calibrated confidence. Weaknesses: "single-ticket buyers choose on recognisable titles" is asserted, a second film concert is called low-cost without mentioning rights or hire fees, and it is a bit long.

O7B: Good on the grant and on tracking metrics, and it states its assumptions. Like N9R, it assumes Fridays are the "standard programme" and keeps the classics there. It claims Unbound "addresses the single-ticket decline directly", which does not follow from a 180-seat foyer series.

H3D: The best insight. It points out that the film sell-out shows accessible music sells, not that contemporary concert music sells. It notes that Unbound's demand may not cross over to the main hall, that the Saturday cause is unproven, and that Ilse's report about the principals should be checked with them. It has a clear "what would change my view" section and cheap first steps (per-concert sales history). One small overreach: it calls €70k "a small fraction" of a budget it has not seen.

N0J: Concrete slot rules, a 60% review trigger, sensible checks with the council, and a useful split between principals worried about ambition and those worried about pay. Its confidence is calibrated. Some lines are generic ("several living composers write concertos..."), and it budgets two or three commissions without any cost context.

Y7Y: It clearly separates what it knows, what it assumes and what it cannot see (soloist and rights fees, rehearsal time). It sizes Unbound at about 1,000 seats in total, sets a margin target, adds a review point and compares the plans on the same measures. It is well calibrated and adds no machinery. Two or three film or crossover Saturdays is a heavy bet on one data point, though the reply does frame it as a test.

E6C: It plans on the strict grant reading, which is prudent, and does the opener arithmetic (about 29%). It says the principals' warning should be tested with them. Its advice on Saturdays is vague ("better programming... better marketing"), and it still anchors the classics on Fridays, which are already full.

D3Y: Internally inconsistent. It says mixed concerts fail, then recommends a living-composer work in most concerts. It invents claims: that the Friday audience is "built on familiar repertoire", that donors exist because of the orchestra's distinctiveness, and that a classics pitch "has already been made".
