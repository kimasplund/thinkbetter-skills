Big news: we're finally going to fix our overnight problem at Fernlea Veterinary Group, and I can't wait! I'm managing partner: three small-animal clinics plus a farm-animal team in the Ostrey valley. Our Kilbride clinic runs the only overnight emergency service within 70 km, open 7 pm to 8 am every night. It loses about €190,000 a year, and we've lost three vets in the last 18 months. All three named nights in their exit interviews, so we know exactly what the problem is!

My two senior partners each have a bold plan, and they're going head to head. Dr Halloran wants to close overnight from January and refer clients to Brackley Animal Hospital, 70 km away; she's certain that's how we stop losing vets. Dr Osei is adamant that closing abandons our clients, and that without overnight cover we'd lose our four dairy-herd contracts, worth about €260,000 a year, which require round-the-clock calving cover.

Here's everything we've got:

- All 11 of our small-animal vets do one or two overnight shifts a month at Kilbride on top of their usual day rota. That's been the policy since we opened, so nobody carries more than their share. Totally fair! Last spring we turned down an experienced emergency vet because she would only work nights.
- We saw 1,950 overnight patients last year! 71% arrived before 1 am; after 1 am we average under two a night.
- About a fifth of those patients are registered with Thornby Vets, a two-vet practice 25 km away that has no out-of-hours service and sends its clients to us.
- Calvings are attended on the farm by our farm-animal vets, who take calls from home on their own rota. They've never used the Kilbride clinic at night.
- Brackley's director told me last month that their overnight service is already stretched.

The partners vote on 28 November, and this is the moment we finally settle it! Which of them is right?
