Neither of your partners is wholly right, and the vote on 28 November is less of a choice between "close" and "keep" than it looks. Each of them is right about a different part of the problem.

**What Dr Halloran has right**

The current model is what is losing your vets. Every one of the 11 small-animal vets does one or two nights a month on top of a full day rota, and all three who left named the nights. You also turned down an experienced emergency vet because she would only work nights, which tells you the job is no longer attractive to people you would want to hire. Fairness across the group does not fix that. Spreading an unpopular duty evenly across everyone still leaves everyone carrying it.

**What Dr Osei has right**

Closing overnight outright would drop about 1,950 patients a year onto other providers, and Brackley is 70 km away. Brackley's director has also told you their service is already stretched, so you cannot assume they will absorb your clients. Your dairy contracts are a real risk as well, though the facts you gave point to a narrower one than Dr Osei may think. Your farm vets take calving calls from home and have never used the Kilbride clinic at night. The contracts may depend on the clinic only in a looser sense, such as keeping a 24-hour veterinary presence in the group. Check the wording of those four contracts before you assume that closing Kilbride overnight breaks them.

**Where the disagreement actually sits**

The two partners are arguing about whether the service exists, but the numbers say the problem is when it runs. Of your overnight patients, 71% arrive before 1 am. After 1 am you average under two a night. The service is busy in the early evening and nearly empty in the small hours, yet the cover is the same for the whole night and is staffed by people doing it as a favour on top of their day work.

That points to a different arrangement from either proposal:

- **Keep emergency cover at Kilbride from 7 pm to around 1 am, and staff it from a dedicated emergency team.** This means a small group of vets whose main job is emergency work, paid for it, and rostered so that night work is a defined role. This addresses the reason vets are leaving. Hiring an emergency specialist who wants nights becomes possible again.
- **After 1 am, move to telephone triage and an on-call vet who can attend, with referral to a 24-hour centre for anything serious.** At under two patients a night, most of the 2 am to 8 am load is handled by phone advice and by sending a few cases on. This removes most of the nightly staffing burden from your general vets.
- **Agree a written referral arrangement with Brackley now, before the vote.** Their stretched capacity is a reason to talk to them early, and it is not a reason to abandon the referral route.
- **Put the Thornby relationship into the plan.** About a fifth of your overnight patients are theirs, and they have no out-of-hours service of their own. A formal service agreement with Thornby, with a fee or a referral commitment, would bring in revenue and make the evening model more secure.
- **Protect the dairy contracts directly.** Confirm exactly what cover each contract requires, and keep the farm-animal on-call rota exactly as it is. Calving cover runs from the farm team, so the contracts may be safe under either the revised or the current model. Get the answer in writing before the vote.

**What this does not resolve**

The after-1 am change is only as good as the triage and the on-call vet's response times. If a client with a serious case gets a poor answer at 3 am, the complaint will land on the practice, so the triage protocol needs to be designed carefully. The cost saving is also uncertain. Dedicated emergency staff cost money, and the €190,000 loss may not fall by much unless the evening volume brings in enough revenue. The figures you gave do not show whether a dedicated team would cover its cost, so that needs a costed rota before 28 November. Finally, the staffing market for emergency vets is outside your control, and the plan assumes you can hire at least two or three people who want the role.

**What I would put to the partners**

Ask Dr Halloran to accept that closing overnight is the wrong fix, since it abandons clients and does not address the staffing problem. Ask Dr Osei to accept that the current everyone-does-nights model is ending, and that it is the cause of the departures. Ask both to vote on a shorter, staffed overnight service with a telephone-led night period, subject to three checks before implementation: a costed rota, written referral terms with Brackley, and confirmation of what the dairy contracts require. If those checks fail, the fallback is a closure date with a proper referral agreement in place, not an abrupt January closure.
