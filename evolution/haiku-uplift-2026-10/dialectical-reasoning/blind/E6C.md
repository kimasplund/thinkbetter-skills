Short answer: you don't have to choose between Gerrit's plan and Ilse's. The grant condition, as you quoted it, is about works, not concerts, and that changes the arithmetic a lot.

**1. Check what the grant actually requires**

The condition says "at least 20% of works programmed across the season must be by living composers." Ilse reads it as a fifth of concerts. Those are different tests.

Last season's 16 main-stage concerts had 3 or 4 works each, so roughly 48 to 64 works in total. Twenty percent of that is about 10 to 13 living-composer works. You don't need a contemporary programme. You need about one contemporary piece per concert, or a few concentrated in some concerts.

Before March, get the arts council's written interpretation. Ask three things:
- Does "works programmed across the season" include the Unbound chamber series, or only main-stage concerts?
- Does "living composer" cover new arrangements of older music, or only original works?
- Is a work counted when it is programmed or when it is performed?

Until you have that answer, plan on the strict reading (main stage only, original works) so you don't rely on an interpretation that may not hold.

**2. A structure that meets the condition without a greatest-hits season**

- **Open most classic concerts with a short living-composer work** (8 to 12 minutes). This puts a contemporary piece in front of the core subscription audience, which is already 81% full on Fridays, and counts as one work each. Across 16 concerts, that alone gives about 16 living-composer works out of roughly 56, close to 29%. That clears 20% with a margin, and the Beethoven and Tchaikovsky stay in the programme.
- **Use the film and video-game result.** The only main-stage sell-out was a concert of living-composer music. A single themed Saturday concert of that kind, with several living composers, would probably sell better than a standard Saturday at 48% full, and it counts toward the condition.
- **Put one commission in the plan, not several.** A 20-minute commission at about €35,000 is a real cost against a 38% grant share, so one commission for a high-profile concert is reasonable. The rest of the living-composer works can come from existing repertoire, which is much cheaper.

**3. Take Unbound seriously as a pipeline**

Six sold-out nights, a median audience age of 31 against 64 in the main hall, and mostly new music: this is the strongest evidence in the brief that a younger audience exists for contemporary work. It also gives Ilse a place to develop and try new pieces before they go on the main stage. Whether Unbound counts toward the grant is the question in point 1. Either way, it is a useful argument for keeping the programme's contemporary element.

**4. What to tell the board**

- Gerrit's concern, falling single-ticket sales, is real. The Saturday concerts averaging 48% full are the problem. More greatest hits may not fix that. Saturdays need better programming, possibly a mixed or themed format, and better marketing to new audiences.
- Ilse's concern, losing the grant and the orchestra's identity, rests on a reading of the condition that the wording may not support. Confirm that reading before the board commits to a direction.
- The principal players' warning should be tested, not assumed. Ask them directly what would keep them, since a programme with a clear contemporary element may suit them better than a greatest-hits season.

**Assumptions I made:** the grant is for the whole orchestra's season, not one concert series; Unbound's status is unknown; the 20% test is applied to the count of works, not minutes of music. If the council's answer differs on any of these, the numbers change, but the approach of one contemporary work per classic concert and a mixed Saturday format still holds.

**Recommendation:** Build the 2027–28 season on Gerrit's classics for the Friday subscribers, add a living-composer opener to most concerts to meet the grant with margin, make one Saturday concert a themed living-composer programme, and use Unbound as the place to test new work. Get the council's written answer before the board votes in March.
