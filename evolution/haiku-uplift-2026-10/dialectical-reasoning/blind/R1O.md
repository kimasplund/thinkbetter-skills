I don't think you have to choose between Gerrit's season and Ilse's. The grant wording may let you meet the condition with far less contemporary programming than Ilse expects, and your own data points to a hybrid. Here is my reasoning, with the assumptions I've made.

**1. Read the grant condition closely before you plan around it.**

The condition as you quoted it says "at least 20% of works programmed across the season must be by living composers." That is a share of works, not of concerts. Ilse's reading, that a fifth of concerts must be contemporary programmes, is a stricter interpretation than the text you've quoted.

Rough arithmetic: 16 main-stage concerts with three or four works each is about 48 to 64 works. Twenty percent of that is roughly 10 to 13 living-composer works across the whole season. That is a very different target from a fifth of the concerts (about 3 full contemporary programmes).

Before the board decides, get the exact wording and the council's guidance in writing. Ask whether works counted this way include encores and the Unbound series, and whether the council counts a living composer's arrangement or a piece with a living co-composer. The answer changes the target.

**2. Build the season so the living-composer works sit where they sell.**

Your data suggests the audience problem is concentrated in Saturday single tickets (48% full), not in the repertoire as a whole. Friday subscribers fill 81% of seats for the standard programme. That argues for keeping the core classics on the subscription nights and placing the new work in formats that already work for you:

- **Mix living-composer works into the classical programmes.** A contemporary overture or a short new piece as an opener, paired with Beethoven or Tchaikovsky, can meet the quota without a whole "new music" concert that subscribers may skip. One living-composer work in each of 10 to 12 programmes would meet the target on its own.
- **Use the film and video-game model for a main-stage crossover.** That concert was your only sell-out, and its entire programme was by living composers. Ilse dismisses it as a one-off, but it is evidence that a living-composer programme can draw a full house. One or two such concerts in the season would add several works to the count and help on the box office side.
- **Expand Unbound.** Six sold-out nights, a median audience age of 31 against 64 in the main hall, and a mostly new-music programme is the strongest evidence you have for reaching younger audiences. Whether Unbound counts toward the grant is a question for the council (see point 1). Even if it doesn't count, it is cheap, it keeps the players engaged, and it addresses the single-ticket decline directly.

**3. Use the commission carefully.**

A €35,000 commission gives you one new 20-minute work. It is a useful anchor, since one premiere can be repeated across programmes or venues, but on its own it will not meet the quota. Budget it as a centrepiece for the season, not as the whole answer. Check whether the council will fund or partly fund it, since the grant is 38% of your income and the council has a stake in new work.

**4. Address the principals' concern directly.**

Ilse's warning that players will leave if the orchestra becomes a greatest-hits band is a retention risk, not just an artistic preference. A season with a large share of the classics and no new repertoire would be a real problem for the players, and Unbound and the commission show they are willing to play new music when it's done well. The hybrid answers that concern without asking Gerrit to give up the subscription classics that fill the Friday hall.

**5. Recommendation for the board.**

Neither side is simply right, and the split is probably about definitions as much as artistic values. My recommendation:

1. Get the grant condition clarified in writing before the March decision.
2. Plan a season of roughly 16 concerts where the classics stay on the Friday subscriptions and each programme includes one or more living-composer works, so the count meets the 20% target with room to spare.
3. Add one or two main-stage crossover concerts by living composers, modelled on the sell-out.
4. Expand Unbound, and commission one new work as the season's centrepiece.
5. Track Saturday single-ticket sales and the median age of audiences against last season, so the board can judge after one season whether the mix is working.

**Assumptions I've made:** I'm assuming the grant text you quoted is complete and that "works" means the individual pieces in each programme. I'm assuming the council has no other conditions, and that the 81% and 48% figures are fair averages across the season. If the council's guidance differs from the text you quoted, the numbers in point 2 change, but the direction of the recommendation does not.
