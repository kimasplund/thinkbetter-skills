Neither partner is entirely right, and the evidence points to a different question than "open or close overnight." Here is how I'd break it down before the 28 November vote.

**What the facts support**

- **Dr Halloran is right that the current model is failing.** Three vets have left in 18 months, and all three named the night shifts. Every vet doing one or two nights a month, on top of day work, is a fixed cost in fatigue and goodwill that the clinic is paying for with attrition. Turning down an experienced emergency vet who would only work nights shows the rota is also limiting who you can hire.
- **Dr Osei is right that closing the service abandons clients, but the dairy argument is weaker than it sounds.** Your calving cover is provided by farm-animal vets taking calls from home, and they have never used Kilbride at night. So the dairy contracts may depend on round-the-clock *vet availability* rather than on the overnight clinic itself. Check the wording of the four contracts. If they require an on-call vet and not a clinic open at night, closing Kilbride overnight may not breach them. If they name Kilbride or specific facilities, Osei's risk is real and needs a legal read.
- **The demand pattern suggests a narrower service.** 71% of overnight patients arrive before 1 am, and after 1 am you average under two a night. The hardest part of the night, and presumably the part most likely to produce burnout, is the stretch with the least demand.
- **Thornby matters more than its size suggests.** About a fifth of overnight patients (roughly 390 a year) come from a two-vet practice with no out-of-hours service of its own. Closing overnight entirely would make Thornby's clients Brackley's problem, and Thornby could move its wider work elsewhere.
- **Brackley is not a safe referral destination.** Its director says their overnight service is already stretched. Referring 70 km away, at night, into a service at capacity is a patient-safety risk, and it could also reduce the referral goodwill Thornby and others rely on.

**Options worth putting to the vote instead of a binary choice**

1. **Shorten the window.** Run Kilbride emergency from 7 pm to 1 am, then refer later cases to Brackley or a pre-agreed partner. This captures most demand and removes the least-used hours from the rota. You would need the cost and the vet-coverage numbers for those hours, which I don't have.
2. **Change how nights are staffed.** Keep the full window but move from "all 11 small-animal vets, one or two nights a month" to a dedicated emergency team, paid as a specialist role, possibly with a mix of employed and sessional emergency vets. This addresses the attrition cause directly and is the option that would let you recruit the vet you turned down.
3. **Fix the economics.** €190,000 a year is a loss, not necessarily a reason to close. Ask whether Thornby (and other referring practices) would pay a per-patient or retainer fee for overnight access. Even partial cost recovery changes the arithmetic.
4. **Only then decide on full closure,** if the first three fail and you have a confirmed referral arrangement that Brackley can actually absorb.

**What I'd do before 28 November**

- Get the four dairy contracts reviewed for the exact cover wording. This is the single fact that most changes Osei's position.
- Ask Thornby what they would do if overnight closed, and whether they would pay for access.
- Get a written answer from Brackley on capacity, not just a verbal "stretched."
- Ask the vets who left, or the current team, whether a shorter window or a paid specialist rota would have kept them.
- Cost options 1 and 2 against the €190,000 loss, using real rota hours.

**Assumptions I've made:** the €190,000 figure is net of the overnight vets' normal pay (it is the net loss of running the service); the dairy contracts may or may not name Kilbride; and the 71% and under-two figures cover the full year, not a seasonal peak. If any of these is wrong, the picture changes, especially for the dairy contracts.

My direct answer: Halloran is right that the status quo can't continue, and Osei is right that an abrupt full closure, with Brackley already stretched, is the riskiest version of the fix. The likeliest good outcome is a shorter, professionally staffed overnight service, not a choice between the two positions as framed.
