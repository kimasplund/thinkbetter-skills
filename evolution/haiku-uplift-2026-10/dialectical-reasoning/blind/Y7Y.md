**Short answer: don't pick either side. Gerrit and Ilse are arguing about a false choice, and your own numbers show a third option that serves both.**

**1. The grant condition doesn't say what Ilse thinks it says.**
The text is "at least 20% of works programmed across the season must be by living composers." That is 20% of works, not a fifth of concerts, and it is not "contemporary programmes". With 16 concerts of 3 to 4 works you have roughly 48 to 64 works, so you need about 10 to 13 works by living composers. They can sit inside otherwise popular concerts: a short opener of 8 to 12 minutes ahead of Tchaikovsky counts in full. Ilse's reading is the stricter one, and it is what makes Gerrit's plan look impossible. Her reading isn't the grant's.

Two things to check, because 38% of income rides on them:
- Ask the arts council in writing how they count works. Do they count by piece, or by minutes? Do film and game scores count? Do Unbound works count as "main-season" programming? I'm assuming a work is a work and that living composers of any genre qualify, but get confirmation before you build a season on it.
- Ask what proof they require, and whether there is a tolerance. Leave yourself a margin, say 22 to 25%, so one cancelled piece doesn't put you under.

**2. What the data says about audiences.**
- Saturday single-ticket concerts are at 48% and Friday subscriptions at 81%. Your single-ticket problem is a Saturday problem, which suggests that is where programming change would pay off. The Friday base is healthy, so don't disturb it.
- The only sell-out was film and game music, all by living composers. Ilse calls it a one-off, but it is one data point that cuts against both camps: a living-composer programme can fill the hall, and "popular" does not have to mean "dead composers". It is one concert, so treat it as a signal worth testing, not as proof.
- Unbound sold out all six nights with a median age of 31 against 64 in the main hall. That is your audience-renewal evidence, and it is mostly new music. The caveat is capacity: 180 seats, six nights, so about 1,000 seats in total. It shows demand and a younger audience, but it doesn't replace main-hall revenue.

So the evidence does not support "new music kills sales" (Unbound, the game concert) or "only classics sell" (the one sell-out wasn't classics). Neither side's story is fully borne out.

**3. A proposal for 2027-28**
- **Saturdays (the problem slot):** lead with recognisable repertoire or accessible themes. Add 2 to 3 film/game or crossover programmes with living composers. These also earn grant credit.
- **Fridays (the stable base):** keep the subscription core of Beethoven, Tchaikovsky and the Romantic concertos that Gerrit wants, and put a short living-composer piece on most of them, with the occasional bolder programme. Pair each new piece with a well-loved work so the risk is spread.
- **Unbound:** keep and, if the foyer and players allow, add nights. Treat it as the pipeline for younger audiences, and run a trial to see if anyone moves from Unbound to the main hall (collect emails and ticket data).
- **Commissions:** one or two at €35,000 each (€35,000 to €70,000), chosen for accessibility and a player or soloist connection. You do not need to commission to hit 20%, because plenty of existing living-composer repertoire is available at no commission cost. Commission only where the work serves the story you tell audiences.
- **Quantity:** aim for about 12 to 14 living-composer works out of roughly 56, which gives 21 to 25%.

**4. Why this addresses each concern**
- Gerrit gets a season that leads with accessible and well-known work, aimed at the weak Saturday sales.
- Ilse keeps an identity that includes living composers, and the players avoid being a "greatest-hits band". Ask her to name which principals worry her and what they would need. Their concern may be more about artistic ambition than about the percentage.
- The board gets a plan with the grant protected, instead of a vote between two camps.

**5. Risks and what I can't tell from here**
- I don't know the cost side: soloist fees, rights fees for film/game scores, and how much rehearsal time new works need. Do a budget before committing.
- Popular classics may well lift single tickets, and I have no evidence either way on how much. A middle path might under-deliver on both goals. To limit that, set a review point (say after the first third of the season) with a clear metric, such as Saturday occupancy rising from 48% to a target the board sets, and a pre-agreed adjustment if it misses.
- Don't take my 20% arithmetic as final. Count your actual draft programme against the council's wording.
