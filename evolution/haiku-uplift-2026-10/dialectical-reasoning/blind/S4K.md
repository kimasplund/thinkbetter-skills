**Short answer:** Don't pick either camp's plan. Gerrit is right about what to put on Saturdays, and Ilse is right about the orchestra's identity and its players. The grant condition, as you've quoted it, is also much easier to meet than Ilse believes.

## The grant rule is about works, not concerts

The condition says 20% of *works* programmed across the season must be by living composers. Ilse reads it as a fifth of the *concerts* being contemporary programmes. Those are very different obligations.

- 16 concerts with 3 or 4 works each is roughly 56 works. 20% is about 11 or 12 works, and the exact number depends on your final programme count.
- One living-composer piece in 12 of the 16 concerts would meet the rule. That could be a 10-minute opener ahead of Tchaikovsky.
- Putting one in every concert would give you about 16 works, near 28%. That leaves a safety margin if a programme changes.

So a popular-classics season can satisfy the grant. Nobody has to give up the 38% of income.

Confirm two things with the arts council in writing before March, because the whole plan rests on them:
1. That "works" means individual pieces, as the wording suggests, and not programmes.
2. Whether film and game scores by living composers count, and whether Unbound performances count toward the season.

If both answers are yes, you have more room than anyone on the board assumes.

## What each side has right

**Gerrit.** The problem is concentrated. Fridays average 81% full and Saturday single-ticket nights average 48%. Your subscribers are loyal, and the 22% fall is in people deciding night by night. Single-ticket buyers choose on recognisable titles, so familiar repertoire on the nights that need to sell is a reasonable response.

**Ilse.** A greatest-hits season carries real risks. Principal players may leave, and that is costly and slow to repair. A programme with no new work also puts the grant at risk if the council reads "works" more strictly than you do. Your two best signals both came from living composers: the film and video-game sell-out, and Unbound, with six sell-out nights and a median audience age of 31 against 64 in the main hall. Gerrit's plan would drop the content that is currently working with the audience you need to grow.

Ilse's "one-off" label on the film-score concert isn't well supported. It is a single data point, but it is the only sell-out you had, and it points the opposite way from the claim that audiences won't come for living composers.

## Where the two positions really conflict

Both want the same thing, a full hall and an orchestra worth playing in. They disagree about what draws a single-ticket buyer: a famous composer's name or a familiar kind of experience. Your data suggests both can work. The film concert and Unbound sold out because the audience knew what they were getting and wanted it, not because the music was old or new.

A plain 50/50 split would likely fail. Half-contemporary Saturdays could deepen the single-ticket problem, and half-classics Fridays could upset subscribers who are already loyal. The better approach is to treat each slot differently.

## Recommended direction

**1. Saturdays (the weak spot): anchor on recognisable titles.** Use Beethoven, Tchaikovsky and the big concertos, with a short living-composer piece placed in the programme. Choose pieces that are accessible and clearly programmed with intent, not tacked on. Gerrit gets his headline repertoire where sales need it.

**2. Fridays (the strong spot): leave the subscription model alone.** At 81%, it works. Carry the more adventurous music here, including a few larger contemporary works, because this audience has already shown it will show up. Don't risk it for a theory.

**3. Add a second film or game-score concert.** If the first sold out, a second is a modest test of whether it was a one-off. Programme it as a main-stage event and count it toward the 20% if the council agrees. Cost is low, since existing scores need no commission.

**4. Protect and expand Unbound.** Six nights is your clearest sign of demand among younger listeners. Adding nights costs far less than a main-stage concert and keeps the players engaged in work they care about. Check the foyer's capacity and the players' willingness first.

**5. Commission selectively.** At about €35,000 each, commissioning all 11 or 12 works would cost around €385,000, which is unnecessary. Existing living-composer works cost no commission. Commission one or two pieces, such as an Unbound-linked opener, as a flagship statement for Ilse and the players, and fill the rest of the quota from the existing repertoire.

**6. Talk to the principals now.** Show them the plan: the core repertoire is stronger and the contemporary and chamber work is staying. Their view on which contemporary pieces to programme will carry weight with the board and reduce the departure risk Ilse has raised.

## How to present it to the board

The board is split because it has been given a choice between two plans. Offer a third: every concert has some familiar and some new music, Saturdays lean familiar, Fridays and Unbound lean adventurous, and the grant is covered with margin. Gerrit gets sellable Saturdays, and Ilse gets a season the players will stay for.

## What could go wrong

- **The council reads the rule more strictly than the wording suggests.** Confirm this first. If it does require contemporary programmes, you would need to rework the plan, and Unbound and Friday programmes would carry more weight.
- **Short openers may be ignored or resented.** A 10-minute piece before a concerto can feel like a chore. It works only if the pieces are well chosen and presented.
- **Classics may not fix Saturdays.** The 48% figure could reflect price, timing or marketing as much as repertoire. Check what Saturday buyers say before treating repertoire as the cause.
- **The film concert is a single result.** The second concert is a test, and the result should be read as such.
- **Unbound may not scale.** A 180-seat foyer sells out partly because it is small and intimate. Moving the same music to the main hall may not give the same result.

## Confidence

About 70% that this direction is better than either original plan. I'm confident in the point that the grant is measured in works and in the Friday/Saturday split, since both come from your numbers. I'm less certain about whether classics will lift Saturdays and about the council's reading, and the second of those needs to be settled before March.
