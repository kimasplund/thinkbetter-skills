Short answer: neither partner is fully right, and the vote should not be a straight choice between "close from January" and "keep it as it is". The facts you've given cut against both of them in places.

**Where Dr Halloran is right**

- The current model is failing on people. Three vets have left in 18 months, and all three named nights. Every small-animal vet carries one or two overnight shifts a month, and you've already had to turn away an experienced emergency vet who would only work nights. Keeping things as they are is not a stable option.
- The money is real. Kilbride loses about €190,000 a year.
- Brackley's director says their overnight service is already stretched. That weakens the plan to refer everyone there, so the referral route can't be assumed to work as things stand.

**Where Dr Osei is right**

- Closing overnight entirely would drop about 1,950 patients a year, and roughly a fifth of them come from Thornby Vets. Thornby has no out-of-hours service of its own, so closing would likely cost you those referrals and the goodwill that goes with them.
- Dr Osei's €260,000 dairy-contract argument is the weakest part of his case on the facts you've given. Calvings are attended by the farm-animal team on their own home rota, and they have never used Kilbride at night. So the round-the-clock calving cover the contracts require appears to come from the farm team, not from the clinic. The contracts could still require clinic-based overnight cover, but the facts don't show that. Check the actual contract wording before 28 November.

**What the numbers do and don't show**

- The €190,000 loss is a net figure. The number that matters for the decision is the avoidable loss: what you actually save by closing, since some building and fixed costs remain whatever you do.
- The €260,000 is contract revenue, not margin. Compare the margin you would lose on those contracts with the saving, not revenue with loss.
- The patient pattern points to a middle option. 71% of overnight patients arrive before 1 am. After 1 am you average under two a night, which is roughly 1.5 a night across the year. A clinic that covers about 7 pm to 1 am would keep most of the service, and the after-1 am cases could go to a confirmed referral route.

**Options to put to the partners**

1. Close fully from January, as Dr Halloran proposes. This is the cheapest option but abandons clients and Thornby, and depends on Brackley accepting extra cases.
2. Keep the full service as it is. This keeps losing vets and money.
3. Narrow the service to about 7 pm to 1 am, with a written referral arrangement for later emergencies. This cuts the night shifts most vets dislike and most of the cost, and keeps most patients.
4. Keep the full service but staff it with dedicated night emergency clinicians, possibly hired on night-only contracts. The candidate you turned down suggests such people exist, and this tackles the retention problem directly, though it may cost more than the current loss.
5. Any of the above, plus a formal arrangement with Thornby (a retainer or referral agreement) and a confirmed capacity agreement with Brackley.

**What I would take to the vote**

Don't vote on closure as a yes or no. Ask for a decision on option 3 or 4, conditional on three checks completed before 28 November or shortly after:

- The dairy contract wording on overnight and calving cover.
- The avoidable cost of running Kilbride overnight, and the margin on the dairy contracts.
- Written confirmation from Brackley of how many extra overnight cases they can take, and from Thornby of what they would do if Kilbride narrowed.

**Assumptions I've made**

- The dairy contracts are fulfilled by the farm team and not by the clinic. This is my reading of your facts; the contract text is the real test.
- The €190,000 is a net figure, and some of it would remain after closure.
- Thornby's referrals would not all stay if overnight cover stopped. I have not seen their view.

Tell me the contract wording and the cost breakdown and I can test which option holds up.
