Neither partner is entirely right, and the vote as framed (keep Kilbride open overnight, or close it from January) is a false choice. The facts point to a third option: keep a shorter overnight service with dedicated staff, and handle the late hours differently.

**What Dr Halloran gets right**

The cost and the staffing are both real. Kilbride loses about €190,000 a year, and three vets have left in 18 months, all citing nights. Every one of your 11 small-animal vets carries one or two nights a month on top of a full day rota. That is a heavy, permanent load, and it is the most likely reason the next resignation happens. Turning away an experienced emergency vet because she would only work nights shows that the current model is also limiting who you can hire.

**What Dr Osei gets right**

Abandoning overnight clients is a real harm. About 1,950 patients came through last year, and the 7 pm to 1 am window carries most of them. Thornby Vets sends roughly a fifth of those patients (around 390 a year) to you, and they have no out-of-hours service of their own. A full closure would leave them without a route for emergencies, and Brackley is 70 km away and, by its director's account, already stretched.

**Where the dairy argument is weaker than it looks**

Your farm-animal vets take calving calls from home and have never used Kilbride at night. So the €260,000 of dairy contracts does not obviously depend on the clinic being open overnight. It depends on what the contracts say. If they require a 24-hour facility for hospitalising or operating on animals, that matters, and it needs checking against the written terms before 28 November. If they only require on-call vet attendance on the farm, closing Kilbride overnight may not put them at risk.

**The tension underneath**

Each partner is solving a different problem. Halloran is trying to stop the staffing model from driving vets out. Osei is trying to keep an emergency service for clients who need it. The two goals conflict only because the current design makes every vet cover every night, and the service runs at full cost until 8 am even though after 1 am it averages under two patients. Shortening the hours alone would not fix the staffing problem, and a dedicated team alone would not fix the cost, so the answer has to address both.

**A synthesis to put to the partners**

1. **Keep full emergency cover from 7 pm to 1 am.** This is where 71% of demand falls, and it protects Thornby's clients and your own.
2. **After 1 am, switch to telephone triage and referral.** A vet on call handles calls and sends the few animals that need hospital care to Brackley, with a written agreement on how they will take them. Under two patients a night is a manageable load for that.
3. **Staff the 7 pm to 1 am window with a dedicated emergency team,** not the full 11-vet rota. Hire or contract two to four emergency vets, and make the experienced vet you turned down a serious offer. Set the rota so that each day-team vet covers far fewer nights, or none.
4. **Negotiate a service arrangement with Thornby,** such as a per-visit fee or a retainer, so the service is paid for by the practices that rely on it.
5. **Check the dairy contracts in writing.** If they require 24-hour clinic cover, the after-1 am model fails that test and the plan has to be reconsidered. If they do not, the dairy risk is much smaller than Osei has presented it.

This changes the question from "open or close overnight" to "which hours need which staffing." Halloran's concern about attrition is addressed by changing who works nights and how many. Osei's concern about clients is addressed by keeping the hours where most of the demand is.

**Before 28 November, I would get three things**

- The exact wording of the four dairy contracts on out-of-hours clinic cover.
- A costing of the 7 pm to 1 am model with dedicated staff, compared against the current €190,000 loss.
- A written answer from Brackley on whether they can take referrals after 1 am, given what their director said about capacity.

**Residual tensions**

Some cost will remain, and it may still be higher than you can justify. The after-1 am model depends on Brackley's reliability, which you do not control. Recruiting emergency vets who will work evenings is not guaranteed, and the cheapest path may still lose some clients. If the contracts turn out to require a full 24-hour clinic, the choice between dairy income and the Kilbride cost becomes a real trade-off that the partners will need to decide on explicitly.

Moderate. The shape of the synthesis follows from your numbers, but the decision turns on two facts I do not have: the dairy contract terms and the true cost of a dedicated team.
