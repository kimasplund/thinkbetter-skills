## Amendments and rulings
- **Amendment 5 (owner decision, before this round ran):** after round 6 reverted the review mode, the owner approved a second attempt that keeps the checks and drops the delivery template. Round 6 failed on ceremony: executors turned the Deliver line ("the corrected answer first; then each issue that changed the conclusion...") into headings. Judges also flagged a rewritten draft and a change list that repeated each other, and "below is X, then Y" signposting. The candidate now describes what the reply must contain, not its layout, and does three things. It bans headings that describe the review itself. It asks for a rewritten draft or a list of fixes, not both. It adds one backstage check of the reply's own consistency. Fresh briefs are written, and the designer is told to avoid every earlier pack, round 6's included.
- **Audit check (after scoring, before apply):** the REVERT comes from the ceremony audit alone. The edit had 3 hits, and the shipped text and no skill had 1 each, so the edit was 2 over against a tolerance of one extra hit. The quotes are real and correctly attributed. This time none is a heading that describes the review. Two are closing summary sections ("Conclusion", "What I would tell the board"), and the shipped text drew a hit for the same kind of section. One is a "The question being answered" label, used where the reply points out that the draft answers an easier question. These are borderline calls, but the auditor is the pre-registered instrument, so the verdict stands. A FAIL is not INCONCLUSIVE, so there was no extension.
- **What the second attempt fixed:** the delivery-template headings disappeared. G1 (proportionality) rose from 5.00 for the shipped text to 5.83, and G2 (machinery leakage) from 6.83 to 7.00. The edit won the forced rankings 5–1 against no skill and 3–2 against the shipped text. Its key rate was again the best (0.98, against 0.92 shipped and 0.94 no skill). G3 (calibration) fell to 4.83 from 5.67. The gate counts that as noise, but it fell on two of the three briefs, and one judge noted inferences stated as fact. The two cherry-pick rounds together suggest the checks help flaw-finding but come with a closing-summary habit and a slight loss of calibration. Without an owner decision, no third round is planned.
