I'm CFO at Larchmont Scheduling. We sell booking software to physiotherapy clinics: 820 clinics are on our monthly plan at £90 a month and 330 are on our annual plan at £900 a year, paid upfront. Over the past three years, monthly clinics have churned at 25% a year and annual clinics at 6%.

Our head of revenue, Anya Petrov, wants to withdraw the monthly plan from 1 July, so each monthly clinic must move to annual at its next renewal or leave. The board asked me one question: a year from now, will we have more recurring revenue with this change than without it? Anya's note is below. I present on Tuesday. Can you check her reasoning?

---

**Moving to annual-only billing**

Annual clinics are our most loyal customers, churning at 6% a year against 25% on monthly. Once every clinic is on annual, churn across the whole base falls to 6%.

Clinics will accept the change. In the first quarter we tested annual-only pricing on new sign-ups, and 212 of the 360 clinics we quoted went ahead. On that evidence I expect at least 95% of our monthly clinics to switch.

Some clinics may leave, but that loses us nothing real. A clinic that values Larchmont will move to annual; a clinic that leaves rather than switch has shown it was never committed, so we would have lost it anyway.

Annual billing also brings cash forward and cuts card-processing fees.

Conclusion: annual clinics stay far longer and are worth more over their lifetime than monthly ones. Moving everyone to annual makes our customer base more valuable, and we should do it.
