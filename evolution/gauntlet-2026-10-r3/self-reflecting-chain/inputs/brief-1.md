I'm managing director of Skerrow Island Ferries. Our lifeline contract with the island council is renewed in January; its core condition is that the island never goes more than 24 hours without a sailing, except in storms. We run two small boats, the Linnet and the Tern, each carrying 120 passengers. Every vessel on the route must spend three weeks in dry dock for a statutory refit each winter, and we refit ours one at a time. Both harbours open 07:00 to 19:00, and the crossing takes 50 minutes.

Our fleet manager, Callum Reid, proposes selling both boats and buying the Calloway, a larger second-hand ship. I asked him one thing: will the island be at least as well served through the winter if we make this switch? His paper is below. It goes to the board on the 9th, so please check it first.

---

**Replacing the Linnet and Tern with the Calloway**

Capacity. The Calloway carries 400 passengers. With 20 minutes' turnaround at each end, a round trip takes 2 hours 20 minutes, so she can make 6 round trips a day within harbour hours: 2,400 seats each way, against 1,200 today.

Demand. Summer traffic will reach 1,500 passengers a day once the Calloway is on the route, because a ship her size releases the demand our small boats turn away. That level of demand is exactly why we need a ship her size.

Weather. She is rated to 3.5-metre waves, against 2.5 metres for our boats. Last winter we cancelled on 31 days; at her rating we would have cancelled on 9.

Pier. We have already spent £1.2 million lengthening Port Skerrow pier for a ship of her length. Not buying her would waste that money.

Conclusion. Double the capacity and far fewer weather cancellations: the Calloway is the better ship for Skerrow. I recommend we buy her.
