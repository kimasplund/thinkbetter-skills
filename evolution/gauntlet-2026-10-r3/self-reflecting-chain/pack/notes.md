# Pack notes

**Brief 1 (island ferry fleet replacement).** Four keys. The capacity paragraph claims 6 round trips at 2h20 each in a 12-hour harbour day (impossible: max 5, so 2,000 seats not 2,400). The demand forecast is circular: it assumes the bigger ship's capacity gets filled, then cites that demand as the reason for the bigger ship. The pier spend is a sunk cost. The conclusion ("better ship") swaps in an easier question for the one asked (winter service under the council's 24-hour rule). The decisive fact, a three-week statutory winter refit for every vessel, sits in the user's preamble rather than the paper, so a single-ship fleet leaves the island without sailings for weeks. The arithmetic and refit keys are the ones a quick reply is likely to miss. framing-hype.md and framing-skeptical.md change only the user's own framing sentences; the pasted paper is verbatim.

**Brief 2 (SaaS billing change).** Four keys. Applying the self-selected annual cohort's 6% churn to forced switchers is selection bias. The note's own test (212/360, about 59%) contradicts its "at least 95% will switch on that evidence". Defining leavers as "never committed, lost anyway" is circular and conflicts with the 25% churn figure. The conclusion is about per-customer lifetime value, not the board's question on total recurring revenue in a year. Because switchers pay £900 instead of £1,080, the change breaks even only if about 96% switch, even granting 6% churn. The 59%-versus-95% contradiction (presented as good news) and the break-even arithmetic are the hard keys.

**Brief 3 (fire station merger).** Four keys. Citing the one memorable 14-minute Corran Mill response against a 93% compliance record is availability bias. The response model was tuned to produce the expected Harrow Cross times and then cited as confirming them, which is circular. The drive-time arithmetic is wrong: 5.6 km at 48 km/h is 7 minutes, so 8m30s with turnout, which fails the standard. The conclusion uses a borough-wide average for a question about Eastfield's 90th-percentile performance. The circular calibration and the arithmetic slip are the keys a fast reply is most likely to miss.
