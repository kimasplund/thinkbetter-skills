Short answer: don't circulate it as written. The summary does not answer the question members asked, and its one direct calculation is wrong in a way that reverses its conclusion.

**1. The arithmetic error (most serious)**

5.6 km at 48 km/h is 7 minutes, not 5. (5.6 / 48 = 0.1167 hours = 7.0 minutes.) Adding the 90-second turnout gives 8 minutes 30 seconds, not 6 minutes 30 seconds. On the summary's own figures, a journey to the centre of the Eastfield estates is outside the 8-minute standard.

This is a single-point estimate, so it doesn't prove Harrow Cross would fail on 90% of calls. It does mean the summary's own evidence points the wrong way. The estate centre is only one point. Calls come from across the estates, and some will be nearer and some further than 5.6 km.

**2. The model "confirms" Harrow Cross meets the standard only because it was built to**

The summary says road speeds were set so the model reproduces the times expected from a ring-road site. That is circular. If the inputs are tuned to give the expected answer, the output can't confirm it. It tells you what the assumption implies, not whether the assumption is true.

The model needs to be validated against something independent. Examples are actual blue-light times on the roads that serve Eastfield, and how well it reproduces today's 93% at Eastfield station. Ask Rhona for the speed assumptions by road type. Also ask whether the ring-road speeds are justified by observed data. They look high compared with the 48 km/h average used in the next paragraph. The two paragraphs seem to use different speed assumptions.

**3. The borough average is the wrong measure**

Members asked whether Eastfield would still get a first engine within 8 minutes on at least 90% of emergency calls. The summary offers a borough-wide average first-engine time, falling from 7:40 to 7:05. That cannot answer the question, for two reasons.

- An average across the borough can improve while one area gets worse. Areas near the new site gain, and areas that lose their local station lose. The average hides that.
- An average is not the same as a percentage of calls within a threshold. A fast average can sit alongside a long tail of slow responses.

What members need is the modelled percentage of Eastfield calls reached within 8 minutes from Harrow Cross, compared with today's 93%. The summary never gives that figure. The conclusion that Eastfield residents "will be better protected" does not follow from a borough average.

**4. The Corran Mill fire doesn't support the case**

One incident, 14 minutes, tells you little about the station's overall record. Eastfield meets the standard on 93% of calls, so 7% of calls are already outside it, and some are bound to be long. The summary doesn't say whether Eastfield's own engine was the one that attended, whether it was already committed elsewhere, or whether the delay came from something else. Even if the station was at fault, the incident doesn't show Harrow Cross would have done better. It also gives the impression Eastfield underperforms, when its stated record is 93% against a 90% standard. I would remove the paragraph, or replace it with the station's actual performance data.

**5. Smaller points to check**

- The 90-second turnout should match what Harrow Cross crews would achieve. Whether the crews are wholetime or on-call matters, and the figure should be the same one used to measure today's 93%.
- Check that the 8 minutes is measured the same way before and after (from call answer or from mobilisation, to arrival of the first engine).
- The conclusion, "faster on average, so better protected", also ignores the 90% standard completely, and the standard is what members asked about.

**What I would do before the 21st**

1. Correct the journey-time calculation, or have Rhona show where the 5 minutes came from.
2. Get the modelled percentage of Eastfield calls within 8 minutes from Harrow Cross, shown against the 93% today and the 90% threshold. Ideally break this down by estate or by area of the Eastfield catchment.
3. Get the model validated independently, not tuned to the expected answer.
4. Report the same figure for the areas around Millgate, since those lose a local station too.
5. Cut or substantiate the Corran Mill paragraph.
6. Hold the recommendation until the above is in. If the corrected figure for Eastfield falls below 90%, members need to know that before voting, not after.

As it stands, circulating this would expose the Authority to a challenge on the vote. On the summary's own numbers, the stated 8:30 response time fails the standard members set.
