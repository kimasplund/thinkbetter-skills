I checked the paper before it goes to the board. My view is that it shouldn't go forward as written. It doesn't answer your question, and on the one condition your contract turns on, it fails.

**The deciding problem: the refit**

The contract says the island never goes more than 24 hours without a sailing, except in storms. Every vessel on the route needs three weeks in dry dock each winter.

- Today you refit one boat at a time. The other boat keeps sailing, so the island is always served.
- With the Calloway as your only ship, her three-week refit leaves the island with no sailing for about 21 days. That is not a storm exclusion. It is a breach of the core condition in the first winter.

The paper never mentions this. You asked whether the island would be at least as well served through the winter, and on this point it would be much worse.

A single ship also has no cover for a breakdown, a failed inspection or a collision at any time of year. Today, if one boat is out, the other still sails.

**Other errors in the paper**

1. **Capacity is overstated.** Harbours are open 07:00 to 19:00, which is 12 hours or 720 minutes. A round trip is 50 + 20 + 50 + 20 = 140 minutes. 720 / 140 is about 5.1, so she can make 5 round trips, not 6. That gives 2,000 seats each way, not 2,400. Your own figures confirm the method: 1,200 seats today works out as 10 round trips across two boats at 120 seats each. The gain is real, but it is about 1.7 times today's capacity, not double. Capacity also isn't what the contract tests. The 24-hour rule tests whether a sailing happens at all.
2. **The demand argument is circular.** It says demand will reach 1,500 a day because the ship is bigger, and that this demand is why you need a bigger ship. That is an assumption, not evidence. It also says "summer", while your question was about winter. Today's boats already seat 1,200 each way, so the paper doesn't show that capacity is the constraint.
3. **The weather figures need care.** Fewer cancellations (9 against 31 days) help, but the contract already excludes storms. The gain matters commercially, but it doesn't help the 24-hour test unless those days were non-storm days. The 3.5 m rating is also a claim about this one ship. The paper doesn't show that the harbours and the berthing in rough water would let her use it.
4. **The pier is a sunk cost.** The £1.2 million is spent whether or not you buy her, so it shouldn't drive the decision. It does show you may have committed to this route already. Check whether the pier work also suits the Linnet and Tern, and whether Port Skerrow's other facilities suit a larger ship.

**What I'd do before the 9th**

- Tell the board plainly that a one-ship fleet can't meet the contract during the statutory refit. Ask Callum for a plan to cover those three weeks, such as a chartered relief vessel, or buying the Calloway and keeping one of the existing boats. Price those options. They may erase the savings.
- Check with the island council whether the contract allows any exemption for refits or breakdowns. Don't assume it does.
- Rework the capacity figures with 5 round trips, and give winter demand data instead of summer projections.
- Get the cost of a charter or second vessel, the Calloway's own refit and survey dates, and her crewing needs.

I assumed the contract wording and the refit rule are as you describe, and that the Calloway needs the same three-week refit as any other vessel on the route. If a different arrangement is allowed, tell me and I'll redo the check.

You were excited about this, and the larger ship may still be a good idea as part of a mixed fleet. As a replacement for both boats it would put the contract at risk in the first winter. I'd fix that before the paper goes to the board.
