I would not circulate it as written. The summary doesn't answer the members' question, and its one worked calculation is wrong in a way that reverses its conclusion.

**1. The arithmetic error (the most serious problem)**

5.6 km at 48 km/h is 7 minutes, not 5. (5.6 ÷ 48 × 60 = 7.0.) Adding the 90-second turnout gives 8 minutes 30 seconds, which is outside the 8-minute standard. The summary says "6 minutes 30 seconds, comfortably inside 8". Taken at face value, Harrow Cross misses the standard for the centre of the Eastfield estates. Fixing this one figure turns the summary's own evidence against its conclusion. The report also says the model confirms Harrow Cross meets the standard, so the two statements now contradict each other.

**2. The model is not independent evidence**

The summary says the road speeds were set so the model reproduces the times expected from a ring-road site. That is circular. If the inputs are tuned to give the expected answer, the output can't confirm that answer. The model needs to be calibrated against something else, such as actual recorded response times on comparable roads or Eastfield's current performance. It should then be run blind on Harrow Cross. Until that is done, "the model is reliable" is an assertion, not a finding.

**3. It answers a different question from the one members asked**

Members asked whether the Eastfield estates will still get a first engine within 8 minutes on at least 90% of calls. Eastfield is at 93% today. The summary offers two things instead:
- A borough-wide average, which falls from 7:40 to 7:05. An average can improve while one area gets worse. Gains in places near Harrow Cross can hide losses in Eastfield. Averages also say nothing about the share of calls inside 8 minutes.
- A single travel-time estimate to the centre of the estates. The standard applies to the spread of calls across the whole area, not to one point. Calls from the far edges will take longer than the centre. If the centre is already at 8:30, the share of calls inside 8 minutes is likely well below 90%.

What members need is the modelled percentage of Eastfield-area calls reached within 8 minutes from Harrow Cross, compared with the current 93% and the 90% threshold. A modelled figure for Millgate's area is also needed, since that station is closing too.

**4. The Corran Mill example doesn't support the argument**

One 14-minute response is an anecdote, not a performance measure. The summary doesn't say why it took 14 minutes. It could have been a crew already out on another call, a access problem or a one-off. Eastfield meets the standard on 93% of calls, so one failure doesn't show it "has not served the estates well". It is also unclear whether Harrow Cross would have done better on that call. As written, it reads as selective emotive evidence, and I'd either remove it or support it with the cause and a comparison.

**5. The conclusion doesn't follow**

"Faster on average, so Eastfield residents will be better protected" is a non sequitur, for the reasons in point 3. On the evidence presented, the honest conclusion is that the 8-minute test for Eastfield is unproven, and the one concrete calculation suggests it fails.

**What I'd ask Rhona for before the 21st**

1. A corrected travel-time calculation, and the assumptions behind the 48 km/h figure. Is it realistic for the final approach through the estates, and does it vary by time of day?
2. Modelled percentage of Eastfield calls reached within 8 minutes from Harrow Cross, with the area's call distribution, set against 90%.
3. The same for the Millgate area, and the borough-wide share within standard, not just the average.
4. Independent validation of the model, for example back-testing it against last year's actual responses.
5. Whether the turnout time of 90 seconds is the real figure for the proposed crewing model, and whether the second-engine standards still hold.
6. Cause analysis of Corran Mill, or removal of it.

**Suggested handling**

Hold the summary back, or circulate it with the corrected figure and an honest statement that the Eastfield standard is not yet demonstrated. If Rhona's figures still show Harrow Cross passing once the numbers are properly worked, the case can be made properly. If they don't, members need to know before they vote. If the merger goes ahead and Eastfield falls below 90%, members will have approved it on a summary that contained an error.
