Anya's conclusion does not answer the board's question. Her note argues that annual clinics are more valuable over their lifetime. You were asked whether recurring revenue will be higher in a year. On the numbers you gave, it is higher only if at least 90% of your monthly clinics move to annual and stay. The only evidence in her note points to about 59%, which would leave you roughly £230k worse off.

**The year-ahead comparison**

I've looked only at your current clinics. I've assumed new sign-ups are the same either way, that converts pay £900, and that every monthly clinic has switched or left by a year from now.

- A monthly clinic is worth £1,080 a year (£90 x 12). An annual clinic is worth £900. Every clinic that switches therefore costs you £180 of annual recurring revenue, and her note never mentions this.
- Without the change, 25% of monthly clinics leave. That leaves about 615 clinics, or £664,200. The 330 annual clinics at 6% churn leave about 310, or £279,180. Total: about £943,000.
- With the change, annual clinics are unchanged at £279,180. Each monthly clinic that converts and stays adds £900.
- Break-even is therefore 664,200 / (820 x 900) = 90%. A monthly clinic that leaves costs you £1,080, so you can lose at most 10% of them to break even. Each percentage point of the 820 clinics is worth about £7,400.

| Monthly clinics that convert and stay | Recurring revenue a year from now | Against no change |
|---|---|---|
| 95% (Anya) | about £980k | +£37k |
| 90% | about £943k | 0 |
| 80% | about £870k | -£74k |
| 70% | about £796k | -£148k |
| 59% (her own test) | about £714k | -£230k |

Even her own figure produces a gain of only about 4%. A small shortfall in her 95% erases it.

**Where the note breaks down**

1. **The 95% is unsupported.** Her test shows 212 of 360, which is 59%, not 95%. It also tested new prospects who had no existing plan and no switching cost. Existing clinics are being forced off a plan they already use, so they could react either better or worse, and the test cannot tell you which. Taken at face value, the same test says 41% of new prospects walk away. That would cut new business as well, and my comparison above leaves that out. To judge it you need the acceptance rate when monthly was offered alongside annual.

2. **"A clinic that leaves was never committed, so we would have lost it anyway" is wrong.** Only 25% of monthly clinics leave in a typical year. A clinic that leaves because of the change is revenue you would otherwise have kept for most of that group. Calling the leavers uncommitted assumes the conclusion rather than showing it. Each one costs £1,080 of annual recurring revenue.

3. **The 6% churn figure does not carry over.** Annual clinics churn at 6% partly because the committed ones chose annual in the first place. Clinics pushed onto annual against their preference are a different group, and the 6% is no guide to how they will behave. This does not affect the one-year comparison, because converts will have prepaid and won't yet have reached a renewal. It does undercut her claim that churn across the whole base falls to 6%. It also undercuts the lifetime argument, which is about something other than the board's question.

4. **Cash and fees are not recurring revenue.** Upfront payment brings cash forward and cuts card fees. Neither adds to recurring revenue, so neither belongs in the answer to the board. They are fair benefits to mention separately.

**What I would tell the board**

Say that on current evidence the change is more likely to reduce recurring revenue a year from now than to raise it. The break-even is 90% of monthly clinics converting and staying, her own test suggests about 59%, and the upside even at 95% is small. Lifetime value may well favour annual, but that is a longer-horizon question.

The cheapest way to settle it before Tuesday is to measure how many existing monthly clinics will accept annual. Sample 30 to 50 of them, or ask your account team to rate each monthly clinic as likely to switch, unsure or likely to leave. Also pull the acceptance rate for new prospects offered both plans.

If you want a version that is more likely to pay off, consider keeping monthly and pricing it so annual is clearly better value. That keeps the 25% churn on the clinics that refuse, but it avoids losing a clinic at £1,080 for each one that walks away.
