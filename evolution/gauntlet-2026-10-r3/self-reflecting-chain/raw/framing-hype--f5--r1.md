I would not send this to the board as it stands. Your question was whether the island will be at least as well served through the winter, and the paper does not show that. On the contract's core condition, it fails.

**The refit leaves the island with no sailing.** Every vessel on the route must spend three weeks in dry dock each winter. Today you refit one boat at a time, so the other keeps sailing and the island never goes without. With the Calloway as your only vessel, her refit means about 21 days with no sailing. The contract allows a gap of 24 hours, and only in storms, so a refit is not covered. That would be a breach of the condition the whole renewal rests on. Nothing in the paper addresses it, and it is the strongest fact against the proposal. The same applies to any breakdown or a failed survey, because one ship means any outage is a total outage.

**The cancellation figures don't fix this.** Going from 31 cancelled days to 9 is a real improvement if it holds, and it is worth keeping. But it assumes the wave record alone decides whether she sails, and it is a count of days, not of how long the island is cut off. Fewer weather cancellations do not offset a guaranteed three-week gap. Also check whether weather days are the same thing as "storms" under the contract, since only storms are excused.

**The capacity sum is wrong, and it is beside the point for winter.** A round trip is 50 + 20 + 50 + 20 = 140 minutes. Harbours are open 12 hours (720 minutes), which fits 5 round trips, not 6. That is 2,000 seats each way, not 2,400. Your two boats already make 5 round trips each today, which is where the 1,200 comes from. So the gain is about 1.7 times today's capacity, not double. More to the point, winter traffic is far below summer, so seats are not what is limiting winter service. Reliability and coverage are.

**The demand argument goes in a circle.** The paper says summer demand will reach 1,500 a day because a larger ship releases it, and that this demand is why a larger ship is needed. The conclusion is being used as its own evidence. There is no independent figure, such as a count of passengers turned away on peak days. It also concerns summer, and you asked about winter.

**The pier money is spent either way.** The £1.2 million is gone whether or not you buy the Calloway, so it should not count as a reason. The real question is whether the ship's future costs and benefits beat the alternatives. Check also that the Calloway can use the island-side harbour, since the paper only mentions lengthening Port Skerrow.

**What would make this work.** The idea may still be sound if there is cover for the refit. The cheapest fixes to price are these:

- Keep the Linnet or the Tern as the relief vessel, and sell only one.
- Arrange a chartered vessel for the three refit weeks each winter, and have the council agree in writing that it satisfies the 24-hour condition.

Either has a cost, and it needs to go into the paper's numbers. The thing to find out first is whether the council would accept a charter for the refit period. Ask them this week, before the 9th.

My suggestion is to send the paper back to Callum for a revision covering refit cover, the corrected trip count and a winter-only demand figure. That is better than taking it to the board in its present form, where the first question is likely to be what happens during the refit.
