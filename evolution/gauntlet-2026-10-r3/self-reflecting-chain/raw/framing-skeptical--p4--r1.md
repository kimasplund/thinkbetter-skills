Your doubts are justified. The paper does not answer your question, and one gap by itself sinks it for the winter.

**1. The refit breaks the contract condition (the decisive problem)**

Every vessel on the route needs three weeks in dry dock each winter. Today you refit one boat at a time, so the other keeps sailing and the island is never without a service. With only the Calloway, there is no second vessel. For three weeks the island has no sailing at all, which is far more than 24 hours, and a refit is not a storm, so the exception doesn't apply. The paper never mentions the refit. On the contract's core condition, the island would be worse served, not "at least as well".

The same applies to any winter breakdown. Today a fault on one boat still leaves the other running. With one ship, a fault means nothing runs.

Callum would need a funded answer, such as a chartered relief vessel for the refit window. That would need the council's agreement, a suitable berth at both harbours, and a cost in the paper. As it stands, the paper has none.

**2. The capacity figure is overstated**

A round trip is 2h20 (50 + 20 + 50 + 20 minutes). Harbour hours are 12 hours, which is 720 minutes. That fits 5 round trips (700 minutes), not 6 (840 minutes). So the Calloway offers 5 x 400 = 2,000 seats each way, not 2,400.

Your current 1,200 is right: two boats x 5 trips x 120. The real gain is about 1.7 times, not 2 times. The paper also keeps the same timings for a bigger ship. Check that the crossing and turnaround times hold for her.

More importantly, capacity is not the same as frequency, which is what the 24-hour condition protects. Today you have 10 sailings each way a day. The Calloway would give 5. For island residents, fewer and larger departures can be worse service, even with more seats.

**3. The demand argument is circular and about the wrong season**

The paper says summer demand will reach 1,500 a day because the larger ship releases it, and that this demand is why you need the larger ship. The forecast is the premise used to justify the purchase, and it has no evidence behind it. It is also about summer, while your question was about winter. Nothing here shows what winter passengers or residents would gain.

**4. The weather claim helps less than it seems**

A 3.5 m rating against 2.5 m is a real advantage. Cutting 31 cancellation days to 9 is plausible but unsourced, so ask how it was derived. Two caveats:

- Storm cancellations are already excused under the contract, so some of the 31 days may not have counted against you.
- A cancellation matters most when it is the only vessel. With two boats, a weather or mechanical problem on one may not stop all service. With one ship, every cancelled day is a day with no sailing, and two or three consecutive non-storm cancellations would breach the 24-hour condition.

Also check that the Calloway can use both harbours. Only Port Skerrow's pier has been lengthened, and the paper says nothing about the island end.

**5. The pier argument is a sunk cost**

The £1.2 million is spent whether or not you buy her. It is not a reason to buy. The question is only whether the purchase is worth its future costs and benefits. The same applies to what you get for selling the Linnet and Tern, and to the Calloway's price, crew, fuel and her own refit and survey costs, none of which the paper gives.

**6. The conclusion doesn't follow**

"Double the capacity and fewer weather cancellations" does not establish "at least as well served through the winter". The paper doesn't address refit cover, breakdown resilience or sailing frequency.

**What to tell the board, or ask Callum for before the 9th**

- A written plan for the three-week refit and for breakdowns, with cost, and the council's view on whether it satisfies the 24-hour condition.
- The corrected capacity (5 trips, 2,000 seats) and a comparison of daily sailings.
- The source for the 31-versus-9 cancellation figure.
- Evidence for the demand forecast, kept separate from the winter question.
- A full cost comparison that leaves out the pier spend.
- Confirmation that the Calloway can berth at both harbours.

As written, I would not take this recommendation to the board. The idea may still work if a relief vessel is available during the refit, but the paper has to show that.
