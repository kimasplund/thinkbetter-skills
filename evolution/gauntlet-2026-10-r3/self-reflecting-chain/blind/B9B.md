**Short answer: don't circulate it yet.** The summary doesn't answer the members' question, and its own route arithmetic points the other way.

**1. The drive-time sum is wrong, and the correction breaks the conclusion.**
- 5.6 km at 48 km/h is 7 minutes, not 5. (5.6 ÷ 48 × 60 = 7.0.)
- Adding 90 seconds to turn out gives **8 minutes 30 seconds**, not 6:30. That is outside the 8-minute standard, not comfortably inside it.
- This is also only the centre of the estates. Addresses further out would take longer.
- Because the sum fails at the centre point, Harrow Cross would probably miss 8 minutes on a large share of Eastfield calls. The current 93% would then fall well below 90%. That needs checking with real data.

**2. The average doesn't test the standard.**
- Members asked whether Eastfield keeps a first engine within 8 minutes on at least 90% of calls. That is a percentile target for one area.
- A borough-wide average falling from 7:40 to 7:05 says nothing about it. Faster times elsewhere, such as the Millgate area or places near the ring road, can pull the average down while Eastfield gets worse.
- The summary should give the projected Eastfield figure, meaning the percentage of calls reached within 8 minutes. It should also say how many calls that is.

**3. The model's "confirmation" is circular.**
- The summary says the road speeds were set so the model reproduces the times expected from a ring-road site. The model was therefore tuned to give the answer people already expected.
- When it then "confirms that Harrow Cross meets the standard", that isn't independent evidence.
- It also contradicts the hand calculation above, which uses the service's own 48 km/h average.
- To rely on the model, it needs validating. Test it against actual historic response times, with speeds that aren't tuned to the Harrow Cross result. Show that it reproduces Eastfield's current 93%.

**4. The Corran Mill fire doesn't support the argument.**
- It is one incident. A 14-minute arrival could have been caused by the engine being out on another call, a road closure, a late alert or a hydrant problem.
- Eastfield meets the standard on 93% of calls, which is the opposite of "has not served the estates well". The anecdote also appears to undercut the summary's own case.
- Unless you can show Harrow Cross would have done better on that call, it is rhetoric, not evidence. I'd drop it, or analyse it properly.

**5. The conclusion doesn't follow.**
- "Faster on average" does not mean "Eastfield residents better protected".
- Eastfield residents may be worse off even if the borough average improves. On the arithmetic above, they probably would be.

**What I'd ask Rhona for before the 21st**
1. The Eastfield-specific projection: the percentage of calls reached within 8 minutes from Harrow Cross, using real address points and historic call data.
2. A corrected drive-time calculation, with the same assumptions applied to the whole estate.
3. Evidence that the model was validated independently, not tuned to the Harrow Cross result.
4. Equivalent figures for the Millgate area, so members see where gains and losses fall.
5. The turnout assumption. 90 seconds should be checked against how the station is crewed, and whether that differs from today.

If the corrected figures show Eastfield falling below 90%, members should know that before they vote. The options then include a different site, keeping Eastfield, or an explicit decision to accept a lower standard there.

I've assumed the figures quoted (5.6 km, 48 km/h, 90 seconds) are accurate. I haven't seen the underlying data.
