I wouldn't circulate it as it stands. The conclusion doesn't follow from the evidence, and the one calculation that speaks directly to your members' question points the other way.

**The question being answered.** Members asked whether the Eastfield estates keep a first engine within 8 minutes on at least 90% of calls. The summary answers a different question, whether the borough average gets faster. An average of 7:40 falling to 7:05 can happen while Eastfield drops from 93% to well under 90%, because gains elsewhere (presumably around the new site) can outweigh losses in the estates. Neither the borough mean nor "faster on average" says anything about a 90% threshold in one area. The summary needs Eastfield's own figure: the share of Eastfield-estate calls reached in 8 minutes under the merger, set against today's 93%.

**The model paragraph.** It says the road speeds were set so the model reproduces the times expected from a ring-road site, and that it then confirms Harrow Cross meets the standard. That is circular. If the inputs were tuned to give the expected answer, the output cannot count as independent confirmation. Either show the speeds were fitted to real blue-light runs (for example GPS data from recent incidents on those roads) and that the model reproduces Eastfield's actual 93% when run for today's layout, or say plainly that the merger result is an assumption. A back-test against today's known performance is the cheapest way to earn the word "reliable".

**The driving-time arithmetic.** 5.6 km at 48 km/h is 7 minutes, not 5 (5.6 ÷ 48 × 60 = 7.0). Add the 90 seconds to turn out and the centre of the estates is reached in 8 minutes 30 seconds, which is outside the standard, not "comfortably inside". This is the summary's own figure, so it undercuts the conclusion. It is also worse than it looks:
- The centre is a midpoint. Roughly half of Eastfield calls come from addresses further out, so they would take longer still.
- A 90% standard needs the 90th-percentile call inside 8 minutes, not the typical one. At 48 km/h and 90 seconds turnout, the most you can drive in 8 minutes is 5.2 km, and the call at the centre is already beyond it.
- A single average speed ignores time of day, junctions and ring-road congestion. If 48 km/h is a borough-wide average, it may not hold on the Harrow Cross to Eastfield run.

Unless Rhona has a faster verified route or a different turnout time, the honest reading of this paragraph is that Harrow Cross probably fails the Eastfield standard.

**Corran Mill.** One 14-minute response is a vivid case, not a rate. Eastfield meets the standard on 93% of calls today, which means roughly 7 in 100 are slower, and the fire in question is one of those. By itself it does not show that the station serves the estates badly, and it gives no reason to expect Harrow Cross would have done better on that call. It could have been a late-turning-out crew, a second engine, an engine already committed elsewhere, or a road problem that would affect Harrow Cross equally. Either drop it or give the cause, and show that the new site would have changed the outcome. As it stands it works against the 93% figure the members already know, so it will draw questions rather than settle them.

**Conclusion.** "Faster on average, so Eastfield residents will be better protected" is a leap on two counts: average to local threshold, and speed to protection (a mean says nothing about the slowest calls). The recommendation to approve is not supported by what is written.

**What a defensible version would contain**
1. Eastfield-estates calls reached in 8 minutes today (93%) and under the merger, from incident-level data and not a mean, with the estimate and its range.
2. The model's road speeds validated against real runs, and its reproduction of today's Eastfield figure.
3. The corrected drive time (7:00 driving, 8:30 with turnout, to the centre) and what the merger plan does about it, such as a faster route, a different turnout assumption or a mitigation in the estates.
4. Millgate's effect shown separately, since the closure of two stations affects overlap and second-engine cover.

If the validated figure for Eastfield comes out at 90% or above, the recommendation can stand and the case becomes much stronger. If it comes out below, members should hear that before the vote on the 21st, not after. Whichever it is, one piece of data, the incident-level Eastfield run for the merged layout, settles it.
