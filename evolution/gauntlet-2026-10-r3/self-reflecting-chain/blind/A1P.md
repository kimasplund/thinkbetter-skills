I wouldn't circulate it as it stands. The conclusion, that Eastfield residents will be better protected, isn't supported, and the one calculation that bears directly on members' question is wrong in a way that points the other way.

Members asked whether the Eastfield estates will still get a first engine within 8 minutes on at least 90% of calls. The summary answers a different question, whether the borough average gets faster. Here is how each part fares.

**The average.** A fall from 7:40 to 7:05 across the borough says nothing about Eastfield. Eastfield is one part of the borough, and an average can improve while one area gets worse. A site on the ring road can speed up many calls elsewhere while slowing the estates. The average also isn't the measure members set. They asked for a share of calls within 8 minutes, so the figure that answers them is the modelled percentage for Eastfield calls, set against today's 93%.

**The model.** The summary says the road speeds were set so the model reproduces the times expected from a ring-road site, and then that the model confirms Harrow Cross meets the standard. That is circular. The model was tuned to expect a good result, so it can't confirm one. The reliability claim needs a test the model could fail. Run it on today's stations, Eastfield and Millgate, with the same speed settings, and compare its output with the actual response times for the last two or three years. If it gives about 93% for Eastfield, that is real evidence. If it doesn't, the Harrow Cross numbers can't be relied on.

**The drive-time sum.** 5.6 km at 48 km/h takes 7 minutes, not 5. Five minutes would need about 67 km/h. Adding the 90 seconds to turn out gives 8 minutes 30 seconds, which is over the standard and not "comfortably inside 8". That is the estates' centre point. If it takes 8:30 to reach the centre, a large share of calls to the estates will take longer than that, and 90% within 8 minutes looks out of reach on the summary's own figures. This is the key point, and it undercuts the recommendation. If a faster speed is justified for this route, for example from measured blue-light runs on the ring road, the summary should say so and show the evidence. It should also confirm that the 8-minute clock starts at the same point as the 93% figure does. If that figure counts from call handling and the 6:30 doesn't, the two aren't comparable.

**Corran Mill.** One 14-minute response is a vivid case, but it doesn't outweigh a station that meets the standard on 93% of calls. It also contradicts the claim that Eastfield "has not served the estates well". If anything, the 93% shows it serves them well. The summary doesn't say which engine arrived first at Corran Mill or why it took 14 minutes. If Eastfield's engine was out on another call and the first engine came from further away, the incident says nothing about the station. Nor does it show Harrow Cross would have been quicker. I would cut it, or replace it with the incident's cause and a statement of how a Harrow Cross engine would have done.

**The conclusion.** "Faster on average, so Eastfield residents will be better protected" doesn't follow from anything above. It needs Eastfield's own modelled performance, and the summary doesn't give it.

What I would send instead:

- Lead with the Eastfield figure: the modelled percentage of Eastfield calls reached within 8 minutes from Harrow Cross, against 93% today. Give it for the estates specifically, and ideally for the worst-served part of them.
- Correct the drive-time sum to 7 minutes plus 90 seconds, or support a faster speed with measured runs.
- Validate the model against actual results at Eastfield and Millgate before calling it reliable.
- Keep the borough average as secondary context, labelled as such.
- Make the recommendation depend on the Eastfield result. If it falls below 90%, say so and tell members what would close the gap, such as a different site, keeping one station, or an additional appliance.

The cheapest check, since the vote is on the 21st, is to ask Rhona for the Eastfield-only modelled result and the model's backtest against actual Eastfield times. That is probably a day's work, and it settles the question members asked.
