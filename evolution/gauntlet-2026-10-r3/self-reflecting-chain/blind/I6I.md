I would not circulate it as written. The one question members asked was whether Eastfield keeps a first engine within 8 minutes on at least 90% of calls. The summary doesn't show that, and its one calculation on the point is wrong.

**1. The drive-time arithmetic is wrong, and the correction flips the result.**
5.6 km at 48 km/h takes 7 minutes, not 5 (5.6 ÷ 48 × 60 = 7.0). Adding the 90 seconds to turn out gives 8 minutes 30 seconds. That is outside the 8-minute standard, not "comfortably inside". To get 5 minutes over 5.6 km you would need about 67 km/h.

This is also only the journey to the centre of the estates. Calls from the far side of the estates will take longer. The standard is about 90% of calls, so a centre-point figure that is already over 8 minutes suggests Eastfield falls below 90%, not above it. I can't tell you the exact figure from this, but the summary's own numbers point that way.

**2. The model's "confirmation" doesn't count as evidence.**
The summary says the road speeds were set "so that it reproduces the times we expect" for a ring-road site, and then that the model confirms Harrow Cross meets the standard. That is circular. If the inputs were tuned to produce the expected answer, the output can't confirm it. Members will ask what the model says when it is calibrated against actual recorded response times, such as Eastfield's own incident data. The summary should say how the speeds were set, and it should show Harrow Cross results with speeds validated against real journeys. The 48 km/h average also needs checking against the ring road and the estate streets separately, since the two will differ.

**3. A borough-wide average doesn't answer the Eastfield question.**
The fall from 7:40 to 7:05 is an average across the whole borough. An average can improve while one area gets worse, for example if the new station helps a dense area near the ring road and the Eastfield estates lose their nearest engine. The question was a specific percentage for a specific area. The summary should give the share of Eastfield calls reached within 8 minutes under the merger, compared with today's 93%. Also say what happens to cover in the Millgate area, as that is part of the same vote.

**4. The Corran Mill fire doesn't support the point, and it needs care.**
One incident, with no cause given, can't show that Eastfield station "has not served the estates well". Today it meets the standard on 93% of calls, so a single 14-minute response is consistent with it being one of the other 7%. You would need to know why it took 14 minutes (the station's own engine was out on another call, a road closure, a late alert, or something else). If the cause was a second call or a mobilising delay, a different station location wouldn't fix it. Using it to imply the existing service is poor is also a risk if the paragraph is challenged in the meeting. I would either remove it or replace it with data on the whole distribution of Eastfield response times.

**5. The conclusion doesn't follow.**
"Faster on average, so Eastfield residents will be better protected" assumes the borough average describes Eastfield, and the evidence above suggests it may not. On the figures given, the honest statement is that the merger improves the borough average and may take Eastfield below the standard members set. A recommendation to approve can't rest on that.

**What I would do before circulating**
- Correct the journey calculation (7 minutes driving, 8:30 in total) and say what that means for the standard.
- Re-run the model for the Eastfield estates, with road speeds validated against recorded incident times, and report the percentage of calls within 8 minutes against the 93% today. Show it by part of the estates, not just the centre.
- Report the same figure for the Millgate area.
- Replace the Corran Mill paragraph with the cause of that delay, or drop it.
- Redraft the conclusion to match the result. If Eastfield falls below 90%, members need to know, with any mitigation (for example a different site, a retained appliance, or a revised standard) set out as a separate decision.

Ms Baird may have the correct figure elsewhere, and the 5-minute figure may simply be a slip. But as it stands, the summary would tell members the standard is met when its own numbers show it isn't, and that is better corrected before the 21st than raised from the floor.
