Short answer: the paper does not show that the island will be at least as well served through the winter. As written, the switch would put you in breach of the contract's core condition every winter, so I would not send it to the board as it stands.

**1. The refit problem (decisive)**
- Every vessel on the route needs three weeks in dry dock each winter. Today you refit one boat at a time, so the other boat keeps sailing.
- With only the Calloway, her refit leaves the island with no sailing for about 21 days. That is far beyond the 24-hour limit, and a refit is not a storm, so the exception doesn't apply.
- Any long breakdown has the same effect. Today a breakdown of one boat leaves the other running.
- The paper never mentions this. It is the exact question you asked, so it needs a worked answer before the board sees it. The options are a chartered or relief vessel for the refit period (with its cost and availability), keeping one of the existing boats, or a written agreement with the council. The paper has none of these.

**2. The capacity arithmetic is wrong**
- A round trip is 50 + 20 + 50 + 20 = 140 minutes. Harbours are open for 12 hours, which is 720 minutes. 720 / 140 is about 5.1, so she can make 5 round trips, not 6. Six would need 840 minutes.
- That gives 5 x 400 = 2,000 seats each way, not 2,400.
- Your 1,200 figure for today implies 5 round trips per boat (5 x 120 x 2 boats), so the comparison is 2,000 against 1,200. That is still a real gain in seats, but about 17% smaller than claimed.
- Sailings per day each way fall from 10 to 5. The island would have half as many departures, with longer gaps between them and more passengers affected by each cancellation. More seats don't make up for that.
- Check also that the 20-minute turnaround is realistic for a 400-passenger ship, and that the mainland harbour can take her. The paper mentions only the Port Skerrow pier.

**3. The demand argument is circular and off-topic**
- The paper says demand will reach 1,500 a day because the ship is big, and that ship size is needed because of that demand. This is an assumption, not evidence.
- It is a summer figure, but you asked about winter. Winter demand is not shown, and it is probably much lower.
- It is also unclear whether 1,500 is one-way or both ways.
- Winter service is limited by frequency and resilience, not by seats, so extra seats don't help there.

**4. The weather figures need support**
- "9 cancellation days instead of 31" assumes every cancelled day last winter had waves between 2.5 and 3.5 metres, and that a 3.5 m rating means she would have sailed on all of them. The paper doesn't show this. Wind, visibility and berthing limits also stop sailings.
- Even if the figures hold, it is a gain of 22 days a year, set against a refit gap of 21 days. Cancellations in a storm are exempt from the contract, so the weather benefit may matter less than it looks. The refit gap is not exempt.
- With one ship, each remaining cancellation also means no sailing that day, whereas two boats can sometimes sail in a gap in the weather.

**5. The pier is a sunk cost**
- The £1.2m is already spent and won't be recovered whichever way you decide, so it isn't a reason to buy. The real question is whether the Calloway is the best use of the money from here, compared with the alternatives.
- Also check whether the lengthened pier is useful with the existing boats. It may be.

**What I'd tell the board**
- The paper claims "double the capacity", but the real figure is about 1.7 times. It doesn't answer the question you asked, and the plan as written breaches the contract during the refit.
- Ask Callum for a revised paper covering: the refit and breakdown cover (with costs), a corrected timetable (5 round trips, and what 5 departures a day means for the island), winter-only demand figures, the evidence behind the weather numbers, the mainland berth, and a comparison with the alternatives (keeping one boat plus the Calloway, for example). Keeping at least two hulls is the obvious way to meet the 24-hour condition.

Assumptions: I took the 12-hour harbour window, 50-minute crossing, 20-minute turnaround and three-week refit as stated. I assumed the refit can't be done in a few days or split up, and that the first sailing leaves at 07:00 at the earliest.
