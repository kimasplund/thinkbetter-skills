# Reasoning checks

Run these on a finished answer in review mode, and on each step while building a chain. They are backstage: the reply reports what was wrong and how it was fixed, in the problem's own words. It never shows check names, this list, or a pass log.

## 1. Circular reasoning

The conclusion, or a restatement of it, is used as one of its own premises.

- **Test:** remove every premise that just rewords the conclusion. Does anything still support it?
- **Fix:** supply independent evidence, or say plainly that the claim is assumed rather than shown.

## 2. Named biases

| Bias | Test | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmation | Was evidence against the conclusion looked for, or only evidence for it? | Name the strongest fact against it and say why it does not decide the case |
| Anchoring | Does an early number or first option shape everything after it? | Re-derive the estimate from scratch, or compare it with a second reference point |
| Availability | Does a vivid or recent case stand in for how often something happens? | Ask for, or state, the base rate |
| Base-rate neglect | Is a specific story judged without how common the outcome is? | Start from the base rate, then adjust for the specifics |
| Sunk cost | Does past spending argue for continuing? | Judge only future costs against future benefits |
| Overconfidence | Is a confident claim resting on one source, or on none? | Lower the confidence, or name what would confirm it |
| Framing | Would the conclusion change if the same facts were put hopefully or doubtfully? | Restate the facts neutrally and decide again |
| Survivorship | Are only the cases that succeeded, or that were reported, being counted? | Ask what happened to the cases that are not visible |

## 3. Contradictions

- Numbers that do not add up, or that change between sections.
- Dates or steps out of order: something happening before what it depends on.
- An "all", "never" or "always" claim contradicted elsewhere in the same answer.
- **Fix:** keep the version the facts support, correct the other, and recheck anything derived from the wrong one.

## 4. Drift from the question

- Restate in one line the question the answer must settle.
- Every section should serve that question. Cut or shorten what does not.
- The conclusion must answer that exact question, not a nearby, easier one.

## 5. Counterfactual probe

- What would have to be true for the opposite conclusion to hold?
- If the facts given rule it out, say which fact does.
- If they don't, name it as the key uncertainty, and give the cheapest way to check it.

## 6. Unsupported leaps

- A step whose conclusion needs a fact that was not given is an assumption, not a finding.
- **Fix:** state it as an assumption, or drop the step.
