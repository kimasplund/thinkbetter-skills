I'm head of highways at Wenlow District Council. Ferrisford Bridge, a three-arch stone bridge built in 1794, carries the main road into town, about 9,000 vehicles a day. Our August 2026 inspection found cracking in the central arch and scour under the central pier. We've put on an emergency 26-tonne limit, which our engineers say is safe for about two years. Cabinet picks the long-term answer in December.

The consultants' options:

- Demolish it and build a new single-span bridge: €5.2M over 20 months, including two summers of work in the river. The regional infrastructure fund pays 75% of new or replacement structures if the money is spent by March 2028, so our share would be €1.3M. Our cabinet member for transport wants this.
- Strengthen the arches and armour the pier: €2.4M, one summer in the river. The fund doesn't cover repairs, so it's all ours.
- Impose a permanent 7.5-tonne limit and send heavier traffic round by Cott Lane, which adds 6 km. Signs and enforcement only, about €150k. Our finance director's favourite.
- Hire a temporary steel bridge alongside for three years (€0.9M) and decide later.
- Turn it into a footbridge and build a new crossing at Holm Ford, 1 km upstream: €7M, about three years.

Also: since the emergency limit went on, the 32-tonne quarry tippers have used Cott Lane, which dips under the railway through a 4.0 m arch, without any trouble. The Ferris is a salmon river, so the Environment Board only allows work in the channel from July to September. Ferris Valley Coaches' two double-deckers bring 380 pupils to Wenlow High over the bridge each day. And the bridge has been on the national heritage register since 1971; it's on every postcard of the town.

Which should I recommend?
