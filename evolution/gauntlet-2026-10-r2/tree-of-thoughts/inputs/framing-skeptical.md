I'll be honest: I'm not convinced there's a good answer here. I chair the Aldmere Running Trust, the charity behind the Aldmere Marathon and Half, held on the last Sunday of June. About 8,400 people run the marathon and 5,000 the half. The last two races were hot: 30 °C by 11:00, around 140 runners treated each year, and this June one man spent two days in intensive care. Our insurer will only renew if we have a credible heat plan, and the board picks a direction on 18 November. I'm wary of anything that just sounds good in a board meeting.

The proposals, for what they're worth:

- Move to mid-April, when mornings average 9 °C. Corin, our race director, is all for it.
- Keep the date but start at 6:00 instead of 8:30. This was the insurer's suggestion.
- Switch to a Saturday-evening race starting at 19:00. Our headline sponsor loves the idea of a floodlit finish.
- Reroute the middle 20 km onto the Osier towpath, shaded nearly all the way. Our medical lead, Dr Ilka Brandt, prefers this.
- Keep everything, add a heat-cancellation rule and double the cooling stations.
- Drop the marathon and keep only the half.

Some background, not that it makes this any easier. Marathon entries are up 40% since 2023, when the national federation moved its championship to October and set the qualifying window at 1 May to 30 September; most of the growth is club runners chasing a qualifying time. About three-quarters of our runners arrive by train on race morning, and the start is a ten-minute walk from Aldmere Central (first Sunday arrival 06:50). We keep a six-hour cut-off so walkers can finish, which only just fits the city's events policy: full road closures only on Sunday mornings, 05:00 to 14:30. The towpath also hosts a Saturday 5k, which the navigation trust caps at 500 runners because the path is 2.5 m wide.

I don't want a sales pitch. Be blunt: what's our best option, if we have one?
