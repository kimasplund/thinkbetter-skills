**Recommendation: move the race to a Sunday in early-to-mid May, keep the 08:30 start and the marathon, and add a heat-cancellation rule plus extra cooling stations as backup.** This is a variant of Corin's idea. April is out, but May gets most of the same cooling benefit.

Why May and not April: the federation's qualifying window runs 1 May to 30 September. A mid-April race would no longer count for qualifying, and club runners chasing times drive most of your 40% growth in marathon entries. Any date from 1 May onward keeps that draw. Cooler spring mornings should also cut heat illness substantially, which is the root cause. The other proposals only manage the heat.

Why not the others:
- **06:00 start in June:** about three-quarters of your runners come by train, and the first Sunday train reaches Aldmere Central at 06:50. They would miss the start unless the rail operator adds early services, which you don't control. The finish would also still run into the late morning heat, since 06:00 plus a six-hour cut-off is 12:00. It stays as the fallback if May fails the checks below.
- **Saturday 19:00 start:** the city allows full road closures only on Sunday mornings. A six-hour cut-off would also push the last finishers to 01:00. The sponsor's floodlit finish can't be delivered under the current policy.
- **Osier towpath:** the path is 2.5 m wide and the navigation trust caps the Saturday 5k at 500 runners. You have 13,400 entrants, so this is not workable, however good the shade.
- **Keep everything, add cancellation and more cooling stations:** this leaves roughly the same number of runners in 30 °C heat. It also means you might cancel on the day, with 13,400 people already travelling. It is worth keeping as a supplement but doesn't answer the insurer by itself.
- **Drop the marathon:** the half would still run in the heat, and you would lose the growing part of the event and its income.

**Steps before 18 November**
1. Pull ten years of weather records for 11:00 on Sundays from 3 to 24 May, and choose the date with the best cool-morning record. Aim for a date where 11:00 rarely tops about 18 °C.
2. Get written confirmation from the federation that a race on that date counts for the qualifying window, and ask whether the course certification needs any change.
3. Ask the city for a Sunday road-closure slot on that date. The 05:00 to 14:30 window already fits the 08:30 start and the six-hour cut-off.
4. Take the date, the cancellation rule (with clear temperature or wet-bulb triggers and a plan for the day) and the doubled cooling stations to the insurer, with Dr Brandt's sign-off on the medical plan. Her shade concern can be met with extra water, ice and shade at fixed points on the existing route.
5. Give the sponsor an alternative: a finish-line evening event or a lit finish arch with a post-race celebration. Brief Corin on the reason for May rather than April.

**Assumptions and what would change the answer**
- *May mornings are cool enough.* If records show frequent heat above about 20 °C by 11:00, use the 06:00 start in June instead, which needs the check below. Cheapest check: the weather lookup in step 1.
- *Early-season entrants can be ready.* The training block moves about six weeks earlier, so check whether entries or the field would drop. Cheapest check: ask your last year's entrants in a short survey.
- *The federation counts races from 1 May.* If it does not, or May clashes with another major marathon, use the June route. Cheapest check: one email to the federation.
- *The insurer accepts a date change as a credible plan.* If not, ask exactly what they need. Cheapest check: send a one-page outline now, not on 18 November.
- *If you fall back to June with a 06:00 start:* it only works if the rail operator will run trains arriving by about 05:15. Cheapest check: one call to the operator.

I am fairly confident in May. The deciding unknown is whether the weather record for early to mid May is actually much cooler than late June.
