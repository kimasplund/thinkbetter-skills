**Why each proposal fails on your own facts**

1. **Mid-April (Corin).** The qualifying window runs 1 May to 30 September, so an April race gives no qualifying times. Most of your 40% marathon growth is club runners chasing those times, so you would lose the entries that drive growth and income. Cooler weather is the right idea, but April is the wrong month.
2. **6:00 start (insurer).** About three-quarters of runners come by train, and the first Sunday train arrives at 06:50. Add the ten-minute walk and most of your field can't be at a 6:00 start. It would only work with new early trains, and that is unlikely for 13,400 people. The direction is right, though, because it moves the run away from the 11:00 peak.
3. **Saturday 19:00.** City policy allows full road closures only on Sunday mornings, 05:00-14:30. A six-hour cut-off would also run past 01:00, in the dark. The evening is still warm in late June, so it only partly solves the heat problem. The sponsor will like the floodlit finish, but you can't get the permit.
4. **Osier towpath (Dr Brandt).** The path is 2.5 m wide and the navigation trust caps a 5k there at 500 runners. You could not send 13,400 people along it, and the Saturday 5k is another conflict. Shade is valuable, so look for shaded or tree-lined sections elsewhere, or add shade structures. The towpath itself is not workable.
5. **Cancellation rule plus double cooling.** This helps with safety but doesn't reduce heat exposure. Cancelling on the morning would strand thousands of train arrivals and cost you the entry fees. The insurer is unlikely to accept this alone as a credible plan. It works as a backup.
6. **Drop the marathon.** This cuts the number of people running in the heat at its worst, but it gives up your growth and your qualifier appeal. It is a last resort if the other measures fail.

**What I'd propose instead**

- **Date.** Pick a Sunday in May, ideally the second half of May, once 1 May has passed. That keeps qualifying status and is much cooler than late June. Corin gets his cooler morning and you keep the club runners.
- **Start time.** Aim for 07:00-07:30. This works with the 06:50 train plus the walk only if the start is no earlier than about 07:15. Ask the rail operator for one or two earlier Sunday trains, which would let you start at 07:00. A 07:00 start with a six-hour cut-off finishes at 13:00, inside the 14:30 closure window. That gives you spare time, so you can trim the course-closure plan.
- **Heat plan for the insurer.** Set a temperature trigger such as a wet-bulb globe temperature (WBGT) threshold. Define what happens at each level: more water, cooling stations and medics, a later cut-off or a shortened course, and a cancellation rule only for extreme days. Doubled cooling stations and a medical plan signed off by Dr Brandt would make this credible. Add the shaded course sections she wants wherever the roads allow.
- **Shorter half-marathon exposure.** Let the half start slightly later or earlier, whichever spreads the load, since its runners finish well before the heat peaks.

**Assumptions to check before 18 November**

- May mornings average about 12-15 °C. I haven't seen your data, so confirm it, and check that a heat-risk day in May is rare enough.
- The city will approve a new date, and no clash with other events or with the federation's calendar.
- The rail operator can run earlier trains.
- The insurer accepts this plan. Show it to them before the board meets.

If May is refused, the fallback is a 07:15 start in late June with the full heat protocol. The insurer may still hesitate, and it leaves most of the heat risk in place.
