There is a best option, but it is not the one anyone is championing. It is an earlier start, as far forward as the trains allow, plus a heat protocol. Four of your six proposals fail on constraints you gave me. I've assumed both races currently start around 8:30.

**Eliminated by your own facts**

- **Mid-April:** The qualifying window runs 1 May to 30 September. An April race gives no qualifying times, and club runners chasing a qualifier are most of your 40% growth. You would lose the marathon's growth engine to fix the heat. Corin likes it because it is the easiest race to direct, but it isn't the best for the Trust.
- **Saturday 19:00:** Full road closures are allowed only on Sunday mornings. This doesn't fit the events policy, so it can't be done. A floodlit finish is also a poor reason to run 8,400 people into a warm evening. It would look good on the sponsor slides and add a darkness risk.
- **Osier towpath:** The path is 2.5 m wide, and the navigation trust caps a 5k at 500 runners. You would be sending 13,400 runners down it, with 20 km of marathon pack on a single narrow route. Shade is valuable, but this route can't carry the field. It might work as a short shaded section on a wider path. Ask Dr Brandt what she is really after, which is probably shade at the hot hours and a reachable medical access route. You can get some of that by other means.
- **Drop the marathon:** This cuts the exposure, but it also removes the qualifier-driven growth and probably much of your income. It is a last resort. Don't choose it before you've tried the options below.

**What's left**

1. **Earlier start (the insurer's idea).** It is right in principle, because heat accumulates on slower runners. The 6:00 start as proposed doesn't work. The first Sunday train arrives at 06:50, and three-quarters of your runners come by train. At 6:00 most of the field would miss the start, or you would be asking them to find another way in. Even 7:00 is tight, because 06:50 plus a ten-minute walk leaves no time for bag drop and toilets.

   What the timetable allows today is about 7:30. A 7:30 start finishes the six-hour cut-off at 13:30, inside the 14:30 closure. It gains one hour, which helps but does not solve the problem. A typical 4:15 marathoner would still finish near 11:45, after the temperature has passed 30 °C. So an hour is not enough on its own.

2. **Get the start earlier by fixing the trains.** This is the work that matters. Ask the rail operator for one or two extra early services on race day, from about 05:15 to 05:45 arrival, to allow a 06:30 to 07:00 start. If they say no, consider a shuttle from a station with earlier services, or an optional runner hotel or hostel block. Your insurer proposed the date, so ask them to support the request to the operator. That would be a useful use of their goodwill.

3. **Heat protocol, which you need anyway.** Your insurer wants a credible plan, and a cancellation rule on its own isn't one. Build it around a measured heat index (wet-bulb globe temperature) forecast, with set thresholds on the days before and on the morning:
   - Moderate: extra water points, a pace advisory, more medical cover.
   - High: marathon cut-off shortened, medics at more points, an option to defer.
   - Extreme: delay, shorten or cancel, with the rules published in advance and the entry terms written to match.

   The cancellation rule is cheap, and it needs to be written so that entrants know it in advance. Doubling the cooling stations helps, but don't treat it as the answer. It helps people who are already running in heat. It doesn't reduce the number of runners exposed to the hottest hours.

**My recommendation:** Pursue an earlier start of 06:30 to 07:00, conditional on securing early trains. Fall back to 07:30 if you can't. Pair either with the heat protocol, and tighten the cut-off or use a rolling sweep if conditions go above your threshold. Take this to the board on 18 November as one package: the date and time, a rail agreement in progress, and the protocol. Ask the board to approve the start-time range now and delegate the final fix to a date once the rail operator answers. That gives the insurer something specific.

**What I can't tell from here**

- How much cooler 6:30 to 7:30 is than 8:30 on your hot days. Look at your own hourly temperature records for the last two Junes. If 7:30 still isn't cool enough, the board needs to face the harder choices sooner.
- Whether the half should start earlier too. I'd suggest staggering the two so the half field doesn't crowd the marathon start, but that depends on your course.
- Whether the events policy would allow an earlier closure. You said closures start at 05:00, so the closure window shouldn't block a 06:30 start.

If the rail operator refuses and 7:30 isn't enough, the next option would be capping marathon entries or shortening the cut-off. That should be the board's second conversation, and I would have it before reaching for the April date or dropping the marathon.
