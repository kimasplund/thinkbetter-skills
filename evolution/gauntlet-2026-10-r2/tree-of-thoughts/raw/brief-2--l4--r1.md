**Recommend strengthening the arches and armouring the pier (€2.4M), with the work done in the July to September 2027 river window.** It is the only option that fits the river-work window and keeps the school buses running. It also keeps the bridge, which is on the heritage register. Keep the 26-tonne limit until the work is finished.

**Why not the others**

- **Demolish and rebuild (€5.2M, council share €1.3M).** The grant deadline looks unreachable. The river is only workable July to September, so the first summer is 2027 and the second is 2028. That puts the second summer, and so the last of the spend, after March 2028. A 20-month programme starting in early 2027 would also finish around autumn 2028, which is when the two-year safe period on the emergency limit runs out. A demolition of a 1794 registered bridge would also need heritage consent, and that is far from certain. If the fund confirms in writing that spend in the 2028 summer qualifies, this option comes back into play (see the checks below).
- **Permanent 7.5-tonne limit (€150k).** The two Ferris Valley Coaches double-deckers exceed 7.5 tonnes. Cott Lane's 4.0 m railway arch is almost certainly too low for them, so 380 pupils would have no obvious route to Wenlow High. The limit does nothing about the scour under the pier, which is a river problem and not a traffic-load problem. It would also push every bus, refuse lorry and delivery vehicle onto a 6 km detour. The quarry tippers using Cott Lane is not evidence that the detour works for other vehicles. It only shows that vehicles under 4.0 m can use it.
- **Temporary steel bridge (€0.9M, three years).** It carries traffic but repairs nothing. You would still have to pay for a permanent fix afterwards, and it would still need river work in the same July to September window.
- **Footbridge plus Holm Ford crossing (€7M, about three years).** It costs the most and takes the longest. It also misses the March 2028 grant deadline, moves the main road away from the town, and gives up the bridge.

**Steps**

1. **Now to December.** Do the groundwork before Cabinet votes, because the 2027 window is the only one before the emergency limit expires.
   - Commission the detailed design and a scour assessment.
   - Open pre-application talks on heritage consent and on the Environment Board's in-channel permit.
   - Get the engineers to state in writing the load class and design life the strengthening delivers.
2. **December.** Ask Cabinet to approve €2.4M plus a contingency. Present it to the transport member as keeping the town's best-known landmark and as a bridge that stays in service. Present it to the finance director as the cheapest option that doesn't strand the school buses.
3. **Winter 2026-27.** Monitor the pier for scour, and agree a flood trigger for closing or restricting the bridge.
4. **July to September 2027.** Carry out the works, which fall mostly in the school summer holiday. Ask the contractor for a traffic plan that keeps one lane open. If the engineers say the bridge must close, hire the temporary bridge for this period only. Cott Lane is not an option for the double-deckers.
5. **After completion.** Lift the 26-tonne limit to the strengthened rating.

**Assumptions, what would change the answer, and the cheapest check**

- **The grant deadline means spend, not commitment.** If the fund will accept spend through 2028 or grant an extension, the council's share drops to €1.3M against €2.4M, and demolition becomes the cheaper choice. It would still carry the heritage-consent risk. Check: email the fund this week and ask for a written answer on the 2028 summer spend.
- **Consents can be obtained in time for a July 2027 start.** If the heritage body or the Environment Board signals a delay, the work slips to 2028 and the temporary bridge becomes necessary. Check: hold pre-application meetings in the next month.
- **Strengthening gives a long, full-load life.** If the engineers say it only buys 10 to 15 years or needs a permanent weight limit, the case for replacement strengthens. Check: get the design life in writing before the Cabinet paper goes out.
- **The double-deckers are taller than 4.0 m.** Check: confirm their height with Ferris Valley Coaches and ask them for any alternative route they can use.
