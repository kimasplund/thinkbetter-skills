**Recommendation: move the race to a Sunday in mid-May (for example the second or third Sunday), keep the 8:30 morning start, and keep a heat-cancellation rule as a backstop.**

This is close to Corin's April idea, but it keeps the 1 May to 30 September qualifying window. That window matters because your 40% marathon growth is mostly club runners chasing a qualifying time. An April race would no longer count for them, and you would likely lose the growth you're proudest of. May also fits everything else you described. It is a Sunday morning, so the city's 05:00 to 14:30 closure rule applies, and trains still bring three-quarters of runners to a start ten minutes from Aldmere Central. Mid-May mornings should be far cooler than June, but that is my assumption, so check it (see below).

**Why not the others**

- **April:** the race would fall outside the qualifying window, as above.
- **6:00 start (the insurer's idea):** the first Sunday train arrives at 06:50, so most runners could not reach the start. It only works if the rail operator adds early services, and nothing in your message suggests that is on offer.
- **Saturday evening:** full road closures are allowed only on Sunday mornings, so the city's policy rules this out. The sponsor's floodlit finish would need a policy exception and a new transport plan.
- **Osier towpath:** the path is 2.5 m wide and the navigation trust caps even the Saturday 5k at 500 runners. Putting around 13,000 runners on it is not realistic, however much shade it has. Dr Brandt's concern is right, though, so use shade and cooling points on the existing route.
- **Heat rule and double cooling stations alone:** this helps, but it leaves the June heat in place. The first heat-wave year then ends in either a cancelled race or more hospital cases. It works as a backstop to a date change, but it is a weak plan on its own.
- **Half only:** this cuts the heat exposure and the growth together. The 5,000 half runners would still be racing in warm weather.

**Steps before 18 November**

1. Ask the insurer whether a mid-May date plus a written heat protocol meets their renewal condition. Put their answer in the board paper.
2. Ask the national federation to confirm that a mid-May Sunday date counts for qualifying and that no federation event clashes with it.
3. Get the city's confirmation that the Sunday 05:00 to 14:30 closure and permits can move to that date, and check that the venues, police and marshals are available.
4. Write the heat protocol: a wet-bulb or temperature trigger on the day, a clear cancel or shorten decision-maker, and extra cooling stations and medical cover at the points where runners were treated in the last two years. Dr Brandt should own the medical trigger.
5. Tell the sponsor and runners early. Offer the sponsor another way to get floodlit visuals, such as an expo evening or a finish-line display.

**Assumptions, what would change the answer, and the cheapest check**

- **Mid-May is cool enough.** If historical mid-May temperatures at 11:00 are above about 20 °C in many years, the date change helps less. Check the last ten years of local weather records for that weekend.
- **The federation's window is fixed.** If the window is moved, or a May date is not accepted for qualifying, the case changes. One email to the federation settles it.
- **The insurer accepts a date move.** If they insist on an early start, you need rail extra services. Ask the operator whether a 05:45 arrival is possible and what it costs. A 7:30 start with a heat rule then becomes the fallback, since 06:50 trains plus a ten-minute walk gives about 07:00.
- **The city can move the permit date.** If it cannot, the fallback is again the 7:30 start plus heat rule.

My confidence is moderate to high. The main unknown is whether the federation and insurer both accept a mid-May date.
