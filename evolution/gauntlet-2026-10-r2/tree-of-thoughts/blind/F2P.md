**Recommendation: strengthen the arches and armour the pier (option 2, €2.4M), keeping the 26-tonne limit until the work is done.** Before cabinet meets, run one check that could change this answer (see the end).

I'm assuming the consultants' costs and timings are sound, and that the 2-year safe life of the 26-tonne limit runs from August 2026, so to about mid-2028.

**Why the two favourites fail**

- **Demolish and rebuild (€1.3M net) probably misses the funding deadline.**
  - Cabinet decides in December 2026. The work can't start in the river before July 2027, because the Environment Board only allows channel work from July to September.
  - Twenty months with two river summers means summer 2027 and summer 2028. Completion would be around spring 2029 at best, a year after the March 2028 cut-off.
  - The 75% grant is conditional on the money being spent by March 2028. Unless the fund will accept spend rather than completion, or will extend the date, the real cost is €5.2M, not €1.3M. That is more than twice option 2.
  - It also means demolishing a register-listed 1794 bridge that is on every postcard. Expect listed-building consent to be difficult and slow, and expect public opposition. That adds programme risk to a scheme that has no spare time.
  - Even a delay of one season would run past the 26-tonne limit's safe life.
- **The permanent 7.5-tonne limit (€150k) doesn't work for the school buses.**
  - Ferris Valley Coaches' double-deckers carry 380 pupils a day and weigh well over 7.5 tonnes, so they would be barred from the bridge.
  - Cott Lane has a 4.0 m railway arch, and a double-decker is typically about 4.3 to 4.4 m high, so they almost certainly can't use the diversion. Check the exact vehicle heights, but I'd expect the school run to have no route at all.
  - The 32-tonne tippers getting through Cott Lane says nothing about the buses. Tippers are low and buses are not.
  - Other buses and emergency vehicles would be affected too, and 6 km extra on every heavy trip is a real cost to local businesses. The low price leaves out those costs.

**Why option 2 fits**

- It is the only option that is cheap, fits inside the river window, and can be finished well before the 26-tonne limit's two-year life runs out. Design and consents over winter 2026/27 allow the work in July to September 2027.
- It keeps the listed structure and avoids the consent fight, so it is the least contentious route for a heritage asset.
- It keeps the road open at full width, so the school buses and 9,000 vehicles a day keep their route.
- At €2.4M it costs €1.1M more than the rebuild would on paper, but it is likely far less than the rebuild would cost once the grant is lost.

**Risks to put in front of cabinet**

- Cracking in the central arch plus scour at the pier may be harder to fix than the consultants' estimate assumes. Ask them for the risk range and a contingency of perhaps 20 to 30%. Commission ground and pier investigations now, in the window before summer 2027.
- One summer is tight. If the work slips past September 2027, it goes to summer 2028 and uses up the safe life of the limit. Agree a monitoring regime and a fallback trigger, such as a deterioration threshold that prompts a lower limit or the temporary bridge.
- The strengthened bridge will probably still need a weight limit. Ask the consultants what limit it will carry afterwards, and whether the quarry tippers and buses can use it.
- Option 2 loses the grant. Ask the fund whether any heritage or resilience stream could help.

**Why not the others**

- **Temporary bridge (€0.9M, three years, decide later)** only defers the decision, and it needs river work too, in the same window. It is useful as a contingency if option 2 hits trouble, not as the main plan.
- **Footbridge plus Holm Ford crossing (€7M, about three years)** is the most expensive. It would take 9,000 vehicles a day to a different alignment, and it probably can't be completed or funded in time either. Check the grant position on it, because a new crossing may be eligible, but it looks too slow and costly.

**The check before December**

Phone the regional infrastructure fund this week and ask two questions. Does "spent by March 2028" mean money spent or the bridge completed? And could they extend the deadline for a listed structure? If they say spend only, and the consultants can show a programme that commits €5.2M by then, and heritage consent for demolition looks achievable, the rebuild's €1.3M net cost becomes attractive and I would revisit it. I think that's unlikely, so I'd go to cabinet with option 2 as the recommendation and the fund's written answer attached.
