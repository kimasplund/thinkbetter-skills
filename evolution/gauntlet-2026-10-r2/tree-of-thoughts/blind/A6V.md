There is a good answer, but it isn't any one of the six proposals. Four of them fail on facts you gave me, and the other two only work together.

**Recommendation: keep late June, move the marathon start as early as the trains allow (about 07:30), and pair it with a written heat protocol and more cooling.**

- Start the marathon at 07:30 instead of 08:30. Send the slowest finishers off first, in waves, so the people who'll be out longest get the coolest hours. The half can start after the marathon field clears, since half runners are mostly home by late morning. This is the insurer's idea, adapted so it actually works.
- Adopt a heat protocol with set triggers, agreed with Dr Brandt and the insurer in advance. For example: at a forecast wet-bulb or temperature threshold, shorten the marathon cut-off, close the course to the back of the field at a set point, or move to half-only. Cancelling outright is the last tier, not the whole plan.
- Double the cooling stations, but put the extra ones at kilometres 25 to 40 and on the half's second hour, where the back of the field will be in the heat. Add medical cover there too.

**Why the rest fail**

- **6:00 start:** 75% of runners come by train, and the first Sunday train arrives at 06:50. Most of your field couldn't reach the start line. That is why the realistic floor is about 07:15 to 07:30.
- **Mid-April:** This is the only option that fixes the heat itself, so it's tempting. But the federation's qualifying window runs 1 May to 30 September. An April race wouldn't count, and qualifier-chasing club runners are most of your 40% growth. You'd trade a safety problem for an entry and income problem, probably a bigger one than the board expects.
- **Saturday 19:00:** The city allows full road closures only on Sunday mornings. It's also incompatible with a six-hour cut-off, which would run to 01:00. The sponsor's floodlit finish isn't available.
- **Towpath reroute:** The path is 2.5 m wide, and the navigation trust caps a 5k there at 500 runners. Putting 13,400 runners on 20 km of it, with ambulances unable to reach them, is a hazard in its own right.
- **Heat rule plus cooling alone:** It helps on bad days, but on the ordinary hot day, which is what you've had twice, runners are still out in the heat for hours. It's part of the answer, not all of it.
- **Half only:** This is the only other option that would clearly cut heat exposure, because the marathon's slow finishers face the worst of it. But it costs the growth segment and probably a large share of income. Keep it as the fallback, not the plan.

**Assumptions that matter, and the cheapest check for each**

1. **The extra hour is enough to matter.** A marathoner finishing after 11:00 still runs in 30 °C. Ask Dr Brandt to split the last two years' 140 treatments by the time each runner was on the course. If most cases came from people running past 11:00, the earlier start helps. If they came earlier or from the half, it's much weaker, and half-only moves up.
2. **Trains can't be improved.** Ask the rail operator whether a special or earlier Sunday service is possible. A 06:50 arrival caps the start, and an earlier train would buy another 30 to 45 minutes.
3. **The insurer accepts 07:30 plus a protocol.** They proposed 06:00, so send them this plan and the trigger table before 18 November. If they refuse anything short of 06:00, the real choice becomes half-only or the April cost.
4. **The federation won't help with April.** One email to ask whether a race outside the window can be recognised for qualifying costs nothing. If the answer is yes, April becomes the best option. I'd expect no.

My confidence is moderate. The deciding unknown is how many of your casualties occur after 11:00, and the casualty timing from step 1 answers that.
