**Recommend strengthening the arches and armouring the pier (€2.4M), keeping the 26-tonne limit until the work is done, with the work in the river in July to September 2027.**

Why this one: it is the only option that fixes both defects (the central arch cracking and the scour under the pier), keeps the listed bridge, needs one river season, and costs the least of the options that actually solve the problem. The 26-tonne limit already lets the double-deckers over, since a loaded double-decker is roughly 18 to 26 tonnes. It also costs little, because the 32-tonne tippers have shown that Cott Lane works for the heaviest traffic.

Why not the others, one reason each:

- **Demolish and rebuild (€1.3M net).** The headline saving is probably not real. Twenty months starting after a December decision ends around autumn 2028, and design and consents come before that. The fund needs the money spent by March 2028, and only one of the two river summers (2027) falls before that date. The bridge is on the national heritage register, so you would also need consent to demolish it. That is a hard and slow case when a repair is available at €2.4M. If the fund will not pay in full, you are carrying €5.2M or a programme that slips past the point where the 26-tonne limit stops being safe.
- **Permanent 7.5-tonne limit (€150k).** It is cheap, but it bans the two Ferris Valley double-deckers from the bridge, and they cannot take Cott Lane either. A double-decker is about 4.3 to 4.4 m high and the railway arch is 4.0 m. That leaves 380 pupils without a route to Wenlow High. It would also stop fire engines and bin lorries from using the main road into town. It does nothing about the cracking or the scour, so the bridge keeps deteriorating.
- **Temporary steel bridge (€0.9M for three years).** It buys time but does not remove the problem. You would still need a permanent decision, and by then the fund deadline will have passed. It is only worth reconsidering if the repair cannot be done without closing the road.
- **Footbridge plus Holm Ford crossing (€7M).** It is the most expensive option, takes about three years, and also misses the March 2028 fund date. A crossing 1 km upstream does not serve the town-centre approach the way the existing road does.

**Steps if cabinet agrees**

1. Commission design and listed-building consent for the strengthening now, so the contractor is appointed by spring 2027 and ready for the July 2027 window. If that slips, summer 2028 is the fallback, but it leaves very little margin inside the two-year safe period.
2. Keep the 26-tonne limit, with enforcement, and have the engineers carry out a scour and crack inspection after the winter high flows, with a trigger level at which the limit is cut or the bridge is closed.
3. Bring the Environment Board in at design stage so the in-channel method (cofferdam or similar) is agreed before tender.
4. Tell the cabinet member for transport directly why the replacement funding is unlikely to arrive in time, and show the programme dates.

**Assumptions that matter, what would change the recommendation, and the cheapest check**

- **Fund timing.** If the fund confirms in writing that a December 2026 start with a 20-month programme qualifies, for example because milestone or committed spend counts, then replacement is worth reopening. Check: email the fund this week with the programme and ask.
- **Strengthening restores enough capacity.** If the engineers say the repair cannot support at least the current 26-tonne limit long term, or the cracking is progressive, the case shifts. Check: ask the consultants for the post-repair capacity and design life in writing before December.
- **Consent for demolition would be refused or slow.** I assume Historic England and local opposition would contest it. Check: a short pre-application enquiry on listed-building consent would show how hard it is.
- **Double-deckers are within 26 tonnes.** Check the operator's plated weights.

I am fairly confident in this recommendation. The main unknown is whether the fund would accept a spend profile that runs past March 2028.
