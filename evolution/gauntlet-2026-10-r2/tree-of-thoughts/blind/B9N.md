**Recommendation: take option 5 (keep the date and course, add a heat-cancellation rule and more cooling), but move the start to about 07:30. That is a modest change from the insurer's 06:00. Do not pick any of the other four.**

**Why the others fail on facts you gave me**

- **Mid-April:** The federation's qualifying window runs 1 May to 30 September. An April race wouldn't give qualifying times. Most of your 40% marathon growth is club runners chasing those times, so that growth would likely disappear. Corin's enthusiasm is understandable, but this undermines the event's main draw.
- **06:00 start:** About three-quarters of runners come by train, and the first Sunday arrival is 06:50. Most of them couldn't reach a 06:00 start. The same problem applies to anything much before 07:30, unless the rail operator adds earlier trains (see below).
- **Saturday evening:** The city only allows full road closures on Sunday mornings, 05:00 to 14:30. A six-hour cut-off from a 19:00 start also runs to 01:00. The floodlit finish is appealing, but it can't be permitted under the current policy.
- **Towpath for the middle 20 km:** The path is 2.5 m wide, and the navigation trust caps even a 5k there at 500 runners. Putting up to 8,400 marathoners on it would cause dangerous crowding and a likely refusal. Shade is a real benefit, but the width rules it out. Dr Brandt's concern is right even if this fix isn't. It could still work for a small side role, such as a cooler walking or recovery route, but not as the race course.
- **Drop the marathon:** This removes the heat exposure but also the fastest-growing part of the event. It would cost entry income and club loyalty. Treat it as a last resort if the insurer rejects everything else.

**What the plan should contain**

1. **Earlier start, about 07:30 (marathon first, half shortly after).**
   - It is realistic for train arrivals: the first train gets in at 06:50, plus a ten-minute walk.
   - A six-hour cut-off ends at 13:30, inside the 14:30 road closure.
   - It gives the insurer a real change to the time of day. Ask the rail operator whether a 06:00 or 06:15 arrival is possible. If so, a 07:00 start becomes feasible.
2. **Heat-cancellation or modification rule, set in advance.**
   - Use WBGT, not air temperature alone. Set published thresholds, for example amber to add stops and warnings, red to shorten, delay or cancel.
   - Name who decides (the race director with the medical lead) and check the forecast at fixed points, for example 7 days, 48 hours and race morning.
   - State the refund or deferral policy in advance, so the rule can be used without a fight.
3. **Cooling stations and medical cover.**
   - Doubling is a good start. Put them where the heat is worst: the last third of the marathon, in the open and late in the morning.
   - Add water, ice, sponges and shaded stops, plus medical points and cooling facilities able to treat heat illness (for example cold-water immersion for suspected heatstroke).
   - Brief staff on spotting early heat illness. Dr Brandt's team should design this.
4. **Communication to runners.** Warn them early about pacing, hydration and slower targets in heat. Allow free transfers or deferrals if the forecast is bad, which lowers the pressure to run in dangerous conditions.
5. **Review the cut-off.** Slower walkers are in the hottest hours, so the earlier start protects them most. Keep six hours for now, because shortening it conflicts with the finishing opportunity you want to preserve.

**How to present this to the board and insurer**

- The insurer wants a credible plan, not a specific time. A documented trigger system, an earlier start and stronger medical cover is credible. Show them the plan and ask them to confirm it in writing before 18 November.
- Tell the sponsor that the evening race is blocked by city policy, but ask about alternatives, such as an early finish-line celebration or branding on the cooling stations.
- Tell Corin and Dr Brandt what you took from each: Corin's concern for runner safety shaped the plan, and Dr Brandt's shade idea shows up in the medical and cooling design.

**Assumptions and what to check**

- I assumed 07:30 is workable for logistics, marshalling and road opening from 05:00. Confirm with the city.
- I assumed the rail operator can't add early trains. If it can, start earlier.
- I assumed the insurer will accept a start before 8:30 but after 06:00 together with the other measures. This is the key risk, so test it first.
