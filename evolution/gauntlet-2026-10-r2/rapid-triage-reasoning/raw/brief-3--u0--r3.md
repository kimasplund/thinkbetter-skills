My recommendation is to hold the splash unless the document is independently verified by about 22:35. Holding costs you a possible scoop. Running a forged report costs a libel action and a pulped or corrected run, and you can't undo either. If the story is true, it will still be true at 08:30.

The swap takes about 20 minutes, so the real deadline is 22:35-22:40, not 23:00. Everything below is built around that.

**Do now (22:05-22:15)**

1. **Stop the 00:01 web and app alert.** Unschedule it or pull the alert so nothing can fire while you decide. This is fully reversible.
2. **Warn the sister titles.** Tell them on the group wire that the splash is under review and the 00:01 embargo is not confirmed. Ask them to hold it until you confirm. They are working to the same clock, and a recall after they've set pages is much harder.
3. **Tell the subs to start preparing the held-over feature,** but not to swap it yet. Preparing costs nothing and keeps the fallback open.
4. **Keep trying to reach Ines.** Ring her again, text her, and try her partner or a family member who is at the wedding. If that fails, go up the chain to whoever else has authority, such as the managing editor, the publisher, or the on-call lawyer or legal adviser. A splash that accuses a named firm of faking fire-safety sign-off on a care home should not be decided by you alone, and nobody can fault you for escalating it.

**Verification (22:10-22:35), running in parallel**

- **Dagny:**
  - Ring her now. Ask how she knows the source is who they say they are, and whether anything in the report has been confirmed by a second person or a second document.
  - Ask her to check the details against known facts. Do the officers whose initials appear exist, and were they working in that office then? Does the reference number, format and stamp match other reports the office has issued? Do the dates, site address and extension description fit the public planning record?
  - Ask her to go back to the source tonight and ask who else can confirm it.
- **The lawyer's email:** Do not send him the scan. The routing stamp and the officers' initials could help identify your source. Sending it would also give him a chance to prepare a rebuttal, or to tip off the office, before you've published. Reply briefly instead. Say you are putting the allegations to Vosk for comment. Give a short deadline, such as 22:30. Ask him to state in writing which parts of the report are false and to send whatever evidence he has. If he says he can only show it's fake by seeing your copy, note that and move on. His claim that it's a forgery is a claim from an interested party, not proof. His response, or lack of one, also goes in the story if you run it.
- **Building control:** Press officers are off until 08:30, but try any out-of-hours or duty number you have. You could also ask Dagny whether she has a second contact in the office, the fire service or the care home's regulator who can confirm the inspection and the sign-off.

**Decide at 22:35**

- **Run the splash only if** at least one independent confirmation is in hand (a second source, or an official or documentary check of the report's details), and Ines or another senior person has agreed. Then add Vosk's response or the fact that they denied it, and tighten the wording to what the document shows. Say "a report seen by the Courier states" rather than asserting fraud as fact.
- **Otherwise swap in the feature.** Don't gamble on 38,000 copies. Ines can decide later whether the story belongs online in the morning.

If the report can't be checked at all in time but you are worried about residents' safety, don't use the front page as the way to raise it. Dagny or the paper can tell the fire service or the county tonight, which is also much faster and does more for the residents than publication would.

**On the Gazette:** the risk that they beat you is real, but they are working from the same unverified material or from calls to Vosk. If they run it and it's forged, they carry the problem. If it's genuine, you can still publish a better-checked story in the morning, with the press office's response and Vosk's reply.

**Keep notes as you go:** times of calls, who you spoke to, what the lawyer wrote, what Dagny confirmed. You'll need them for Ines and for any legal challenge.

**Afterwards**

- Brief Ines first thing, with the decision and your reasons.
- Chase building control at 08:30 for confirmation or denial.
- Take legal advice before any publication on the story.
- Review how the splash was scheduled to go out on the wire and online before it had final sign-off, so one person isn't left holding this decision at 22:00 again.
