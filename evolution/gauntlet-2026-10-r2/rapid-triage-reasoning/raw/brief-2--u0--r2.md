Book the Polar Quay van now, well before 08:30, and get the soaked material frozen today. Do not put anything in the skip, and do not use the heaters. Freezing is cheap and can be undone, and it stops the damage. Mould starts within about 48 to 72 hours in a warm, muggy room, so waiting until Monday is risky.

**In the next 10 minutes**

1. **Call Polar Quay and confirm the collection.** Ask for the price for about 60 boxes plus the prints and catalogues, and whether there is a minimum term. At £9 a box, 60 boxes is about £540 a month, which you can afford out of £4,000 for a few weeks while the insurer and a conservator get involved. Get the terms by email. Also ask whether they can take boxes back out individually later.
2. **Stop the heaters now.** Heat sets the coated paper. The hard edges on the catalogue stack are the early sign of that. Switch both heaters off and don't try to dry the stuck catalogues or photos in the hall. Cool, moving air from the fans is fine. Open windows if it isn't raining in.
3. **Keep the strongroom electrics off.** Water came through the light fitting, so don't run extension leads in there. Leave the wall switch off. Use battery torches. Ask the plumber or an electrician to isolate that circuit at the consumer unit and check it before anything is powered up. Run the fans from a dry circuit outside the room, with an RCD adaptor if you have one.
4. **Tell the landlord's agent that nothing gets thrown away.** The wet boxes will be out of the building by about 09:00, which deals with the smell. The material is the Trust's, and you have to show the insurer what was lost. If it's skipped before the loss adjuster sees it, you may weaken your claim. Don't rely on "the insurer will pay anyway" from the agent. Put it in an email today: "Nothing is to be disposed of until our insurer's loss adjuster has inspected."
5. **Phone the Trust's insurer's emergency or claims line.** Give the claim reference, ask them to confirm Tuesday's visit, and ask whether they have an approved salvage contractor. Check that freezing is acceptable to them. It almost always is.

**How to pack for the van**

- **Ledgers and letters:** Leave them in their boxes or bag them as they are, and freeze them. Don't open, separate or squeeze them out. Wet paper tears very easily.
- **Photographic prints:** Freeze these too. Don't peel any that are stuck. If you can, put a sheet of baking parchment or polythene around each stack, and keep the stacks together. A conservator can free them later, and separating them wet usually destroys the image.
- **Stuck catalogues:** Freeze them as they are, with the hardened stack from the heater. Don't try to pull them apart.
- **Boxes that are only damp, not wet:** Sort these out as you pack. Stand them open in the hall with the fans on, not near a heater, and interleave the damp pages with clean paper or unprinted newsprint. If any look doubtful or start to smell musty by lunchtime, add them to a second freezer trip.
- **Label as you go:** Write a box number and a one-line description on the outside of every box. Keep a list of what goes where.
- **Photograph before you move.** Take a couple of phone photos of the room, the shelves and the water damage, and of a few boxes before you pack them. This is your evidence for the insurer.

**Volunteers**

- Gloves and basic dust or mould masks, and no one on wet floors near the light fitting.
- Split the six: two or three carrying and packing, one making the list and taking photos, one rigging the fans and ventilation in the hall, and one on the phone.
- Move the wettest material first, starting with the photographs and coated-paper catalogues, which are the most vulnerable. Then the ledgers, and the least wet boxes last.

**Afterwards**

- **Tuesday:** Show the loss adjuster the room, the photographs and the list. Ask them to agree vacuum freeze-drying or specialist salvage through the claim, and the cost of storing the boxes in the freezer in the meantime.
- **This week:** Get an archive conservator's advice on drying the ledgers and the photographs. The Archives and Records Association or a county record office can usually recommend one.
- **Environment:** Get a dehumidifier into the strongroom once the electrics are cleared, and check the shelves for mould over the next week.
- **Cost:** Keep a record of all costs, including the freezer, and check the claim covers them.
- **Landlord:** Ask for a written account of the cause of the leak and who is responsible for the repair.

The main thing that could go wrong is the 08:30 cut-off. If the van can't come this morning, ask Polar Quay for another slot, or ask the insurer whether their salvage contractor can collect. Until then, keep the boxes cool, spread out and ventilated, but out of the heat.
