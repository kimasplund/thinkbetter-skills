**Short answer: say yes to the freezer before 08:30, switch the heaters off, keep every wet item out of the skip, and keep power out of the strongroom.** Below is the order to do it in.

**In the next 5 minutes**

1. **Stop the heater trial and unplug both heaters.** Heat is what hardened those catalogue edges. Shiny coated paper that dries while stuck together fuses into a solid block, and the photographic prints will do the same. Fans in the hall are fine, but with unheated air only.
2. **Keep the strongroom electrics dead.** Water came through the light fitting, so don't run extension leads into that room. Switch that lighting circuit off at the fuse board, not only the wall switch. Use torches and daylight inside. Get an electrician to check it before anything is switched back on. Run the fans from sockets in the hall.
3. **Tell the landlord's agent that nothing leaves the building until the Trust says so.** Nothing goes in the skip. The boxes belong to the Trust and are irreplaceable. If they are binned before the loss adjuster sees them, your claim could be damaged, and the insurer's agreement to pay is only the agent's word. The smell can be handled by moving the boxes out of the strongroom this morning, which you're doing anyway.

**By 08:30: confirm Polar Quay**

- Freezing is the right move for the soaked material. It stops mould, which can start within 48 to 72 hours in a warm, muggy room like yours. It also holds the stuck catalogues and prints exactly as they are, so they can be separated properly later, by a conservator or by freeze-drying, instead of being torn apart wet. Sixty boxes is about £540 a month, which you can afford out of £4,000. If you wait until Monday, you give the mould two more days in a 22 °C room.
- It is also the easy option to undo. Anything can be thawed and air-dried later. Drying 60 boxes by hand today with six volunteers is not realistic, and drying them badly can't be reversed.
- Confirm these points when you ring: price per box, any minimum term, whether they'll accept wet boxes in bags, and whether they can collect from the door. If the answers are unacceptable, say so before 08:30. Otherwise confirm.
- Don't sort or unpack. Don't try to peel anything apart. Put each soaked box, as it is, into a bin bag or polythene sleeve to stop leaks. Lay the stuck catalogues flat and keep each stack together. Wet paper is weak and heavy, so lift boxes from underneath. Wear gloves and a mask because of mould spores.
- Put the 300 prints and the catalogues in the first boxes the van takes. The ledgers and letters are the most tolerant of the three, so they go last if space runs short.

**While the van is on its way**

- Photograph the room, the shelves, the ceiling, the light fitting and each box before it moves. Number the boxes and keep a simple list. This is your evidence for the claim.
- Ring the insurer now to notify the loss, even though the adjuster can't come until Tuesday. Ask if they want anything else kept as evidence, and ask whether they have a salvage or recovery contractor they would prefer. Ask the plumber for a written note of the cause.
- Anything that is only damp, not soaked, can be spread on tables in the hall with fans on and no heat. Leave the windows open if it's drier outside than in, and turn the strongroom heating down so the room is cooler. Check these daily for mould.
- Ask the landlord's agent to let you keep the strongroom door open and run a dehumidifier. Hire one this morning if you can.

**Afterwards**

- Ask the insurer or a conservator (the Archives and Records Association or a local record office can give names) about vacuum freeze-drying for the frozen material. Get cost estimates before the Tuesday visit.
- Check whether the policy covers the freezer fees and the cost of conservation.
- Have the pipe and the light fitting repaired and inspected before any archives return to the room, and move the lower-shelf material up or elsewhere.

**What I'm assuming:** the freezer takes wet boxes in bags, and the insurer has been or will be told today. If either assumption is wrong, the plan still holds: freeze what you can by 08:30 and keep everything else cool, unheated and out of the skip.
