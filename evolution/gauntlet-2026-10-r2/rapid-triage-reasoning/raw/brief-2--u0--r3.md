Freeze the wet material this morning, stop the heaters, and don't let anything go in the skip. Do these in order.

**Next 5 minutes**
1. Turn the heaters off and keep them out of the strongroom. Heat is what hardened the catalogue edges: it dries the surface, bakes the coating and fuses the sheets. Don't try to dry the soaked material at room temperature either. At 22 °C and muggy, mould can start within 48 hours, and sooner on wet paper.
2. Keep the strongroom electrics off. Water came through the light fitting, so don't run extension leads in there. Switch off that lighting circuit at the consumer unit as well as the wall switch if you can. Volunteers can carry boxes out by daylight or a torch. Run the fans from sockets outside the wet area, ideally on an RCD plug, and aim them to move air through the room. Don't point them straight at the boxes.
3. Tell the volunteers the plan: boxes come out, they don't get dried in the hall.

**Before 08:30: confirm Polar Quay**
- Say yes. Freezing stops mould and stops further damage, so it buys you time. It is also the standard first step for soaked paper, and it can be reversed: you can thaw the boxes or send them for vacuum freeze-drying later.
- It is the right call for the glossy prints and shiny catalogues. Coated paper that dries while stuck together usually fuses permanently. Frozen, it can be separated or freeze-dried later by a conservator.
- It costs about £540 a month for 60 boxes. That is affordable on £4,000 for a few weeks, but the money is not unlimited. Ask Polar Quay for a minimum term and whether they'll invoice rather than take payment now.
- Send the soaked ledgers and letters, the 300 prints and the stuck catalogues. Wet bound volumes are the hardest to air-dry without distortion and mould.

**Packing, while the van is on its way**
- Don't peel anything apart. Don't blot, wipe or press the prints. Leave stuck stacks stuck.
- Keep each box's contents together and keep the box labelling. Put a loose sheet of clean paper on top if you have it. If you have polythene bags, bag each box or stack. Bin bags will do if they're all you have.
- Boxes that are only damp, not soaked, can stay out. Spread them somewhere cool and airy on clean paper with a fan moving the air in the room. If you're unsure whether a box is soaked or damp, freeze it. Damp paper is the one that goes mouldy.
- Wear gloves. Open a window or door in the strongroom for the smell.

**The skip**
- Say no, politely and in writing: "Nothing is to be disposed of. These are the Trust's records and the insurer needs to see them." The agent can't dispose of your property, and "the insurer will pay anyway" is wrong if the evidence is gone. A claim on destroyed items you can't show is a claim you may not win. Irreplaceable boatyard records are also not something money replaces.
- If the smell is the real problem, the answer is to get the boxes out of the building. The van does that by 09:00 and the rest can go into the hall or a vehicle.

**Before the boxes leave**
- Take photos now: the ceiling, the light fitting, the shelves, and the boxes in place and in their stacks. Before the van fills up, have someone list roughly what is going: box numbers, content, rough count of prints. This is your evidence for Tuesday.

**Insurer**
- Phone the claims line today, Saturday if they have an emergency line, and notify the claim. Don't wait for Tuesday. Tell them you're freezing the material to prevent mould, say what it costs, and ask whether they object. Note the time, who you spoke to and any reference.
- Ask the plumber for a written note of the cause and a receipt.

**Follow-up for next week**
- [ ] Ask the insurer's adjuster or a conservator about vacuum freeze-drying the frozen material, and get the price and who pays.
- [ ] Contact the regional archive service or a conservation body for advice. Many run a salvage helpline.
- [ ] Get a written agreement from the landlord's agent on the strongroom and the ceiling repair before the records go back.
- [ ] Have the electrics in the strongroom checked before anyone uses them again.
- [ ] Move the unaffected boxes off the bottom shelves.
- [ ] Check the damp-but-not-frozen boxes daily for mould for the next week.
