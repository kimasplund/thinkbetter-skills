Do these in order. The first four take minutes.

**Right now (before 08:00)**

1. **Keep the strongroom light off, and don't run extension leads in.** Water came through the light fitting, so treat the room's electrics as unsafe until an electrician has checked them. Don't use the fan heaters in there. If the lights or sockets are on a circuit you can isolate at the consumer unit, switch that circuit off. Use battery torches or lights brought in from outside, and keep people out of any standing water.
2. **Turn the heaters off and stop the heat drying.** Heat is what hardened the catalogue edges. Heat dries paper unevenly, warps it, sets the stuck sheets together, and speeds up mould. Take the trial stack away from the heater and leave it as it is. Don't peel anything apart.
3. **Stop the mould clock.** Mould can start within 48 to 72 hours in warm, muggy air. Cool, moving, dry air helps. Use the fans to move air, not heat. Open windows if it's drier outside, and bring in a dehumidifier if you can get one today.
4. **Put someone on a log.** Photograph the room and the boxes before anything moves. Number the boxes and note where each one was. Tell the volunteers to touch only what you've assigned.

**The 08:30 freezer decision: say yes**

Confirm the Polar Quay van now. The reasons:

- Freezing stops mould and stops further deterioration. It buys you time to decide properly later, and that's what you need on a Saturday with the adjuster not due until Tuesday.
- It's cheap. 60 boxes at £9 a box per month is about £540 a month. Even if you freeze everything, you can afford a few months while the insurer and a conservator sort it out.
- The alternative is bad. 60 boxes can't be laid out and dried by six volunteers on hall tables before mould sets in, and you've already seen what heat does.

What to send for freezing:

- **Send:** the soaked ledgers, letters and the stuck-together catalogues. Freezing is the standard first step for wet paper and bound volumes. The stuck catalogues and the glossy prints are the most urgent, because coated paper blocks (fuses into a solid mass) as it dries. Frozen blocks can often be separated later by a conservator.
- **Pack:** keep each box as it is, in a sturdy bag if you have one (bin liners are fine). Don't unpack. Don't try to separate the stuck items first. Wet paper is fragile, so lift boxes from underneath. Label every box with the number from your log.
- **Photographic prints:** put these in with the rest if they're wet. Don't let wet prints dry stuck to each other or to their sleeves. If you can, interleave them with greaseproof paper or baking parchment, not tissue or newspaper. If that isn't practical in the time, freeze them as they are.
- **Damp, not soaked:** boxes that are only damp are a lower priority for freezing. If space or money is a worry, these could be air-dried later. For today, if in doubt, freeze.

Ask Polar Quay on the phone whether they'll take the boxes bagged and whether there is a minimum term. Also ask for the freezer location in writing, for the insurer.

**The skip: no, not before the adjuster has seen it**

Tell the landlord's agent, politely and firmly, that nothing goes in the skip. The agent doesn't own the collection, and the Trust does. Reasons:

- The insurer will want to see and assess the damage, and a skipped item is a lost claim. "The insurer will pay for it all" doesn't help if you've destroyed the evidence, and it won't replace unique records from the Cobbold boatyard anyway.
- Archives like these can't be replaced. Money doesn't bring back a ledger.
- The smell is a fair concern, but it's solved by getting the boxes out of the building. The freezer van does that, and so does moving boxes into sealed bags. Offer the agent that: wet material will be bagged and gone before 09:00.

Also tell your insurer's claims line today, even on a Saturday, if there's an out-of-hours number. Give them the facts, ask them to confirm the Tuesday visit in writing, and ask whether they'll appoint a salvage firm sooner. Check your policy for a salvage or emergency-response cover.

**Volunteers: what to give them to do**

- Two people on lifting and bagging boxes from the lower shelves, working from the doorway out.
- One person on the log and photos.
- One person on ringing: Polar Quay, the insurer, an electrician.
- Others can prepare a clear space in the hall with plastic sheeting and absorbent paper for any items that won't go to the freezer. Don't start drying anything with heat.
- Wear gloves and masks, because the water and any mould spores are a health risk.

**Later today and next week**

- Call a conservator or your county archive service. Many have a disaster contact, and some will advise by phone on a Saturday. The Archives and Records Association or a similar body also keeps lists of disaster response firms.
- Ask the conservator how to thaw and dry the frozen material in batches. Vacuum freeze-drying is the usual method for large volumes, and it may be covered by the insurance.
- Keep any receipts: freezer fees, hire costs, the plumber.
- Have the electrician check the strongroom before anyone turns the power back on.

**Money**

You have about £4,000. Freezing costs about £540 a month, so you can hold the material for several months while the claim is settled. Ask the insurer to pay or reimburse the freezing and salvage costs. Don't spend large sums on drying equipment today.

**In short:** confirm the freezer van before 08:30, turn off the heaters, keep the power off in the strongroom, don't let anything go into the skip, and get the soaked boxes bagged, numbered and out of the building before 09:00.

I've assumed the policy covers water damage and that the collection is Trust-owned. If either isn't true, the plan is the same, but check it on Monday.
