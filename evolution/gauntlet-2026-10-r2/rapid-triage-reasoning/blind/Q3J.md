Here is what I would do, in order. You have until 08:30 for the freezer decision, so the first few steps are fast.

**Right now (next 5 minutes)**

1. **Stop the volunteers running leads into the strongroom.** Water came through the light fitting and the circuit may be wet. Keep the strongroom light off at the wall. Ask someone to switch off that circuit at the consumer unit if they can do it safely. No heaters, no extension leads in there. Use battery torches or work lights from the doorway.
2. **Stop the heater drying.** Hard edges on the catalogues mean the coated paper is already damaged. Heat makes glossy paper and photographs fuse and warp. Move that stack away from the heater, keep it wet and flat, and don't try to peel it apart.
3. **Tell the landlord's agent nothing goes in the skip.** Say it plainly and politely: the Trust is not authorising disposal, the material is irreplaceable and is part of an insurance claim, and the loss adjuster has to see it first. Nobody should throw anything away before Tuesday. Ask for a short written note of when they raised the skip, so you have a record. If the smell is the real problem, offer to get the wet boxes out of the building this morning, which is what step 5 does anyway.
4. **Photograph everything before you move it.** Take wide shots of the room, the leak, the ceiling and the shelves, then close-ups of the boxes. This is your evidence for the insurer. Five minutes is enough. Use phones.

**Decide by 08:15 whether to book the freezer**

I would say yes, for the things that are stuck together or heavily soaked. Freezing stops mould (which starts within 48 to 72 hours at 22 °C and humid) and stops further damage. It is fully reversible, because you can thaw and dry items later in a controlled way, and it buys you time until the loss adjuster and a conservator can advise. Waiting until Monday in a warm, muggy room is the worst outcome for wet paper.

- **Freeze:** the stuck catalogues and the glossy photographic prints, plus any box that is dripping or soaked right through. Coated paper and photographs are the items that cannot be air dried successfully by volunteers. Frozen, they can be vacuum freeze-dried or separated by a professional.
- **Cost:** £9 per box per month. If you freeze everything, 60 boxes is about £540 a month. The catalogues, photographs and the worst boxes are likely to be a fraction of that. Ask Polar Quay for a minimum term and the cost of releasing boxes, and confirm in writing that you can take them out in small batches. Freezing only what needs it keeps you well within £4,000 for several months, and the insurer may cover it. Tell them it is a claim-related mitigation cost.
- **Pack for the van:** put each wet stack in a clean polythene bag or wrap it in freezer paper if you have it, with a sheet of waxed or silicone paper between layers where you can do it without force. Do not try to separate stuck pages. Label each box with its original shelf location. Pack upright in crates or boxes. Keep a list of what goes.

**What volunteers can dry in the hall (Saturday)**

Only the boxes that are damp rather than soaked, and only plain paper: the Cobbold ledgers and letters that are merely damp and not stuck.

- Air dry at room temperature, with fans moving air across but not blowing directly on the paper. No heaters.
- Interleave with clean, absorbent paper (plain unprinted newsprint, blotting paper or paper towels), and change it as it dampens.
- Lay letters flat. Stand bound ledgers on their tail edge, fanned open slightly, rather than hanging them.
- Don't separate pages that are stuck. Leave those for freezing.
- If a ledger is soaked right through, freeze it rather than drying it in the hall. Thick bound volumes dry slowly and will mould in the middle.
- Check for mould every few hours. Anyone who sees fuzzy growth should wear a mask and gloves, isolate the item, and tell you.

**Get the room itself under control**

- Open windows or run a dehumidifier if the Trust can borrow one. Muggy 22 °C air is what grows mould. Ask a volunteer to hire or borrow one this morning. Running it in the strongroom needs a dry, safe power source, so plug it in outside the wet area, or wait until an electrician has checked the circuit.
- Keep the unaffected boxes away from the wet ones, and move dry boxes up and out if you can do it without touching the wet area.

**Calls to make this morning**

1. **Insurer (their emergency line, not just the loss adjuster).** Report the loss, say you are taking mitigation steps, and ask them to confirm in writing that freezing and drying costs are covered. Ask if they can bring the loss adjuster forward or accept photographs and a list. Don't accept the agent's word that the insurer will pay for everything. Get that from the insurer.
2. **A conservator or archive advice line.** In the UK, The National Archives and the Institute of Conservation (Conservation Register) can point you to someone, and many regional record offices will advise on a Saturday emergency. Tell them what you have frozen and ask what to do about the glossy items.
3. **Plumber or an electrician** to confirm the strongroom wiring is safe before anything is plugged in.

**Be clear about the limits**

- I can't see the material, so the split between freeze and dry is a judgement based on your description. When in doubt, freeze. It is the safer choice.
- Freezing is not the final fix. It is a pause. The long-term plan (freeze-drying, separating the photographs) should come from a conservator and the insurer.
- Whether the insurer pays for storage is not certain until they say so in writing. Keep receipts.

**Record as you go**

Keep a simple list: what was wet, what went to the freezer (box numbers and contents), what you dried, and what time. Keep the Polar Quay receipt and photos. The loss adjuster will want all of it on Tuesday.
