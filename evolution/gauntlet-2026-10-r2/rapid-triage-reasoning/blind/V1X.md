Short version: stop the drying, stop the skip, get the wettest material cold and booked for the 08:30 van, and keep everything else cool, still and documented. Assumptions are flagged at the end.

**Right now (next 10 minutes)**

1. **Stop the heaters and fans pointed at the material. Do not run extension leads into the strongroom.** The ceiling leaked through the light fitting, so the wiring is wet. Keep the light off at the wall. Ask the plumber or an electrician to isolate that circuit at the consumer unit if you can reach them. Nobody should stand in standing water near live fittings. Heat and fast drying are the worst thing for this material. The catalogue stack that went hard at the edges is your evidence: glossy coated paper fuses into a solid block and warps, and ledgers cockle and the ink can run.
2. **Tell the landlord's agent: nothing goes in the skip.** Say it plainly: "These are records and potentially insured items. Nothing is to be disposed of until the loss adjuster has seen and listed it on Tuesday." Disposal before the adjuster's visit could undermine the claim. The agent's claim that "the insurer will pay anyway" doesn't make the archive replaceable. The Cobbold records are unique. Note down what the agent said and the time. If the smell is the issue, the answer is to move the boxes out of the building, not to bin them (see below).
3. **Phone the insurer's claims line now** (or the broker). Report the loss, say that the loss adjuster is coming Tuesday, and ask whether they authorise emergency mitigation. Most policies require you to limit further damage, and freezing is standard. Ask whether they will agree to the freezer cost. Get the claim number and the name of the person you spoke to. Do this before 08:30 if you possibly can, but don't let a slow answer stop the van if you decide to freeze.

**The 08:30 van decision: confirm, but for the right items**

Freezing stops mould (it starts growing in about 48 to 72 hours at 22 °C and muggy conditions, so by Monday you'd be at the edge) and stops further damage. It buys you time to arrange proper vacuum freeze-drying or air-drying with conservators. It is the standard emergency response for wet paper records. Confirm the collection.

- **Cost:** £9 per box per month. 60 boxes is about £540 a month. This is affordable against £4,000 for a month or two, and probably claimable. If you freeze only part, the cost is lower. Ask Polar Quay for a minimum term and for pallet or bulk rates.
- **What goes first:** the soaked boxes, and above all the glossy photographic prints and the shiny-paper catalogues. Coated paper blocks (sticks together) fast once it starts to dry, and freezing before it dries is what lets a conservator separate it. Don't try to pull stuck items apart now.
- **Ledgers and letters, if you can't fit everything:** soaked ones go. Merely damp ones can wait, or be handled as below.
- **How to pack:** leave things in their boxes or stacks. If you can, put the boxes into clean bin bags or polythene (open enough not to trap water), or use crates, so nothing falls apart in transit. Do not unpack, separate or press. Label each box with its shelf location and a number, as you go. If a box is falling apart, put it, contents and all, into a crate or bag. Write the box number on it.
- **Photos first:** before boxes leave, have one volunteer take quick phone photos of the shelves, the damage, the ceiling and the light fitting, and any boxes as they come off. That is your insurance record.

**For the rest of the morning**

- **Move the cold chain, not the heat.** Anything that is wet but not going this morning should stay cool and still, in the coolest room you have, not in a warm hall. Keep it away from the heaters. Use fans only to move air in the room, not on the material, and only on a safe circuit. A dehumidifier in the room helps if you can power it safely.
- **Take the volunteers off drying and give them jobs:** one person on the phone to the insurer, one photographing and listing, two carrying boxes (from the wet shelves, lowest first), one labelling, one on the van.
- **Don't air-dry the photographs and catalogues at all.** If any prints are just damp and not stuck, you could lay them face up on clean blotting paper or unprinted paper, but if in doubt, freeze them.
- **Mould:** wear gloves and a mask if you see any. If something is already mouldy, bag it separately and freeze it too, and don't brush it.
- **Smell and the 09:00 deadline:** the boxes will be out of the building on the van. Anything left should be moved from the strongroom into a closed, cool space. That should address the agent's concern.

**Next few days**

- Monday: call a conservator or the regional archive service. Many county record offices and the National Archives have emergency advice lines for disasters. Ask about vacuum freeze-drying and how to prioritise boxes. Ask them to join the adjuster visit if possible.
- Tuesday: give the adjuster the photos, the box list and your notes. Ask for written confirmation that freezing and conservation costs are covered.
- Keep the receipts and the plumber's report.

**Assumptions:** I've assumed the insurer is reachable on a Saturday (many have 24-hour claims lines), that the freezer will accept boxes in bags or crates, that the Trust owns the records and is the policyholder, and that the electrics are not yet isolated, which is why I've said to stay out until they are. If you can't get through to the insurer before 08:30, confirm the van anyway. A month of freezing costs a few hundred pounds. Losing the Cobbold records can't be undone.
