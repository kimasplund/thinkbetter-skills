Confirm the Polar Quay van now, before 08:30. Freeze everything that is soaked, keep the skip away from the boxes, and turn the heaters off.

**Do these first, in this order**

1. **Phone Polar Quay and confirm the collection.** Freezing stops mould and further damage, and you can thaw and dry the items later at whatever pace you can afford. Drying 60 soaked boxes by hand in a 22 °C, muggy building is not realistic. Mould typically starts within 48 to 72 hours, and Monday is already near that limit. At £9 a box, 60 boxes is about £540 a month, which you can afford out of £4,000. Ask them three things on the call: whether there is a minimum term, whether there is a collection charge, and whether the van can take everything in one trip. If it can't, send the photographic prints, the shiny catalogues and the wettest ledgers first.

2. **Switch the fan heaters off and put no extension leads into the strongroom.** Water came through the light fitting, so the room's wiring and fittings are wet. Ask someone to switch that lighting circuit off at the consumer unit, not just at the wall switch. Keep the volunteers out of any standing water near sockets. Use torches or battery lamps in the room. The fans are fine in the hall, plugged in there. They move air without adding heat.

3. **Don't dry the glossy paper.** Heat is what hardened the catalogue edges. The photographic prints and shiny catalogues are the worst items to air-dry, because the coating sticks to whatever it touches. Leave stuck stacks stuck. Don't peel them apart. Freezer first, and a conservator can separate them later. Put the heated stack in the freezer too.

4. **Don't let anything go in the skip.** That decision can't be undone, and it would make your claim harder. The loss adjuster can't assess what they can't see, and an agent has no authority to promise what the insurer will pay. Tell the agent politely that nothing is being thrown away today. The wet boxes will be out of the building by about 08:30, and the smell will fade once they are gone. Ask them to put any request to dispose of anything in writing.

**While the boxes go out, over the next 40 minutes**

- **Photograph before you move.** Take a few wide shots of the room and the shelves, and a close shot of the worst boxes. This costs about five minutes and is your evidence for the insurer.
- **Move the boxes carefully.** Wet paper is weak, so support each box from underneath and don't lift by the handles. Carry upright, don't unpack, and don't sort. Put each box in a bin bag or crate liner if you have one. Number the boxes with a marker on the outside as they leave so you can list them later.
- **Split the work.** Two or three volunteers carry, one writes the box list, and one stays at the freezer van.
- **Boxes that are only damp, not soaked:** keep them in the hall, spread out upright with air moving from the fans and no heat, and check them every hour. Anything that is still wet by Sunday should go in the freezer on Monday.
- **Strongroom:** open any windows or vents you can and bring in a dehumidifier if you can get one today. Empty shelves can wait.

**Calls to make this morning (not later)**

- **Insurer:** most policies have an emergency or claims line that is open at weekends. Report the loss today, say that you are freezing the material to stop mould, and ask them to confirm that the storage costs are covered. Keep the policy number and a note of who you spoke to. Ask whether they want to send someone sooner than Tuesday. Tuesday is fine for the adjuster, but they should know the freezer is happening.
- **Conservator:** leave a message with a paper conservator or your county record office. Many have an emergency advice line, and some will help with recovery of local archives. Freeze-drying or vacuum-drying is the likely route for the coated material, and they can say what it costs.

**Afterwards**

- Keep the box list and Polar Quay's receipt.
- Don't thaw anything until you have a drying plan and the conservator's advice.
- Have the plumber's visit and the cause of the leak written up for the insurer.
- Find out which part of the building is the landlord's responsibility. The leak came from the flat above, and that may affect who pays.

**The one thing I'm assuming:** that "soaked" means actually wet through. If some of the ledgers are only slightly damp, dry them in the hall with fans and no heat, and freeze the rest.
