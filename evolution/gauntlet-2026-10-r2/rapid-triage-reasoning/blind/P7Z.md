Confirm the freezer collection with Polar Quay before 08:30, and send nothing to the skip. Freezing is the one option that stops mould and stops the glossy material fusing further, and it is fully reversible: the boxes can be thawed or freeze-dried later. It costs about £540 a month for 60 boxes, which you can afford for a few months while the insurer is sorted out.

**Next 10 minutes**

1. **Phone Polar Quay now and confirm.** Ask for crates or bags if they supply them, and ask what they need for labelling. Ask them to hold the −20 °C space for everything wet, not only the 60 boxes if more turn up.
2. **Stop the heater and lead plan.** Do not run extension leads into the strongroom. Water came through the light fitting, so that circuit and the room are an electrocution risk. Switch the strongroom circuit off at the consumer unit, not just the wall switch. Don't use the fans or heaters on the wet material at all. Heat on stuck, coated paper hardens it, as the catalogues have shown, and warm air at 22 °C and humid is what mould wants. The volunteers can use the fans in the hall, plugged in there, only to move air through the hall, not at the boxes.
3. **Don't lay everything out to dry.** Spreading out 60 boxes with ledgers, letters, 300 glossy prints and stuck catalogues would take days. The prints and shiny catalogues will stick fast to whatever they dry against. Mould typically starts within 48 hours in warm, damp conditions, so the clock is already running. Air drying is only for items that are merely damp, not wet.
4. **Photograph before you move anything.** Two volunteers take quick phone photos of the room, the ceiling, the light fitting and the shelves, and photos of the boxes in place. That is your evidence for the insurer and the loss adjuster, and it takes five minutes.
5. **Pack the wet boxes as they are.** Don't unpack, separate, unfold or peel anything. Leave stuck catalogues and prints stuck together. Lift each box with two hands from underneath, because wet cardboard and paper fail at the base. Put each in a bag or crate, label it with its shelf position and contents, and keep a simple list on paper as you go. Prioritise the dripping boxes and the photographs and glossy catalogues first, then the ledgers and letters.
6. **Include the catalogues from the heater.** Freeze them too and note on the list that they were heated. Do not try to open them.
7. **Anything only slightly damp** (box damp but contents dry) can be moved to the hall, spread on tables, and left in cool, moving air. If in doubt, freeze it.

**The landlord's agent and the skip**

Say no, politely and firmly: the boxes are leaving the building in the van this morning, which solves the smell, and nothing is going in the skip. Once they are in the skip they are gone for good. If the agent insists on removing material, ask for that in writing.

**Insurer**

Ring the insurer's claims line today, even though it's Saturday, and leave a message if it's out of hours. Report the date, the cause and the loss, and ask whether you can proceed with freezing as emergency mitigation and whether the freezer cost is covered. Ask for it in writing or note the name and time of the call. Keep every receipt.

**Money**

Freezing 60 boxes at £9 a month is £540 a month, plus any crates or bags. That fits within £4,000 for now. If the claim stalls, the Trust has several months before cash becomes a problem, but ask the insurer to cover it from the start.

**What to check once the boxes are away**

- Is the strongroom circuit isolated, and has an electrician checked the light fitting and wiring before anyone switches anything on?
- Get a dehumidifier into the strongroom if it can be powered safely from outside, and open doors or vents. Keep the room cool. The shelves above may be damp even if they look dry, so check them again later today.
- Are there more boxes elsewhere, including on higher shelves, that got damp? Check them this morning and move them if they are cool and damp.
