If you can't do that by about 22:40, swap the splash. The decision is yours to make and document, but try hard to reach a senior person first.

**Why not simply run it or simply hold it**
- Running it is hard to undo: 38,000 copies, plus a 00:01 web and app alert to 70,000 readers and a sister-title wire. If the report is forged, you've published a false fire-safety fraud claim about a named company, and you have a written legal warning on file that you were told it was forged. That is the worst position to be in for libel and for the Courier's credibility.
- Holding costs you little that can't be recovered. The Gazette might get there first, but being second and right beats being first and wrong. Swapping the splash takes about 20 minutes, and the story can run tomorrow online or the day after.
- The lawyer's claim is unverified, and so is yours. His offer may be sincere, or it may be a way to learn what you have, find your source, or get the document quarantined. Treat it as neither true nor false.

**Timeline (adjust to your clock)**

*22:05-22:15: make the safe moves first*
1. Do not email the scan to the lawyer. Sending it exposes your source's route (the stamps and initials are potentially identifying) and gives him a chance to pre-empt or discredit it. Reply with a short holding line: you have received his message, you are considering it, and you'll respond properly. Ask him to put in writing exactly what he says is false (stamps, initials, dates, content), without sending anything. Admit nothing about the document.
2. Prevent the automatic release. Pull or unschedule the 00:01 web and app alert now, so a decision at 22:50 isn't undone by a system that fires on its own. Then check it actually is unscheduled.
3. Handle the wire. Contact the sister titles' desks (or the wire desk) and tell them the 00:01 embargo item is under review and must not be used without a further message from you. Follow with a written note. Their systems may auto-publish, so confirm receipt rather than assuming.
4. Keep trying Ines in several ways (calls, texts, her partner or family, whoever's on the wedding party list). Also reach whoever is the next level up: the deputy or managing editor, the Courier's legal adviser or on-call lawyer, or the group's duty editor. A 20-second voicemail with "decision needed by 22:45, here's the one-line question" is better than silence. If anyone senior answers, they decide. If nobody does, you decide and record why.

*22:15-22:40: test the document in ways that don't involve Vosk*
5. Call Dagny now and ask:
   - How was the document obtained and how well does she trust the source (track record, motive)?
   - Has she had it independently confirmed by a second person, or seen the original or a matching entry in a register?
   - Has Vosk or the county been approached for comment before tonight? If not, that's a fairness gap you need to close.
   - What corroboration exists apart from the report itself (a fire-safety officer, the care home, a council member, planning or building control records, the fire service)?
6. Examine the scan yourselves: the file's metadata and scan history, whether the stamp style and initials match other genuine documents from that office that you have on file, dates and reference numbers, whether names and job titles are consistent with the people actually working there, and whether the inspection and sign-off dates make sense. This won't prove it, but a visible anomaly would settle the question toward holding.
7. Look for a source-protected route to authenticity. Ask the source, through Dagny, whether they can confirm to a second person or point to a public record (completion certificate, building control register, fire-service consultation record) that corroborates the key fact. A county officer who can say "yes, that's the genuine report" is worth more than anything the lawyer offers. The key fact is the sign-off being faked, not just the report's existence.
8. Try the county out of hours. The press office is closed, but there may be an out-of-hours press line, a duty officer, a councillor or the chief executive's office. Ask a narrow question (does this reference number exist, and was an inspection recorded on that date?) without sending the document.
9. Consider the care home and the fire service. If there's a genuine live safety risk, that's a separate reason to act: someone should alert the relevant authority even if the story is held.

*22:40-22:50: decide*
Run it only if all of these are true:
- Someone with the authority to decide has agreed, ideally Ines or a deputy plus a lawyer, and you can say who and when.
- The document has been corroborated independently, not just by the source.
- Vosk has had a real chance to respond. A comment request with a short deadline and the substance of the allegation, but not the document, is the minimum, and the lawyer's claim should be reported in the story.
- The story is worded to what you can prove, not stronger than the evidence.

Otherwise, swap the splash at 22:50 so the plates make the 23:00 deadline with margin. A held-over feature in a 20-minute swap fits, but only if you start by about 22:35-22:40. Tell the subs to be ready from 22:30 so you aren't deciding and scrambling at once.
