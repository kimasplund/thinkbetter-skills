Book the freezer van now, and stop the heaters. Freezing is the one move that holds everything as it is while you wait for the adjuster and for expert advice. Skipping the boxes is the one move you can never undo.

**Do these in order**

1. **07:45, switch off both fan heaters and keep all leads out of the strongroom.** Water has run through the light fitting, so treat the room's wiring as unsafe until an electrician has checked it. Use torches or hall lighting. If you want fans, plug them in out in the hall, not in the strongroom. Heat is the wrong tool here. Your trial stack shows it: the catalogue edges hardened and the sheets are still stuck. At 22 °C and muggy, mould can start within 48 to 72 hours, so the aim is to stop the clock, not to dry things fast.

2. **Confirm Polar Quay before 08:30.** The cost is about £540 a month for 60 boxes, which you can afford from £4,000. It is also fully reversible, because frozen material can be thawed or vacuum freeze-dried later. When you ring, ask four things: the van's arrival time, whether the £9 is per box or per archive box of any size, whether there is a minimum term, and whether they will take loose items in sealed bags if some boxes fall apart. Confirming does not mean everything has to be packed by 08:30. It means the van comes this morning.

3. **Pack what goes in the freezer.** Everything wet that you can't dry in the hall within about 48 hours:
   - the photographic prints
   - the glossy sale catalogues
   - the soaked ledgers and letters

   Leave stuck stacks stuck. Don't peel, separate or rub anything. Put each box, or each stuck stack, in a polythene bag if you have bin liners or similar to hand. Label the bag with a box number. Keep the original box order. Pull out the wettest and most important Cobbold ledgers first if you run short of time, then work down.

4. **Only air-dry material that is merely damp.** That means plain, uncoated paper that isn't soaked and isn't stuck. Lay it out in the hall, cool, with moving air from fans and no heat. Interleave with clean blotting paper or plain paper if you have it, and turn it regularly. If in doubt, freeze it.

5. **Deal with the landlord's agent.** Tell them in writing (a text or email is enough) that the wet boxes are leaving the building in the freezer van this morning, which solves the smell before 09:00. They are not going in the skip. Don't rely on "the insurer will pay for it all". A skip makes the loss permanent. Ledgers and photographs from the Cobbold yard can't be replaced, and the adjuster hasn't yet seen what you have. Also don't accept an offer to bin anything before the adjuster has seen it.

6. **Record before you move.** Before the first box leaves, have one volunteer take wide photos of the room, the leak and the ceiling, then of the shelves, then of the boxes. Keep a simple list with box numbers, what each holds and its condition. This protects your claim.

7. **Notify the insurer today.** Ring or email the Trust's insurer or broker, report the loss, say you are freezing the archive to prevent further damage, and ask them to confirm that the freezer cost is covered. Keep the plumber's invoice. Ask whether the adjuster can come earlier than Tuesday.

**Safety for volunteers.** Wet paper in a warm room grows mould quickly. Gloves, a mask if anyone has asthma, and a window or door open for ventilation. If you have a dehumidifier, run it in the room, powered from a dry circuit outside it.

**Next 24 to 72 hours**
- Get an electrician to check the strongroom wiring before anyone turns the light back on.
- Contact a conservator or archive-salvage service (your county record office or a national archives advisory service will usually give free first advice) about vacuum freeze-drying the frozen boxes. It is the standard treatment for stuck coated paper and photographs.
- Find the source of the leak, and have the landlord or plumber fix the pipe properly. Check whether the lease or the landlord's insurance puts any of the cost on them.
- Check the remaining shelves for hidden damp or mould over the next few days.
- Decide on the frozen material only once the adjuster has seen the records and you have a drying quote. Freezer fees are running at £540 a month, so don't leave it indefinitely.
