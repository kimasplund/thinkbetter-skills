# J-B scores, brief-2

Notes:
- O3F: Covers the essentials well (electrics, heat, freeze, skip, insurer), and the volunteer roles are useful. It's long. "Bagged and gone before 09:00" assumes the van arrives in time, which the brief doesn't say. The assumption line at the end is thin.
- P7Z: Good catches: freeze the heated stack and note it was heated, keep anything that must leave before 09:00 bagged in the hall rather than skipped, and check the higher shelves. "Fully reversible" overclaims, and vacuum freeze-drying for photographs is offered without any caveat. No assumptions stated.
- Q3J: Sensible selective freeze with good air-drying procedure, and an honest limits section. But it claims photographs "cannot be air dried successfully by volunteers", which is doubtful, and it's the longest and most scaffolded reply (it ends in a checklist). It names the plumber as someone who can confirm the wiring.
- V1X: Strongest reasoning. It says to confirm the van even if the insurer can't be reached, notes the duty to mitigate, ink run and bagging mouldy items separately, and has an explicit assumptions block. It assumes the van clears the building by 09:00. "Assumptions are flagged at the end" is a small trace of scaffolding.
- U6A: Puts human safety first and says why. It notes that the chair doesn't know the Trust's own cover, warns about water-soluble ink, and offers a fallback ("out of the strongroom" if not out of the building). Assumptions are clear, and it closes with a useful "three things before 08:30".
- S0I: Tight and actionable. But "affordable for a few weeks" understates a runway of about 7 months, "Damp paper is the one that goes mouldy" is muddled, and it says flatly that the van gets everything out by 09:00. No assumptions stated.
- Y6K: Solid. It flags the risk of the wet ceiling and asks the insurer to confirm in writing that nothing should be discarded. "The insurer should reimburse it" is overconfident, and calling the sink-pipe water "relatively clean" is an unflagged assumption.
- G0E: Clear priority order for the van, and it lists sensible questions to put to Polar Quay. It invents "strongroom heating" to turn down, and asking the agent for permission to keep the door open is odd. The assumptions block is reasonable.
- O5I: Has a subtle point the others miss: confirming at 08:30 doesn't mean everything is packed by 08:30. It also asks about box-size pricing and raises the landlord's liability. Its packing priority (wettest ledgers first) conflicts with its own prints-first logic, and "standard treatment for ... photographs" overstates.
- N1L: Says plainly that the agent has no authority to promise what the insurer will pay. Notes that the leak came from the flat above, so liability matters, and freezes any damp boxes still wet by Sunday on Monday. But it invents a time ("out of the building by about 08:30").
- G9M: Its fallback if the van is missed and its handling of a possible insurer objection are both good, and only it raises the deposited-material point. "Handled wet and unfrozen, it can't be saved" overstates. It's long.
- F3B: Spots that the prints and catalogues may be extra to the 60 boxes, and asks about releasing boxes individually. It tells the chair to "give the claim reference" when no claim exists yet, and "few weeks" and "almost always is" are loose.
