In the power industry, demand response means getting customers to lower or shift their electricity use at times when the grid is stressed, instead of building or running more generating capacity to meet the peak. Utilities do it with price signals (time-of-use or peak pricing), with payments to customers who agree to cut load when called (large factories, or irrigation pumpers on interruptible rates), or with direct control of equipment such as air conditioners and pumps. The idea is that reducing demand at the right moment is often cheaper and faster than adding supply, which is probably why she is raising it with you: an irrigation district can offer a similar lever by shifting deliveries or pumping to off-peak hours, or by asking users to defer or reduce diversions during tight periods.
