I coordinate the Mossgate Starter Commons, a volunteer-run nonprofit that keeps 140 heirloom fermentation cultures: sourdough starters, kefir grains, vinegar mothers and a few koji strains, nearly all donated by local families. So far only volunteers have had access. We open to the public in March, and demand is already clear: 230 people have signed up, and 80 of them want the same 1890s rye starter.

Our board member Ines Varga ran the county library service for twenty years and has drafted the rules. Members borrow one jar at a time for four weeks, with up to two renewals. Popular cultures get a holds queue. Overdue jars cost €3 a week, and there's a replacement charge if a jar comes back dead or spoiled. Returned jars go back on the shelf for the next borrower. Her view is that we're a library whose books happen to need feeding.

How things run today: each culture lives in two jars in our back-room fridges and is fed on its own schedule. One volunteer, Teodor, does the feeding, about six hours a week, and he says he's at his limit. Many families asked, when they donated, that their original line be kept going. Last spring two kefir lines picked up a wild yeast and one was lost for good; we traced it to a volunteer who had topped up the shelf jars with grains she'd been keeping at home. Several volunteers also mention that their home portions of the rye starter smell and rise quite differently from ours after a few months.

Membership cards go to the printer in three weeks. I'd like a second opinion on Ines's rules before then. What would you change, and why?
