I'm registrar of the Eastmarch Deposit Tribunal, which decides disputes between tenants and landlords over rental deposits. We have six adjudicators and 9,400 open cases, and the median wait from filing to hearing is now 14 months. New filings run at about 640 a month, and the backlog has grown by roughly 50 a month for two years.

Our new president, Dr Halvard Messer, ran hospital emergency departments for fifteen years before moving into tribunal administration, and he wants us to triage like an ED. A triage officer would review each new filing within 48 hours and place it in one of five urgency categories, each with a target time to hearing, from three weeks for category 1 down to "as capacity allows" for category 5. The category would be set from the claim form, which already has a free-text "reasons for urgency" box that about 70% of tenant filers complete.

Some background. Today every case gets a hearing date at filing, strictly in order of receipt. Around 60% of cases settle or are withdrawn before a hearing, and adjudicators complain that most of those settlements land in the four weeks before the hearing date, after the hearing notice has gone out, so prepared slots go to waste. In this jurisdiction the landlord holds the deposit until there is a ruling or a settlement. About 85% of claims are brought by tenants seeking their deposit back.

Messer wants a pilot running from January. I've been asked to advise the president's committee on whether the emergency-department approach suits us and how the pilot should be designed. What should I tell them?
