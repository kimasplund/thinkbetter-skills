# Pack notes

**Brief 1 (independent school campus consolidation).** The visible conflict is parents and teachers against the closure, with the board vote as the stated hurdle. The four keys sit elsewhere. The Halloran deed wording (k1) is dressed as plaque nostalgia but may make the heirs or a charity regulator a veto holder over the sale. The enrolment ceiling (k2) is waved away by "space isn't the issue" and "never come close to", yet 640 students on the south campus almost certainly breaches it and hands the Sycamore Lane neighbours a planning-board veto. The Ardelle pledge (k3) is offered as good fundraising news but is restricted to the campus being closed. Re-enrolment timing (k4) makes families, not trustees, the real veto: the vote lands mid-window and can drive attrition below the 92% assumption. k1 and k2 are the main misses for a quick answer; k3 is a likely miss.

**Brief 2 (irrigation district drought allocation).** The salient fight is orchards against row crops over priority versus equal shares. The Dunmere seepage agreement (k1) is buried in a budget line and is breached by the manager's own "efficiency" closure of the east lateral. The board chair's offhand remark about fallowing (k2) reveals that open trading at any price makes him the main seller, so trading rules decide whether orchard growers accept. Acreage-weighted ratification means Quillon, not the vocal tenant Benning, holds the 2,900-acre vote (k3). The orchard growers' real need is post-harvest timing rather than volume (k4), which the August 1 closure destroys but which can be met inside equal shares. k1 and k3 are the main misses; k2 is a likely miss.

**Brief 3 (shopping-centre anchor replacement).** The surface question is whether to accept the departing grocer's rent cut or re-let to a gym and clinic. The inline tenants' grocery co-tenancy protections (k1) are the easiest key. The user's line "we don't need anything from Harrowfield" points the wrong way: Harrowfield's fuel pad and the recorded reciprocal easement (k2) likely give it consent rights over the re-let. Its loyalty-card link between fuel and in-store spend (k3) shows its real interest is grocery traffic, which is both a motive to block and a basis for a deal. The lender's "always routine" sign-off (k4) becomes a real veto once the grocery anchor goes. k2, k3 and k4 are the main misses for a quick answer.
