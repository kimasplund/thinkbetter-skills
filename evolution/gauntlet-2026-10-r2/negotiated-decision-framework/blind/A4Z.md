**Recommendation:** Take the gym-plus-clinic plan to the November 20 committee, but ask for conditional approval, not a clean go. The premise that you don't need anything from Harrowfield is wrong in three places. Each could sink the plan or cost real money, and each can be checked in the next few weeks. Don't tell Harrowfield, the inline tenants or the lender anything until you have checked them.

## What can block this, and what to do about each

**1. The 26 inline leases (the biggest risk).** The 2015 grocery co-tenancy protections were the price of filling the centre. Pull them and read the exact triggers. If the clauses fire when the grocery goes dark, or when it is replaced by a non-grocery use, you could face rent reductions, kick-out rights, or both across all 26 shops. That would wipe out the 30% uplift quickly. Three things to settle:
- Does a fitness or medical anchor count as a replacement anchor? Many clauses name "supermarket" or a minimum-sales food use, so check the wording.
- How long is the dark-period cushion? Harrowfield's expiry is next December, so you can plan a handover with no gap. A gap is what triggers most of these clauses.
- Do any inline leases carry exclusives or prohibited-use clauses (pharmacy, physio, wellness) that Sorrelwood's clinic or the gym would breach?

If the clauses are triggered, the fix is to negotiate amendments before you sign the new anchors. Offer the inline tenants something concrete: a waiver of the grocery clause in return for a marketing fund, a short rent abatement, or a footfall guarantee backed by the two operators' opening dates. Include the 24-hour gym and urgent care in the pitch. Both generate steady daily visits, which a struggling grocery no longer does.

**2. Harrowfield, through the fuel station and the easement.** Harrowfield won't be gone when the lease ends. It owns the pad outright and holds recorded rights over your entrances and parking. Get the easement and the 2009 sale documents read by counsel for:
- Any limit on use changes, hours or traffic at the shared entrances (a 24-hour gym changes the traffic pattern).
- Any parking guarantee for the station, or rules on sight lines and signage.
- Any right of first refusal, or restriction on competing fuel or grocery uses.

The loyalty card matters here. Harrowfield's fuel discount depends on in-store spend, so losing the store hurts its fuel business too. That gives it a reason to cooperate, and also a reason to resist. Expect it to be difficult if it thinks you are cutting off its fuel traffic. It also gives you a trade. If you want clean easement terms and a quiet exit, consider a modest accommodation: a signage position, a no-objection agreement covering the new uses, or a short extension on better terms. Open that conversation only after the easement review, and only with counsel in the room.

Also check whether the lease has a renewal option or a notice deadline coming up. If Harrowfield can renew at the existing terms, or can hold over, "the lease simply runs out" does not hold.

**3. Marchbank Life.** Both new leases (22,000 and 16,000 sq ft) exceed the 15,000 sq ft consent threshold, so Marchbank has to approve each one. Sign-off has been routine for grocery-anchored leases, but here you are replacing the credit anchor with two new operators, and Marchbank will look hard at the co-tenancy exposure above. Approach them early with the plan, the inline-tenant position and the covenant strength of Ironvale and Sorrelwood. Make signed lender consent a condition in both new leases, so you aren't left holding a commitment you can't deliver.

## Two smaller points on the new tenants

- **Parking.** "Rarely half full" describes a grocery-anchored lot. A gym peaks at 5-8 am and 5-9 pm, and an urgent-care clinic peaks at midday and evenings. Ask for a simple count of occupied stalls at those hours, and check the easement's share of the lot. 610 stalls is probably enough, but the evidence takes a day to collect.
- **Letters of intent.** They are not binding, and two operators with a combined 38,000 sq ft of build-out cost and a clinic's regulatory approvals are exposed to delays. Ask about timing, landlord contributions and permitted-use approvals now. The 30% rent uplift should be shown net of tenant improvement allowances, free rent and downtime, because that is what the committee will probe.

## How to bring people in, in order

1. **Now to early November:** Read the co-tenancy and exclusive clauses and the easement. Count peak parking. Talk to Ironvale and Sorrelwood about timing and approvals, and ask each to confirm in writing that it will accept a condition tied to lender consent.
2. **Before November 20:** Give Marchbank a short outline and get a read on their appetite. Brief the committee on the three conditions (inline waivers, easement clearance, lender consent) and on what you will do if any one fails.
3. **After approval:** Meet the inline tenants one-on-one, starting with those whose leases have the strongest protections. Bring the amendment offer and the new operators' opening plan. They will mostly care about footfall and about when the grocery's loss shows up in their sales.
4. **Then Harrowfield:** Talk to them once counsel has told you what the easement allows. They may prefer a clean exit with some concessions to a drawn-out fight.

## What the committee should see

The headline is that replacing Harrowfield is likely the better deal, with roughly 30% more rent from tenants who bring daily traffic. The ask is for approval subject to three conditions: inline-tenant waivers or acceptable amendments, easement clearance, and lender consent. Show the downside case in which the co-tenancy clauses fire, with the rent at risk across the 26 shops set against the anchor uplift.

## What would change this

If the co-tenancy clauses are triggered by any non-grocery replacement and the inline tenants won't waive them, the uplift may be smaller than the exposure, and a discounted Harrowfield renewal could be cheaper. The fastest check is to read the clauses and total the rent they put at risk. That takes a few days and is worth doing before anyone else is told.
