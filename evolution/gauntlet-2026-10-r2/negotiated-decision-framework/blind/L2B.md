**Recommendation:** Don't take the sale to a vote on February 12 as it stands. Take a decision to close the north campus in principle, conditional on four checks, with the sale itself voted only when those checks come back clean. Three of the checks are legal or contractual facts that could stop the plan after you've spent your credibility on it. You should know the answers before you ask ten of fifteen trustees to commit.

## The four things that can sink this, in order

1. **The Halloran gift.** The plaque says "for the education of the children of Brennock." Get the original deed or gift instrument in front of your counsel now. If it carries a use restriction or a reverter, a $13.5M sale to a homebuilder may need the attorney general's or a court's approval, or may not be allowed at all. Vellacott's letter of intent is only as good as your title. Opponents will find the plaque even if you don't raise it, and a trustee who hears about it first from a parent will vote no. Ask counsel three questions. Is the restriction binding? Can the proceeds be tied to Brennock children's education, for example by putting the $8M into the lower school and the rest into financial aid? Is there a court or AG process, and how long does it take? If approval takes months, the February 12 date doesn't work.

2. **The Ardelle pledge.** $800k is still to come, and it's for the north-campus outdoor learning program. Read the pledge agreement and call the foundation before anyone else does. Closing the site may let them stop paying or demand money back, and it will certainly upset them if they hear it second-hand. The better outcome is that they agree to move the program to the south campus, ideally with a named outdoor learning space in the new lower-school building. Ask them, don't announce it to them. Their view also helps with the Hallorans' descendants and the town.

3. **The Sycamore Lane conditions.** You said the enrolment ceiling has never been close. That is a reason to read it, not to ignore it. Find the actual number. If it sits below 700, or below 640 plus growth, you need the planning board to change it, in front of the same neighbours who fought the gym. Adding a lower-school building almost certainly needs its own approval, and moving 230 young children will make the traffic marshal and drop-off conditions much more demanding. Neighbours are the party that can stop the south-campus half of the plan, and so far no one has talked to them. Meet with them before the vote, and bring concrete offers: a revised drop-off plan, staggered start times, a separate lower-school entrance, and a traffic cap written into the approval. Do not sign the sale agreement until you know what the board will approve.

4. **Vellacott's terms.** A letter of intent is usually non-binding, and buyers like this typically make closing conditional on rezoning. Find out the closing conditions, the price adjustments if rezoning is delayed or reduced, the deposit, and whether there is a long exclusivity period. Check that $13.5M reflects the zoning you expect, and whether another buyer, including the town or a conservation trust, would pay close to that for a site with community value. A second bidder gives you leverage and also an answer to "why Vellacott?"

## Why this is also your vote strategy

You need ten of fifteen trustees, so you can lose five. The trustees who worry will not be worried about the deficit. They will worry about being the people who sold the Halloran land, lost the Ardelle money and got sued or embarrassed by neighbours. A proposal that arrives with the legal, donor and permitting answers in hand removes most of those objections. A proposal that doesn't will lose the swing votes, and you won't know until the roll call.

Do this before February 12:
- Count the votes individually now, one conversation per trustee, and ask what would make each a yes. Don't rely on the committee meeting. If you're short of ten, you have time to fix the plan.
- Show the trustees the alternatives you rejected, with numbers: staying open with cuts, a partial consolidation (for example, keeping K–2 north), leasing the north site rather than selling it, and a sale-leaseback of part of it. If the trustees can see why the others fail, they can defend the choice to parents. A deficit of $1.1M on $19M is about 6%. Show what the sale does and what each alternative would do to the deficit.
- Keep the board's own sequence clean: first a vote on the principle of consolidation, then a vote on the sale once the checks are in. Two votes, each easier to win than one big one.

## Families and staff

**Timing is your biggest risk.** Re-enrolment contracts go out January 10 and are due March 1, and the board votes in between. Closure is not until fall 2028, so the contracts cover fall 2027 and the north campus is still open for it. But if families read the announcement as "the school is closing our campus," many of the 8% margin in your 92% will go. Decide what you say on January 10. You have two honest choices. Either tell families before the contracts go out that consolidation is under consideration and that the earliest move is fall 2028, or hold the announcement until after March 1. The second is tempting and probably wrong: if parents feel they re-enrolled before being told, you will lose their trust, and that will cost more than the lost enrolments. Tell them early. Then model the re-enrolment hit explicitly. At 85% instead of 92%, how much does the deficit grow, and does the sale still cover it? Put that number in front of the board.

**Lower-school parents** have two real worries, and they are different. The commute (nine kilometres each way) is a logistics problem, so offer bus routes, a before-and-after-school programme, and a guarantee of places for siblings. Young children sharing a campus with teenagers is a safety and culture worry, and only design answers it: a separate building with its own entrance, play area and drop-off, different bell times, and no unsupervised shared spaces. Give a small group of lower-school parents a real role in the design brief and a visible veto on the safety features. Show them examples of other K–12 schools that do this. If you can't offer a physically separate lower school, expect the families with the choice, who are the paying ones, to leave.

**Teachers** are worried about jobs, and the honest answer matters. Consolidation will reduce some posts unless enrolment holds. Say now what you know. If you can commit that no one will be laid off for the move itself and that reductions will come through attrition and retirements, say so. If you can't, say when you will know and what support you will provide. Don't let it be a rumour. Include lower-school teachers in the design of the new building: they know what 5-year-olds need and they will be the loudest voices for or against.

**Make the north site part of the story.** The community is not mourning a building. It is mourning a promise. If the sale is allowed, commit part of the proceeds in public to something that carries the Halloran line forward: a financial-aid fund for Brennock children named for the family, an outdoor learning space on the south campus, or a community-use agreement for part of the north site. Ask the Halloran family's descendants, if there are any, to help shape it before the announcement.

## What the plan should include

- A one-page timeline from now to fall 2028, with the legal, planning, donor and enrolment gates on it and a named owner for each.
- A financial case that shows the deficit under three re-enrolment scenarios, the full cost of the lower-school building (including contingency, since $8M is a number you should test), and what happens to the $13.5M if rezoning fails or delays.
- A fallback if any gate fails: for example, a smaller sale, a lease, or keeping K–2 north for a further two years.
- A communications plan with dates: trustees first, then Ardelle, then Sycamore Lane and the planning board, then staff, then families, then the press. No group should hear from another group.
- A fixed point for parents and staff to give input, with a visible list of what changed because of it.

## What would change this recommendation

If counsel says the Halloran gift is unrestricted and the Ardelle agreement lets the money follow the program, the plan gets much simpler and you could take the sale to the board on February 12 as you intended. The cheapest check is a short conversation with your lawyer this week. Read the deed, the pledge agreement and the 2004 planning approval, and you will know within days which version of this plan you're running.
