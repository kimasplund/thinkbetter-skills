**Short answer:** Your instinct is probably right on economics. But "we don't need anything from Harrowfield" is the assumption most likely to be wrong, and I wouldn't take a "replace the anchor" recommendation to committee until four items are checked. I'd recommend a conditional approval: proceed with Ironvale and Sorrelwood only if the checks below clear, and keep Harrowfield's counter-offer alive as the fallback until they do.

## Why the premise needs testing

1. **The 2015 co-tenancy clauses (biggest exposure).** Your 26 inline shops were leased on grocery co-tenancy protections. If Harrowfield goes dark or the anchor is replaced by a non-grocery use, those clauses may let tenants pay reduced rent, or terminate. Read every one. Key questions are what counts as a qualifying replacement (a gym and a clinic probably don't), the cure period, the remedy (rent abatement or termination), and when each lease expires. A 30% uplift on 38,000 sq ft can be wiped out by abatements across 26 shops. Also check whether any tenant has a food-related exclusive that interacts with Harrowfield leaving.

2. **The fuel station and the reciprocal easement.** Harrowfield owns the pad outright and has shared entrances and parking rights under a 2009 easement. That easement is perpetual and independent of the lease expiry. Check whether it has:
   - parking ratios or protected stall counts near the pad,
   - consent rights over changes to access, the site plan or the use of the main building,
   - use restrictions or a covenant tied to a grocery or retail anchor,
   - any right of first refusal or similar.
   A 24-hour gym and a clinic change traffic and parking patterns. Harrowfield, as a departing tenant that still owns the front of your lot, has real leverage and little reason to be cooperative. It could also keep the station and sell the pad to someone else. Also, the loyalty card (10 cents a litre per $100 in-store) means the grocery drives fuel sales. Without the store, the station's economics weaken. That may make Harrowfield either more willing to deal or more inclined to litigate over the easement. Get counsel's reading before anyone speaks to Harrowfield about the future.

3. **Marchbank's consent.** Both new leases exceed 15,000 sq ft (22,000 and 16,000), so each needs sign-off. "Routine" has applied to renewals of a grocer. Replacing an anchor with a gym and a medical use is a different credit story to a lender: new tenants, shorter operating histories, possible loss of co-tenancy-protected income, and a re-tenanting period. Open the conversation early, bring the LOIs and tenant financials, and make consent a condition in both leases. Also check whether the mortgage has a debt-service or occupancy covenant that a vacancy gap could trip.

4. **Parking and use compliance.** 610 stalls and a lot "rarely half full" is a fair start, but the numbers shift. A 24-hour gym peaks evenings and early mornings, and urgent care peaks midday and evenings. Do a stall demand count by time of day, not an average. Confirm zoning and permitted use for medical and imaging (including any shielding or fit-out needs), parking by-law minimums per use, and any parking or exclusive-use covenants in the existing leases and the easement.

## Also check the basics

- **Is the lease really expiring?** Confirm there is no renewal option, right of first offer or holdover clause. A 40% rent cut offer suggests Harrowfield is negotiating, not leaving. Their sales decline works in your favour but doesn't remove their rights.
- **LOI strength.** LOIs are generally non-binding. Their terms need to survive the co-tenancy analysis, fit-out cost, free rent, TI allowance and timing. Compare net effective rent, not face rent. Check the covenant strength of both operators (a franchised or single-site gym is not the same credit as a grocer).
- **Downtime.** Harrowfield leaves next December. Two conversions of a grocery box (demising, services, medical build-out) take time. Check the fit-out period against rent commencement.
- **Rent comparison.** "30% more than today" is against Harrowfield's current rent, which may be above market. The real comparison is the new deal versus the 40%-cut renewal and versus the downside scenarios.

## Recommendation structure for November 20

Present three options with numbers, not two:
- **A. Renew Harrowfield at -40%.** Keeps co-tenancy intact and the fuel station relationship. Lowest risk, lowest income, and a weakening anchor.
- **B. Replace with Ironvale + Sorrelwood.** Highest headline income, with risks in co-tenancy, the easement, lender consent and parking.
- **C. Negotiated middle.** For example, a shorter or smaller Harrowfield renewal, or a reduced grocer footprint, with a gym or clinic taking the balance. Only worth modelling if the grocery has value to the inline shops or the fuel pad.

Ask the committee to approve B subject to conditions: counsel's opinion on the easement, a co-tenancy exposure schedule, lender consent in principle, a parking study, and conditions precedent in both leases. Include a downside case (every co-tenancy trigger fires) so they see the worst outcome.

## Bringing everyone along

Sequence matters. Do not announce anything until the legal review and lender conversation are done.

1. **Internally first:** legal counsel, the leasing team (who know what was promised in 2015), then the investment committee.
2. **Marchbank:** early and informal, before the committee if possible, so you can tell the committee what the lender thinks.
3. **Inline tenants:** once you are ready to decide, meet them before they hear rumours. Lead with what is in it for them: gym traffic through the day, a medical anchor drawing regular visits, and that the centre will remain well tenanted. Be straight about what changes. If their leases have co-tenancy rights, be prepared to offer something in exchange for waiving them (for example a modest rent concession, marketing support, or lease extension), because they will have counsel read the clauses anyway. A signed waiver or amendment from the key tenants is much better than hoping the clauses won't be invoked. Shops that rely on grocery traffic (bakery, deli, produce, pharmacy) will be the most concerned, so speak to them individually.
4. **Ironvale and Sorrelwood:** keep both engaged with exclusivity or a short LOI extension, and be clear that execution depends on lender consent and other conditions. Make sure their parking, signage, hours and operating-noise needs fit each other and the existing tenants. Overnight gym operation and a medical clinic have different expectations about lighting, security and access.
5. **Harrowfield:** this is the delicate one. They are a counterparty with continuing rights, not a former tenant. Decide your approach with counsel first. A cooperative exit (agreeing the easement arrangements, perhaps an early surrender, plus parking and signage terms for the station) is likely worth more than a standoff. Consider whether the fuel pad is something you would want to buy, or whether you can make the station's continued access an agreed item.

## What I'd say in one line to the committee

"Replacing Harrowfield looks attractive on income, and we recommend proceeding, but only subject to confirming that the inline co-tenancy clauses, the 2009 easement, Marchbank's consent and a parking study don't erode the uplift. We'll return with those answers, and Harrowfield's renewal stays available as a fallback until they are in hand."

Assumptions I made: your documents are as you describe them, the LOIs are non-binding, I haven't seen the co-tenancy language or the easement, and the above is a business framework rather than legal advice, so have counsel confirm the legal points.
