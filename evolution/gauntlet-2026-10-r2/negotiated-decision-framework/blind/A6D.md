**Recommendation:** Take the Ironvale and Sorrelwood deal to the November 20 committee, but ask for approval to proceed conditional on three document checks, not for a clean go. The money case is strong. The premise "we don't need anything from Harrowfield" is not safe yet, and the committee will be asked to rely on it.

## Why this is still probably the right call

Harrowfield's offer of 40% less rent on a declining store is a weak alternative. Two tenants paying about 30% more than Harrowfield pays today is roughly double the rent of the renewal case before costs, and a gym and an urgent-care/imaging clinic both create regular visits, which helps the inline shops over time. The parking is not the problem you might fear. 610 stalls on 140,000 sq ft is about 4.4 per 1,000, the lot is rarely half full, and a gym and clinic will use it differently from a grocer (evening peaks for the gym, daytime for the clinic), so their peaks overlap less than you would think. Check that against the easement's parking terms and local code before you promise anything.

The real cost is what the grocery leaving does to everyone else, and how much of that you have already contractually promised away. That is a document question, and you can answer it in a couple of weeks.

## What to do before November 20

1. **Read the 26 inline leases for the co-tenancy language.** Build one table: which tenants have rent reduction or termination rights, what counts as a replacement anchor, how long you have to cure, and the annual rent at stake. Then net it against the 30% uplift. This number decides whether the plan works.
2. **Read the 2009 reciprocal easement with counsel.** Look for use restrictions, exclusives, parking ratios or minimums, rights over the entrances and signage, consent rights over changes to the site, and what happens to the shared access if the grocery use ends. Do the same for the Harrowfield lease itself: renewal options, rights of first refusal or offer on the box, go-dark terms, and any restoration obligations on surrender.
3. **Call Marchbank informally.** Ask what they need to see for consent on two leases that replace the anchor, and whether they have conditions on tenant credit, term or use.
4. **Check zoning and permits.** A 24-hour gym and a medical imaging use may need different approvals, and the imaging tenant may have radiation shielding and build-out needs that affect timing and cost. Confirm the landlord work, who pays, and the realistic opening dates, because downtime between Harrowfield's exit and the openings is lost rent.
5. **Tighten the Ironvale and Sorrelwood terms.** Get the credit strength, term, guarantees, tenant improvement allowance and exclusives they will ask for, and confirm the 22,000/16,000 split works physically (demising wall, separate entrances, loading, mechanical).

## How to bring people along

- **Do not tell the inline tenants through a rumour.** Brief them after the committee decision and before the Harrowfield departure is public, with the opening dates for the new operators. Lead with what is coming and the traffic it will bring, and deal with the co-tenancy clauses tenant by tenant.
- **Approach Harrowfield early and separately about the easement.** They will hear about this either way. A short, respectful conversation that treats them as a neighbour with continuing rights will cost you less than a dispute later. Be ready for them to ask for something, such as pylon signage, a convenience or grocery tenant, or protected access for fuel deliveries, and decide in advance what you can give.
- **Keep the new operators in the loop on timing.** Tell them honestly about the easement and Marchbank steps rather than letting them find out later.
- **Take the committee a decision with guardrails.** For example: proceed to lease execution if inline exposure is below an agreed figure, the easement has no blocking restriction, and Marchbank has pre-approved. If any fails, fall back to a short Harrowfield extension while you reprice, and keep the two LOIs warm.

## What would change this recommendation

If the co-tenancy review shows that a large share of the inline rent can be cut or terminated when the grocer leaves, and the exposure is more than a year or two of the 30% uplift, then the replacement plan loses its advantage and a discounted Harrowfield renewal, or a different grocery or food anchor, becomes the better answer. The same applies if the easement gives Harrowfield a veto over the new uses. The cheapest first check is the co-tenancy table across the 26 leases, because it takes days and uses documents you already hold.
