**Short answer:** Your plan is financially plausible, but I would not take it to a vote on February 12 as it stands. Three things in your own message could stop it, or sour the vote, and none of them is about parents or teachers. Fix those first. Then the people questions get much easier.

## Three issues that could sink the vote

**The Halloran gift.** "For the education of the children of Brennock" is a purpose statement. The plaque is the part everyone can see. What matters is what the 1958 deed or gift instrument says: a restriction, a reverter clause, a charitable-trust purpose, or only a sentimental inscription. If the land is held in a restricted way, a sale to a developer may need court approval or the attorney general's sign-off, and the family or its heirs may have standing to object. A trustee who is worried about this will vote no on February 12. A Vellacott closing can also fail on title grounds. Get a title and gift-instrument opinion from counsel now, before you present anything. If the restriction is real, the plan has to change shape (see option C below).

**The Ardelle pledge.** The $800k still to come is for a program that is tied to the north campus. Read the pledge agreement. If the funds are restricted to that site or program, closing the campus may let Ardelle redirect or stop the payments. Call the foundation before the board hears about it from anyone else. A good outcome is that they agree to move the program to the south campus, or to keep part of it running at the north site. Treat the $800k as at risk in your model until you have that in writing.

**The Sycamore Lane conditions.** The 2004 approval limits drop-off, evening events and enrolment. You have never come near the enrolment ceiling, but "700 students" and "enrolment ceiling" are probably going to collide. Find the exact number. Check whether a new $8M lower-school building, its footprint and its construction traffic need an amendment to the approval, and whether moving 230 young children onto the campus changes drop-off in a way the traffic-marshal condition covers. If an amendment is needed, you are asking the same neighbours who fought the gym for a new approval. Their ability to delay is real, and it affects the fall 2028 date and the cost of the $8M building. Talk to the neighbours early. They should not first hear about it in a public notice.

If any of these three comes back badly, you will want to know before February 12. Trustees will ask about all three. The worst version of the meeting is the one where a trustee raises the plaque and you don't have an answer.

## The timeline problem

Re-enrolment contracts go out January 10 and are due March 1. The board votes February 12. So families will be deciding whether to re-enrol in the middle of the biggest uncertainty the school has ever created, and your budget needs 92% to return. At north-campus families the risk is highest, because they are the ones being asked to accept a change before they know whether it will happen.

Some options:

- **Move the vote earlier**, to before January 10, so contracts go out with a settled answer. This only works if the three issues above are resolved by then, which is unlikely.
- **Keep the vote date, and tell families before contracts go out** that a decision on the future of the north campus is coming, give the date, and say what they will be told and when. Don't be silent, and don't pretend nothing is happening. Rumours will do more damage than the facts.
- **Add a short extension for north-campus families only**, for example a two-week return window after the vote, with a deposit that is refundable if the board decides in a way that changes their circumstances.

Whichever you choose, model the real downside. If lower-school re-enrolment falls to 85% instead of 92%, what does the budget look like? Trustees will ask, and a plan that has the answer is more persuasive than one that doesn't.

Also ask yourself why the vote needs to happen this February for a fall 2028 closing. Two and a half years is long. If the legal and neighbour work needs more time, a vote in the spring or fall with contracts unaffected may be better than a rushed vote now. Check what the Vellacott letter of intent actually requires and when. Its deadlines are the real constraint, not the date you've chosen.

## How to win ten of fifteen

A two-thirds supermajority means you need to lose no more than five trustees. Don't count on enthusiasm. Count on the board's concerns being answered.

- **Count the board privately before February.** Meet each trustee one to one. You want to know who is a yes, who is a probable yes and what would move them, and who has a reason to say no (a Brennock family connection to the Hallorans, a north-campus parent, an alumnus). If you find you have seven or eight likely votes, that is information, and it tells you what the plan still needs.
- **Give the board the case in numbers.** The deficit is $1.1M a year. The sale brings in $13.5M and the building costs $8M, leaving $5.5M. What is the net annual saving from running one campus? What does the $5.5M do: pay down the deficit, endow financial aid, fund faculty pay? A board that sees how the money turns into a sustainable school is easier to bring along than one that sees only a sale.
- **Show the alternatives you considered.** Trustees need to see that "do nothing", "sell and keep a smaller north campus", "lease rather than sell", and "close in phases" were looked at, and why you prefer your plan. A board that thinks there was no alternative, or that you didn't look, resents it.
- **Make the vote a decision they can defend.** Each trustee will have to explain it to friends in Brennock. Give them a short, honest account: the school's finances are not sustainable on two campuses, the sale keeps the school alive, and the land's purpose will continue through [whatever the plan does about it].
- **Use independent voices.** A financial adviser or an outside counsel presenting the risks carries more weight than your own view.

## The land and its purpose

If the Halloran gift allows a sale, the plaque is still a symbolic and reputational problem, and a developer with a housing project will draw a public response. Consider whether the plan can honour the gift's purpose in a visible way:

- Dedicate part of the proceeds to a Halloran scholarship fund for Brennock children, so the money from the land serves "the education of the children of Brennock".
- Ask Vellacott whether a piece of the site can stay: an outdoor learning area, a community green, or a lease-back so the Ardelle program continues for a period.
- Invite the Halloran family or descendants into the conversation before any announcement. They are better as consulted partners than as surprised critics, and their reaction matters to trustees and to local press.

Option C, if the legal opinion is bad: a restructured deal where the school keeps ownership and leases or partially sells the north site, or sells to an educational or community user. It gives less money, but it avoids a legal fight.

## Keeping parents and staff

**Lower-school parents** have two separate worries, and they need different answers.

- *The commute.* Nine kilometres is a real change. Offer something concrete: a bus route with set times, extended before and after care, and a transition period (for example, a year where lower-school families get priority on bus places and a travel subsidy for those furthest away). Ask a parent group what would actually help rather than guessing.
- *Little kids and teenagers on one campus.* This is a design question. A separate lower-school building with its own entrance, play area, drop-off and schedule answers most of it. Say plainly how it will be separated, and invite a few parents to sit on the design committee for the new building. Parents who help shape it are much less likely to fight it. Show them how comparable K–12 schools handle it.

**Teachers** are worried about jobs, and that fear will drive rumours until you address it. Decide before the announcement what you can promise. If lower-school teachers will move to the new building, say so; if some positions will go, say how many, how it will be decided, and what severance, notice and placement help will be offered. Vague reassurance is worse than a clear, hard answer. Tell staff before parents, and tell them before the news reaches them from anyone else.

**Principles for all communication:**

- Tell people the truth about the finances: a $1.1M annual deficit and falling enrolment. People accept a hard decision more readily when they can see the reason.
- Say what is decided and what is not. If the board has not voted, say so, and say when and how people can give input.
- Give a real way to be heard, such as listening sessions and a written question channel, and publish what you heard and what changed because of it.

## What the plan should contain

1. **By mid-November:** the legal opinion on the Halloran gift, the Ardelle pledge terms, and the 2004 approval conditions, plus a call with the Vellacott team on their deadlines.
2. **By early December:** one-to-one meetings with all fifteen trustees, a financial model with a downside case (re-enrolment at 85-88%), and a first conversation with the Sycamore Lane neighbours and the planning office.
3. **Before January 10:** a communication plan for families and staff, a decision on the contract timing and any north-campus window, and a staff-support package.
4. **January:** listening sessions with north-campus families, a staff meeting, a parent design group, and a board briefing pack with alternatives, risks and the net numbers.
5. **February 12:** a vote on a proposal that already states how the Halloran purpose is honoured, how Ardelle is handled, how the neighbours will be dealt with, and what is offered to families and staff.
6. **After the vote:** monthly re-enrolment tracking against the 92% budget line, with a pre-agreed trigger (for example, below 88% by mid-February) to review spending or the Vellacott timetable.
7. **A fallback:** what you will do if the vote fails, or the sale hits legal trouble. A pre-considered plan B keeps the deficit under control and shows the board you've thought it through.
