The financial case is strong, but three facts in your own message could stop the plan before any trustee or parent does. Check those first, and build the vote around them.

## Three things to settle before February 12

**1. The Halloran gift.** The plaque says the land was given "for the education of the children of Brennock." Whether that binds you depends on the deed and gift instrument. It could be a plain gift, a restriction, a reverter clause, or a charitable trust. Have counsel read the original documents now. If there is any restriction, selling to a developer may need the family's consent, a court's approval, or the state attorney general's involvement. Vellacott's LOI will not close without clear title anyway, so this is a deal risk as well as a trust and reputation risk. Contact the Halloran family before they hear about it from a parent or the local paper. Ask them how they would like the gift's purpose honoured. Possible answers include a named building, a scholarship fund, a public-use portion of the proceeds, or a covenant on how the site is used. A trustee who is unsure about the sale is far more likely to vote yes if the family has been consulted and gave their blessing.

**2. The Sycamore Lane conditions.** You say the enrolment ceiling has never been close, but the number matters. Moving from 410 to about 640 students is a 56% increase. Find the exact ceiling in the 2004 approval. Your consultant's 700-student capacity is about physical space, not what the permit allows. Also check whether an $8M lower-school building needs its own approvals. If it does, the neighbours will have a say, and the traffic marshals and 9pm conditions will come back into play. Neighbours who fought you once will notice 230 more children arriving. Meet them early with a traffic and drop-off plan, and treat the permit as a gate on your timeline. If the ceiling is below 640, you need a variance before you can promise this plan to anyone.

**3. The Ardelle pledge.** $800k is still to come, and it is tied to the north-campus outdoor learning program. Closing that campus may let the foundation stop paying, or even claw back funds, depending on the pledge terms. Read the agreement and call the foundation before the vote. Ask whether the program can move to the south campus, or to part of the site. The foundation may be glad to be asked early. It would be much worse for it to learn of the closure from a letter. Treat the $800k as at risk until they confirm otherwise.

## Why your timeline is the main risk

Re-enrolment contracts go out January 10 and are due March 1. The board votes February 12, in the middle of that window. That has two consequences.

- Families will decide whether to re-enrol while the school's future is still undecided. Your budget assumes 92% return. If lower-school families hesitate, you could lose enrolment before the consolidation even begins, and the sale proceeds would not cover that.
- If you send contracts without telling families about the plan, and announce the closure after they sign, expect anger and accusations of bad faith. That would hurt re-enrolment more than the honest news would.

Two workable options. One is to announce the proposal and its rationale before January 10, with a real consultation period, so contracts go out with the facts known. The other is to push the vote earlier, or extend the contract deadline until after it. I would do the first. Either way, give lower-school families something concrete in the contract package: a guarantee about fall 2028, a transport offer, and a named person to call.

## Winning ten of fifteen votes

You need two-thirds, so you can afford to lose only five. Count the trustees now, one by one, and treat each as a conversation, not a pitch.

- **Likely yes:** those who focus on the deficit. Show them the numbers: a $1.1M deficit on $19M, enrolment down 11% in five years, and what happens if you do nothing. Show them the do-nothing path honestly, since someone will ask.
- **Persuadable:** those worried about mission and reputation. They need answers on the Halloran gift, the neighbours, and the pledge. Give them those answers before the meeting, not at it.
- **Likely no:** any trustees with a personal tie to the north campus, such as a family connection or the Halloran link. Ask them what would have to be true for them to vote yes. The answer often reveals a specific fix, like naming rights or a protected program. If you can't win them, aim for abstention over opposition.

Also bring them alternatives. Trustees will want to know you considered options such as keeping the north campus open and leasing part of it, selling only part of the six acres, closing it later, or a merger. Show why each fails against the criteria. Be ready for the question of why $13.5M is the right price, so ask for an independent valuation and check that Vellacott's LOI has conditions that protect you: due diligence, financing, zoning, and a deadline you can live with. Consider asking for a floor on the price whatever the rezoning outcome.

Finally, pressure-test the money. $13.5M less $8M for the building leaves about $5.5M, but check selling costs, taxes, bridging costs between the sale and the build, moving and fit-out costs, and the cost of running two campuses until fall 2028. The deficit is $1.1M every year, so decide how much of the net proceeds goes into an endowment-like reserve and how much into operations, and say so openly. If the deficit is fixed only by spending the sale money, trustees will see that.

## Keeping parents and staff with you

**Lower-school parents.** They have two specific worries, commute and little children sharing a campus with teenagers. Answer both with design, not reassurance.
- A separate lower-school building (which your plan already includes) with its own entrance, its own drop-off, its own play areas, and separate break times.
- Supervision and a transition plan for the older students' interactions, and a commitment to reading buddies or similar programs the parents can see as a benefit.
- Transport: a bus route or subsidised shuttle from the north side of Brennock, with a defined number of years of support. Ask them in a survey what would make the commute workable, and publish what you decide.
- A parent group from the lower school helping choose the design of the new building. People support what they helped shape.

**Teachers and staff.** Their fear is jobs. Say what you know and when you will know more. If you can honestly commit that consolidation will not lead to layoffs beyond natural attrition, say so. If you can't, say what you can commit to: a hiring freeze first, redeployment, a retention bonus for lower-school staff through the move, severance terms, and a date by which every person will know their position. Meet staff before or at the same time as the parents. Staff who hear about this second will assume the worst, and they talk to parents.

**Everyone.** Hold open meetings, publish an FAQ that covers the awkward questions (the gift, the neighbours, the pledge, the money), and report back on what you changed because of feedback. People accept a decision more readily when they saw that their input changed something, even if the outcome is the same.

## What the plan should include

1. **A legal and approvals workstream (now to January):** the Halloran documents, the 2004 planning conditions, the building permit path, and the Ardelle terms. Each should have an owner and a date, with results reported to the board before the vote.
2. **A stakeholder timeline:** Halloran family and Ardelle first, then trustees, then staff, then families, then Sycamore Lane neighbours, all before January 10.
3. **Board package:** the financial model with downside cases (for example, 85% re-enrolment and a delayed sale), alternatives considered, the valuation, LOI conditions, and the legal findings.
4. **Commitments to families and staff:** transport, design input, programme protection, employment terms, and a point of contact.
5. **A way to honour the north site's history:** a named space or garden in the new lower school, an annual Halloran day, and a use of part of the proceeds that fits the gift's purpose. Whatever the lawyers allow, show you have taken the gift seriously.
6. **Triggers and fallbacks:** what you will do if re-enrolment falls below, say, 88%, if the permit is refused or capped, if the Halloran issue blocks the sale, or if Ardelle withdraws. Decide the answers now so a setback does not turn into a crisis. A conditional approval by the board (sale proceeds only if title is clear and the permit is in hand) may also be an easier vote than an unconditional one.
7. **A review schedule:** report to the board and community each term through 2028 on enrolment, budget, construction, and complaints.

## Where I would push back

Your plan may well be right, and the numbers support it. But "win this vote" is the wrong framing, because a bare ten-of-fifteen win that leaves parents, staff, the family, and the neighbours feeling ambushed will cost you re-enrolment and may end in a lawsuit or a stalled permit. The better aim is a vote that carries comfortably because trustees can see that the legal, community, and financial risks have been checked and that the people most affected were heard. If the Halloran or planning answers come back badly, you will want to know before February 12, not after.

I have assumed that you have not yet read these documents in detail and that the LOI is non-binding. If either is wrong, tell me and I will adjust the sequence.
