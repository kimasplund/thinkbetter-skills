**Short answer:** Recommend replacing Harrowfield with Ironvale and Sorrelwood, but as a conditional approval, not a clean "let them go." The economics are good. The premise that you don't need anything from Harrowfield is the weak point, and it's what could sink the plan. Fix it before November 20, or tell the committee plainly that it's an open risk.

## Why the premise doesn't hold

1. **Harrowfield owns the front pad and shares your site.** It bought the pad in 2009, and the recorded reciprocal easement covers your entrances and parking. When its lease expires, the easement stays. I'd read the easement closely for three things:
   - Does it set a parking ratio or reserve stalls for the station?
   - Does it restrict changes to the entrances, or to uses of the lot?
   - Does it give Harrowfield approval or consent rights over changes to the centre?

   A 24-hour gym plus an urgent-care and imaging clinic will change traffic patterns, peak hours and the late-night profile of the lot. A departing anchor that owns the front of your site and has lost its tenancy has leverage and a motive to use it, whether through consent rights, litigation or simply slow-walking. If it also sells the pad, you'll have an unknown neighbour with the same easement rights.

2. **The fuel station depends on the grocery store.** The 10-cents-a-litre loyalty card only works with in-store spend. When the store goes, the station loses its traffic driver. It may also ask to change or end the easement, ask for compensation, or look for a way to stay tied to the centre. That is a negotiation to have on purpose, before it happens by default.

3. **Your inline tenants have co-tenancy protection.** The 2015 leasing deal filled 26 shops with standard grocery co-tenancy terms. Anchor loss usually triggers rent reductions, then termination rights if no replacement opens within a set time. Check each lease for:
   - How "anchor" is defined. A gym and a clinic may not qualify as a replacement anchor unless the wording is generous.
   - The cure period and the opening deadline.
   - The rent-reduction mechanics, and any retroactive claims.

   This could be a real cost. Twenty-six shops with alternative-rent clauses can erase much of the 30% uplift, and a cluster of termination rights is the bigger risk. Quantify it before the committee meets.

4. **Exclusives and use restrictions.** Harrowfield's lease, and some inline leases, may carry grocery or pharmacy exclusives, use restrictions, or limits on medical and fitness uses. An imaging clinic with a pharmacy counter, for example, may collide with an exclusive. Check this now, while the LOIs are non-binding.

## What to put in front of the committee

**Recommendation:** Approve negotiating binding leases with Ironvale and Sorrelwood, with signing conditional on the items below. Don't commit to terminating Harrowfield's talks until those are cleared.

**The economics.** The two deals pay about 30% more than Harrowfield pays now, against a renewal that would be 40% below it. Total rent for the box is roughly 117% of current if the LOI figures hold (versus 60% under the renewal). Present that as a range, net of:
- Tenant improvement allowances and free rent for two conversions of a large grocery box (these can be large and are often left out of headline comparisons)
- Downtime and construction between Harrowfield's exit and rent commencement
- Co-tenancy rent concessions to inline tenants
- Any easement settlement with Harrowfield

**Parking.** "Rarely half full" is true for a declining grocery store, but it isn't the right test. Gyms spike in early morning and evening, and clinics run steady all-day turnover. Medical and fitness uses are often parked at a higher ratio than retail under local bylaws. Run a use-by-use stall count at peak, check the zoning minimum and the easement, and compare against 610 stalls. If you're close, a shared-parking study or a limit on the gym's peak-hour memberships may be the fix.

**Timing.** The lease runs out next December and the committee meets in November, so you have time. Use it to make the LOIs binding in stages, not to rush.

## How to bring everyone along

**Marchbank Life (lender).** Both leases exceed 15,000 sq ft, so each needs sign-off. "Routine" has been the pattern with grocery tenants, but a gym and an urgent-care clinic are a different credit and use profile. Approach them early with the LOIs, tenant financials and a rent roll showing the uplift. Ask for written approval in principle before Nov 20 if you can, and make lender consent a closing condition in both leases. Check whether the loan has anchor-loss or cash-sweep provisions that Harrowfield's exit will trigger.

**Harrowfield.** Talk to them first, and on your terms, not after the LOIs surface. The aim is a package: an orderly exit or short extension, an amended easement that reflects the new uses, perhaps a role for the station in the new site plan, and a release of any claims. You hold the stronger hand on the lease; they hold the stronger hand on the pad. A short extension (six to twelve months at an agreed rent) may be worth paying for if it buys easement certainty and a smooth handover.

**Inline tenants.** Tell them before they hear it from a competitor. The message is that a 24-hour gym and a medical clinic bring daily, high-frequency visitors (different from grocery, but real), and that you have a plan for the transition. Offer what the leases already require, and consider offering something small in return for waiving or softening co-tenancy rights: marketing support, signage, or a short rent deferral. Start with the shops that have the strongest termination rights and the highest sales dependency on grocery traffic.

**Ironvale and Sorrelwood.** Keep both warm, but don't let them set the timetable. Ask each to accept conditions: lender consent, the easement outcome, and parking allocation. Check they're not relying on exclusives that conflict with each other (a gym and a clinic with physio or wellness services could overlap). Also consider the loss risk: if one drops out, the other can't take the whole box, so it's worth knowing the fallback.

## Sequence

1. Within two weeks: pull the easement, Harrowfield's lease, and all 26 inline leases. Have counsel summarise co-tenancy, exclusives and use clauses.
2. Run the parking count and a rough co-tenancy cost model.
3. Open talks with Marchbank and Harrowfield in parallel.
4. Brief the inline tenants once you know the terms you can offer.
5. Present to the committee on Nov 20 with a conditional approval, the risk register above, and a walk-away: if the easement or co-tenancy cost wipes out the uplift, the fallback is a short Harrowfield extension while you restructure.

- If the easement gives Harrowfield consent rights over the lot, or the inline leases give broad termination rights, a negotiated package with Harrowfield becomes the first priority, and the 40%-off renewal could even be the better short-term deal.
