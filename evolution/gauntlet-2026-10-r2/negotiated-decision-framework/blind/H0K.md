**Short answer:** Take a conditional "yes, proceed" to the November 20 committee, not an unconditional "let Harrowfield go". The Ironvale and Sorrelwood deal looks better than a 40% rent cut on its face. But the premise that you "don't need anything from Harrowfield" is the weakest part of the plan, and at least three other parties can stop or reprice it.

## Why "their lease just runs out" isn't the whole story

**1. Harrowfield is still your neighbour after the lease ends.** It owns the fuel pad outright and holds recorded rights over your entrances and parking. If you replace it, you have a motivated, well-informed party on your front corner with easement rights you can't terminate. Read the easement before you do anything else. In particular, check:
- Does it fix a parking ratio, a stall count, or protected stalls near the pad?
- Does it protect sight lines, signage or pylon rights?
- Does it restrict uses on your land (many reciprocal easements do) or give Harrowfield approval rights over changes?
- Does it guarantee access routes that a gym's evening peak or a clinic's queue could clog?

Harrowfield also has a commercial reason to be difficult. Its fuel discount only works with in-store spend, so losing the store may hurt the pad's volume. Expect it to contest anything that touches access or parking. The danger is a lawsuit or a quiet refusal to cooperate while Ironvale and Sorrelwood are building out.

**2. The 2015 co-tenancy protections are probably your biggest hidden cost.** You filled 26 shops by promising grocery co-tenancy. Pull every inline lease this week and find out:
- Exactly what the clause requires (a grocery of a certain size? any anchor? a named operator?).
- What happens if the requirement fails: rent abatement (often to percentage rent or 50%), and a termination right after a set period.
- Whether it is triggered by a go-dark or by a change of anchor, and how long you have to cure.
- Whether a gym plus a medical clinic could count as a substitute anchor. Often they don't, because the clause says "supermarket".

If 26 shops go to reduced rent or can walk, the "30% more" on 38,000 sq ft could be wiped out. Add up that exposure in dollars. It is the number the committee most needs, and it decides whether you should be negotiating with the inline tenants before you sign anything.

**3. Marchbank's sign-off is "routine" only for a routine change.** You would be asking for two consents over 15,000 sq ft, replacing a grocery anchor with two different credits and uses, at the moment inline rents may be abated. Ask Marchbank now and informally. Find out whether the loan documents tie anything to anchor tenancy, to a minimum-occupancy or debt-service test, or to medical/hazardous uses (imaging equipment). A "no" or a re-pricing in the spring would be far worse than a conversation in the fall.

**4. The two LOIs are not leases.** Test them:
- Are there conditions on permits, zoning, medical licensing, or build-out timing?
- Who funds the demising wall, utilities, and the structural and shielding work for imaging?
- What are the termination rights and the credit support (guarantees, deposits)?
- How long is the downtime between Harrowfield's last day and rent commencement? Free rent and fit-out periods can make year-one income lower than renewal at a 40% cut. Do the year-by-year comparison, not just the headline.

**5. Parking.** "Rarely half full" is a grocery-profile statement. A 24-hour gym peaks at 5 to 8 pm and drives overnight use, and an urgent-care clinic has high-turnover, patient-heavy demand. Overlap them with inline shop hours and the fuel station's customers, then compare the result with the easement. Have a traffic and parking consultant do a short peak-hour study. Also check lighting, noise and security for the 24-hour operation as they affect the inline tenants and any neighbouring residents.

## What to recommend to committee

Recommend the re-tenanting plan, with a sequence of approvals and exit points:

1. **Authorize negotiation of leases** with Ironvale and Sorrelwood, signed only after the conditions below are met.
2. **Conditions precedent:** (a) co-tenancy exposure quantified and within a tolerance the committee sets; (b) easement review confirms the new uses and parking load are permitted; (c) written lender indication from Marchbank; (d) the year-by-year income comparison shows the new deal ahead of the Harrowfield renewal, net of exposure and downtime.
3. **Keep a fallback alive.** Don't reject Harrowfield outright; tell them you are exploring alternatives. A realistic fallback is a smaller or restructured Harrowfield renewal, at a rent between 0 and -40%, in return for a shorter term, a percentage rent, and perhaps an option to give up space to one of the new operators. Its value is that if conditions fail you still have a deal and the leverage that comes from having a credible alternative.
4. **Set a decision date** (for example by the end of Q1) with a go/no-go for Harrowfield's notice requirements, since you will want to know their renewal-notice deadlines and whether their lease obliges them to keep operating until the end.

If the co-tenancy cost turns out to be large, there is a middle design worth putting on the table: put in one of the two operators and a smaller grocer or specialty food operator, or ask the inline tenants to waive or amend co-tenancy in exchange for something they want (rent relief on a trade-off, a term extension, signage, or marketing support).

## How to bring everyone along

**Committee (Nov 20).** Present the recommendation as a decision with conditions, plus a one-page table: new-deal vs. renewal income over the term, the co-tenancy exposure range, and the four gating items with owners and dates. A committee will find it easier to approve "proceed, subject to these four checks" than a bare switch.

**Harrowfield.** Sequence matters. Don't let it learn about the LOIs from the inline shops. Have a direct conversation early with a senior person, listen to what it actually needs (the fuel business, its brand, a way to exit with dignity), and tell it plainly what you are considering for the building. Ask for its position on the easement in writing. If you can offer something it cares about, such as protected pad access, signage, or a clean negotiated settlement of any use issues, that is cheaper than a dispute. Check your own lease for renewal options, ROFO/ROFR rights, and operating covenants before the call.

**Inline tenants.** They will hear about this either way, so be first. Meet the larger and most co-tenancy-sensitive ones individually, before any announcement. Share what is committed, what the new draw is (gym and clinic bring daily and appointment-based foot traffic, and likely more of it than a declining grocery), and what you will do to protect them. Be honest about what is not yet certain. If you can, offer a trade: a co-tenancy waiver or amendment in return for an extended term or a marketing commitment, rather than waiting for the clause to bite.

**Ironvale and Sorrelwood.** Both are eager, which gives you leverage, but they will care about each other's use (noise, hours, shared entrances, loading, and the clinic's need for quiet and privacy). Put shared-parking rules, hours, and construction sequencing into both leases, and give each a say on the other's fit-out in a limited way. Don't let one lease close with terms that make the other impossible.

**Marchbank.** Take it a short package: the leases' key terms, tenant financials, and the income comparison, with a plain statement of how you will handle the inline co-tenancy risk.

## Confidence and what would change it

I think the re-tenanting plan is probably right, since a failing anchor at a 40% discount is a weak base for a mortgage and for the inline shops either way. But I'd put roughly 65% on it surviving the checks above. The two items most likely to change the answer are the co-tenancy terms and the easement. If either is bad, the better recommendation becomes a restructured Harrowfield deal combined with one new operator, or a negotiated exit with Harrowfield plus inline concessions before you sign anything.
