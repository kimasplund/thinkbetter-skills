**Recommendation:** Don't go to the February 12 vote with "close north, sell to Vellacott" as a finished plan. Go with a conditional resolution: approve consolidation and the sale only if three gating items clear first. Those items are the Halloran gift terms, the Sycamore Lane enrolment ceiling, and the Ardelle pledge. Pair that with a published commitment package for families and staff. Your financial case is strong. What can sink you is a legal or neighbour surprise after the vote, or a re-enrolment drop before it.

## What can block you, and what to do about each

| Who | What they need | How they could block | What to offer |
|---|---|---|---|
| The four or more trustees who can deny you the ten votes | Confidence the sale is legal, the numbers hold, and they won't be blamed for betraying the Halloran gift | Voting no, or abstaining (with 15 trustees, six non-yes votes defeats you) | Counsel's written opinion on the gift, a downside case on enrolment, and a way to say yes to a plan with safeguards rather than yes to a closure |
| Halloran heirs, the attorney general or charity regulator, and the court if one is involved | That the land is used "for the education of the children of Brennock" | Injunction, a reverter claim, a press campaign, or a demand that sale proceeds go to a stated purpose | Get counsel to read the 1958 deed and gift documents now. Contact the family before the sale is public. Show proceeds going to children's education in Brennock, and consider a lasting recognition such as naming the new lower school for the Hallorans |
| Sycamore Lane neighbours and the planning board | Predictable traffic and noise | Insisting the 2004 conditions bind a bigger school, objecting to the new building's permit, or contesting any change to the ceiling | Find the actual ceiling figure this week. Adding 230 children takes the south campus from 410 to 640 students, and nobody has checked that against the cap. Open talks with them before the vote, not after, and offer to keep or strengthen the marshals and the 9pm limit |
| Ardelle Foundation | That the outdoor learning money is used as pledged | Stopping the remaining $800k, or asking for a refund | Ask them now, in writing, whether the grant can follow the program to a south-campus site. A partial loss is better to know about before the vote than after |
| Vellacott Homes | A deal they can close | Walking away, or retrading the price if rezoning or title questions linger | Check what the letter of intent is conditional on. Negotiate a due-diligence period that runs past the vote and a closing date that lets you open the new building first |

## Two timing problems to solve first

1. **Re-enrolment opens January 10 and the vote is February 12.** Families will decide whether to sign for 2027-28 before the board has decided, and the closure rumour will reach them before then. Silence will cost you more than disclosure. Either announce the proposal and your commitments before January 10, or move the vote earlier than the contract window. A reasonable middle path is to tell families in early December that the board is considering consolidation for fall 2028, that nothing changes for 2027-28, and what you are promising. Your budget assumes 92% return. Model 88% and 85% now, and show trustees what each does to the deficit, because a vote taken on the 92% number will look reckless if early signings come in low.
2. **The gate items need answers before the vote, not after.** Counsel's opinion on the gift, the ceiling figure, and Ardelle's response should all be in hand by mid-January. If any one comes back bad, you want to learn it while you can still change the proposal.

## Design the proposal so the objections lose their force

- **Little kids with teenagers:** Build the $8M lower school as a separate wing with its own entrance, drop-off, playground and schedule, and no shared corridors. Publish the design brief before the vote. That turns a vague fear into a specific commitment families can hold you to.
- **Commute:** The south site is nine kilometres away, so put money on it. Fund buses from the north side of town, extended before- and after-care, and a guaranteed transition year. Name a number in the budget.
- **Outdoor learning program:** Say where it goes. If the Ardelle money is tied to that program, building a comparable outdoor space on the south campus is cheap insurance for both the grant and the families who value it.
- **Staff:** Offer redeployment first, with a published list of which roles are affected, and put a dated commitment in writing.
- **The land:** Don't let the sale look like a developer winning. If the legal terms allow it, consider a conservation easement over part of the site, public access to some green space, or a legacy fund in the Halloran name for Brennock children. The gesture matters most to the people who remember the plaque.

## Winning the ten votes

- Meet every trustee individually before the board meeting. Ask each what would make them vote yes, and find out who you are short of.
- Put to the board the cost of doing nothing. A $1.1M annual deficit on $19M with falling enrolment compounds, so show the five-year path if nothing is done.
- Give trustees a third option to compare against: a delayed or partial version, such as selling only part of the site, or a staged move that starts with grades K-2. Trustees who feel they chose between real options will commit more firmly than ones asked to rubber-stamp.
- Make the resolution conditional. "Proceed if legal clearance, ceiling confirmation and Ardelle consent are in hand by a stated date" is far easier for a nervous trustee to support than an unconditional closure, and it protects you if one of them fails.
- Agree in advance what happens if the vote is eight or nine in favour. Don't leave this to be improvised in the room.

## What the plan should contain

- Legal clearance on the gift and the sale proceeds, with counsel's written opinion shared with the board.
- Confirmed enrolment ceiling, and a modification application if needed, with a neighbour consultation plan.
- Ardelle's written position on moving the program.
- Vellacott's conditions, timeline and fallback if they walk.
- Enrolment scenarios at 92%, 88% and 85%, with the savings and the deficit under each.
- The $8M building budget, with contingency, and an opening date before north campus closes so no cohort is left without a home.
- A family communication calendar, starting before January 10, with a named point of contact and a dated Q&A.
- A staff plan, with a date by which every role is settled.
- A decision date for each gate and a trigger for pausing.

## What would change this recommendation

The fact that would flip it is the 1958 gift terms. If the deed restricts the use of the land or requires it to revert, the sale as designed may not be available at all, and the plan would shift to retaining the site, leasing it or selling with court approval. The cheapest check is a few hours of a lawyer's time reading the original gift documents, which you can commission this week. The second thing to confirm is the Sycamore Lane ceiling number. If it is below 640, you need a planning modification before you can promise families a 2028 move.
