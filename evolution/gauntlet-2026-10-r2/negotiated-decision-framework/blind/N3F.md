Your doubts are well placed, and the plan is sound in finance but not yet ready for a vote. Three things can stop it, and none of them is a parent or teacher. Settle those first. The vote and the family messaging come after.

**My recommendation:** keep the consolidation goal, but don't ask the board on February 12 for an unconditional sale. Ask for approval of a sale that depends on legal clearance, with a signed-off plan for families and staff. Then use the time before fall 2028 to remove the three blockers below.

## What can actually stop this

**1. The Halloran gift (the biggest risk).** A plaque saying "for the education of the children of Brennock" may only be a dedication, or it may be a deed restriction, a reverter clause or a charitable-trust condition. Get your attorney to read the 1958 deed and gift documents now, before anything else. Depending on the answer, you may need the family's consent, the state attorney general's sign-off, or a court petition to apply the restriction to different land. A court petition is slow, but it is a known process and fits inside a 2028 timeline. Selling with this unexamined would hand a trustee, a parent or a Halloran descendant a lawsuit and a news story. The Vellacott letter of intent should already have a title and legal-clearance contingency. If it doesn't, add one. Also bring the Halloran family in early and personally. Better they hear it from you, along with how the proceeds will serve Brennock's children, than from a neighbour.

**2. The Sycamore Lane conditions.** Space isn't your constraint, but the 2004 approval is. Three things to check:
- The enrolment ceiling. You've never come close to it, but moving 230 more students onto the south campus takes you from 410 to 640. Find the actual number and whether it counts students on site or school-wide.
- Whether an $8M building, plus changes to drop-off and traffic, needs a new planning-board approval. If it does, the neighbours get another hearing, and they remember the last one.
- Traffic. Putting little kids' drop-off and teenage parking on the same road is exactly what the marshal condition was meant to control.

Meet Sycamore Lane before you announce, with a concrete traffic plan, and consider offering to formalise the existing conditions in writing. A neighbour who has been asked and has said yes is worth more at a hearing than any consultant's report.

**3. The Ardelle Foundation's $800k.** The pledge is for the north-campus outdoor learning program. Once you close the north campus, the foundation can reasonably stop paying or ask for money back. Call them before the board does. Offer a revised use, such as an outdoor learning space designed into the new lower-school building, and get the change in writing. The $800k is small next to $13.5M, but a foundation that feels ambushed could hurt your wider fundraising.

## The timing problem you haven't mentioned

Re-enrolment contracts go out January 10 and are due March 1, and the board votes February 12, in the middle of that window. Families will be signing without knowing whether their children's campus will exist. That leaves you two bad choices: stay quiet and have the news break while contracts are out, or announce early and risk losing families before the vote.

I would announce early. Tell lower-school families in late November or early December that the board is considering consolidation for fall 2028, that nothing happens before then, and that no child's placement for 2026-27 changes. Hold sessions with each group before contracts go out. The cost is some anxiety now. The alternative is a trust collapse in February, with the news appearing to come right after you collected their deposits.

Your budget assumes 92% of families return. Test that figure for lower-school families specifically. If 15% of them walk, you lose around 35 students, which at typical independent-school tuition is a seven-figure revenue hit that eats a large part of the savings. Build a downside case at 85% and show the board the plan still works.

## Winning ten of fifteen votes

You need two-thirds, so five trustees can sink it. Count before you schedule. Meet each trustee individually, starting with the likeliest skeptics, and find out what each one needs to say yes. Expect these concerns:
- **Mission and legacy trustees:** they need the Halloran question answered and a visible way for the proceeds to serve Brennock's children.
- **Finance trustees:** they need a hard number. The $5.5M left after the $8M building is the cushion, but they will ask what annual operating savings the closure brings, and your deficit is $1.1M. Present the multi-year budget with the savings, the downside enrolment case and the cost of a failed sale.
- **Trustees who are lower-school parents:** they have a conflict to manage, and you should discuss recusal with your chair and counsel early rather than discovering it on the night.

If you are short of ten, a conditional vote is your fallback. The board approves the sale subject to legal clearance and planning approval, with the final go decision delegated to a committee. Several trustees who won't commit to an irreversible step will accept a reversible one.

## What the plan must include for parents and staff

**Lower-school families**
- A real answer on the commute: buses or a shuttle between campuses for the transition years, and a stated end date.
- A concrete answer on little children sharing a campus with teenagers. Show them the floor plan: a separate entrance, separate drop-off, separate playground, and different bell times. Let a parent group review it before it is final. Parents accept a plan they helped shape much more readily than one they were shown.
- A guarantee that the lower-school team stays together as a unit, if you can honestly give it.

**Teachers and staff**
- Say plainly how many positions change and when. Vagueness feels worse than bad news, and the rumour will always be larger than the truth. If the consolidation saves money mostly on the buildings rather than on people, say so and put it in writing.
- Where roles are at risk, give a job-offer commitment, a timeline, and severance or retraining terms. Involve a few lower-school teachers in designing the new building.

**The whole community**
- A named, funded program that keeps the north site's purpose alive: for example, a Halloran learning garden at the new building, or a community-use agreement for part of the old site if Vellacott will accept one. This is also your best response to the plaque.
- A date by which you will report back on the first year.

## What would change my recommendation

If the deed turns out to forbid sale or require the land to remain a school, the whole plan changes. You would be looking at leasing the site to another educational user, a partial sale, or a different savings plan. Reading the 1958 documents is cheap and quick, and it is the first thing to do, before you spend another hour on messaging.
