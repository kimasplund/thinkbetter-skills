Three items in your own background undercut the premise that you don't need anything from Harrowfield. I'd recommend a conditional, staged approval: authorise pursuing Ironvale and Sorrelwood, subject to specific gates that you close or price before any lease is signed.

I'm working from your message only. I haven't seen the leases, the easement or the loan documents, so treat what follows as what to check, not as conclusions.

## Why "we don't need anything from Harrowfield" is shaky

**1. The 2015 co-tenancy protections are the biggest exposure.**
Your 26 inline shops were signed on grocery co-tenancy terms. When the anchor goes dark or is replaced by a non-grocery use, those clauses commonly allow rent reductions (often to percentage rent or a fixed fraction), and after a set period they allow termination. A gym and a clinic are unlikely to count as a "replacement grocery anchor" under typical wording.
- Pull every inline lease and tabulate: the trigger (anchor leaves, goes dark, or is replaced), the remedy, the cure period, the qualifying replacement criteria, and the termination right.
- Then net the effect against the headline. "30% more than Harrowfield pays" compares one tenant's rent with another's. The real comparison is total centre NOI. If 26 shops go to reduced rent, or some leave, the 30% uplift on 38,000 sq ft could be wiped out or reversed.
- Also check whether the 2015 leases have exclusives or use restrictions that a gym or medical user could breach (for example a fitness, physiotherapy or pharmacy exclusive).

**2. The easement and the fuel station tie Harrowfield to your site beyond the lease.**
Harrowfield owns the pad outright, and the reciprocal easement was recorded in 2009. It survives the lease expiry, so Harrowfield keeps rights over shared entrances and parking whatever happens to the store lease.
- Read it for parking ratios or protected stalls, restrictions on use of the common area, consent rights over changes to access, any "no competing fuel or convenience use" provisions, and maintenance cost sharing.
- A 24-hour gym changes traffic patterns at the entrances Harrowfield's station relies on. A change that burdens the easement could give Harrowfield a claim or a veto.
- Harrowfield will also own a fuel station next to a centre it has left, and it has a motive to be difficult. Its loyalty programme (10 cents off per litre per $100 spent in-store) loses its purpose once it leaves the centre. That could hurt the station's volumes, which gives it a reason to negotiate or to litigate.

**3. Marchbank's consent is probably not routine this time.**
Both new leases exceed 15,000 sq ft, so each needs sign-off. "Routine" was the experience with like-for-like renewals. This time the lender will see a grocery anchor replaced by two non-traditional tenants, a co-tenancy cascade risk, and a possible easement dispute. Approach Marchbank early, informally, with the rent roll and a co-tenancy analysis. Don't present them with signed leases and a deadline. Ask what they need: updated NOI, tenant financials for both operators, and possibly a reserve for TI and downtime.

## Other things to test

- **Letters of intent are non-binding.** Find out what is actually committed: term, rent, free rent, TI allowance, conditions, and the deadline for the LOIs to lapse. Check both operators' credit. A new 24-hour gym operator and a medical operator both usually need heavy build-out money from you or from them.
- **Downtime and capital.** Harrowfield's lease runs to next December. A demolition, subdivision, and two fit-outs (imaging needs shielding, plumbing, and power) could take 9 to 18 months. Model the rent gap and the TI cost, not just the stabilised rent.
- **Parking.** 610 stalls and a lot "rarely half full" is a grocery-era observation. Gym peaks are early morning and evening, and clinics peak in the daytime, so these two may overlap less than you fear. But count it properly, test it against zoning minimums and any easement stall allocations, and check the zoning permits medical and assembly uses.
- **Harrowfield's own lease terms.** Look for renewal options, notice dates, restoration obligations and any go-dark, relocation or right-of-first-offer provisions. A tenant asking for 40% less rent is also negotiating. Its sales are weak, but it has leverage through the easement and the inline co-tenancies.
- **Compare against real alternatives.** Don't present only "gym plus clinic" versus "Harrowfield at -40%". Include: renew Harrowfield at a lower rent for a shorter term with a kick-out; replace with another grocer; and a hybrid in which one of the new operators takes part of the box while Harrowfield (or another food user) shrinks into the rest. A hybrid might satisfy the co-tenancy language and reduce the risk.

## What to take to the committee on November 20

Recommend a **conditional go**, not a final decision:

1. Approve negotiating binding leases with Ironvale and Sorrelwood, with signing conditional on: (a) a co-tenancy exposure analysis showing total centre NOI at least equal to or better than the Harrowfield renewal case, (b) Marchbank's written consent, (c) legal confirmation that the easement permits the planned uses and traffic, and (d) satisfactory credit and TI terms.
2. Show three scenarios with total-centre numbers (including downtime, TI and leasing costs): replacement with the two new operators; renewal at -40%; and the hybrid. Show the downside case in which inline tenants invoke co-tenancy relief.
3. Name the decisions you're asking for now: authority to spend on legal and engineering diligence, authority to open conversations with Marchbank and the inline tenants, and a target date for a go/no-go on signing.

## How to bring everyone along

**Inline tenants (most sensitive).** Don't let them hear it from Harrowfield or from the market. Go to them before anything is public, with a message built around the new traffic: a 24-hour gym generates frequent daily visits, and a clinic brings steady daytime visitors. Offer something concrete in return for waiving or amending co-tenancy: a revised clause tied to occupancy or total traffic rather than to "grocery", a marketing fund, or a modest rent adjustment. Do it tenant by tenant, starting with those with the strongest termination rights. Get written waivers before you sign the new leases. Without them, you are telling tenants a story while holding a liability.

**Ironvale and Sorrelwood.** Keep both warm, but don't let either lock you in before the gates above are cleared. Be honest about the timeline and the conditions. Agree on a lapse date and a path for each to stay committed (an exclusivity period, perhaps with a deposit). Check that their plans don't conflict with each other, for example hours of operation next to a clinic, or noise and shared walls, since they would share one box.

**Marchbank.** Early and informal, as above. Treat them as a partner in the decision.

**Harrowfield.** Don't treat it as an afterthought. Open a managed conversation, ideally before the committee decides. Possible outcomes: a negotiated early exit, a shorter or smaller renewal, or an agreed amendment to the easement that deals with traffic, signage and parking. In exchange for peaceful co-operation, you could offer something it values: signage, access improvements for the pad, or a clean separation of the loyalty programme. It's better to know its position than to find it out through a dispute after the new tenants have signed.

**Investment committee and Ashby Ridge.** Give them a clear recommendation, the risks named openly, the gates, and a decision calendar. Committees tend to approve staged plans with clear exit points more easily than a leap.
