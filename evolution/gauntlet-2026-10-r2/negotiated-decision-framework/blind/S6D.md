The plan is financially sensible, but as it stands it is not ready for a February 12 vote. Several of the facts you listed as supporting points could each stop the sale or the build. Settle those first and the vote becomes much easier to win. Winning ten of 15 on enthusiasm alone is risky.

## What could actually stop this

**1. The Halloran gift.** A plaque saying the land was given "for the education of the children of Brennock" is exactly the kind of wording that can come with a reverter clause, a restrictive covenant, or a charitable-trust duty. Selling to a developer for housing may be barred outright, or may need the family's consent, a court's approval, or the attorney general's. Vellacott's letter of intent doesn't protect you here. Before anything goes public, have counsel pull the original deed and the 1958 gift documents and give a written opinion. Then talk to the Halloran family yourself, privately and early. If they learn about the sale from a news story, they could be the loudest opponents you have. If they are consulted and honoured, for example with a named space or a scholarship fund in the family's name, they could be your best advocates.

**2. The 2004 planning conditions.** "Space isn't the issue" holds only for physical capacity. You accepted an enrolment ceiling, and you haven't said what it is. You say you have never come close to it, but you have never planned to add 230 children and a new building either. Check three things:
- Whether 640 now, or 700 later, sits under that ceiling.
- Whether an $8M lower-school building needs a new approval or a modification.
- Whether the Sycamore Lane neighbours can reopen the whole approval, including drop-off traffic, when you apply.

Younger children mean more parent drop-offs, so traffic is the likeliest flashpoint. Get a land-use lawyer's read and a traffic study before the vote. Trustees will ask, "What if the board says no after we've sold north?" Build that answer in. The strongest form is a sale agreement that closes only after the south-campus approval is final.

**3. The Ardelle pledge.** The $800k left is pledged to a north-campus outdoor learning program. If the program leaves its site, the foundation may be free to stop paying, or entitled to ask for money back. Ask them now, and offer a version they can support, such as an outdoor learning area designed into the new south-campus build. Don't count that $800k in the plan until they confirm in writing.

**4. The enrolment timing.** Re-enrolment contracts go out January 10, and the board votes February 12, so families will sign or decline with no idea of the plan, or will hear it halfway through. Both are bad. If you announce before the vote, families may leave in alarm, and your budget already assumes 92% stay. If you announce after, the early returns will look like a vote of confidence that they were not, and some parents will feel ambushed. The lower-school parents are the people most likely to leave, and they are the families who have sat on the Halloran land for years. Each point of retention is about $190k of revenue on a $19M budget, so a drop from 92% to 85% costs roughly $1.3M. That is more than the whole deficit. Decide the order deliberately:
- Tell families before contracts go out, with a clear transition promise.
- Or push contract timing back, or add a short grace period, so the vote doesn't land in the middle of re-enrolment.

Either way, model 85% and 80% retention, not just 92%.

## What to get right in the plan

- **The money story.** $13.5M less transaction costs, less the $8M building, leaves a cushion, but a $1.1M annual deficit is operating, not capital. Selling the land won't fix it unless you also change enrolment and cost trends. Show trustees a year-by-year projection (operating deficit, building cost with a contingency of 15 to 20%, interest, one-time move costs) and what the plan does if enrolment keeps falling. Don't lead with "breathing room" alone; a trustee who sees only a one-time windfall covering recurring losses will vote no.
- **The gap years.** A fall 2028 move needs a building that is approved, built and ready, and a plan for what happens if it isn't. Say where the lower school sits until then, and what the fallback is if approvals slip. A phased plan, for example moving in 2028 only after the approval is final, is easier to sell than a fixed date.
- **The child-safety case.** The worry about little kids sharing a campus with teenagers is the one most likely to move parents. Answer it in design (separate entrance, separate play area, separate lunch and drop-off times), not in reassurance.
- **Alternatives.** Trustees who are undecided will want to know why not lease part of the north site, sell part of it, or sell with a covenant that keeps some of it for education. Have those ready, with honest numbers, so that the proposal looks chosen and not rushed.
- **The vote.** Don't schedule February 12 as the first discussion. Brief trustees individually in November and December, take the legal opinion to a special meeting, and ask for a vote only when you have counted ten. If you are at seven or eight, delay the vote; a failed vote is far harder to recover from than a postponed one.

## Suggested sequence

1. This week: engage counsel on the Halloran documents and the 2004 conditions; ask the planning office what ceiling applies.
2. By early December: contact the Ardelle Foundation and the Halloran family; start trustee one-to-ones; build the retention scenarios.
3. Before January 10: decide how and when families hear, and have the lower-school and staffing commitments ready to publish alongside the news.
4. Before the vote: counsel's opinion, traffic study, a conditional sale agreement tied to approvals, and a head count of at least ten.

## What would change this recommendation

If counsel finds the Halloran gift bars a sale or needs court approval, the plan changes shape entirely, toward a long-term lease, a partial sale, or consolidating a different way. That is the first and cheapest check, and it costs a few days of a lawyer's time. If the 2004 ceiling turns out to sit near 640, the south-campus building is in doubt too, and the same applies.
