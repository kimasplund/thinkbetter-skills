**Recommendation in one line:** Take the gym and clinic path to the committee as the preferred option, but as a conditional recommendation. Ask for approval to proceed subject to four gates (below), and keep a negotiated Harrowfield renewal as a live fallback. I would not present it as "we don't need anything from Harrowfield." That premise is the weakest part of the plan.

## Why the premise doesn't hold

Harrowfield's lease ending doesn't end its relationship with the property. Four things survive it or are triggered by it.

1. **Inline co-tenancy.** In 2015 you filled 26 inline shops by offering grocery co-tenancy protections. If Harrowfield leaves, those clauses probably fire. The usual remedies are rent abatement (often to a percentage of sales or a fixed lower rate), then a termination right if the replacement isn't in place within a set time. A gym and a clinic are unlikely to count as a "grocery" or "comparable anchor" under the definition. Pull all 26 leases and tabulate for each one:
   - the trigger and the definition of a qualifying replacement
   - the cure period
   - the remedy
   - any dark-period or go-dark timing

   The result is a dollar figure: the inline rent at risk if the grocery goes and the replacement doesn't qualify. The "30% more rent" headline has to be netted against it. If the inline abatements and terminations cost more than the gain, the plan fails.

2. **The reciprocal easement and the fuel pad.** Harrowfield owns the pad outright and shares your entrances and parking under the 2009 easement. It will have rights you can't terminate when the lease ends. Have counsel read the easement for:
   - any parking ratio, access, visibility or signage rights
   - any restrictions on use of the remaining parcel, or approval rights over changes
   - maintenance and cost-sharing terms
   - whether it limits the density or the type of use of the parking field

   If Harrowfield doesn't want the grocery replaced, the easement and the pad are where it has leverage. It may also have its own reasons to want to stay in the deal, since a fuel station with no grocery traffic is a weaker asset.

3. **The fuel-card link.** The 10 cents a litre per $100 in-store loyalty benefit ties pad traffic to the grocery. When the store leaves, the pad loses much of its draw and its owner has a strong incentive to contest the change, or to ask for something (signage, parking guarantees, a re-negotiated easement) in return for cooperation. Expect a response from them rather than silence.

4. **The "40% less" ask is an opening position.** Sales have slid for three years, so they probably mean it, but it's also a starting offer. Check the lease for a renewal option, its notice dates and its rent-reset method. If there is an option at a formula rent, the "simply runs out" idea may be wrong in the other direction, and they may be able to hold over.

## Other things I'd check before November 20

- **Marchbank Life consent.** Both new leases exceed 15,000 sq ft (22,000 and 16,000), so you need two sign-offs, not one. "Routine" was the experience with a grocery anchor. Replacing a credit anchor with a gym and a medical operator may look different to a lender, who may also have covenants on anchor tenancy, minimum rent or co-tenancy. Approach Marchbank informally now, ahead of the committee, and ask what it needs (tenant financials, lease forms, SNDAs, estoppels).
- **LOIs are non-binding.** Find out what the LOIs commit each party to, what the deal timeline is and what they need from you (landlord work, allowances, free rent, parking). The "about 30% more" figure should be shown net of tenant improvement allowances, free-rent periods and downtime between the grocery's exit and the gym's and clinic's openings. Imaging and urgent care need heavy build-out. Show the gap in income during the period without a grocery.
- **Tenant credit and use risk.** A 24-hour gym franchisee and a clinic operator have different covenant strengths than a grocer. Ask for guarantees or security deposits and check the operators' financials.
- **Parking.** The lot is "rarely half full" with a grocery, but gyms peak in early morning and evening, and urgent-care and imaging run steady all day, so the peaks overlap more than they do for retail. At 610 stalls on 140,000 sq ft you are at about 4.4 per thousand. Medical use usually asks for 5 or more per thousand on its own. Do a parking demand study and check zoning and bylaw requirements for the changed use mix, and for imaging equipment (shielding, permits). A 24-hour operation also raises noise, lighting and security issues.
- **Exclusives and use clauses.** A clinic may conflict with an existing pharmacy, physio or health-related exclusive among the inline tenants. A gym may conflict with another fitness tenant. Check all 26 leases for this.
- **Fallbacks.** The committee should see the plan with a downside: what happens if the co-tenancy cost is high, the lender won't consent or the easement blocks the plan. Alternatives include a short renewal with Harrowfield at a rent somewhere between 60% and 100% of today's, a different grocer, or a partial plan (clinic and gym with a smaller grocery component).

## How to bring everyone along

Sequence matters. The aim is that no party hears about this from someone else first.

1. **Now to early November, privately and in this order:**
   - *Counsel and your own leases.* Complete the co-tenancy table and the easement review. This tells you how much leverage each party really has.
   - *Marchbank.* An informal read on consent.
   - *Ironvale and Sorrelwood.* Confirm terms and ask for a firm timetable. Ask them to agree to confidentiality and to extend the LOIs through the date you need.
2. **Harrowfield, before the committee if you can.** You aren't asking for permission, but it is better to talk to them about the easement, the fuel pad and the transition on your terms than for them to find out and lawyer up. Ask what they actually need: a reduced-footprint store, a short extension, signage, parking guarantees. A low-cost way to keep them cooperative may exist, and their answer is useful information for the committee either way.
3. **Investment committee, November 20.** Present the preferred option with the gates, the numbers net of co-tenancy cost and the fallback, rather than a bare recommendation.
4. **The inline tenants, after you have a signed or near-signed replacement plan, but not so late that they hear it from the grapevine.** Tell them early that a change is coming, and what it means for them: more daytime and evening foot traffic from the gym and clinic, new uses that draw different customers, and any commitments you can make (marketing support, parking, signage). If co-tenancy rights are triggered, talk to them about waivers or amended terms in exchange for something concrete. They will care most about traffic and rent, so show them evidence. Treat each differently according to its clause and its sales. A few may be better kept with a rent adjustment than lost.
5. **Ironvale and Sorrelwood.** Their interest is certainty on timing, parking and build-out. Offer both in return for firm commitments and security.

## What I'd put in front of the committee

- Recommendation: proceed with the gym and clinic plan, conditional on (1) inline co-tenancy exposure being below an agreed amount, (2) written Marchbank consent for both leases, (3) the easement not blocking the plan or its cost being known, and (4) signed leases rather than LOIs, with parking confirmed.
- A table of income: current Harrowfield rent, a renewal at minus 40%, and the new plan, each net of co-tenancy cost, incentives and vacancy.
- Fallback and decision dates.

## Assumptions

I haven't seen the leases, the easement or the LOIs, so the points above are what I would check and not conclusions. If the co-tenancy exposure turns out to be small and the easement is clean, the plan is probably strong. If either is bad, a negotiated renewal could be worth more than the headline suggests.
