# J-A scores, brief-3

- J9S: Covers co-tenancy, the easement, lender consent and parking well, with a sensible order of operations and three options with conditions. It states the easement is "perpetual" without support, and it never quantifies the trade-off or sets a decision threshold.
- Q6A: The decision rule (inline exposure against an uplift of about 0.7x current rent) is the most usable decision aid of the six, and the confidence statement is honest. One slip: it says the leases exceed 15,000 sq ft "in aggregate", but each one exceeds it on its own. The interest-mapping section adds a little structure.
- C6M: The most complete on substance. It covers holdover and renewal-formula risk, two separate lender consents, lender covenants, SNDAs and estoppels, the parking ratio arithmetic (about 4.4 per 1,000 sq ft against a typical medical requirement of 5 or more), and exclusives. It also specifies what goes in the committee package. It is long, but nearly all of it is usable.
- Q7Y: A strong "what would change this" section with the cheapest first check. The party table leans toward method scaffolding. It says Marchbank gets "better covenant strength", which contradicts its own credit concern, and it mentions ambulance traffic at an urgent-care clinic.
- A4Z: Tight, well ordered and practical, and it ties lender scrutiny to co-tenancy exposure. Its "handover with no gap" ignores the months needed to convert a grocery box. Approaching Harrowfield last, after the inline tenants, risks Harrowfield hearing about it secondhand.
- C1X: Thorough. Useful points: the threshold against the 60% renewal, a replacement food use as a way to satisfy co-tenancy, and the 2029 refinancing. It asserts without support that the new tenants are healthier than the grocer, and it is somewhat long.
