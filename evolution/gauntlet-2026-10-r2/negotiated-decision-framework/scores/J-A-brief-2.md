# J-A notes, brief-2

- K1F: Covers the main issues: the lateral, Dunmere, Orrin's conflict and Quillon's vote. The steps are less concrete, with no dates or windows, and "one package with two or three options" contradicts itself.
- P1A: Thorough and practical. It counts orchard seats on the board, notes the orchards would likely lose a weighted vote, and has a good "what could go wrong" section. Long.
- H7N: Compact and sharp. It is the only reply to note that the canal's "loss" has a counterparty (it is Dunmere's recharge). It suggests temporary trades only and asks whether the 9% is a whole-season figure. Sized well.
- G0Z: The most complete map of who holds power, and it explains why to bring a package rather than a menu. It states its key assumption (the post-harvest volume is small). Long, but the content earns most of it.
- F5W: Strong on the fact that east-lateral orchards cannot physically receive their 55%, and on whether timing counts as a departure from equal shares. The table and section headers lean toward framework. It calls this a "2026" plan, a year the brief never gives.
- O3B: Tight and well judged. It warns against engineering around a vote members expect, offers a perennial hardship reserve, and lists its assumptions plainly.
- A7U: Clear recommendation, says what would change it and gives the cheapest check. But proposing to use Dunmere's seepage money to line the lateral undercuts the very recharge Dunmere pays for. It says "never needs the vote" without first asking counsel.
- O8T: Its idea of charging conveyance losses to trades is useful. The 6,300-acre vote block mixes acres farmed with acres owned (Neil's acres now vote with Quillon), and it says the orchards sit "mostly" on the east lateral, which overstates the brief.
- A2J: Actionable: ask the ditch rider for the loss in each delivery window, and Orrin recuses on trading. "Quillon can outvote you" overstates 2,900 of 21,000 acres. The stakeholder table reads as a framework.
- X4R: Asks whether the 9% is of the east lateral's flow or the district's conveyance, writes down what happens at 45% and 70%, and sees Dunmere as a possible ally. One confusing line about the pool costing nothing.
- V8F: Good. It notes Dunmere may want late-season recharge, which is a second reason to run the lateral later, and suggests a per-acre orchard reserve. Says what would change the advice and the cheapest check. The table adds some ceremony.
- O5Q: Practical. It says to check whether reservoir and basin rules allow carrying water into late season, and to get the loss by week. Its "what would change this" section includes the case where the orchards are only a small share of the lateral's users.
