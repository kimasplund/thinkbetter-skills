# J-A scores, brief-3

Q6A: Catches co-tenancy, easement, lender and loyalty traffic. Its decision rule (exposure vs. roughly 0.7x current rent a year) is something I could use straight away. Muddled line on whether the 16k lease needs consent. Leaves downtime and TI out of the rule. The "interests" section is a slight framework trace.
Q7Y: Covers the right issues and lands a conditional yes with the cheapest check. But it says Marchbank "gets better covenant strength", which is unsupported and cuts against its own worry, and it adds ambulance traffic and a routine-consent history the brief doesn't give. The give/get blocker table reads like method scaffolding.
K8W: Compact and practical. Nets in TI, commissions and downtime, compares three outcomes and names the cheapest check. It slightly invents that consent was routine "only when the anchor was a grocer". Its sequencing of the Harrowfield conversation (early vs. after approval) contradicts itself.
H0K: The most complete. Specific easement questions, an LOI stress test (year-one income vs. a renewal at 40% off), a committee-set tolerance, a structured fallback and a decision date tied to notice deadlines. States 65% confidence and its assumptions. A bit long.
X8D: Thorough. Frames the comparison as total-centre NOI, models downtime and capital, compares against real alternatives (another grocer, a hybrid), wants waivers before signing and gives a timeline. The caveats are clear. Slightly long, and it has no explicit "what would change this" section.
A6D: Tight and accurate. The parking ratio (4.4/1,000) is computed correctly, it checks whether the box can physically be split, and it gives a sensible threshold (exposure vs. a year or two of uplift). The blocker table is a mild method trace, and it is a bit confident about parking overlap.
A4Z: Good subtle point that a dark gap is what triggers most clauses. But it assumes a no-gap handover, which is unrealistic given the build-out. It puts Harrowfield last, which risks a leak, and offers few alternatives beyond a discounted renewal. Clean prose.
C2N: Good points: higher parking ratios for medical use, loan cash-sweep provisions, the risk of one operator dropping out, the pad being sold. But it says box rent would be "roughly 117% of current" when the brief implies 130%, and it assumes the rents are net of allowances while telling me to net them. Arithmetic error in the core economics.
J9S: Good subtle points: current rent may be above market, bring in the leasing team who know the 2015 promises, consider buying the pad. Clear A/B/C options and a one-line pitch. It states the easement is "perpetual" as fact, which is unknown.
C6M: Comprehensive and mostly well calibrated (medical parking ratios, SNDAs and estoppels, a renewal fallback range). Long and list-heavy for what it adds. No explicit cheapest-first check.
B4O: Strong on the economics of a split box conversion. Makes the sharp point that Harrowfield's departure may trigger co-tenancy whatever replaces it. Gives the hybrid option and a go/no-go date. Its "what would change it" and cheapest-check guidance is less explicit.
C1X: Good on the 2029 refinancing, the lease-expiry weighting of inline exposure and the parking minimums. But it asserts that the new tenants are "healthier than a grocer" and are "recognised operators", and tells me to pitch better covenant strength to Marchbank, all without support.
