I manage the Sallow Creek Irrigation District: 64 member farms and about 21,000 irrigated acres. The reservoir is at 38%, and the basin authority says to expect 55% of a normal allocation this season. Our seven-member board, all growers, has to adopt a shortage plan on March 18.

My draft: every farm gets 55% of its normal entitlement; members can trade water with each other inside the district at whatever price they agree; and we shut the east lateral on August 1. That canal is unlined, so closing it early cuts our conveyance losses by about 9%.

The orchard growers, mostly cherries and many of them on the east lateral, want priority because trees take years to replace. They say the water that matters most comes after harvest, when the trees set next year's fruit buds. The row-crop growers say equal shares is what the bylaws promise. Orrin Castellane, our board chair, farms 3,400 acres of alfalfa and is firmly for equal shares and open trading; he mentioned he'll probably fallow half his hay anyway with prices where they are. Neil Benning, who grows 2,900 acres of processing tomatoes, has backed Orrin at every meeting. The Bennings sold that land to Quillon Land Partners last year and lease it back. Any plan that departs from equal shares must be ratified by a member vote weighted by acreage owned.

Budget-wise we're fine: member assessments, plus $40,000 a year from the town of Dunmere under a 1997 agreement covering seepage from the east lateral, which recharges the town's wells.

How do I get a plan through that both the orchard and row-crop growers will actually stick to?
