## Short answer

Recommend the Ironvale and Sorrelwood re-tenanting, but not as a clean "let Harrowfield go". Ask the committee on November 20 for approval to proceed, with four conditions that must clear before you sign anything binding. I'd also push back on one sentence in your note: "we don't need anything from Harrowfield." Their lease runs out, but three other things they hold don't. Two of them could stop or cost you this plan, and you haven't yet seen what either document says.

## Why the plan is attractive

- About 30% more rent from the same 38,000 sq ft, against a renewal that would cut rent 40%. That is roughly a 70-point swing between the two paths.
- Two signed LOIs give you a credible alternative at the table, and the tenants are healthier than a grocer whose sales have fallen for three years.
- Gym and clinic uses drive frequent daily visits, which helps the inline shops if the mix is right.

## What could break it

**1. Co-tenancy clauses in the 26 inline leases.** This is the biggest exposure. In 2015 you filled the shops by offering grocery co-tenancy protection. Those clauses typically allow rent abatement, and sometimes termination, if the grocery anchor goes dark or is replaced by something that doesn't meet the definition. A gym and a clinic almost certainly don't meet a "grocery" definition. If many shops can cut rent or leave, the 30% uplift on the box can be wiped out by losses across 102,000 sq ft of inline space. Before the committee meets, get a schedule of every inline lease showing:
- the trigger (anchor leaves, goes dark, or is replaced)
- the remedy (percentage rent, reduced rent, termination right)
- any cure period, and what counts as a "replacement" anchor
- the expiry date of each lease, since shops expiring soon matter less

**2. Harrowfield's fuel station and the reciprocal easement.** Harrowfield owns the pad outright and shares your entrances and parking under a recorded easement. This is the real source of its leverage, and it holds even after the lease ends. Read the easement for:
- any restriction on use of the shopping centre (some prohibit uses that change traffic or parking materially)
- minimum parking or access commitments
- approval rights over changes to the entrances or common areas
- whether it covers the front of the lot, where you may want to reconfigure for clinic drop-off and gym parking

Also think about the station's position. Its loyalty card ties fuel discounts to in-store spend, so once the grocery leaves, the station loses much of its draw and Harrowfield loses its fuel-discount program with it. They have a real motive to object, to seek an easement-based remedy, or to trade. Treat them as a counterparty with a stake, not as a tenant walking away.

**3. Lender consent.** Marchbank must approve any lease over 15,000 sq ft. Both new leases (22,000 and 16,000) qualify. "Routine" has been true for grocery-style tenants. A medical imaging use, a 24-hour operation, and the loss of an anchor are exactly what a lender looks at. Approach them early and get consent in writing, or at least a clear indication, before you commit. The mortgage runs to 2029, so you'll need them again at refinancing.

**4. Parking and zoning.** 610 stalls that are "rarely half full" is a grocery number. Gyms peak in the early morning and evening, and clinics peak during business hours, so overlap with the inline shops' own peaks matters. Check the municipal parking minimums for medical and fitness use. These are usually higher than for retail, and a change of use may need a permit. Check parking covenants in the inline leases and the easement too.

Smaller points to confirm: LOIs are non-binding, so make sure the definitive leases don't leave you with an empty box if one operator withdraws; check whether either tenant has requirements (exclusives, landlord work contributions, signage) that cut into the 30% headline; check for any right of first refusal, continuous-operation clause, or exclusive in Harrowfield's lease that applies as it winds down; and check the timing, since Harrowfield's lease ends next December and fit-out takes months, so rent may not start for some time after.

## What to recommend on November 20

Ask for approval to proceed to definitive leases with Ironvale and Sorrelwood, conditional on:

1. The co-tenancy exposure being quantified and either acceptable or mitigated. Net impact must still beat the renewal at 60% of current rent.
2. Legal confirmation that the easement and the fuel station don't restrict the plan.
3. Written lender consent or a clear indication from Marchbank.
4. Confirmation of parking and zoning compliance.

Keep Harrowfield's renewal as the fallback. Don't reject it formally yet. If a condition fails, a rent-cut renewal might still beat a vacant box, and having it alive is also your leverage in the talks below.

Give the committee a simple comparison of three options: renew Harrowfield at -40%, replace with the two operators, or a hybrid (a smaller Harrowfield footprint plus one operator). Show each with net rent after likely inline rent losses, downtime, landlord work, and risk. If the co-tenancy review shows large exposure, the hybrid, or a replacement food use that satisfies the co-tenancy definition, may be better than the two-operator plan.

## How to bring everyone along

**Harrowfield.** Talk to them first, before they hear it from a tenant or a broker. Their interest is probably not the box itself, since sales have fallen, but protecting the fuel station and their loyalty program, limiting reputational damage, and exiting on their terms. Possible trades: a negotiated easement amendment or reaffirmation in exchange for clear access and signage for the station, a cooperative marketing arrangement with the new operators (a gym and clinic bring daily traffic past the pumps), or a short extension to give both sides time to transition. Find out what they want before deciding what to offer. If they have a legal remedy under the easement, you want to learn that now, not after the leases are signed.

**Inline tenants.** Tell them early and in a planned way, with the story they care about: who is coming, foot traffic expected, hours, and the timeline. Where a co-tenancy clause is triggered, don't wait for them to invoke it. Offer a negotiated amendment, for example a short-term rent adjustment while the new anchors open, in exchange for waiving termination rights and accepting the new mix as satisfying the clause. Prioritise the shops with the strongest rights and the highest rent. Food and convenience tenants who relied on grocery traffic need the most reassurance, and may be the most open to a deal.

**Ironvale and Sorrelwood.** Keep them committed but not exclusive-feeling. Move quickly to definitive leases, with conditions that match yours (lender consent, easement clearance) so neither side is exposed. Agree up front on parking management, operating hours, noise, and the clinic's drop-off needs. Make sure their expectations for signage, exclusives, and fit-out contributions don't conflict with each other or with inline leases.

**Marchbank.** Present it as a net improvement in income and covenant strength with a tenant mix of recognised operators. Bring the numbers: rent, term, tenant credit, and the plan for the inline tenants.

**Investment committee.** Bring a decision that includes the risks, so they are approving a plan with gates, not a surprise. Say clearly what you don't yet know and when you will.
