**Recommendation:** Keep your 55% equal shares, but change two things before March 18. Don't close the east lateral on a fixed date. Replace "trade at any price" with a district-run water exchange. That keeps the plan inside the bylaws, so it needs no acreage-weighted ratification vote. It also gives the orchard growers what they actually need, which is water after harvest.

**Why your draft will probably fail**

- **The August 1 closure is the orchard growers' real objection.** Cherries come off in June and July, so the water they care about is the post-harvest water in August and September. Closing a canal that many of them sit on is priority for row crops by another route. They will read it as equal shares in name only, and they are the ones with the most to lose and the longest memory. I'd expect them to fight it, lobby members outside the board, or stop cooperating once the season starts.
- **Open trading at any price turns water into a price contest.** Orrin says he'll fallow about half his hay, so he is likely to be the largest seller. An orchard grower facing a loss of trees would pay almost anything, and the board chair would collect the money. Even if Orrin does nothing wrong, it looks bad. Expect the orchard growers to say the process was rigged.
- **The Dunmere agreement may limit closing the lateral.** The town pays $40,000 a year for the seepage that recharges its wells. If a closure cuts that seepage, you could breach the 1997 agreement or lose the payment. Read the agreement's wording on seepage and on any closure or notice requirements before the board votes.

**The plan I'd bring to the board**

1. **Equal 55% entitlement for every farm.** This is the promise in the bylaws, and the row-crop growers can defend it.
2. **Let members move their share in time.** Any member may schedule when they use their 55%, including holding part of it back for August to October. Orchard growers can then plan for post-harvest water without taking anyone else's share.
3. **Keep the east lateral open for the post-harvest window**, with deliveries scheduled in blocks so it isn't running continuously. Close it once that window ends, not on a fixed August 1 date. You give up part of the 9% conveyance saving, but the board can then say it protected the trees. Ask your ditch staff what the saving is if you run the lateral in two or three set delivery windows rather than all season. You may keep most of it.
4. **Replace free-for-all trading with a district exchange.** The district posts a price band (a floor and a cap) and matches sellers with buyers. Members who fallow can sell into it, and orchard growers get first call on the lateral's post-harvest water. A cap keeps the price from reaching the level that desperation would set. Orrin and any other seller still profit, though less than at an open price. It also keeps the board chair out of private deals with the growers he is voting on.
5. **Orrin should disclose his position and recuse himself on the trading rules.** If he'll fallow and sell, he has a direct stake in the price band. Ask him to step out of that one vote, which is better than having an orchard grower raise it for him. He can stay in on the rest.

**Who can block this**

| Who | What they need | How they could block | What they get / give |
|---|---|---|---|
| Orchard growers (many on the east lateral) | Water in August to October so the trees set next year's buds | Reject the closure, lobby members, ignore the schedule | Get: protected post-harvest water, first call on the exchange. Give: no priority in the spring or summer, and a price band |
| Row-crop growers | The bylaws honoured, no quiet shift to the orchards | Say the plan departs from equal shares, which forces a ratification vote | Get: equal 55%, plus income from selling. Give: some of the conveyance saving, and the price cap |
| Orrin Castellane (board chair) | Equal shares, open trading, and a plan that passes | Chair the meeting against any change, or push the vote | Gets: equal shares, which he wanted. Gives: unrestricted pricing, and his vote on trading rules |
| Neil Benning and Quillon Land Partners | Unknown; Quillon owns the 2,900 acres and the Bennings lease them back | Quillon can outvote you in a ratification, if one is needed | See below |
| Town of Dunmere | The seepage to its wells, and its $40,000 agreement | Dispute a closure that cuts seepage | Gets: a lateral that stays open as long as the seepage matters |

**Neil, Quillon and the ratification vote.** The vote is weighted by acreage owned. Quillon owns the land Neil farms, so Quillon, not Neil, holds those votes. Neil's backing of Orrin may not tell you what Quillon wants. Quillon is an investor, and a lease with tenant farmers who face a 45% cut may give it reasons of its own: the rent coming in, and the value of the land with or without its water. Find out who at Quillon votes, and whether the Bennings' lease says anything about water. If you stay with equal shares and no priority, you probably avoid the ratification vote altogether, which is the main reason to keep the plan inside the bylaws. Get a ruling from the district's attorney on whether the exchange or the delivery schedule counts as a departure. Do that before March 18, not after someone raises it at the meeting.

**Getting both sides to stick to it**

- Put the delivery windows and the price band in the adopted plan in writing. Say what happens if the reservoir or the allocation changes mid-season.
- Agree a review in early July, before the post-harvest window opens, to adjust for the actual allocation and for what the orchard growers have used.
- Have the orchard growers and a few row-crop members help set the delivery schedule. People keep to a schedule they helped write.
- Meet Orrin first, and the orchard growers second. Show Orrin that the plan gives him equal shares, his main demand, and ask him to give up only the unrestricted price. Then take the same plan to the orchard growers.

**What would change this**

If Dunmere's agreement requires closing or limiting the lateral at a set date, or if the lateral's losses in the post-harvest window are so high that the water doesn't reach the trees, the plan needs another route for orchard water. The cheapest check is to read the 1997 agreement and ask your ditch rider for the delivery loss on the east lateral in August and September. Both take a day or two. If Quillon's votes turn out to matter and it opposes equal shares, work out its position before you bring the plan forward.
