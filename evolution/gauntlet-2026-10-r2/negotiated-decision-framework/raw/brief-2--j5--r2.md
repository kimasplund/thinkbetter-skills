**Recommendation:** Keep equal 55% shares, so the plan never needs the acreage-weighted ratification vote. Then change two parts of your draft: the August 1 east lateral closure and the unpriced trading. Together they give the orchards the post-harvest water they need without breaking the bylaws.

**Two problems in the draft to fix before March 18**

1. **The August 1 closure hits the orchards where they are most exposed.** Their critical water comes after harvest, and the lateral serves many of them. Cherries are typically picked in June or July, so an August 1 closure may cut off exactly the bud-setting window they are worried about. This is probably why they will fight the plan, and "priority" is their fallback for getting the water back. The closure may also breach your Dunmere agreement. That agreement pays $40,000 a year because east lateral seepage recharges the town's wells. Read the 1997 text before the meeting. Check whether it sets a minimum flow or season, and whether closing early lets the town withhold the payment or sue. The 9% conveyance saving may not be worth the money and legal risk.
2. **Open trading at any price will probably inflate prices.** Orrin plans to fallow half his hay, so he will have water to sell, and the orchards are the obvious buyers. In a shortage the orchards would be paying a scarcity premium to the board chair, and the row-crop growers would see that as unfair too. That will turn a dry year into a fight over who profited.

**What I would put on the table**

- **Equal 55% shares stay.** The row-crop growers get what the bylaws promise, and Orrin and Neil have no reason to oppose it.
- **Members can bank and shift their water within the season.** Any farm can take its allocation late rather than early. Orchards could hold part of their 55% for August to October by taking less in spring. This gives them post-harvest water as an option inside equal shares, with no priority. Row crops that finish early lose nothing.
- **Trading goes through a district pool with a price cap, set by the board.** Growers who fallow offer water to the pool, and buyers draw from it at a posted price. Set it near the cost of water in a normal year, not a scarcity price. Fallowing growers still earn something, since alfalfa at current prices is a weak crop. Orrin sees his open-trading principle kept, and buyers are protected from gouging. Make him disclose his own sales so nobody can claim he wrote the rules to profit.
- **East lateral: close later, or in stages, or leave a minimum flow.** The exact choice depends on the Dunmere agreement. A sensible default is to keep it open through the orchards' post-harvest window and reduce flow rather than shut it off. If you do close it early, orchards on it should be first to receive pool water delivered another way.
- **Offer to fund the real efficiency fix.** Use part of the Dunmere money, or seek a basin or state grant, toward lining or piping the east lateral. That recovers the conveyance loss without closing it early. The town benefits only if seepage continues, so open this with them before committing to it.

**People who can block**

- **Orrin (chair, alfalfa).** He wants equal shares and open trading. He will accept pool trading if it still lets him sell, and he will probably support the plan if it needs no vote. Have him present the equal-shares part himself.
- **The orchard growers.** They need guaranteed water from August through October. They can lobby the board, demand a vote, or ignore limits quietly. Bring them a concrete delivery schedule, not a promise.
- **Neil Benning.** He sold his 2,900 acres to Quillon Land Partners and leases it back. If any vote is ever needed, it is weighted by acreage owned, and Quillon is the owner now. Find out whether Quillon has a view, whether Neil really speaks for it, and whether the lease passes the water entitlement to Neil. This matters most if anything departs from equal shares. A new owner with a different agenda could reverse the board's majority, so talk to Quillon before March 18 rather than after.
- **Dunmere.** The town has a contract and a financial stake in the east lateral. Tell them what you plan before it is public.
- **Row-crop members generally.** They will accept the plan if they see no one jumping the queue, so publish the allocations and trades.

**Making it stick**

- Write the August to October banking rule and the price cap into the adopted plan, with a review in mid-June. If the allocation turns out better or worse than 55%, the plan adjusts automatically rather than reopening the argument.
- Publish each farm's allocation, usage and trades monthly. Quiet overuse is the main way a plan like this fails.
- Agree penalties in advance, such as a charge on overuse set above the pool price.
- Hold a pre-meeting with the orchard growers and with Orrin and Neil separately, so no one first sees the compromise in public.

**What would change this recommendation:** if the Dunmere agreement lets you close the lateral without penalty and the orchards can get post-harvest water by another route, your original closure date can stand. Reading the agreement is the cheapest check, and it takes about an hour. A second check is a call to Quillon asking who holds the vote and what they want.
