**Recommendation for November 20:** Don't ask the committee to approve "let Harrowfield go and sign the gym and clinic." Ask for approval to pursue the Ironvale and Sorrelwood deal, conditional on three checks that you can finish before the meeting or soon after. The deal is probably the right one, because 30% more rent on the same box is hard to beat. The claim that you don't need anything from Harrowfield is the weak point, though. Harrowfield, the inline tenants and Marchbank can each stop or slow this plan.

**Who can block it, and what to do about each**

**What goes to the committee**

1. Compare three outcomes: Harrowfield renews at 40% less rent, the gym-and-clinic deal goes ahead, and a gap or failed deal leaves the box empty for a period. Include the cost of any inline rent relief or lease terminations, the cost of building out the box for two tenants, leasing commissions and downtime. Show the net rent after those costs, not just headline rent.
2. State the conditions: co-tenancy exposure assessed, easement cleared, Marchbank consent in hand, and both new tenants bound.
3. Propose a fallback. If the checks fail, you keep the option of a reduced-rent renewal with Harrowfield, and the lease expiry gives you time to decide. Don't say no to Harrowfield until Marchbank and the easement are settled.

**Sequence**

- This week: counsel reviews the inline leases, the Harrowfield lease, and the easement. This is the cheapest step and it decides how strong the recommendation is.
- Before November 20: quietly sound out Marchbank, and run the parking count. Prepare the numbers.
- After approval: speak to Harrowfield first, then the inline tenants, and then announce. Nobody should hear it second-hand. Sign the new leases only after consent and clearance.

**What would change this recommendation**

If the inline leases let shops terminate or pay sharply reduced rent when the grocer closes, or the easement lets Harrowfield veto the new uses, the extra 30% could disappear, and a renewal at 40% less rent may be better. The cheapest check is a lawyer's one-week read of the co-tenancy clauses and the easement. Do that before anything else.
