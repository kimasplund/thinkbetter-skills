The short version: the financial case is strong, but three things could sink the vote or the sale before parents and staff become the main problem. Fix those first, then build the stakeholder plan around them. I've assumed today is mid-October 2026, so the February 12 vote is about four months out and the fall 2028 move is about 22 months out.

## Three issues to resolve before you count votes

**1. The Halloran gift.** The plaque says "for the education of the children of Brennock." Selling the land to a developer may be fine, or it may trigger a reverter, a charitable-trust restriction, or a need for court or Attorney General approval. I don't know which, and neither do you until counsel reads the 1958 deed and any related documents. Trustees will ask about this, and a few of them will use it as a reason to vote no. A signed LOI with Vellacott doesn't settle it. Get a written legal opinion before January. If approval is needed, start that process now. Also ask whether any Halloran heirs are alive and whether the family has a view. If the plan is to sell and put the proceeds into education for Brennock's children, say that plainly. It is a stronger story than "we sold the gift."

**2. The Ardelle Foundation.** The $800k still to come is tied to the north-campus outdoor learning program. If the north campus closes, the foundation may be entitled to stop paying, or even to reclaim funds. Call them before the board vote, not after. Ask whether they would redirect the pledge to an outdoor learning program on the south campus, and whether they'd be willing to say so in public. Their reaction is also a signal to trustees. If they walk away, the net proceeds drop by up to $800k and the donor base may take a hit.

**3. The Sycamore Lane conditions.** Check the actual wording of the enrolment ceiling in the 2004 approval. "We've never come close" is a comfortable assumption, but 640 students now plus the lower school moving across could put you at or near it, and your consultant's 700 is a capacity figure, not a legal one. Adding a new $8M building will almost certainly need new planning approval, and that reopens the whole 2004 fight, including traffic, drop-off, and the 9pm event limit, with 230 more small children arriving at the same gate. Open a conversation with the Sycamore Lane neighbours early and offer something concrete, such as traffic management, a staggered drop-off, or a community liaison. If the planning approval can't be secured, the sale proceeds have nowhere to go. Make Vellacott's closing conditional on the south-campus approvals, and make sure the LOI gives you enough time. If Vellacott wants to close before approvals land, that is a risk to the school, not just a scheduling issue.

## The timing problem

Re-enrolment contracts go out January 10 and are due March 1. The board votes February 12. That means families will be deciding whether to re-enrol before they know whether the school is closing the north campus, or, if you announce your intention earlier, while the board hasn't yet approved it. Either way, you get uncertainty during the one window when you most need confidence, and your budget depends on 92% returning.

Some ways to handle this:

- **Tell lower-school families in the fall, not in February.** Announce that the board is considering consolidation, give the reasons, and be explicit that nothing changes for the 2027-28 year. Families who hear it from you directly in November are far calmer than families who hear it through the grapevine in January.
- **Make the January contracts say what is and isn't changing.** The 2027-28 year is on the north campus. The move, if approved, is fall 2028. That gives families a full year of runway.
- **Consider moving the vote.** If the legal opinion, the Ardelle conversation, and the planning picture won't be clear by February 12, a vote that passes on incomplete information is more dangerous than a vote that waits a few weeks. Since the move is in 2028, a short delay costs little. A rushed approval followed by a deed problem, a lost pledge, or a planning refusal would cost a lot and would damage the board's trust in you.
- **Set a tripwire.** Decide now what re-enrolment number would make you revisit the plan. If lower-school re-enrolment falls to, say, 85% instead of 92%, what happens to the deficit? Model it so you are not improvising on March 2.

## Winning ten of fifteen votes

You need a two-thirds majority, so five trustees can block you. Don't assume the margin. Meet each trustee one on one in the next four weeks and sort them honestly into yes, leaning yes, undecided, and no. For each, find out what they actually care about, which may be fiduciary responsibility, the school's mission, the Halloran legacy, a child in the lower school, or a personal tie to the neighbourhood. Then bring them what they need:

- **Fiduciary trustees** want the numbers: a five-year model with the deficit, the do-nothing scenario (what happens if the school keeps both campuses, including what you'd have to cut), the downside case at 85% re-enrolment, and the cost overrun case on the $8M building.
- **Mission and legacy trustees** want the Halloran answer and a credible account of how the proceeds serve Brennock's children. Offer to have the board, not just you, decide how some of the proceeds are used, for example a named fund for financial aid or a Halloran scholarship.
- **Trustees with lower-school ties** want to know the little kids will be safe and happy. Show them the design of the lower-school building.

Also put a real alternative in front of the board, so the vote is a choice and not a referendum on you. Ask your finance lead to cost a version where you sell part of the north site, lease it, or sell and lease back, or one where you consolidate later while cutting costs now. If consolidation still wins against honest alternatives, the vote becomes much easier to win. If trustees see only your plan, the dissenters will invent alternatives and suspect you of hiding them.

Finally, check the $13.5M. Is it a fair price, and has anyone else been asked? A trustee who wonders whether the land was undersold, or sold to someone's friend, will vote no. A second appraisal or a brief marketing process, if the LOI allows it, protects you.

## Keeping lower-school parents

Their two stated worries are the commute and little kids sharing space with teenagers. Take both seriously, because they are the easiest to answer with design and policy.

- **Separation:** design the new building as a distinct lower school with its own entrance, its own drop-off, its own play area, and separate lunch and break times. Show parents the plans and let a few of them sit on the design committee. Spell out which spaces are shared (gym, library, performing arts) and when.
- **Older-student contact:** structured programs such as reading buddies can be a real selling point, but only if they are optional and supervised. Don't oversell them.
- **Commute:** the north-campus families live nearer to the north site. Find out where they actually live. Offer a bus or shuttle service, a staggered start time, and before- and after-care. Price these now and include them in the plan, because they cost money and the budget doesn't yet include them.
- **Outdoor learning:** the program is a reason many families chose the north campus. Commit to rebuilding it on the south campus, with the Ardelle Foundation's agreement, and describe what it will look like.
- **Tuition and loyalty:** consider a transition guarantee for current families, such as a locked tuition rate for the first year after the move or priority for siblings. It costs less than losing a few families.
- **Honest listening:** hold small sessions with parents, not only a town hall. Ask them what would make them stay. Some will leave regardless, so plan for a loss rate instead of promising none.

## Keeping staff

Teachers are worried about their jobs, and the worst thing you can do is leave that unanswered for months.

- **Say what you know.** Staffing for K-5 does not drop just because the buildings are combined: you still need the same class teachers. Where roles overlap (administration, facilities, front office), say so honestly and early.
- **Commit to a no-surprises process.** Promise at least a year's notice for any role changes, hiring from within first, and a severance and redeployment policy. Write it down and share it before the vote, not after.
- **Involve teachers in the design.** Lower-school teachers should help specify the new building. It makes the plan theirs, and they know what small children need.
- **Plan for retention.** Your best lower-school teachers are the ones with options elsewhere. Think about retention bonuses or recognition for the transition period.
- **Be careful with promises.** Don't promise "no layoffs" unless you can keep that promise in the downside scenario.

## What the plan should contain

1. **Legal and approvals:** deed opinion, Attorney General or court process if needed, Ardelle agreement, planning approval path, and Vellacott closing conditions tied to these.
2. **Financial case:** the five-year model, the do-nothing case, the downside case, and the use of proceeds (the $8M building, plus a reserve for the deficit, aid, and transition costs). Note that $13.5M minus $8M leaves $5.5M, and a $1.1M annual deficit uses that up faster than it looks. Be clear that the sale fixes the cash position but only consolidation savings and enrolment stability fix the operating deficit. Show how much the operating savings really are.
3. **Timeline:** fall announcement to families and staff, trustee meetings in October and November, legal and Ardelle answers by early January, contracts on January 10 with clear wording, vote February 12 or later if the facts aren't ready, re-enrolment results March 1, planning application, construction, move in fall 2028.
4. **Family plan:** design involvement, transport, care, tuition protection, outdoor learning.
5. **Staff plan:** job commitments, process, communication cadence.
6. **Neighbour plan:** early outreach, traffic and event commitments.
7. **Decision points:** conditions under which you pause or change course, such as a deed problem, loss of the Ardelle pledge, a planning refusal, or re-enrolment below your threshold.
8. **Communication:** one accurate account, told first to the people most affected, with regular updates. Don't let anyone learn about the plan from a rumour or from Vellacott's side.

## What I'd do this week

Instruct counsel on the Halloran deed and the 2004 planning approval, call the Ardelle Foundation, start one-on-one trustee conversations, and decide whether to announce to families in November. Those four steps will tell you whether February 12 is realistic, and they will tell you early if the plan needs to change.

Confidence: moderately high that the consolidation is financially sound. Lower on the vote and timeline until the deed, the pledge, and the planning conditions are checked, since each one could change the plan.
