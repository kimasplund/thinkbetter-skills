**Recommendation:** Don't take the February 12 vote on a plan that has three unchecked legal and funding gaps. Spend the next few weeks closing them, then bring the board a decision that is conditional on them. Holding the vote for the conditions to clear costs you little. A vote that passes and then unravels in court, at the planning board, or in the re-enrolment numbers costs you the school's credibility and probably the sale.

**Three things that can kill this, in order of danger**

1. **The Halloran gift.** The plaque says "for the education of the children of Brennock." If the 1958 deed or gift instrument restricts use, or if a court would read the gift as a charitable trust, selling to a developer for housing may not be yours to do. You may need court approval, the state attorney general's involvement, or the family's consent, and a reverter or heirs' claim is possible. Have counsel read the original instrument this month. Ask whether the sale is permitted, what approval it needs, and how long that takes. Also ask whether Vellacott's letter of intent is conditioned on clean title. If approval is needed, the trustees must know before they vote, and a Halloran descendant or a local reporter who finds the plaque first will frame this as "school sells charity land to a developer." Contact the family and the attorney general's office yourself, early, with a plan for how the proceeds will serve Brennock children.

2. **The Ardelle pledge.** $800k is still to come, and it is tied to the north-campus outdoor learning program. Closing the campus likely ends the purpose it was given for. Read the pledge agreement, then call the foundation before any announcement. Ask whether they would redirect the remaining money to an outdoor program on the south campus, which could be built into the new lower-school design. Foundations usually accept this if asked first and are angry if they learn it from the newsletter. Put the $800k as at risk in your base-case budget until they answer in writing.

3. **The Sycamore Lane conditions.** The 2004 approval was hard-won, and an $8M lower-school building is a new application. That puts the traffic marshals, the 9pm limit and the enrolment ceiling back in front of the same neighbours. You say you've never come close to the ceiling. Find the number. Moving 230 more students takes you from 410 to 640. The consultant says the site can hold 700, but the permit ceiling is what binds. If it sits below roughly 650, you need a variance before the plan works at all. Even if it doesn't, neighbours will fight added drop-off traffic from 230 small children. Meet the Sycamore Lane group before the planners do. Offer concrete things such as staggered lower-school start times, an on-site drop-off loop, and a named contact. Whether Vellacott's offer survives a long approval process is a separate question, so check whether their LOI has an outside date.

**The timing problem inside your own plan**

Contracts go out January 10 and are due March 1, with the vote on February 12 in the middle. Families will decide whether to re-enrol before they know whether their school is closing, or while a vote is pending. North-campus families are the group most likely to leave, and your budget assumes 92% return. Each point is about 6 students, or around $0.2 million. You can't hide the proposal until after March 1, because that would cost you trust, and you shouldn't send contracts that look routine when you know the board may vote to close the campus.

Options:
- **Move the board vote earlier**, to before January 10, so families re-enrol knowing the answer. This is cleanest but compresses your legal checks, so it only works if item 1 clears quickly.
- **Keep February 12 but tell families now** that a proposal is under consideration, give them the date, and add a no-penalty clause or a deadline extension for north families if the vote passes.
- Either way, model the budget at 85%, 88% and 92% return, and tell trustees what the gap does to the deficit. I'd expect some trustees to vote no if they see only the 92% case.

**Winning ten of fifteen**

You need two-thirds, so you can lose five. Count trustees now and sort them into firm yes, persuadable and firm no. The persuadable ones will usually be worried about one of these: the Halloran gift, a lawsuit, or the families who leave. Give each of them a specific answer before the meeting. Don't present the choice as close-or-fail. A plausible alternative to close is a smaller north campus that keeps K–2 or a pre-K. Show the board that you tested it and what it costs, because trustees who feel they were handed one option tend to look for the flaw in it. Ask the board chair to help line up votes. Hold a trustee-only session on the legal findings before the public discussion.

**What to give each group so they stay with you**

- **Lower-school parents** worry about the commute and little children sharing a site with teenagers. Answer both in the design, not in a letter. A separate lower-school entrance, its own play area and schedule, and separated lunch and recess times address the second concern. For the commute, offer a bus or shuttle run from the north site for at least the first two years, with a fixed budget. Form a parent design committee with real input on the building, and publish what it decided.
- **Teachers and staff** worry about jobs. Say clearly what changes: which roles merge and which don't. If you can promise that no one is laid off for the first year because of the move, and that attrition covers the reductions, say so only after the budget confirms it. Offer north-campus teachers first choice of lower-school roles on the new site and put them on the design committee. If cuts are likely, say so now. Staff who hear it in rumours leave first, and the best ones leave earliest.
- **Neighbours of both campuses** are covered above. Add the north site's neighbours, who will see a housing development replace a school, and who have no say unless Vellacott seeks rezoning.
- **Vellacott** has a signed letter of intent, which is not a contract. Ask for a longer due-diligence period, a closing tied to title clearance and planning, and a clause to cover the case where the court or attorney general takes months. If $13.5M depends on a rezoning for housing, find out who carries that risk.

**What the plan should include**

1. A legal opinion on the Halloran instrument, with the approval path and timeline.
2. A written answer from the Ardelle Foundation on the $800k.
3. The exact permit ceiling and a traffic plan agreed in draft with Sycamore Lane.
4. A board vote date and a re-enrolment sequence that fit together, with 85/88/92% budget scenarios.
5. Sources and uses for the $13.5M, showing the $8M building and the reserve, after sale costs, tax and legal fees.
6. A commitments page for families and staff covering transport, the lower-school design, staffing, and the first-year guarantees, with a named person responsible for each.
7. A fallback if the sale or approvals slip, such as bridging funds or a staged move, so a delay doesn't leave the deficit unaddressed.

**What would change this advice**

If counsel finds that the Halloran gift carries no restriction and the Ardelle agreement lets the money move, the case for pressing ahead on February 12 gets much stronger, and your main task becomes the parents and the January timeline. If counsel finds a binding restriction, the north site may not be sellable on this schedule at all, and you should be looking at partial options, such as leasing part of the site or keeping a smaller programme there. The cheapest first check is a one-hour call with your school's lawyer and the pledge letter on the table. Do that this week, before you do anything else.
