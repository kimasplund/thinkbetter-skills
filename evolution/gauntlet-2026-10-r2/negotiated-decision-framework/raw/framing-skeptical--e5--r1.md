Your doubts are well placed. The financial case is sound, but I count four things that can sink the vote or the sale, and three of them aren't about persuading anyone. Fix those first, because they decide whether you can get to ten votes.

## 1. Four risks that could kill this

**The Halloran gift.** "For the education of the children of Brennock" on a plaque may be only sentiment, or it may be a deed restriction or a gift condition. Your lawyer needs to read the 1958 instrument now. If there is a restriction or reverter, you may need the Halloran heirs' consent or a court or attorney general process before you can sell. Vellacott's LOI almost certainly depends on clean title. Even if the deed is clear, trustees and the public will treat the plaque as a moral claim. Find out the legal answer first, then decide how you will handle the moral one.

**The Ardelle pledge.** $800k is still to come, and it is tied to the north-campus outdoor learning program. Closing the north campus may let Ardelle stop paying, or even ask for money back. Call them before the board votes, not after. The best result is a written agreement to redirect the pledge to an equivalent program on the south campus. If you can't get that, subtract $800k from the $5.5M left after the building ($13.5M minus $8M) and show the board the lower number.

**The Sycamore Lane conditions.** You say the enrolment ceiling has never been a problem. But a school moving to 700 students on one site, with a lower school added, will run into it, and the neighbours remember the 2004 fight. Look up the exact ceiling, because it may be below the 700 your consultant says the site can hold. A new $8M building will almost certainly need planning approval, which gives those neighbours a hearing. Traffic marshals and the 9pm limit also have to work with 640 students arriving at one gate. Expect opposition from the neighbours. Meet them early and propose a traffic plan before anyone is forced to object.

**The sequencing.** Re-enrolment contracts go out January 10 and are due March 1, but the board doesn't vote until February 12. Families will be deciding whether to re-enrol before they know the outcome, and during the two weeks after the vote they will be deciding with the news fresh. Your 92% return assumption is the number most likely to fail. A one-point miss at your tuition level is probably a six-figure hole, and a closure that triggers the lower-school exodus could cost far more. Model 88% and 85% before the vote, so the board sees what the plan costs if it goes badly.

## 2. Winning the vote

You need ten of 15, so you can lose five. Count the votes now, trustee by trustee, as firm yes, leaning yes, undecided, or no, and know why each undecided one is hesitating. The 2/3 threshold means a determined group of six can block this, so find out who they are early and talk to them first.

Trustees tend to hold back for three reasons. Some are worried about the legacy and the plaque. Some are worried about the money and want proof that selling is better than doing nothing. Some are worried about the families. You can answer each:

- **Legacy.** Propose a lasting way to honour the Halloran gift. Examples are naming the new lower school for the family, keeping part of the outdoor learning program, or putting the plaque at the new building. Invite the family to the conversation, with your lawyer's advice, before they hear about it from someone else.
- **Money.** Put the alternatives next to the sale. The comparison should show doing nothing at $1.1M a year (about 10 years of reserves, or whatever your real number is), a smaller cut, and leasing or partial sale of the north site. Trustees who see that closure is the only option that fixes the deficit are likelier to vote for it. If a partial option clearly fails, say so; if it doesn't, you need to know that before February.
- **Families.** Bring a concrete answer to the commute and age-mixing worries, described in section 3.

Also consider asking the board to vote in two steps. First vote to authorise due diligence on title, Ardelle, and planning, with a hard date. Then hold the sale vote once those are known. Trustees who are unsure can say yes to the first step and the full vote becomes easier. If the LOI deadline doesn't allow this, ask Vellacott for an extension now; a buyer who has signed an LOI usually wants the deal done right.

## 3. Keeping parents and staff

**Lower-school parents.** The commute and the mix of small children with teenagers are two separate worries, and the second is the one you can fix by design. A separate lower-school building (which your $8M pays for) with its own entrance, drop-off, playground and schedule gives you something concrete to show. Publish the plan with drawings, bell times that stagger the two groups, supervision ratios, and a commitment to a parent design group. For the commute, offer bus routes for the first two years and start a transport working group with real parents on it. Parents who help design the building are much less likely to leave than parents who are only told about it.

**Staff.** Teachers are afraid for their jobs, so address that directly and early. If you can say that no teacher loses a job because of the move, say it in writing. If you can't, say what you can promise: a hiring freeze and attrition first, redeployment to the south campus, a retention bonus for those who stay through the move, and a clear date by which everyone will know where they stand. Tell staff before parents do, and before any leak.

**Timing of communication.** Announce the proposal to staff and families before January 10, once the Ardelle and title checks are done, so contracts don't go out on rumours. Tell people honestly that the vote isn't taken. People take a decision more calmly if they've seen the reasoning and been asked for input than if it lands as a fait accompli.

## 4. What the plan should include

1. **Legal and funding gates, by early December:** Halloran instrument reviewed, Ardelle agreement in writing, planning conditions and approval path confirmed, Vellacott extension and conditions clarified.
2. **A financial model the board can test:** sale proceeds less building cost, less Ardelle risk, less transition costs; annual savings from running one campus; and re-enrolment at 92%, 88% and 85%. Show when the deficit closes under each.
3. **The alternatives comparison** described above.
4. **Lower-school design and transport commitments**, with the parent group named.
5. **A staff commitment** with dates.
6. **Neighbour and planning engagement** before anything is filed.
7. **A legacy plan for the Halloran gift.**
8. **A communication calendar** keyed to January 10, February 12 and March 1, with a named owner for each audience.
9. **Early warning measures:** track intent-to-return weekly from January 10, and set a trigger in advance (for example, below 88% by mid-February) for what the board will do, including delaying the closure date.
10. **A fallback.** If the Halloran or Ardelle checks go badly, what is plan B? Having one makes the board more willing to proceed.

## Where I'd start this week

Get the 1958 deed and the Ardelle grant terms into your lawyer's and development director's hands, and do a private vote count with your board chair. These three things will tell you within days whether this plan can fly, and everything else on the list depends on them. I've assumed the contracts in question are for the year before closure; if they cover fall 2028 itself, the timing risk is smaller but the commitment ask is bigger, so check which it is.
