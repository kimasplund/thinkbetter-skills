The plan has a sound core: a $1.1M deficit, 11% enrolment decline and two campuses nine kilometres apart is a real structural problem, and $13.5M for the north site is a serious offer. But I would not go into a February 12 vote, or send re-enrolment contracts on January 10, until four things are settled. Each could stop the sale outright, and each is cheaper to learn now than in February.

## Four things that can sink the plan

**1. The Halloran gift.** The plaque says the land was given "for the education of the children of Brennock." That may be only a dedication. It may also be a deed restriction, a condition subsequent, or a reverter clause, and selling to a homebuilder is the use most likely to trigger one. Have counsel pull the 1958 deed and any related gift documents. Ask whether the land can be sold at all, whether the Hallorans' heirs hold any rights, and whether the state attorney general or a court must approve a sale of charitable property (a cy-pres or similar proceeding). Vellacott's letter of intent should be contingent on clear title. If there is a restriction, a negotiated release with the family, or a different deal structure such as a long lease, may be the only route. If there isn't, you still want a written legal opinion in the board packet. Several trustees will ask about the plaque, and so will the local press.

**2. The Ardelle pledge.** $800k is still to come, and it is for the north-campus outdoor learning program. Closing that campus probably puts the remaining payments at risk, and it may oblige you to return what has been paid, depending on the pledge agreement. Read the agreement and call the foundation before you announce anything. They should hear this from you first. They may be willing to redirect the grant to a south-campus program, but only if you ask and they agree in writing. Until then, treat the $800k as unavailable in your numbers.

**3. The south campus is not cleared for 700 students.** Your consultant is describing physical capacity. The binding limit is the enrolment ceiling in the 2004 planning-board conditions, and you only know it hasn't been a constraint so far. Find the actual number. Going from 410 to about 640 students, plus a new $8M building, will almost certainly need new approvals or a modification of those conditions. That means going back to the Sycamore Lane neighbours, who fought the gym and who have the traffic marshals and the 9pm cutoff in writing. Expect them to ask for more: drop-off redesign, traffic study, and a limit on construction hours. Find out now whether the conditions allow 640 and what approval the new building needs. A conditional approval or a refusal after you have sold the north site is the worst outcome in this whole plan.

**4. The money is tighter than $13.5M minus $8M.** Take off brokerage and legal fees, any taxes, site-prep and entitlement costs on the south campus, moving and transition costs, severance or retention for staff, and cost overruns on a building that won't open before fall 2028. Then add what the merger actually saves: one campus's running costs, duplicated roles, transport. Build a model with a realistic and a downside case. The remaining "breathing room" may be several million dollars, and a $1.1M annual deficit uses it quickly if enrolment keeps falling. Note also that if lower-school families leave over this, the deficit gets worse, not better (see below).

## The calendar works against you

- Contracts go out January 10 and are due March 1. The board votes February 12. So families must decide whether to re-enrol for 2027-28 before they know whether the north campus will close, or, if you announce the plan first, while the vote is still open. Either way you ask them to commit to something you can't yet describe. Decide in advance what the contract says. Closure is for fall 2028, so 2027-28 is the north campus's last year if you proceed. Tell families that clearly and early. A surprise after they've signed will cost you trust and probably refunds.
- Your 92% return assumption is the budget's weakest point. North families are upset about the commute and about mixing young children with teenagers. Some of them will use this moment to leave for another school, which would hurt exactly where you can least afford it. Model 85% and 88% for the lower school specifically, and decide what the board does if March 1 comes in below your number. A sale that the board approves, followed by a re-enrolment shortfall, leaves you with less money and fewer students.
- The vote needs ten of fifteen. You can lose with six no votes or abstentions. Count trustees now, one by one, rather than assuming the majority is there.

## How to win the vote honestly

Trustees mostly don't vote against a plan because they dislike it. They vote against it because they feel unready to defend it. Your goal is a board that can explain the decision to parents.

1. **Start with the trustees who are uneasy.** Meet each one privately in the next few weeks and ask what would make them comfortable. Find out who is attached to the north campus, who worries about the Halloran family, and who is worried about the money. Their concerns are your agenda.
2. **Give the board the real alternatives.** If the only options are "close north" and "do nothing," the vote becomes a referendum on the status quo, and doubters will pick the status quo. Show what else was considered: leasing part of the north site, selling a portion, keeping the lower school there and merging only grades 6-12 functions, a smaller south campus program, or a phased closure. If you still prefer the full closure, you will be able to say why, and trustees will see you as someone who tested the idea.
3. **Make the decision in stages.** The board could approve proceeding with the sale subject to conditions: clear title, the Ardelle position, planning approval or a written view from the town, and a re-enrolment threshold. This can be easier to pass than an unconditional sale, and it protects you from the risks above. The Vellacott LOI should allow for those conditions and a long enough due-diligence period.
4. **Bring independent advice.** An outside legal opinion on the Halloran gift, a financial model reviewed by someone other than staff, and the planning consultant's written view will carry weight with trustees that your enthusiasm cannot.

## Keeping parents and staff

**Lower-school parents.** Their two objections are different, and both are reasonable.
- The commute: nine kilometres each way. Offer something concrete, such as a bus or van service for the first years, adjusted start times, and an extended-care option. Ask a parent group to help design it before it is announced.
- Little children on a teenage campus: this is the more serious concern. Describe the design before the vote: a separate lower-school building (the $8M project) with its own entrance, drop-off and play area, supervised transitions, and rules on shared spaces. Let a group of north parents sit on the design committee and visit schools that run this way. If you can't give them something specific, expect them to leave.
- Say plainly what is being lost. The north campus matters to them. A farewell year, a role for the outdoor learning program on the south site, and honest acknowledgement of the Halloran gift will help more than reassurance.

**Teachers and staff.** Jobs are the question, and silence is worse than a hard answer.
- Say as soon as you can what the staffing plan is. If no one will be let go, say so and put it in writing. If some roles will go, say how many, how they will be chosen, and what severance and help you will offer.
- Offer retention agreements for key lower-school teachers through the move, and involve them in designing the new building and the program. They know what young children need better than the architects do.
- Hold open sessions where the answers are given by you, not by a communications office.

**Neighbours at Sycamore Lane.** Approach them before the news reaches them, with a draft traffic and construction plan and an offer to talk. They have a record of winning, and a neighbour group that learns of this from a notice will oppose it. One that was consulted may only negotiate.

## What the plan should include

1. **Legal and title:** Halloran deed review and opinion, attorney general position if needed, Vellacott LOI conditions and timeline.
2. **Donors:** Ardelle conversation and written outcome; a plan for other major donors, who may take the closure as a signal.
3. **Approvals:** the exact planning-board ceiling and conditions; the application path for the new building and a larger enrolment; a neighbour engagement plan.
4. **Financial model:** sources and uses, realistic and downside cases, break-even enrolment, contingency for construction overruns, and what happens if the sale falls through or approvals are delayed.
5. **Enrolment strategy:** the contract language for January 10, a re-enrolment target by grade, weekly tracking from January 10 to March 1, and a defined response if the numbers fall short.
6. **Family plan:** transport, extended care, lower-school design committee, farewell year, tours of the new building plans.
7. **Staff plan:** staffing commitments, retention agreements, timing, severance if relevant.
8. **Board process:** one-to-one meetings, alternatives paper, staged resolution, vote count, and a plan for what you do if you are at nine votes on February 11.
9. **Communications calendar:** who hears what and in what order. Order matters here: board, then Ardelle and major donors, then staff, then parents, then neighbours and press, within a short window so nothing leaks.
10. **Timeline with decision points:** a clear point at which you stop or change course if title, approvals or re-enrolment don't work out.

## My honest view

The idea of consolidating may well be right. But the plan as it stands asks the board to approve a sale before it knows whether it can legally sell, whether the south campus can legally hold the students, or whether the money lasts. Bringing those answers to the board, along with a credible plan for families and staff, will win more votes than enthusiasm will. If the answers come back clean, you can present February 12 as a decision made with open eyes. If one of them doesn't, you will have found out while you could still change the plan.
