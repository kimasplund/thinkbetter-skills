**Recommendation:** Don't take the sale to a vote on February 12 as it stands. Take a conditional sale, and clear three things before the vote. Any one of them can sink the plan after you've won the ten votes. The consolidation logic is sound. The risk is in what the plan assumes.

## The three things to clear first

**1. The Halloran gift.** The plaque says the land was given "for the education of the children of Brennock." If the 1958 gift carries a restriction, a reverter or a charitable-purpose condition, selling to a homebuilder could be challenged by the Halloran heirs, the attorney general or a trustee. At best, the sale would need court approval. Vellacott's lawyers will find this in title work anyway.
- Before any trustee is asked to vote, get the original deed and gift documents and a written opinion from counsel.
- Ask counsel whether the sale needs court or attorney-general approval, and how long that takes.
- Ask whether the Halloran heirs should be approached early. Learning about this from a lawyer's letter would be far worse than hearing it from you.
- Make the Vellacott contract conditional on clear title and on any approval, with a long enough closing window.

**2. The Ardelle pledge.** The $800k still to come is for the north-campus outdoor learning program, and closing the campus ends that program. Read the pledge agreement for whether the money can be redirected or must be returned. Then call the foundation yourself, before the board sees the plan. A foundation that hears second-hand that its program is being closed may stop paying. If it will redirect the money to an outdoor program on the south campus, the pledge becomes part of the plan and trustees can see it. If it won't, subtract $800k from your financial case.

**3. The 2004 planning conditions.** You say the enrolment ceiling is one you've "never come close to," but your consultant says the campus can hold 700. Those are two different numbers.
- Find the exact ceiling in the approval. If it is below roughly 650 (the 410 you have now plus the 230 from the north, plus some growth), the plan needs a variance.
- Adding an $8M building will likely need new approvals anyway. That reopens the whole approval with the Sycamore Lane neighbours, who fought hard in 2004 and remember it.
- Plan for the traffic marshals and the 9pm limit to be tested too. K–5 drop-off at a secondary campus is a very different traffic pattern.
- Have a land-use lawyer tell you how long approval would take and what it could cost. Then decide if fall 2028 is realistic.

## Why the vote timing is a problem

The board votes February 12, in the middle of the window when re-enrolment contracts are out (January 10) and due (March 1). Your budget assumes 92% of families return. A leak or an announcement during that window gives lower-school families a reason to leave before they have seen any plan. The closure isn't until fall 2028, but families choose now. Every point below 92% is about $190k of revenue, assuming about $30k per student, which is roughly what $19M over 640 students implies. Check that against your real tuition. A 5-point shortfall would cost you around $1M, nearly the whole deficit you are trying to fix.

Options, in order of preference:
- **Move the vote.** Hold it after March 1, or before January 10 with the plan fully worked out. A vote with unresolved questions followed by a contract season is the worst combination.
- **If the date stays, vote on a smaller decision.** Authorise exploration and a conditional sale subject to title, approvals and the Ardelle answer, with a final vote once those are known. This is easier for wavering trustees to support, and it keeps the closure decision itself for later.
- **Tell families before the contracts go out.** A parent who gets a plan with dates and protections from you will react better than one who hears about it from other parents. Include a fall 2027 re-enrolment guarantee: north stays open that year, and families get priority on places and a fee lock.

## Winning ten of fifteen votes

You need two-thirds, so five trustees can block. Count them before February, one by one, rather than in the meeting.
- **Trustees who want the money and the stability:** they respond to the financial picture. Give them a full model: sale proceeds net of fees and tax, $8M for the building, the Ardelle outcome, higher costs from the transition, and what the enrolment decline does if you do nothing. $13.5M minus $8M leaves $5.5M, but only if the building costs $8M and stays on budget. Show the downside case with 85% re-enrolment.
- **Trustees attached to the north campus, the Halloran legacy or the community:** they won't be moved by finance. Take the gift question seriously, in public. A real answer from counsel and a plan for honouring the gift's purpose is what they want. That could be a named building or garden on the south campus, a community use agreement, or a fund in the family's name. Ask them to help design it rather than defend the sale.
- **Trustees who are parents of lower-school children:** they'll be getting calls. They need the family and staff protections below to be ready before they vote.
- **Offer the no-voters something real.** If there are doubters, ask what they would need to see to vote yes. Put the conditions into the resolution and don't just promise them.

Also look at the Vellacott letter of intent. It is not a binding contract. Ask whether another buyer, a conservation or institutional use, or a lease structure would give the community a better outcome. You do not have to have a competing bid, but trustees will ask whether you tested the market, and a better answer than "we got one offer" will help.

## Keeping parents with you

Lower-school parents have two concerns, the commute and little children sharing a campus with teenagers. Treat them as separate problems.
- **Commute:** the nine kilometres is real. Fund a bus route with the south campus or a shuttle for the first two years, extended drop-off and pick-up hours, and a transport fee waiver for families with a child under 8. Cost it before you offer it. Traffic marshals are required under the 2004 conditions anyway.
- **Safety and age mix:** show a design in which the lower school is a physically separate building with its own entrance, play area, and separate break and lunch times. The $8M building is your best argument, so show the plans or a model early. Offer parents a visit to a school that runs this way. Put two or three lower-school parents on the design committee.
- **A specific date for the final decision on each commitment.** Families respond to dates and written promises, not reassurance.

## Keeping staff with you

Teachers are worried about their jobs. Say plainly whether any positions are being cut. Merging two campuses with a smaller combined headcount usually means some roles overlap, but the plan can avoid layoffs if you manage it through attrition and hold the intake of new hires.
- State the staffing number the plan assumes, and whether it involves any redundancies. If it does, say how they would be decided and what support is offered.
- Promise a placement for every north-campus teacher in good standing, in a role for which they are qualified, for the first transition year.
- Tell staff before families, and give them a direct line to a named person.
- Involve lower-school teachers in designing the new building. They know what the space needs.
- Retention matters: if your best lower-school teachers leave during the uncertainty, families notice and follow.

## What the plan should contain

- Legal: gift opinion, title and approvals conditions in the sale contract, a planning pathway for the 2004 conditions and the new building.
- Money: a net-proceeds model, an Ardelle decision, a re-enrolment sensitivity (92%, 88%, 85%), and a contingency for building cost overruns. Set a floor, such as the minimum re-enrolment at which the sale proceeds.
- Timeline: vote date, contract date, family communication, approvals, construction, a move-in date. Don't commit publicly to fall 2028 before approvals are scoped. If the building slips, the lower school needs a bridge plan, such as temporary classrooms or another year at north.
- People: a staff guarantee, a family transition package, and a named owner for each.
- Neighbours: meet the Sycamore Lane neighbours early, on your terms, and offer them something concrete, such as traffic management or a hotline. Their support or opposition will determine the schedule.
- Community: a plan for honouring the Halloran gift's purpose.
- Fallback: if title, approvals or re-enrolment fail, what is the alternative? Trustees will be more willing to vote yes if they can see an exit.

## What would change this recommendation

If counsel finds the gift has no enforceable restriction, the 2004 ceiling comfortably exceeds 700 and the foundation agrees to move the pledge, then the plan is much stronger, and you can hold the vote as scheduled as long as you protect re-enrolment. If any of the three come back badly, the sale as planned doesn't survive, and you should look at a smaller change, such as leasing part of the north site or merging grades, rather than full closure. The cheapest first check is the deed and the 2004 approval document. Both are probably in your files, and reading them takes a day.
