**Short answer:** Don't recommend "let Harrowfield go" on November 20 as a finished plan. Recommend a conditional path: pursue Ironvale and Sorrelwood, but make approval depend on clearing four gates first. Several things in your brief mean you do need something from Harrowfield and from others, and the economics are less certain than "30% more rent."

## Why "we don't need anything from Harrowfield" is probably wrong

1. **Co-tenancy in the 26 inline leases.** In 2015 you filled the shops by offering grocery co-tenancy protections. If those clauses are keyed to a grocery anchor (or to Harrowfield by name, or to a minimum-GLA "anchor" operating), replacing the grocer with a gym and a clinic could trigger rent abatements (often 50% rent or percentage rent only), and after a cure period, termination rights. That could cost far more than the 30% uplift. Read every clause before the committee meets. Look at the definition of anchor/major tenant, the cure period, the remedies, and whether a replacement "of comparable quality" or a "substitute use" is allowed. Also check whether a go-dark or lease expiry already triggers them, because Harrowfield leaving at all may trigger them regardless of what you do.
2. **The fuel pad and easement.** Harrowfield owns the pad outright and holds a recorded reciprocal easement over your entrances and parking. Check what it says about:
   - parking ratios and protected spaces,
   - access and sight lines,
   - use restrictions or consent rights over changes to the centre,
   - whether the easement runs with the pad regardless of the grocery lease.

   Harrowfield will keep this position after the lease ends. A gym open 24 hours and a medical clinic change traffic, parking and hours. If the easement gives Harrowfield consent or veto rights, or a parking guarantee, it has leverage. Even if it doesn't, a departing grocer that is also your neighbour can be difficult for years.
3. **The loyalty card.** The 10 cents a litre fuel discount ties fuel sales to in-store spend. If the store leaves, the station loses its draw, and the station owner may then want to redevelop, sell, or change its use, or may push for terms on the shared lot. Find out whether it has a reason to be hostile or to cooperate. This also affects how you negotiate: a grocer that wants to keep its fuel business may be more flexible than its "40% off" ask suggests.
4. **Marchbank's consent.** Both new leases exceed 15,000 sq ft (22,000 and 16,000), so each needs sign-off. "Routine" was true when you were re-leasing to a grocer. Lenders look at tenant credit, lease term, co-tenancy exposure, and whether the change to the income stream affects debt coverage. A gym and a clinic that are new to the centre, with a risky vacancy period, may get closer scrutiny. Approach Marchbank early and informally, and don't sign leases subject to a consent you haven't previewed.

## Check the economics before you rely on 30%

- Is it 30% more base rent, net, or gross? Compare on a net effective basis after free rent, tenant improvement allowances, landlord work, leasing commissions and downtime.
- Splitting the 38,000 sq ft box and demising it is expensive. Add new utilities, fire separation, a new storefront and possibly structural work. Imaging needs radiation shielding, floor loading and specialist mechanical systems. Urgent care needs plumbing, medical gas and waste handling.
- Letters of intent are not binding. Compare the credit and track record of Ironvale and Sorrelwood against Harrowfield's. Ask for guarantees, security deposits and a build-out commitment.
- Compare a realistic downside: both deals slip and the box sits dark while the inline shops claim co-tenancy relief.
- Compare the alternative: a negotiated renewal with Harrowfield at a smaller rent cut, a shorter term, a reduced footprint, or a right to recapture part of the box. A 40% cut on a failing store may still be better than a dark box, or may not. Run the numbers.

## Operational and legal checks

- **Parking:** "Rarely half full" is true for a grocery's current pattern. A 24-hour gym peaks early morning and evening, and a clinic is busy through the day, so the mix is different. Do a peak-hour count and check the zoning or parking bylaw ratios and the easement terms. 610 stalls is probably adequate but verify it.
- **Use and exclusives:** Check existing exclusives and prohibited uses in inline leases (for example a fitness, health or pharmacy exclusive), and whether the new uses need zoning, licensing or regulatory approval (for imaging and urgent care, for example).
- **24-hour gym:** Noise, lighting and security overnight, and the effect on neighbours, including the fuel station.
- **Timing:** The lease ends next December. Confirm the expiry date, any renewal option, the notice dates, and the surrender or restoration obligations. Check whether Harrowfield can go dark early.

## How to bring everyone along

- **Inline tenants:** Don't surprise them. If the review shows co-tenancy risk, the choice is to negotiate waivers or amendments in return for something they value (a rent concession, a marketing fund, a better lease term), or accept the abatement cost. Present the gym and clinic as traffic generators with data: visits per week, hours, the number of customers. Meet the largest and most exposed first. Be careful about what you disclose before the leases are signed and confidential terms are protected.
- **Ironvale and Sorrelwood:** Be honest that the LOIs are conditional on lender consent and on third-party approvals. Agree timelines, a mutual exclusivity period, and who pays for what if the deal fails. Coordinate the demising, parking and construction so the two operators don't conflict.
- **Marchbank:** Send a package: tenant financials, lease terms, rent roll before and after, and the co-tenancy position. Ask what they need.
- **Harrowfield:** Treat it as a counterparty, not an obstacle. Open the discussion early and decide what you can offer (timing, exit terms, fuel-site cooperation) and what you need (a clean exit, an easement amendment on parking and access if required).
- **Investment committee:** Show the downside and the gates up front. A recommendation that names the risks and has conditions is easier to approve than one that discovers them later.
