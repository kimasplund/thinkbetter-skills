**Short answer:** Take the replacement plan to the committee, but as a conditional recommendation, not a clean "let Harrowfield go". The premise that you need nothing from Harrowfield is the weak point. Their lease runs out, but three other things they hold or trigger do not.

## Where "we don't need anything from them" breaks down

1. **The co-tenancy protections you gave the 26 inline shops in 2015.** If these are still in the leases, losing a grocery anchor (or letting the box go dark or be re-used for a non-grocery purpose) may let the inline tenants cut rent, pay percentage rent only, or terminate. A gym and a clinic almost certainly don't qualify as a replacement "grocery" under the usual wording. This is the biggest number in the decision. The 30% rent uplift on the box is worth about 0.7 times Harrowfield's current rent a year compared with their 40% cut. Set that against rent exposure across 26 shops. Until someone has read those clauses, you don't know which side is bigger.

2. **The fuel station and the recorded easement.** Harrowfield owns the pad outright and shares your entrances and parking by easement. Nothing in your brief says whether that easement limits what you can do with the lot: protected parking areas, restrictions on competing or non-retail uses, consent rights over changes to access or site layout. A 24-hour gym and a medical and imaging clinic will change parking patterns and circulation. Have counsel read the easement before the committee meets. Also expect Harrowfield to be a neighbour for as long as it owns the pad, whatever happens to the lease. A tenant that has just been pushed out and still controls your front entrance can make your life difficult through the easement, even if it isn't acting in bad faith.

3. **Traffic that the grocery generates.** The loyalty card ties fuel discounts to in-store spend, so Harrowfield drives visits that also feed the inline shops. A gym and clinic bring visits, but different ones: scheduled, short and rarely combined with shopping. Ask whether your inline tenants' sales depend on grocery trips. Their own sales reports will tell you.

4. **Your lease terms with Harrowfield.** Check for a renewal option, a right of first refusal or offer, a go-dark right, and use restrictions or exclusives that would limit what you can put in the box (pharmacy or health uses are common exclusives). Also check whether the "40% less" is a negotiating position or a floor.

## The interests, as I read them

- **You and Ashby Ridge:** higher income, a lower-risk rent roll, and a plan the committee and lender will accept. Keeping a weakening anchor is not a goal in itself.
- **Harrowfield:** sales are down, so it wants lower occupancy cost, and it wants to protect its fuel and loyalty business. Staying at Ellery may matter less to it than keeping the fuel pad working. That gives you room to trade.
- **Inline tenants:** protect their traffic and their co-tenancy rights. They will be most worried about the transition year and what fills the box.
- **Ironvale and Sorrelwood:** certainty on timing, parking and build-out. Both have only LOIs, so they can also walk. Neither is a sure thing.
- **Marchbank:** its consent is "routine" because the income has always been stable. Replacing an anchor with two new, shorter-credit tenants is a material change to the collateral, so do not assume it will be routine this time. Both new leases exceed 15,000 sq ft in aggregate, and the 22,000 sq ft one clearly does.

## What to recommend on November 20

Ask the committee to approve proceeding with the split-box plan **subject to four conditions**, with dates:

1. **Co-tenancy audit** of all 26 inline leases, with a figure for worst-case rent loss and termination exposure.
2. **Easement and Harrowfield lease review** by counsel, covering use limits, parking and access, and any renewal rights.
3. **Parking study** on peak demand: a gym's evening peak and a clinic's weekday daytime peak against your 610 stalls. "Rarely half full" today describes a grocery-led pattern; you need counts for the shared hours, including the fuel station's use of the same lot.
4. **Preliminary Marchbank sign-off**, ideally in writing before you sign either lease.

Keep these alternatives alive until the audit comes back, so the committee sees you have not locked in:

- **Split box, as proposed.** Highest income if co-tenancy exposure is low.
- **Smaller Harrowfield plus one new operator.** For example, a right-sized grocery of around 20,000 to 22,000 sq ft with Ironvale or Sorrelwood taking the rest. It keeps the traffic and may satisfy the inline protections. Harrowfield's rent falls, but total box income may still beat the status quo. This is worth testing if the audit finds large exposure.
- **Renew Harrowfield at a discount.** Last resort. It locks in a falling anchor, but it may be cheapest if co-tenancy exposure is large and the lender is uneasy.

Set a decision rule now: if worst-case inline exposure is above the extra rent from the split box (roughly 0.7 times Harrowfield's current rent per year, over a comparable period), go to the smaller-Harrowfield option instead.

## Bringing people along

- **Talk to Harrowfield first, privately and soon.** Don't let them hear it from the inline tenants. Ask what they actually need: the fuel business, the loyalty programme, a smaller footprint. If they want to leave, agree an orderly exit in return for cooperation on the easement, for example a joint plan for parking, signage and access that protects fuel sales. Keep the discussion about the pad and the lot, separate from the lease.
- **Inline tenants:** talk to them after the audit, not before, so you know what you owe them. Present a concrete transition plan: opening dates, marketing, what the gym and clinic bring (daily visitors, longer dwell times, a medical clientele). Offer something small and real for the disruption year, such as signage or marketing support, instead of promising uplift you can't prove. If some leases carry termination rights, speak to those tenants personally.
- **Ironvale and Sorrelwood:** convert the LOIs into conditional leases with dates tied to Harrowfield's expiry, and ask for their parking and hours requirements early so the study uses real figures. Note that Sorrelwood's imaging use may carry its own landlord obligations (building, vibration, shielding, signage); check them before you agree anything.
- **Marchbank:** brief them before the committee votes. Bring tenant credit information and a rent roll showing the post-change income.
- **The committee:** show the three options, the audit findings and the decision rule. A recommendation that says "here is what we know, here is what we're still checking and when we'll report back" is easier to approve than a clean story that a single clause can undo.
