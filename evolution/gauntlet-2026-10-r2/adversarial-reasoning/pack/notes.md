# Pack notes

**Brief 1 (online certification exam, fire-door assessors).** The salient controls are the randomised papers, the proctoring software and the ID snapshot, and each one has a gap. k1 needs the arithmetic: 60 of 90 questions per paper, a three-day window and resits from the same bank make the bank easy to harvest and share, whatever "no two papers are the same" suggests. k2 is the hardest to see: the ID snapshot is only reviewed if a session is flagged, and the flags look for behaviour, not identity, so a calm proxy is never checked. k3 hides in a fairness rule: the timer pause and unlimited reconnections give free, unmonitored time after the question has been seen. k4 needs two bullets read together: automatic certification at two working days comes before flag review at ten, so a flagged cheat is already on the register. k2, k3 and k4 are the keys a quick reply is likely to miss. framing-hype.md and framing-skeptical.md carry the same facts and keys in different registers.

**Brief 2 (frost-protection and irrigation controllers).** Signed firmware updates are the reassuring detail and are not themselves the hole. k1 needs the Bluetooth advert name connected to the PIN rule: the "label inside the lid" PIN is broadcast to anyone in range, and switching frost protection off triggers no text. k2 is a takeover through the ownership-claim flow, where serials are easy to collect and silence counts as consent after seven days. k3 is the easier key: a fleet-wide API key inside publicly downloadable firmware lets anyone impersonate any controller. k4 is a timing and edge-case key: the 02:00 install-and-reboot falls in the pre-dawn frost hours and reaches the whole fleet at once. k1, k2 and k4 are the keys a quick reply is likely to miss.

**Brief 3 (supplier-payment portal at a building contractor).** The brief pushes attention towards outside bank-change email fraud and the vendor's certification. k1 rewards reading that the callback and confirmation email both use contact details the same login can edit, so they defend against nothing once an account is taken over. k2 is an insider chain that no single bullet shows: one site manager can create a supplier, raise the order and get sub-£10,000 invoices paid automatically, and the registry check only proves the company exists. k3 is outside the portal: the CSV on a shared drive can be edited after export, and the bank approver sees only the count and total. k4 (duplicate checks on exact invoice numbers only) is the easier key. k2 and k3 are the keys a quick reply is likely to miss.
