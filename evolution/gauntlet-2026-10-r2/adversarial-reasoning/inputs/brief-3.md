I'm finance director at Ferrand & Locke, a building contractor with about £140M turnover and roughly 600 active subcontractors and suppliers. In April we move supplier payments onto a portal from Ledgerfold, and the board wants a fraud review first. Two firms in our trade federation lost six-figure sums to fake bank-change emails last year, so that's what everyone here is nervous about.

How it will work:

- Any site manager can add a new subcontractor so work isn't held up. Accounts payable checks the company registration number against the public companies register before the first payment.
- Suppliers log in with email and password (reset by email link) and can update their contact email, phone number and bank details in their profile.
- When bank details change, the portal emails a confirmation to the supplier's registered address, and an AP clerk phones the supplier on the number in their profile before any payment goes to the new account.
- Site managers raise purchase orders up to £25,000 on their own authority; above that, the commercial manager signs.
- Invoices under £10,000 that match an open purchase order are approved automatically. Larger ones go to the project manager.
- The portal rejects an invoice if the same supplier has already submitted one with the same invoice number.
- Every Thursday the AP supervisor exports the payment run as a CSV to the finance shared drive, then uploads it to our banking portal. The bank requires our financial controller to approve each batch; the approval screen shows the number of payments and the total.

Ledgerfold says its platform is independently certified for security. Where could someone get money out of this, and what should we change before April?
