# Pack notes

**Brief 1 (artisan bakery, wholesale supermarket contract).** The capacity numbers are spread across separate sentences: 2 x 4,500 = 9,000 a day, against 6,100 plus 4,200 = 10,300 needed, so "fits comfortably" is false (k1). This is hard to spot because the memo states the conclusion confidently, and a quick reader checks the margin arithmetic, which is correct, instead. The owner's actual question was about protecting existing customers, but the memo concludes on profit (k2). Once k1 is found, the true answer to the real question turns negative. The risk paragraph is circular: commitment proves solidity, and solidity makes the commitment protective (k3). The track record relies on two visible successes and no heard-of failures (k4, survivorship). The framing variants keep every number, name, deadline and claim, and change only the register.

**Brief 2 (city transport committee, bus lane extension).** The "12 weeks after" window cannot exist between 3 February and 31 March, which is about 8 weeks (k1). It is easy to miss because the dates sit in one sentence and the headline 14% looks clean. The analysis evaluates Varrow Road, but the decision is about Kessel Street, whose distinguishing facts (one lane each way, 14 loading bays) appear only in the user's framing and are never addressed (k2). Shopkeeper comments are dismissed while driver comments are counted (k3, confirmation bias). This matters more once k2 is seen, because loading access is Kessel's main issue. Ridership and speed are each used to support the other (k4). The under-budget line is a correct distractor.

**Brief 3 (grain cooperative, dropping a seed variety).** 610 of 2,400 hectares is 25%, not the 19% the paper gives (k1). The coincidence with "19 members" makes the wrong figure look familiar, and the error only shows if you do the division. The board asked about members' income, and the paper answers yield per hectare (k2). The premium, costs and what growers would switch to are left out. Weak demand is argued from niche status, which is itself explained by weak demand, while the £38 premium (counter-evidence) is dismissed (k3). A single vivid 2022 storm loss stands in for a risk base rate (k4, availability). The yield arithmetic (610 x 4.1 = 2,500) is correct, to reward checking rather than suspicion.

**Negative.** This is a one-line arithmetic request (3,000 x £0.35 x 7 = £7,350). The right reply is short and direct, with no review apparatus.
