I would not circulate it as written. The journey-time result looks real, but the recommendation says more than the evidence supports, and the one group the analysis dismisses is the group most affected on Kessel Street. Below is a version that holds up, then what I changed and why.

**Corrected version of the findings**

**Varrow Road bus lane pilot: findings**

The lane opened on 3 February. We compared the weeks before it opened with the weeks after, using route 7 GPS data up to 31 March. That gives about 8 weeks of "after" data, not 12, so the comparison is either 8 weeks against 8 weeks or 12 weeks against 8, and the report should say which.

Average route 7 journey time fell from 35 minutes to 30, about 14%. The "before" period was in winter and the "after" period was late winter into spring, and there was no comparison route, so we cannot say how much of the fall is due to the lane.

Route 7 ridership rose 9% over the same period. This is consistent with faster journeys attracting riders, but it does not show it. Ridership moves with season, weather, fares, school terms and service changes, and the analysis does not rule any of those out.

We received written comments from 38 bus drivers, who were overwhelmingly positive. We also received 61 comments from Varrow Road shopkeepers about delivery access. Those comments were set aside and have not been analysed. They need to be read and summarised alongside the driver comments.

The pilot cost £410,000 against a budget of £450,000. This tells us about delivery of the pilot, not whether the lane was worth having.

Recommendation: the pilot shows a clear journey-time gain for route 7, with the size of the lane's own effect still uncertain. It does not yet show that a lane would work on Kessel Street. The committee should either defer the decision until the gaps below are closed, or approve only a Kessel trial with agreed measures of success, including delivery access.

**What I changed**

- **Date mismatch.** A lane opening on 3 February cannot have 12 weeks of data by 31 March. It has about 8. The 14% figure rests on a shorter and different "after" period than stated.
- **Ridership "confirms" the cause.** The text uses the ridership rise to show that faster journeys drew riders, but the only evidence for that is the rise itself. I downgraded it to "consistent with" and listed the other explanations.
- **Before/after with no comparison.** Winter against late winter and spring, with no other route measured, means the 14% could be partly seasonal or due to other changes. I now say so, rather than attributing all of it to the lane.
- **Drivers counted, shopkeepers dismissed.** 38 comments from one group were treated as evidence and 61 from another as "anecdotal", though both are written comments of the same kind. Drivers benefit directly from the lane, and shopkeepers bear its cost. Kessel has 14 loading bays used by its shops, so delivery access is exactly the issue the committee has to decide. I put the shopkeeper comments back in and asked for them to be analysed.
- **"Under budget" used as proof it worked.** A pilot can cost less than planned and still fail, or the reverse. I limited that line to what it shows.
- **Varrow Road to Kessel Street.** The recommendation assumes the two roads are alike. Kessel is one lane each way with 14 loading bays and two routes (22 and 31). The analysis never says what Varrow Road looks like on any of those points, so the jump to Kessel is an assumption.

**Still unresolved, and the cheapest way to check**

- **How much of the 14% is the lane.** Compare route 7 with a similar route that has no lane over the same dates, or with the same weeks in the previous year. Both are quick if the GPS data exists.
- **Whether the ridership rise came from the lane.** Check the same period last year and any changes to service or fares. Ticketing data already held should be enough.
- **What the shopkeepers said.** Someone should read the 61 comments and sort them by theme: loading, parking, lost trade and so on. It is a few hours' work and could change the decision.
- **How Varrow Road compares with Kessel.** Ask Mara for Varrow Road's lane count, loading provision and number of routes, and for any effect on general traffic and on other roads. Count how many deliveries on Kessel now depend on kerbside bays at peak times.
- **Reliability.** Averages hide variation. Journey-time spread before and after would show whether the lane made buses more dependable.

If you want a single line for members: route 7 got faster after the lane opened, but the report does not yet show how much was down to the lane, what it did to the shops, or whether Kessel will behave the same way.
