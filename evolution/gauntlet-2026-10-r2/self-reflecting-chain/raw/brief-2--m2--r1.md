I wouldn't circulate it as it stands. The arithmetic is fine, but the evidence doesn't support the conclusion, and the weakest points are the ones that matter most for Kessel Street.

**What holds up**
- 35 to 30 minutes is a 14.3% fall, so "14%" is correct.
- £410,000 against £450,000 is under budget, by about 9%.

**Problems to fix before circulating**

1. **The comparison window doesn't add up.** The lane opened on 3 February, and 12 weeks after that runs to about 28 April. The data stops at 31 March, which is only about 8 weeks. Either the "12 weeks after" claim is wrong or the data is incomplete. Mara should say which.

2. **There is no control and no seasonal adjustment.** The "before" period is winter and the "after" period runs into spring. Journey times and ridership both shift with weather, daylight, school terms, roadworks and fuel prices. Without a comparison, such as another route on a similar road or the same weeks a year earlier, the 5-minute gain can't be attributed to the lane. The analysis also gives only averages. It has no spread, no reliability figures and no sample size.

3. **The ridership claim is overstated.** The analysis says the rise "confirms" that faster journeys drew the extra riders. A 9% rise is consistent with that, but it doesn't show it. Seasonal change alone could produce it. It also couldn't plausibly be a full effect within weeks of opening. The wording should be "is consistent with", unless there is evidence on why riders switched.

4. **The evidence is selected one-sidedly.** The 38 driver comments are counted as evidence, but the 61 shopkeeper comments on delivery access are dismissed as "anecdotal". Both are anecdotal, and drivers have an obvious interest in the lane. This matters most for Kessel, which has 14 loading bays that the shops depend on. If the Varrow shopkeepers reported lost delivery access, that is the most relevant finding for your decision. At the least, the comments should be coded and summarised (how many were negative, and about what), and the figures given for both groups.

5. **Cost is not effectiveness.** Coming in under budget says nothing about whether the lane worked. The analysis gives no cost per minute saved or per passenger, and it doesn't count costs to businesses, such as lost deliveries, fines or reduced trade.

6. **Varrow may not be comparable to Kessel.** The analysis doesn't say how Varrow compares on road width, number of loading bays, traffic volume or the number of bus routes. Kessel is one lane each way with 14 bays and two routes (22 and 31). Where does the bus lane go there? Does it take away the bays or the parking? The pilot evidence can't be carried over unless the two roads are shown to be similar. It also covers only route 7.

7. **The recommendation outruns the evidence.** "Clearly worked" isn't supported, and the step from "worked on Varrow" to "approve on Kessel" is a separate argument that the analysis doesn't make.

**What I'd ask Mara for**
- The actual after-period dates and the number of weeks of data.
- A comparison route or last year's equivalent weeks, and ideally the median and spread of journey times, not just the average.
- A summary of the shopkeeper comments, with counts by theme.
- A side-by-side comparison of Varrow and Kessel (loading bays, traffic, routes, lane layout).
- Any data on delivery or business impacts.

**Suggested framing for members**
"Early data from one route shows journey times fell from 35 to 30 minutes after the Varrow lane opened. Ridership rose 9%. Seasonal effects haven't been controlled for. Shopkeeper concerns about delivery access have not been analysed, and that is the main risk for Kessel Street's 14 loading bays."

I've assumed the figures themselves are accurate and that I have the full text of the analysis. If there are appendices, some of these gaps may already be covered.
