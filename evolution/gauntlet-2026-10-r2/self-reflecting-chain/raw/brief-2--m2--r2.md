Short answer: don't circulate it as is. The arithmetic is mostly fine, but several claims go further than the evidence, and the recommendation doesn't follow for Kessel Street.

**What holds up**
- 35 to 30 minutes is a 5-minute drop, which is 14.3%. The 14% figure is correct.
- £410,000 against £450,000 is £40,000 under budget (about 9%). That is correct too. It tells you the project was well run, though, not that the lane worked.

**Problems to fix**

1. **Timing and window don't match.** The lane opened 3 February, and 12 weeks after that is about 28 April. The data stops at 31 March, which is only about 8 weeks. Either the "12 weeks after" is wrong or the data is shorter than stated. Also, 12 weeks before a 3 February opening is all of winter, and 12 weeks after is late winter into spring. Seasonal effects such as weather, daylight, school terms and roadworks could move journey times and ridership by themselves.

2. **No comparison group.** A before/after on one route can't separate the lane's effect from anything else that changed. Ask for a control, for example route 7 timings on unaffected sections, other routes on similar roads, or general traffic speeds on Varrow Road. Also ask whether the 35 and 30 are means or medians, how much journeys vary, and whether the lane affected reliability, which riders often care about more than the average.

3. **The causal claim on ridership is unsupported.** "That rise confirms passengers were getting faster journeys, and the faster journeys are what drew the extra riders" assumes the conclusion. A 9% rise is consistent with the lane, but also with seasonal recovery after winter, fare changes, service frequency changes, or events. It doesn't confirm anything. The wording should be softened to "ridership rose 9%, which is consistent with the lane but not proof of it" unless a control is added.

4. **The evidence on shops was filtered out unfairly.** Thirty-eight driver comments count as evidence, but 61 shopkeeper comments on delivery access are dismissed as "anecdotal and not representative". Both are self-selected comments, and the shopkeeper set is larger. Drivers are also the group that benefits directly. The dismissal is inconsistent and also matters most for Kessel Street, which has 14 loading bays used by shops. The 61 comments should be summarized and presented to members: what did they say, and how many were negative? If delivery access got worse on Varrow Road, members need to know before voting.

5. **Transferability to Kessel Street is not addressed.** The pilot says nothing about whether Varrow Road resembles Kessel. Relevant questions are how many lanes and loading bays Varrow has, how much parking or loading was displaced, how many routes and what frequency, what the traffic volumes are, and where the delays on Kessel actually occur. Kessel is one lane each way, so a bus lane probably means removing the loading bays or general traffic capacity. That trade-off isn't analyzed.

6. **Overreaching recommendation.** "Clearly worked" is too strong given points 1 to 4. "Came in under budget" is also not a reason to build on Kessel. The pilot's cost doesn't say what Kessel would cost, and the cost excludes the effects on shops and general traffic.

7. **Missing outcomes.** There is nothing on effects on general traffic, on other routes, on enforcement and fines, on safety, or on any change in shop turnover.

**Suggested next steps before Thursday**
- Ask Mara to confirm the actual date range and the number of weeks of data, and to rerun the comparison on the same weeks a year earlier, or against a control.
- Ask for the shopkeeper comments to be summarized alongside the driver ones.
- Add a short section comparing Varrow and Kessel, with specific attention to the 14 loading bays and routes 22 and 31.
- Reword the ridership and "clearly worked" statements.

If time is short, you could circulate it with a cover note saying the pilot shows a promising journey-time gain, but that the evidence on cause, shop impact and transfer to Kessel is still being completed. Members should then treat Thursday's decision as provisional, or defer it until these gaps are closed.

I've assumed the figures are accurately reported and haven't seen the underlying data, so these points are about how the conclusions are supported.
