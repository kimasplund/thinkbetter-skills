I wouldn't sign on the strength of this memo. It doesn't show that you can take Pellam on without letting down your café and restaurant customers. On the numbers in the memo itself, you can't.

**Corrected version of the memo's case**

- **Capacity does not fit.** Two shifts at 4,500 loaves is 9,000 a day. Your current 6,100 plus Pellam's 4,200 is 10,300, which is 1,300 loaves a day more than you can bake. Pellam alone would use 93% of one shift's output and push the total to about 114% of capacity. There is also no slack for a bad day, a busy day for your 74 accounts, or an oven being serviced, and Pellam wants delivery every day of the year by 6 a.m.
- **The £628,000 is the best case, not the expected one.** The arithmetic is right (4,200 × £0.41 × 365 is about £628,500). But £0.97 is your cost on your current setup. Closing a 1,300-loaf gap means a third shift, more oven capacity, or both, and the memo doesn't cost either. Overtime, extra labour, equipment and finance could take a large part of the margin. It also assumes you bake every day of the year, which the memo doesn't say you do now.
- **The three-year term protects Pellam at least as much as you.** A grocer asking for a long fixed-price deal gets certainty on its own price and supply, and that tells you little about its finances. What would protect you is in the contract: whether Pellam is committed to buying 4,200 a day or only to a "forecast", what happens if they cut volumes, penalties if you miss a delivery, and whether the £1.38 can move if flour and energy costs rise. Pellam would also become about 40% of your output, so losing them or being squeezed at renewal would hurt badly.
- **The track record is two successes, picked by you.** Ines and Dmitri are bakeries that did the deal and are still around to be asked. You haven't heard from bakeries that took on a supermarket contract and were hurt or closed. "I haven't heard of one" isn't evidence that none exist. Ask them what their capacity looked like beforehand and whether any older customers were dropped or left.
- **The conclusion answers a different question.** "It pays for itself" is about margin on paper. You asked whether you can do it without letting your existing customers down. On the figures above, not without extra capacity, and the memo doesn't price that.

**What I'd do before Friday**

1. Get a real quote for the extra capacity (a third shift or another oven) and recalculate the margin per loaf on all 4,200.
2. Ask Pellam whether they would accept a smaller volume. At about 2,900 loaves a day you would just fit in the 2,900 spare, and the margin would be roughly £434,000 a year. That's only if it truly needs no extra cost or slack, which is unlikely.
3. Ask Pellam for their commitment in writing: minimum volumes, penalties both ways, cost pass-through, and a way out if they fall short.
4. Check their payment terms and credit position directly. Don't infer them from the contract length.
5. Ask for a reply extension if you can't get these by Friday. Better to ask for a few days than to sign something you can't deliver without cutting your existing accounts.

**Still unresolved**

- The true cost of the extra capacity. This decides whether the deal is worth £600,000 a year or much less, and a quote from your equipment supplier and a staffing estimate will settle it quickly.
- Whether your 6,100 a day is an average or includes busier days above it. Check your peak-day numbers for the last year.
- Pellam's financial strength, which you can check through a credit report.

The deal may well be worth doing, possibly at a lower volume or with added capacity. But the memo's reason for it, that it fits comfortably in the capacity you already have, is wrong, so don't sign on that basis.
