I would not circulate it as written. The journey-time result is probably real, but the write-up claims more than the evidence shows, and the recommendation for Kessel Street doesn't follow from it. Below is a corrected version of the findings, then the problems that changed the conclusion, then what is still open.

**Corrected findings (what the analysis can support)**

- The question for Thursday is whether a bus lane will work on Kessel Street, which has one lane each way, 14 loading bays and routes 22 and 31. Varrow Road is evidence for that only to the extent the two streets are alike.
- Route 7's average journey time fell from 35 to 30 minutes (about 14%) after the lane opened on 3 February. This is a before-and-after comparison on one route. It does not show the lane caused the whole change.
- Route 7 ridership rose 9% over the same period. The cause is not established. Seasonal recovery after winter, fare or timetable changes, and school or work patterns could each explain some or all of it.
- Feedback was 38 comments from bus drivers, who were positive, and 61 comments from Varrow Road shopkeepers about delivery access, which the analysis did not assess. The pilot's effect on delivery access is therefore unknown, not "anecdotal".
- The pilot cost £410,000 against a £450,000 budget. That is a point about financial management. It says nothing about whether the lane improved transport.
- Suggested recommendation: Varrow Road shows a promising journey-time gain for buses. Before approving a lane on Kessel Street, the committee should see the shopkeeper comments assessed, and a comparison of Kessel with Varrow on loading demand, traffic and routes. Whether to approve on Thursday is then the committee's call on that fuller picture.

**Issues that changed the conclusion**

1. **The comparison windows don't match the text.** The lane opened on 3 February and the data stops on 31 March. That is about 8 weeks after opening, not 12. Twelve weeks after would run to late April. Either the "after" period is shorter than stated or the data end date is wrong. If it is 12 weeks before against 8 after, the periods are unequal and the before period sits deeper in winter. Fix: state the true window and compare like for like, ideally the same weeks of the year or the same length.
2. **The ridership argument is circular.** It says the rise "confirms" journeys got faster, and that faster journeys caused the rise. The rise can't prove the cause that is then used to explain it. Fix: present 9% as an observed change, and test the cause independently. Compare with ridership on routes not touched by the pilot over the same weeks, or with the same weeks a year earlier.
3. **No control for other causes of the journey-time drop.** Weather, roadworks, school terms and traffic all change between winter and spring. Fix: compare against a nearby route or road with no lane, and against the same period last year.
4. **The shopkeeper comments were dismissed while the driver comments were counted.** Drivers benefit directly from the lane, so their praise is expected. The 61 shopkeeper comments are the larger group, and they cover exactly the issue Kessel Street is most exposed to, with 14 loading bays serving its shops. "Anecdotal" was applied to one side only. Fix: read and code the 61 comments (for example blocked deliveries, lost trade, loading fines), and report them in the findings.
5. **"Clearly worked" and "under budget" overreach.** Being under budget doesn't show the lane met its aims. "Clearly worked" rests on one route, one short period and no control. Kessel is also a different street: one lane each way, with loading bays that a lane would either remove or conflict with, and two routes rather than one. Fix: say what was shown (faster route 7 journeys) and what was not (causation, effects on deliveries and other traffic, transfer to Kessel).

**Still unresolved, and the cheapest check**

- Whether the 8-week or 12-week figure is correct. Check the GPS extract's date range.
- How much of the 5-minute gain is the lane. Pull route 7 times for the same weeks last year, plus one comparable untouched route this year.
- What the shopkeepers actually said. Tally the 61 comments by theme. It's an afternoon's work.
- How Kessel's loading bays would be handled. Ask Mara whether Varrow had comparable bays, and what the Kessel design would do to them.
- Whether journey-time reliability and other road users were affected. Request the pilot data if it was collected.

The most worth watching of these is the shopkeeper comments, since they are the evidence most likely to differ between the two streets.
