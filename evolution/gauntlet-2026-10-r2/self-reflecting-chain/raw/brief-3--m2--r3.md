The paper should not go to the board as written. Its conclusion may still turn out to be right, but the argument doesn't support it, and it never answers the question the board asked.

**What the board asked**

The board asked whether members will earn less next year if you stop distributing Calder Red. The paper's conclusion is that Calder Red has the lowest yield per hectare. Yield is not income. Calder Red carries a £38 a tonne premium, so the comparison has to be money per hectare, and the paper never makes it.

**Problems, most serious first**

1. **The conclusion tests the wrong thing.**
   - Brannock yields 6.3 t/ha and Calder Red 4.1 t/ha. With the premium, Calder Red earns more per hectare only if Brannock's price P satisfies 4.1 × (P + 38) > 6.3 × P. That works out to P below about £71 a tonne.
   - Milling grain normally sells well above that. So Calder Red probably does earn less per hectare, and the paper may reach the right answer. But it needs the actual prices to show that.
   - It also needs growing costs per hectare and any rotation or agronomic role. The paper has neither.
   - "Lowest-yielding variety" is also only shown against Brannock. The paper gives no figures for any other variety.

2. **The demand argument is circular.** "It has stayed a niche grain, which is exactly what you would expect of a grain with weak demand" uses the conclusion as its evidence. Niche status could equally come from limited supply, limited marketing or agronomic limits. The paper then admits mills pay a premium and dismisses it as "scarcity rather than real demand". A buyer paying extra for a product is evidence of demand. If you want to argue weak demand, use volumes the mills will take, whether they would take more, and how the premium has changed over time.

3. **The 2022 storm shows little.** It is one event at two farms, and it rests on "everyone remembers". It is not evidence that the variety is risky in general. To show that, compare lodging or loss rates for Calder Red and Brannock over several seasons. Also check whether Ashby and Toller had something else in common, such as exposure or sowing date. The yield figure of 4.1 t/ha may already include those losses, in which case this adds nothing new.

4. **The 19% figure looks wrong.**
   - 610 of 2,400 hectares is about 25%, not 19%. The 19 in the paper is the number of members growing Calder Red (19 of 46, about 41%), so the two figures may have been confused.
   - If today's share is really 25%, the fall from 31% is much smaller than the paper implies.
   - Even if the trend is right, members moving away from the variety doesn't show that it is a bad choice. It could equally mean members are choosing what pays better. It also cuts against the plan, because 19 members still chose to grow it last year.

5. **The paper leaves out the effect on members.**
   - Nothing says what the 19 growers would plant instead, or what happens to the premium contracts with mills.
   - Those 610 hectares would produce about 3,800 t of Brannock at 6.3 t/ha, against about 2,500 t of Calder Red. That is a different volume to sell, and extra Brannock supply may push its price down.
   - Members who grow Calder Red by choice may have reasons the paper doesn't consider, such as harvest timing or soil.

**What I would ask Pieter to add before the 18th**

- Revenue and margin per hectare for Calder Red and for Brannock, using actual prices, premium and costs.
- The correct share of hectares, with the five-year series.
- Multi-season loss or lodging data, not the single 2022 event.
- A short estimate for each affected member of income now against income after switching.
- Mill demand evidence: volumes, contract terms, and any sign of weakening.

**Verdict:** the arithmetic (610 ha × 4.1 t ≈ 2,500 t) is fine. The 19% share looks like an error. Two of the supporting arguments are circular or anecdotal. The conclusion doesn't answer the board's question. It may well be that stopping Calder Red costs members little or nothing, but the paper hasn't shown that yet. I would hold the vote or send it back for a revision.
