Notes (J-B, brief-3)

- P0J: Catches all core issues (yield vs earnings with the £71 break-even, the 25.4% vs 19% inconsistency, circular demand, the anecdote) and states the selection effect explicitly. Quantifies the premium (£95k), raises who gains and who loses, and offers options short of a full stop. "Normally well above £71/t" is an external claim, though hedged.
- R2V: Thorough and the issues are right, but the bottom line is garbled ("lowers revenue ... less than the headline yield gap suggests. Equally it could raise it") and partly undoes its own break-even analysis. Its deferral and phasing options are useful. "Three years ago" assumes a current year.
- K9B: Solid. The £200 illustration is flagged and the 1,340 t extra-supply figure is correct. Notes Brannock price risk and the risk of concentrating on one variety. States "milling-grade grain sells well above that" flatly. Its landing is weaker on what would change the call.
- W9P: Clean and well-reasoned. It makes the point that scarcity only raises price when someone wants the grain, and has a concrete five-item ask list with a range. One muddled sentence on the 41% figure. Asserts "any realistic milling-grain price" without flagging it.
- V2G: Good break-even, and makes three conditions explicit, including the Brannock price falling under 3,800 t of extra supply. Clear six-step pre-vote plan. Stops short of a firm recommendation beyond "not ready".
- K8J: Strong on the premium possibly rising after the stop and on cheapest checks. But its "honest answer" (members "probably somewhat less" if they switch to Brannock at a normal price) runs the wrong way against its own break-even analysis, which is a material reasoning error on the board's actual question. Template-like headings.
- F6A: The opening line says "the paper's own figures point the other way", which contradicts its own later finding that Brannock probably earns more. The rest is sound: it flags the premium-base assumption, says "I don't have the actual prices", and suggests the table. Template heading on the cheapest check.
- O2U: Best calibrated. It names the three unstated assumptions (same base price, comparable costs, growers reaching 6.3 t/ha on their own land), notes that the premium ends with supply, and gives draft conclusion wording, an explicit reversal condition and the cheapest checks. "Four years ago" assumes a date. The headings are slightly templated.
- Z5X: Sound and readable. It adds phasing and keep-for-growers options and the co-op's handling savings. Leans on "milling grain usually sells well above that". Fewer non-obvious points.
- I3F: Catches the subtle point that 4.1 t/ha may already include storm losses, plus the risk of Brannock supply softening its price. Clear send-back verdict, but "hold or send back" is a soft landing.
- X7U: Clear, and closes with an explicit statement of what it did not see, framing the £71 as an illustration. Calling the premium "the one number that could reverse it" is slightly off, because the base price is the unknown.
- C7J: The ready-to-send redraft is actionable, but it keeps the unverified share-decline claim and adds "fallen steadily", which is not in the brief, right after flagging that figure as wrong. "Near current market levels" is unflagged. It does catch that 4.1 t/ha may include bad years.
