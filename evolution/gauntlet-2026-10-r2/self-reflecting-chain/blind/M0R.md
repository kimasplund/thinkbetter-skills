Short answer: I wouldn't sign on the strength of this memo. The arithmetic is right, but the capacity section, which answers your question about not letting down your café and restaurant customers, is wrong. The other sections lean on weak evidence.

**What checks out**
- £1.38 - £0.97 = £0.41 a loaf. 4,200 x £0.41 x 365 is about £628,500, so the £628,000 figure is fine.

**1. Capacity: the memo's own numbers say it doesn't fit**
- Two shifts at 4,500 loaves each is 9,000 loaves a day.
- You already bake about 6,100 for your 74 accounts. Adding Pellam's 4,200 makes 10,300 a day.
- That is about 1,300 loaves a day (roughly 14%) more than you can bake. It only "fits comfortably" if you compare 4,200 to the 2,900 spare, which it exceeds.
- Closing the gap means a third shift, more ovens, or cutting back existing customers. That is exactly what you asked Tomasz to rule out.
- It gets harder because the contract is every day of the year. You'd have no slack for oven breakdowns, maintenance, or your customers' busy days. The 6,100 is also an average, so your peak days are higher. The 6 a.m. delivery to 11 stores may also collide with your café and restaurant delivery runs.
- The £0.97 is probably an average cost. A third shift, overtime or new equipment would raise the cost of the extra loaves, so £0.41 of margin may not hold.

**2. Risk: the three-year term doesn't show Pellam is low-risk**
- A long commitment shows that Pellam wants a secure supply. It says little about whether Pellam is financially solid. It also binds you for three years.
- What protects you is the contract's terms, which the memo doesn't mention. Check these:
  - Whether the 4,200 a day is a guaranteed minimum or a forecast.
  - Termination and break clauses.
  - Price review and indexation. Flour, energy and labour costs can rise over three years while the £1.38 stays fixed.
  - Payment terms.
  - Penalties if you miss a delivery.
- Concentration is a risk too. Pellam would be about 40% of your volume (4,200 of 10,300). If they leave or renegotiate, you'd have a large hole in your volume and may have lost café accounts along the way.
- I'd look at Pellam's published accounts and ask for trade references.

**3. Track record: two friendly data points aren't evidence**
- Two bakeries that did well, picked by Tomasz, tell you little. Bakeries where it went badly may have closed or may not take his calls, so "I haven't heard of one" is weak.
- Ask Ines and Dmitri specific questions. Did they have to add capacity? Did they lose or neglect any existing customers? How did the supermarket's price pressure and renegotiation go after year one? How much of their volume is the supermarket now?
- Their situations may differ from yours: their spare capacity, their contract terms and how many accounts they had.

**4. Conclusion: the £628,000 is a gross figure**
- It ignores the cost of added capacity, any café accounts you'd lose or underserve, the extra complexity, and the risk of depending on one customer.
- What matters is the net gain: Pellam's margin, minus the cost of the capacity needed, minus the margin on any existing business you'd lose. Your accounts' margin and relationships may be worth more per loaf than Pellam's £0.41.

**What I'd do before Friday**
1. Work out the real cost of 1,300 more loaves a day (a third shift or equipment) and recalculate the margin on all 4,200.
2. Get the actual contract terms and have a solicitor review them. Ask for a volume ramp-up, a termination clause, price indexation and a cap on Pellam's share of your output.
3. Ask Pellam whether they'd take a lower volume, for example 2,500 to 2,900 loaves a day, which fits your spare capacity. That could be a counter-offer.
4. If you can't get an extension, reply on Friday with a conditional answer ("interested, subject to terms and a phased volume") and don't sign as it stands.

I've assumed the figures in the memo are accurate and that the 6,100 is a daily average. If your spare capacity is bigger than the memo implies, for example because there's a third shift you haven't mentioned, tell me and I'll rerun the numbers.
