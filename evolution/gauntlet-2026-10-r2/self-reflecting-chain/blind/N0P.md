I would not circulate it as written. The journey-time figure is sound, but the recommendation says more than the evidence supports, and a few lines would not survive a sharp question from a member. Here is a corrected version, followed by what changed.

**Varrow Road bus lane pilot: findings (corrected)**

The lane opened on 3 February. We compared the weeks before it opened with the weeks after, using route 7 GPS data up to 31 March. That is about 8 weeks of "after" data, not 12. Twelve weeks after 3 February would run to late April. The comparison should either say 8 weeks, or use matched 8-week windows on both sides, or wait for the full 12 weeks of data.

Average route 7 journey time fell from 35 minutes to 30, a 14% improvement. The arithmetic is right. It is a before/after comparison with no control, so it does not rule out other causes. The "before" period was in the depths of winter and the "after" period ran into spring, so weather, roadworks, school terms and general traffic could all have moved journey times. Comparing with the same weeks a year earlier, or with a similar road that had no lane, would tell us how much of the 5 minutes belongs to the lane.

Route 7 ridership rose 9% over the same period. This is consistent with faster journeys attracting riders, but it does not show that. Ridership also rises with the season and with the weather, and the pilot's own evidence does not separate those. Journey time is also not the only thing that changes ridership. The finding should read "ridership rose 9%; we have not established how much, if any, of this is due to the lane."

We received written comments from 38 bus drivers, who were overwhelmingly positive about the lane. We also received 61 comments from Varrow Road shopkeepers about delivery access. These should be summarised, not set aside. Calling them anecdotal while treating the drivers' comments as supporting evidence applies a different standard to each group. The shopkeepers are the group whose experience is most relevant to Kessel Street, which has 14 loading bays in use by its shops. Both sets of comments are opinions from interested parties. The report should say what the shopkeepers actually raised and how many were negative. Drivers are also not passengers, so their comments do not measure the passenger benefit.

The pilot cost £410,000 against a budget of £450,000. Being under budget says the project was run well. It does not say the lane was worth doing, and Kessel's costs may differ.

Recommendation: the pilot shows a substantial journey-time gain on route 7, with an unconfirmed effect on ridership and unresolved concerns from traders about deliveries. It was delivered under budget. It is a reasonable basis for considering a bus lane on Kessel Street, but not on its own for approving one.

**What changed and why**

- The "12 weeks after" claim did not match the data cut-off of 31 March, which gives about 8 weeks. Fix: state the real window or extend the data.
- "That rise confirms... and the faster journeys are what drew the extra riders" assumed the conclusion. The ridership rise was offered as proof of the cause, and the cause was the only reason given for it. Fix: present it as consistent with the lane, not proof.
- The before/after comparison across winter and spring has no control, so the 14% cannot all be credited to the lane. Fix: add a seasonal or comparison-road check.
- Dismissing the 61 shopkeeper comments while counting the 38 driver comments favours the evidence that supports the conclusion. Fix: report both groups.
- "Clearly worked" and "approve" went beyond what route 7 on Varrow Road can say about Kessel Street, and the pilot was not designed to test the Kessel question.

**Still unresolved, and the cheapest way to check**

- Is Varrow Road like Kessel? Kessel is one lane each way, with 14 loading bays and two routes (22 and 31). Ask Mara for Varrow's lane layout, loading provision and number of routes. If Varrow had off-street servicing and Kessel does not, the pilot may understate the effect on traders.
- How much of the journey-time gain is seasonal? Ask for route 7 times for the same weeks last year, or for a nearby route with no lane.
- What did the shopkeepers say? Someone should read and count the 61 comments, which can be done in an afternoon.
- Effect on other traffic, such as delivery vehicles and cars, is not reported. Ask whether any data exists.
- Is the lane built for this? Ask for a Kessel-specific cost estimate. The Varrow cost is not a guide to it.

If the committee needs to decide on Thursday, a fair framing is to approve further design work or a trial on Kessel with loading arrangements, not a permanent lane based on this pilot alone.
