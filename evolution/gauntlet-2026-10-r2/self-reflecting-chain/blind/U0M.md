I wouldn't circulate it as it stands. The arithmetic is fine, but the recommendation says more than the evidence supports, and a member who reads closely will find the gaps quickly. Fixing them should be quick, and the report would be much harder to challenge afterwards.

**What holds up**

- 35 minutes to 30 is a fall of 5 minutes, which is 14.3% of 35. The "14%" is correct.
- £410,000 against £450,000 is £40,000 under budget (about 9%). That is correct too.

**What doesn't hold up**

1. **The "12 weeks after" period doesn't exist in the data.** The lane opened on 3 February and the GPS data stops on 31 March. That is 8 weeks, not 12. So either the comparison is 12 weeks before against 8 weeks after, or the dates are wrong. Mara needs to say which, and the report should state the real window.

2. **The before/after comparison is confounded by season.** Twelve weeks before 3 February is roughly mid-November to the end of January. The after period is February and March. Winter weather, school terms, the Christmas period, roadworks and general traffic levels all differ between those windows. The report gives no comparison route, such as a similar route without a lane over the same dates. Without one, we can't tell how much of the 5 minutes came from the lane. The lane may well have helped, but the report hasn't shown how much.

3. **The ridership claim is causal and unsupported.** The report says the 9% rise "confirms" that faster journeys "drew the extra riders". A rise after the lane opened doesn't show the lane caused it. Seasonal travel changes, fares, events, other service changes or a recovery trend could all explain it. Ridership also usually responds slowly to journey-time changes, so a rise within 8 weeks is a surprising place to find the effect. The defensible wording is that ridership rose 9% over the same period, cause not established. Please also confirm whether the 9% covers the same dates as the journey-time comparison.

4. **The stakeholder evidence is treated unevenly.** The 38 driver comments are reported as "overwhelmingly positive". The 61 shopkeeper comments, which outnumber them, are dismissed as "anecdotal and not representative". Both groups are self-selected written comments, so if one is anecdotal, so is the other. The shopkeepers are also the group most relevant to Kessel Street, which has 14 loading bays that the shops depend on. The report should say what the 61 comments actually said, how many were negative, and what happened to deliveries on Varrow Road. As written it looks like the evidence that complicates the recommendation was left out.

5. **Under budget is not the same as it worked.** The cost figure tells members the project was managed within its budget. It says nothing about whether the lane delivered value. The conclusion that the pilot "clearly worked" rests on one journey-time figure with the problems above.

6. **Nothing shows Varrow Road is a good guide to Kessel Street.** Kessel has one lane each way, 14 loading bays, and two routes (22 and 31). The report doesn't say what Varrow Road looks like, how many loading bays it has, or how many routes use it. The pilot measured only route 7. The committee needs to know how closely the two roads match, and what the lane would do to loading on Kessel in particular.

**What I'd ask Mara to do before Thursday**

- Correct the post-lane window and state the dates for every figure.
- Add a comparison, either a control route or the same weeks a year earlier, so the journey-time gain can be attributed to the lane.
- Reword the ridership sentence to say what the data shows, not what it proves.
- Summarise the shopkeeper comments honestly, including any delivery problems, and give the driver comments the same caveat.
- Add a short section on how Varrow Road differs from Kessel Street, especially on loading bays and the number of routes.
- Change the recommendation from "clearly worked" to what the evidence supports. For example, journey times on route 7 fell by about 14% after the lane opened, the causal share is not yet established, and the loading effect on Kessel needs addressing before a decision.

**For Thursday**

If the committee must decide this week, I'd circulate a corrected version that presents the pilot as encouraging but not conclusive. It could propose approving the lane subject to a loading-bay plan for Kessel, or deferring until the comparison is done. Either is more defensible than "clearly worked", and neither depends on figures the report can't support.
