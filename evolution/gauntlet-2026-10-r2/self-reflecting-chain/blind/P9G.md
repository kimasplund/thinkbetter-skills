I wouldn't circulate it as it stands. The arithmetic is fine, but the recommendation goes further than the evidence. Here are the problems, most serious first.

**1. The shopkeeper comments were dropped, and they matter most for Kessel.**
- Kessel has 14 loading bays that the shops depend on. Delivery access is the main way a bus lane could do harm there.
- The report sets aside 61 shopkeeper comments as "anecdotal and not representative". It keeps 38 driver comments, which are just as anecdotal. Drivers have an obvious interest in the lane, so their praise says little about whether the lane works overall.
- Treating the two groups differently looks selective. Members will notice, and it undermines trust in the rest of the report.
- Ask Mara what the shopkeepers actually said. Ideally she would summarise the 61 comments and add any data on deliveries, parking or shop turnover.

**2. The data period doesn't match the stated design.**
- The lane opened on 3 February, and the "after" period is said to be 12 weeks. But the data stops on 31 March, which is only about 8 weeks.
- So either the "after" period was really about 8 weeks, or the dates are wrong. Check which, and whether the "before" period was matched in length.

**3. The before/after comparison has no control.**
- The "before" period is about November to January and the "after" period is February to March. Weather, school terms, roadworks, holidays and general traffic levels all change over that span.
- Route 7 alone is compared with itself. There is no comparison route or general-traffic baseline to show that the lane caused the change.
- The 5-minute fall (35 to 30, which is 14.3%) may be real, but the report can't say how much of it is the lane. It also doesn't say how variable journey times were, so we can't tell whether 5 minutes is outside normal fluctuation.

**4. The ridership claim is stated as causal, and the data doesn't support that.**
- "That rise confirms passengers were getting faster journeys, and the faster journeys are what drew the extra riders." A 9% rise doesn't confirm that.
- Ridership normally recovers seasonally from the midwinter low. Fares, events and employment can also move it, and a lane that opened in February would take time to change habits.
- The report should say ridership rose alongside the lane and could be partly or wholly due to other factors. A comparison with other routes would help.

**5. "Under budget" says nothing about whether it worked.**
- £410,000 against £450,000 is a statement about cost control. It doesn't show benefit.
- The committee needs a cost-benefit view. That means passenger time saved, ridership gain, any cost to other road users and businesses, and the likely cost of building on Kessel. Kessel's cost won't match Varrow's if the street layout differs.

**6. Varrow Road may not be a good guide to Kessel Street.**
- The report doesn't compare the two streets. Useful points would be lane count, loading and parking provision, traffic volume, and enforcement.
- Varrow had one route. Kessel carries routes 22 and 31, and the report doesn't say how that changes things.
- Kessel is one lane each way. Adding a bus lane may mean removing general traffic capacity or the loading bays, and the pilot says nothing about that.
- Impacts on general traffic, side streets and safety aren't reported.

**7. The recommendation overreaches.**
"Clearly worked" isn't supported. A more defensible reading is: journey times on route 7 improved after the lane opened, the pilot was delivered under budget, and the effects on businesses and other road users are unassessed.

**What I'd suggest before Thursday**
- Get the 61 shopkeeper comments summarised and report them alongside the driver comments.
- Fix and explain the date discrepancy.
- Add a comparison route or a same-period baseline, if the data exists.
- Reword the ridership paragraph so it no longer claims causation.
- Add a short section on how Kessel differs from Varrow, covering the 14 loading bays, and say what mitigation is proposed.
- Change the recommendation to something like: the pilot shows promising journey-time gains, and approval should depend on a loading-bay plan and a Kessel-specific cost estimate.

If there isn't time for all of this, the first two items and the reworded recommendation are the minimum. I assumed the report's figures are accurate as given, and I haven't seen the underlying data.
