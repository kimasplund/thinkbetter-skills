The paper's conclusion may well be right, but as written it doesn't get there. Several arguments fail, and the paper never answers the board's question, which was whether members will earn less next year. I'd send it back for a revision before the vote on the 18th.

**What checks out**
- 610 ha x 4.1 t/ha is about 2,500 t, so that figure is correct.

**Problems, most serious first**

1. **The conclusion answers a different question.** "Lowest-yielding variety per hectare" is about tonnes, but the board asked about member earnings. The paper itself says Calder Red earns a £38/t premium, and it then dismisses that premium. Income per hectare is yield x price, less costs.
   - Calder Red beats Brannock per hectare only if 4.1 x (P + 38) > 6.3 x P, where P is the Brannock price. That works out to P below about £71/t. Milling-grade grain sells well above that, so on these numbers Brannock probably earns more per hectare.
   - Rough illustration, assuming P = £200/t: Calder Red earns about £976/ha (4.1 x £238) and Brannock about £1,260/ha. Across 610 ha, that is roughly £595k against £769k.
   - That is only an illustration. The paper needs the real Brannock price, each variety's costs and margin per hectare, and whether Brannock suits the Calder Red growers' land and rotations.
   - Switching 610 ha to Brannock would add about 1,340 t of supply. The paper should say whether Brannock's price would hold with that extra volume.
   - The "lowest-yielding" claim is also unsupported. Only Brannock is compared, and the other varieties aren't shown.

2. **The demand argument is circular.** "It has stayed a niche grain, which is exactly what you would expect of weak demand" restates the claim as evidence. A niche grain could just as easily have strong demand and limited supply.
   - A premium of £38/t is evidence of demand. Calling it "scarcity rather than real demand" is an assertion, not an argument.
   - Real evidence would be mill offtake commitments, unsold stock, price history, or buyer interviews.

3. **The 2022 storm is an anecdote.** Two farms lost their fields in one storm. That shows lodging happened once, not that the variety is riskier than Brannock.
   - The paper needs multi-year lodging or loss rates for both varieties, and it needs to know whether Ashby and Toller are exposed sites.
   - It also needs to check whether Brannock fields were affected in the same storm.

4. **The numbers contradict each other.** 610 of 2,400 ha is 25.4%, not 19%. The 19 in the paper is the number of growing members, and 19 of 46 is 41%.
   - The "31% to 19%" figure looks like a mix-up. Please ask Pieter which figure is correct and where the 19% came from.
   - If the true share is about 25%, the decline is smaller than claimed.
   - Even with the decline as stated, it doesn't show members are "moving away" because of price or demand. They may be moving for reasons the paper hasn't looked at, such as rotation, contracts, or seed availability. It could also be a symptom of weak distribution rather than weak demand.

5. **Smaller gaps.**
   - Premium, yield and area come from one season only.
   - The paper doesn't consider the 19 growing members or what they'd lose. It also doesn't consider whether a few of them depend on Calder Red for specific mill contracts.
   - It doesn't weigh the costs of stopping against the costs of keeping Calder Red, such as seed handling, a separate supply chain, and the risk of concentrating all members on one variety.

**What to ask Pieter for**
- A per-hectare margin comparison, with prices, costs and the premium, for Calder Red and Brannock.
- Multi-year yield and loss data for both varieties.
- Correct hectare and share figures.
- Direct evidence on buyer demand.
- A view on what switching would do to Brannock prices and to each of the 19 growers.
- A direct answer to the board's question, stated as an estimate of the change in member income with a range.

My view is that the case for stopping may be sound, because the yield gap is large (6.3 against 4.1). But the paper hasn't shown it, and the board shouldn't vote on a conclusion whose argument doesn't hold up.
