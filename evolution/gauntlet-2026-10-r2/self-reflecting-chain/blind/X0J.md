I'd hold off circulating it as written. The 14% arithmetic is right (5 of 35 minutes is 14.3%), but the recommendation goes further than the evidence supports.

**Problems to fix**

1. **The dates don't match.** The lane opened on 3 February, so 12 weeks after ends around 28 April. The GPS data stops at 31 March, which is only about 8 weeks. Either the "after" window is shorter than stated, or the data doesn't cover what the text claims. Mara should confirm which, and whether the before and after windows are the same length.

2. **There is no comparison group.** The analysis compares route 7 before and after, so anything else that changed in that period is counted as an effect of the lane. Examples are the seasonal shift from the holiday period and mid-winter into spring, weather, roadworks, school terms, fare changes and timetable changes. A comparison route or road with no lane, over the same dates, would help a lot. Failing that, the same weeks of the previous year would do.

3. **The ridership claim is a causal claim with no support.** The report says the 9% rise "confirms" that faster journeys drew the extra riders. A rise that happens at the same time doesn't show that. Seasonal recovery after January, fare changes or local events could all explain it, and so could a network-wide trend. At most, the report can say ridership rose while journey times fell. It can't say the lane caused the rise.

4. **The evidence on people is one-sided.** Drivers are naturally positive about a lane that speeds up their work, and 38 positive comments tell us little about passengers. The 61 shopkeeper comments about delivery access were set aside as "anecdotal", while the driver comments were counted. That is inconsistent. It also matters most for Kessel, which has 14 loading bays that the shops depend on. The shopkeeper comments should be summarised, and the report should say what they actually said.

5. **Cost under budget doesn't show the lane worked.** It only shows that the pilot was delivered cheaply. It says nothing about benefits, and nothing about what a Kessel lane would cost. "Clearly worked" is too strong given points 1 to 4.

6. **Varrow Road may not be comparable to Kessel.** The report doesn't say how wide Varrow is, how many lanes it has, whether it has loading bays, or what the traffic is like. Kessel is one lane each way with 14 bays, so a bus lane there would probably mean removing bays or a general traffic lane. The report also looks at one route, while Kessel carries two (22 and 31). Pilot results from a road with more room may not carry over.

**What the evidence does support**

Route 7 journey times were about 14% shorter after the lane opened. That is a promising sign, but it isn't proof.

**Before Thursday, ask Mara for**
- The corrected before and after periods, with data covering the full 12 weeks.
- A comparison route or a prior-year baseline.
- Effects on general traffic and journey times for other vehicles, and on bus reliability, not just average time.
- A summary of the shopkeeper comments, and any data on deliveries, parking or trading on Varrow.
- A comparison of Varrow's layout with Kessel's, and an estimate of what the Kessel lane would cost and how it would handle the 14 bays.

**Suggested wording for the recommendation**

"The Varrow Road pilot shows a promising reduction in route 7 journey times. The evidence is limited, and the effects on traders and on general traffic are not yet assessed. The committee should ask for a Kessel-specific assessment, including loading provision, before deciding."
