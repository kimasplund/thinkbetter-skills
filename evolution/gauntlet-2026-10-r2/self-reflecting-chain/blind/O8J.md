I would not circulate it as written. The route 7 journey-time result is probably real, but the write-up claims more than the evidence supports, and it leaves out the evidence most relevant to Kessel Street. Here is a corrected version of the findings and recommendation that you could send instead.

**Varrow Road bus lane pilot: findings (corrected)**

- **Journey time:** Average route 7 journey time fell from 35 to 30 minutes (about 14%) after the lane opened on 3 February. The "after" period runs to 31 March, which is about 8 weeks, not 12. The comparison is therefore 12 weeks before against roughly 8 weeks after. The "before" weeks fall in deep winter, so part of the change could be seasonal (weather, daylight, school terms, roadworks). No comparison route or general-traffic data is given to rule that out.
- **Ridership:** Route 7 ridership rose 9% over the period. This is consistent with a benefit from faster journeys, but it does not show it. Season, fares, service changes or events could each explain it, and the pilot did not test any of them.
- **Stakeholder comments:** 38 bus drivers were overwhelmingly positive. 61 shopkeepers wrote about delivery access, and their comments were set aside as anecdotal. That is not a fair split. The drivers' comments are just as anecdotal, and the shopkeepers sent more of them. Their content has not been reported at all, so we do not know whether they were positive, negative or mixed.
- **Cost:** The pilot cost £410,000 against a £450,000 budget. This says the pilot was run within budget. It does not say the lane delivered value, and it does not predict what Kessel would cost.
- **Recommendation:** The Varrow Road pilot shows a likely journey-time gain for route 7. It does not yet show that a lane on Kessel Street would work. Kessel is one lane each way, has 14 loading bays that shops rely on, and carries two routes (22 and 31), so the committee should not approve a lane on this evidence alone.

**Issues that change the conclusion**

1. The "12 weeks after" period is really about 8 weeks (3 Feb to 31 Mar). Either the wording is wrong or data is missing. Fix: state the true period, or add the missing weeks.
2. "That rise confirms... faster journeys are what drew the extra riders" assumes the cause it is trying to prove. Fix: report the 9% rise as an observation and say the cause is untested.
3. A before/after comparison across winter into spring has no control. Fix: say so, and add a comparison (see below).
4. Shopkeeper comments were dismissed while driver comments were counted. This is the evidence closest to Kessel's main risk, its 14 loading bays. Fix: summarise what the 61 comments actually say, and apply the same standard to both groups.
5. "Clearly worked" and "under budget" do not support "approve on Kessel". Varrow Road may differ from Kessel in width, loading provision, traffic volume and number of routes. Fix: the recommendation must rest on how comparable the two streets are.

**Still unresolved, with the cheapest way to check**

- Whether the 30-minute figure holds against a control: compare route 7 with a nearby route that had no lane over the same weeks, or with the same weeks a year earlier. Ask Mara for this.
- What the 61 shopkeeper comments say, and whether Varrow had loading bays or kerbside delivery like Kessel's. Mara can code the comments as positive, negative or neutral, and note the number of bays on Varrow.
- Whether general traffic, side streets or other routes got worse. Traffic counts on Varrow before and after would show this.
- Whether the gain was in average time only, or also in reliability. The variation in journey time would show it.
- Whether the £410,000 scales to Kessel. Ask for the cost per kilometre and a Kessel estimate.

If the committee must decide on Thursday, the safest framing is to ask for approval in principle, or a Kessel trial that includes loading-bay arrangements. This should be conditional on the shopkeeper evidence and a control comparison.
