I chair the board of the Hessary Grain Cooperative, which supplies seed to 46 member farms. At our March meeting the board asked one question: if we stop distributing Calder Red seed, will our members earn less next year? Our agronomist, Pieter Vos, wrote the paper below in answer. The vote is on the 18th. Please check it before it goes to the board.

---

**Proposal: discontinue Calder Red seed**

Last season 19 members grew Calder Red on 610 hectares, out of the 2,400 hectares our members farm in total. It averaged 4.1 tonnes per hectare against 6.3 for Brannock, our main variety, giving about 2,500 tonnes.

Buyer demand for Calder Red is weak. The clearest sign is that it has stayed a niche grain, which is exactly what you would expect of a grain with weak demand. Mills do pay a premium of £38 a tonne for it, but that reflects scarcity rather than real demand.

Everyone here remembers August 2022, when the Calder Red fields at Ashby and Toller farms went flat in the storm and were written off. That shows how risky the variety is.

Calder Red's share of our members' hectares has fallen from 31% to 19% over the last five years, so members are already moving away from it.

Conclusion: Calder Red is our lowest-yielding variety per hectare, so we should stop distributing it.
