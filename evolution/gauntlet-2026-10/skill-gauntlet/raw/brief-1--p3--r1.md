Short answer: no, don't tell the clinics v2.4 goes live Monday. Under the rule you wrote on September 2, v2.4 fails. Keep v2.3 live.

**1. Clarity: it missed the registered bar.**
The pre-registered requirement was +1.0 on owner clarity. v2.4 got +0.8, so it failed. Lowering the bar to 0.5 on September 10 was a change made after you saw the results. The reasoning may be sound, because 1.0 on a 7-point scale probably was a carry-over error. But a bar changed after the data arrives can't be used to adopt. Report both readings: under the registered 1.0 it fails, and under the amended 0.5 it passes. The registered text decides. If you think 0.5 is the right bar, register it now and run a fresh round. Don't apply it to this one.

Also, +0.8 on 12 notes, two runs each, is a small sample. Without a spread or a paired comparison across the 12 notes, I would treat the margin as unproven even if the bar had been 0.5.

**2. Dosing accuracy: this is a regression, and it is the one that matters.**
Your rule said "no worse than v2.3" on dosing accuracy. The judges' 5.0 vs 5.0 doesn't settle that. The line-by-line pharmacy check found two v2.4 tables that wrote a twice-daily drug as once daily, and none in v2.3. That is a concrete, checkable difference on the exact dimension you said must not regress, and it is a halving of a dose that goes home to owners. When the judges and the line-by-line check disagree on a safety dimension, use the stricter reading. Here the stricter reading is the pharmacy check, because it compared outputs directly to the source notes. The judges scored holistically and did not catch it. Being clinicians doesn't make their read better than a literal check. The new medication-schedule table is the likely source, so this looks like a failure in the new feature rather than noise. It also fails the guard even if you ignore the clarity miss.

**3. Length: fine.** "About even" meets "no worse".

**4. The carprofen sanity check tells you little.**
The missed-carprofen question is one of the three worked examples you added to v2.4. So v2.4 had seen that exact case, and the test can't show how it handles a new question. The behavior difference is real, though. v2.4 gave a short answer to a short question, and v2.3 produced a full discharge sheet. That is a point for v2.4 and worth keeping.
