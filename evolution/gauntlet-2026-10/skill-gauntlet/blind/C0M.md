No. Do not tell the clinics it goes live Monday. Henrik is right to hold off, and the evidence you've described doesn't support shipping v2.4 on your own rule.

**1. Dosing accuracy is a failed guard, not a tie.**
Your pre-registered rule said v2.4 must be no worse than v2.3 on dosing accuracy. The pharmacy tech checked every output line by line against the source notes. She found two v2.4 tables where a twice-daily drug was written as once daily, and none in v2.3. That is a measured regression on the one dimension where a mistake can hurt an animal, because underdosing means an untreated or under-treated pet.

The judges' 5.0 for both versions doesn't cancel this out. Clinicians reading for overall quality are poor at catching a transposed frequency inside a table. A ceiling score of 5.0 on both arms also means the scale can't tell the versions apart. The line-by-line check against source is the better instrument for this question, because it compares the output to the notes directly. Where the two disagree on a safety dimension, go with the stricter reading. Two errors in 24 outputs, all in the new feature, is also not noise to wave away. It points at the table, which is the main thing v2.4 adds.

**2. The clarity win is below your own bar.**
v2.4 was ahead by 0.8, and the bar you wrote down on September 2 was 1.0. It fails. The change to 0.5 on September 10 came after you saw the results, and 0.8 happens to clear 0.5. Your reason for the change may well be true, since 1.0 on a 7-point scale is a big ask. But anyone reading this later, including a regulator, an insurer or a partner, will see a threshold lowered by exactly the amount needed to pass. The honest way to report it is that the pre-registered rule said no, and a revised rule said yes. Under the original text, the result is a fail. I'd also treat 0.8 as unproven. With 12 notes, two runs each and three judges, I'd want to see the spread before calling it a real gain. Henrik's "barely anything" is not unreasonable.

**3. The judging was not independent.**
Dr. Achterberg wrote v2.4 and was one of three judges. Even blinded, the author knows the case types and likely recognises her own phrasing and structure. Her scores should not count toward a decision about her own work. At minimum, re-score without her. Also check whether the A/B relabelling held up, because a table is an obvious format tell.

**4. The carprofen sanity check tells you nothing in v2.4's favour.**
v2.4 answered a quick question in two sentences and v2.3 produced a full discharge sheet. That is a difference in behaviour on a task that isn't the product's job, and it's an anecdote with one sample. It could even be a point against v2.3's judgement, but it says nothing about discharge-note quality. Also note that the missed carprofen dose is one of v2.4's three worked examples, so it is not a fresh test. It's the very case the new prompt was taught. Don't count it as evidence.

**5. What would change my answer.**
- Fix the table so frequency is copied exactly from the source note, for instance by having the prompt restate frequency in words next to each row. Then re-run on fresh notes, not the same 12, and include twice-daily, three-times-daily and every-other-day drugs on purpose.
- Make the pharmacy tech's line-by-line check a named, binding gate: zero dosing errors, not "no worse than".
- Keep the clarity bar at its original text for this round, or state both readings openly: "0.8 against the original 1.0 bar, 0.5 as later revised." Better still, set the revised bar before the next run.
- Score with judges who didn't write v2.4, and have them see outputs in a consistent format.
- Add a small restraint test: a trivial note where a short instruction is right, and check each version doesn't over-produce.

**What to say to the clinics:** v2.3 stays live for now. v2.4 showed promise on clarity but a dosing-frequency issue in the new medication table needs fixing before release. Give a re-test date instead of Monday.

One caution about assumptions: I'm relying on your summary. If the two once-daily errors turn out to be mis-transcribed source notes and not the prompt's fault, that changes the diagnosis, but you'd still want to see it proved before shipping.
