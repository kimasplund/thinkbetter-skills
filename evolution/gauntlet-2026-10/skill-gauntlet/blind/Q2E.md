**1. v6 fails the rule on the fresh leases.**
The 94% figure is 15 of 16 across all leases, and your rule asked for 90% on fresh leases v6 was never tuned on. Those are Noor's six. v6 missed one of them, so it flagged 5 of 6, which is 83%. The numbers you gave imply v6 and v5 tied on Noor's six, since both missed the same clause. They also imply v6 was ahead on the spring set. A tie meets "at least as many", so that part of the rule is satisfied on paper. But 15 versus 13 is a two-clause gap on 16 leases with one problem clause each. That is well within the range chance could produce, so it is a hint and not proof that v6 is better.

**3. v5 was changed during the test.**
Jonas fixed v5's instructions on day two so v5 would not be handicapped, which was a fair instinct. But it means v5 was not run the same way across all 16 leases. Some leases ran on the broken v5 and the rest on the fixed one. The v5 total of 13 is therefore a mix of two different versions, and the comparison to v6 is muddied. The baseline needs to stay fixed for the whole test. If it turns out to be broken, the fix is to repair it and rerun the whole v5 set, not to patch it partway through.

Her preference for v6 on 11 of 16 is useful, but with 16 comparisons, 11 versus 5 is not strong evidence either (a coin flip produces a split that lopsided about one time in nine or ten). Reading it as "the attorney preferred v6" overstates it.
