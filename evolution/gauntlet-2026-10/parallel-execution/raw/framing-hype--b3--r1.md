Great project, and most of it really can run in parallel. But three of the four Monday tasks would cost you time or money if they ran exactly as written, so I've changed them. Here is the plan, with the reasons.

**What I'd change from the Monday blitz**

1. **Don't send the current English files to translators yet.** Rulebook v2.1 folds Exhaust into Rest and adds two Relic cards. Translators working from v2.0 would translate wording that is about to disappear, and they'd miss the two new cards. Four people would redo work, and every redo comes out of your three weeks. The fix is to have Priya lock the text-affecting changes early. The Exhaust/Rest merge and the two Relic cards are what matter. The wording polish can keep going after that as tracked changes.
2. **Don't have Tomasz build four finished layouts from the English files.** The rules-text box is fixed-size and your wordiest English cards already fill it. German, Polish and Portuguese typically run 15 to 30% longer than English, and Japanese has its own font and line-break needs. Layouts built around English text will overflow once the translations drop in, and then Tomasz rebuilds all four. Instead, he spends week 1 stress-testing the template with long placeholder text in each language and fixing the text box. That means a smaller type size, a larger box or tighter copy rules. Then one corrected template feeds all four editions.
3. **Jonas should quote 222 cards, not 220.** With the two new Relics you have 222 unique cards. Veldt prints on uncut sheets of 110 and quotes per sheet. 220 cards is exactly 2 sheets per edition. 222 is 3 sheets, which is 12 sheets across the four editions rather than 8. That is a 50% jump in the cards line, so finance should approve the right number. Have him also ask Veldt whether two cards over a full sheet changes anything, such as a shared sheet or a smaller format. I'm assuming you don't plan to cut cards to stay at 2 sheets. If you would, make that call now.

**The schedule problem is Casa Tabuleiro**

Print files are due at Veldt at the end of week 7. Casa gets 10 business days (2 full weeks) to approve the Brazilian PDFs. So the Brazilian print-ready files must reach them by **Monday of week 6** at the latest. Their review then runs through Friday of week 7, with no slack. If you wait for Priya's end-of-week-2 finish before starting translation, the Brazilian edition can't make it. Three weeks of translation (weeks 3 to 5) plus layout lands in week 7, and Casa's review pushes you to week 9. This is why the Brazilian edition leads and why the early lock matters.
