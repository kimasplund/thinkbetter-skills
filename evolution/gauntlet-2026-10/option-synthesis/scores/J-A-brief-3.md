Notes:
- F1H: Clean, compact and correct on cost, the 12 March sailing, ISPM 15 crates and the bridge. Says the van timing is "workable", but two sequential 5-day round trips can't both land inside 14-18 April, so the counter-case is misjudged. Hedges unnecessarily on whether loaded 7.5 t vans can cross the bridge.
- W9W: Has the most practical content. It correctly sees that the second van trip lands on the 19th or 20th, proposes using the vans only for the last leg over the bridge, gives a good decision rule and sets dated tripwires. However, it leaks method ("compared in one context, not blind", "interested party", "the option I didn't pick"), it gives invented percentages (30%/20%/40%) and "four drivers" as if they were findings, and it contradicts itself on keeping groupage.
- X9V: The key points are buried under heavy scaffolding: pre-registration, a "seal", an adoption rule, a noise band and scoring tables. Its van timing arithmetic is off (it has the second trip arriving on the 18th inside a 5-day round trip), and many cost and probability figures are weakly sourced. It adds a sensible haulier option and flags duties and taxes, but this is far out of proportion for the reader.
- A3M: Concise, correct and actionable. It covers the crates (with three concrete fixes), the bridge questions to put to Quintell, the van payload, and weight versus volume charging on groupage. It does not explicitly test whether the van timing works, and it says little about what would change the call.
