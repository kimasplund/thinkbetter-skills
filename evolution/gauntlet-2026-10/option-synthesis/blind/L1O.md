**Short answer:** Rent generators again this year, but change the campsite and medical setup to cope with the new noise condition. Start the grid connection now for next year.

**1. The grid can't be ready this year.**
You are 11 weeks out and the lead time is 14 weeks, so the connection arrives about 3 weeks after the festival, even if you sign today. It also wouldn't cover everything. 400 A three-phase is roughly 275 kVA, or about 220 kW usable. Your peak is about 520 kW, so you would still need generators or batteries for the main site.

It is still worth doing for next year. Your current spend is about £25,200 hire plus £16,660 fuel, which is about £41,900 a year. Your half of the connection is £19,000. Even if it only displaces the campsite and part of the main-site load, it pays back within a few years on a ten-year lease. Accept Gwen's offer and ask the operator to confirm the date in writing. Ask whether a temporary supply or a different connection size could be ready sooner. Plan the electrical design so a grid-plus-battery setup can take over from generators later.

**2. The noise condition is the real constraint this year.**
From 23:00 to 07:00 no generator within 150 m of the campsite may run. That is 8 hours. The medical tent still needs uninterrupted power, and the campsite draws about 45 kW. You have two ways to meet this.

- **Move the sets.** Put generators more than 150 m from the campsite and run cable to the campsite and medical tent. This avoids buying new kit but costs cable, distribution and labour. It also depends on your site layout, so check whether the council measures 150 m to the campsite boundary or to the nearest tent. Medical power needs a redundant feed or a small UPS so a cable fault doesn't cut it.
- **Run the campsite on batteries overnight.** This is where hybrids help. 45 kW for 8 hours is about 360 kWh. One pack is 120 kWh, and you can't use all of it. At about 80% usable (my assumption), that's roughly 96 kWh per pack, so you need 4 hybrids on the campsite to carry the night with some margin. The packs must be fully charged by 23:00, and the generators can't recharge them during the silent window. Two hybrids, which is the same number of sets as last year, would last only about 5 hours. The sets would then have to restart before 07:00 or breach the licence.

**3. The hybrids' claims.**
Hybrids cost £5,100 against £4,200, which is £900 more per set. Gantry's 55% fuel saving would take 9,800 L down to about 4,400 L. That is about £9,200 saved at £1.70 a litre. Six hybrids would cost about £30,600 plus about £7,500 of fuel, around £38,100 in total. That is roughly £3,800 less than your current £41,900. The saving is small and rests on a claim you haven't tested. I'd treat the 55% as the best case for steady, low loads, not a guarantee for stage and bar peaks. Ask Gantry to put the fuel saving in writing, or to price fuel at a guaranteed rate, before you rely on it.

Six hybrids at up to 180 kW each is also far more capacity than the 520 kW peak needs. You don't need hybrids everywhere. You need them where the noise rule bites.

**4. Recommended plan for this year.**
- **Campsite and medical:** 4 hybrids, about £20,400. This gives about 480 kWh nominal (about 380 kWh usable at my assumed 80%), enough for the 360 kWh night with a thin margin. Ask for a fifth pack or a battery-only unit as spare if the budget allows. Charge fully by 23:00. Generators run in the day and evening only, and they sit as far from the campsite as practical.
- **Main site:** 4 conventional 200 kVA sets, about £16,800, for the stages and bars. They are probably above 150 m from the campsite, so the rule shouldn't apply to them. If they aren't, move them. 4 sets at about 160 kW each give roughly 640 kW at a 0.8 power factor, so about 20% headroom over the 520 kW peak. Many stages stop around midnight, so the main-site load is lower late at night. Confirm your own load profile and whether you want a spare set.
- **Total:** roughly £37,000 of hire, plus fuel. Fuel should fall from last year's level because the campsite sets run less, but I can't give a reliable figure without testing.

**5. Before the end of the month, get these from Gantry in writing.**
1. Usable battery capacity per pack, and how long it takes to charge.
2. Runtime at 45 kW, and a load test.
3. The noise level when the engines start, and the time to switch from battery to engine without a break for the medical tent.
4. A spare unit and a replacement time if a set fails.
5. A discount for taking 4 hybrids, since you are the buyer.

Ask the council whether the 150 m rule means measured from the generator to the nearest tent, because that decides whether you need to move anything.

**Assumptions:** I don't know your site layout, which sets are within 150 m, or the evening load curve. I'm assuming 80% usable battery. If your campsite load is above 45 kW at any point overnight, add packs. Check the numbers with your electrical contractor before you sign.
