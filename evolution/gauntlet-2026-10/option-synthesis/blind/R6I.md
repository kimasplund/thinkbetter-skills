Use 2:1 (two parts sugar to one part water, by weight or volume) for fall feeding.

Why: in fall the bees need to store syrup as winter food. At 2:1 they have to remove less water to cure and cap it, so they spend less energy and get it stored faster before it turns cold. 1:1 is better in spring, when you want to stimulate brood rearing and comb building.

Practical tips:
- Feed while daytime temperatures are still reliably above about 10 C (50 F). Bees won't take syrup well once it's cold.
- Dissolve the sugar in hot (not boiling) water and let it cool before feeding. Don't scorch it.
- Use plain white cane sugar only. No brown sugar, raw sugar or honey from unknown sources.
- Keep feeders clean and watch for robbing. Reduce entrances if the bees from your 12 hives start robbing weaker ones.
- Check hive weights or inspect the stores. A common target is roughly 18 to 27 kg (40 to 60 lb) total hive weight, depending on your climate and colony size. Colder, longer winters need more.

Assumption: you're in a temperate climate with a real winter. If you're somewhere mild, the stores target will be lower, and local beekeepers can tell you the right number.
