# Options: powering Thistledown
**Decide by:** end of this month · **Objective:** three nights with zero licence breaches, zero medical-tent power breaks and total power cost at or under £52,000 · **Horizon:** two weeks after the festival closes
**Assumptions I'm working from:** the 45 kW overnight figure includes the medical tent; the festival runs three nights (23:00 to 07:00 each); the pain threshold is about £10,000 over last year's roughly £41,900 (hire £25,200 plus fuel £16,660); the arena carries about 460 kW of the 520 kW peak; "usable" battery energy is about 80% of the 120 kWh label (96 kWh per pack); Gantry's 55% fuel claim is unverified; I have no quotes from any other supplier.

## The recommendation
Do not keep last year's plan, and do not take the grid or all six hybrids this year. Hire a mixed fleet: two of Gantry's hybrids at the campsite edge and four conventional sets for the arena, with the campsite's overnight load cut to about 20 kW, and make the order conditional on three written confirmations that you can get this week. Start the grid conversation with Gwen now, but as next year's project.

Why, in brief:
- Last year's setup breaks the new licence condition as it stands. Two running diesels sit at the campsite edge, and the condition bans any generator within 150 m of the campsite from running 23:00 to 07:00.
- The grid cannot arrive in time: a 14-week lead time against 11 weeks left lands three weeks after the festival. It also would not carry the site. 400 A three-phase is roughly 275 kVA, or about 250 kW, against a 520 kW peak. "No generators, ever" is not what that quote buys, though it would take over the campsite and base load in later years.
- Six hybrids as pitched do not clear the night. The campsite needs about 360 kWh (45 kW for 8 hours). Two packs give about 190 kWh, which lasts around 4 hours at 45 kW. After that an engine restarts within 150 m of the tents. At 20 to 24 kW the same two packs last the full 8 hours. So the cut in overnight load is what makes the hybrids work.
- Hybrid packs also give the medical tent a battery behind its supply, which is better continuity than a diesel with a changeover switch, provided Gantry confirms zero-break transfer in writing.

Chance this misses the objective by the horizon: ~20%, because the plan rests on a measured 24 kW night load nobody has yet measured, an unfamiliar kit commissioning, and the council's reading of "running". Reference class: festivals adding battery hybrids under a new noise condition usually hit one commissioning snag, and a snag at the campsite is what breaches the licence. Disanalogy: you have a fallback (relocated diesel) that most of those events did not price in.

Compared in one context, not blind. I generated this recommendation and I favour it, so treat the scoring as an interested party's. I could not start a separate context to attack it; that attack has not been run, and you should have someone who did not read this report try to break the plan before you sign.

## What the choice had to satisfy
Written before I listed any option. Nothing below was added or changed after the options appeared. I could not commit or hash it in this conversation, so it is recorded here rather than independently sealed.

- **Must pass (violation removes the option):** no engine within 150 m of the campsite running 23:00 to 07:00; medical tent powered without a break all night; delivered and commissioned within the 11 weeks; covers the 520 kW peak.
- **Must be at least as good as the incumbent's compliant form:** medical continuity (including a single-unit failure); total cost within the £52,000 ceiling; reversibility (no spend that outlasts the festival unless chosen deliberately).
- **Compete on (1/3/5/7, anchored):** cost of hire plus fuel (7 = at least £5k under last year; 5 = within £2.5k of last year; 3 = £2.5k to £7.5k over; 1 = more than £7.5k over); noise and complaint exposure (7 = campsite silent all night and fewer engine hours; 5 = engines at least 150 m away; 3 = engines near the boundary; 1 = engines audible in the campsite); what it teaches for next year (7 = tests hybrids and settles the grid case; 3 = tests one thing; 1 = nothing new); delivery and crew risk (7 = proven kit, known supplier; 5 = one new item on two units; 3 = new kit on every unit; 1 = unquoted or unconfirmed).
- **Adoption:** the incumbent as written fails the noise condition, so its strongest legal form stands in for it: the same six diesels with the campsite pair moved at least 150 m away and cabled in. A challenger replaces that form only if it passes every must-pass item, is at least as good on every guard, leads on value by 4 points or more with no value line more than 2 below, and its failure analysis leaves no unmitigated fatal flaw. A lead under 4 points is inconclusive.
- **Combinations:** a combination built after the scoring is adopted only after its own test against each parent and the baseline.

## How each finalist dies
### Relocate (the competent default)
**Strongest case:** nothing new to learn. Proven kit, known supplier, and the condition is met by distance. Cable for 45 kW over 150 to 200 m is routine.
**Arithmetic:** £25,200 hire, about £16,700 fuel, plus cable, distribution and a battery or UPS for the medical tent, which I put at £5,000 to £8,000 (weakly sourced, no quote). Total about £47,000 to £50,000, inside the ceiling but over last year.
**Killing modes:** the cable and distribution bill runs past £8,000 and the plan stops fitting the ceiling (~20%, because I priced it without a quote); the medical tent sees a break at the changeover when a relocated set trips (~15%, because a diesel with a transfer switch has no ride-through unless the battery is sized and tested); the cable run is not laid and tested before the gates open, with hire crews stretched in the build week (~10%, because ordinary slippage). Success-kill: it works, so the fuel bill and the engine hours stay as they are and the grid question never gets revisited while the lease runs.
**Chance it misses the objective:** ~30%, reference class: a like-for-like repeat with one added cable run, which usually lands but rarely on first-quote cost. Disanalogy: this event has no spare day in the build week.
**Most dangerous:** the medical break, since it is silent until the night it happens. **Hidden assumption:** that 45 kW overnight splits so the medical tent can sit on a small battery. **Cheapest falsifier:** ask Gantry for last year's hour-meter and load readings for the campsite pair, plus a site walk with an electrician; £0 to £300, 3 days; a medical-plus-lighting load above about 25 kW kills the small-battery idea.

### Six hybrids
**Strongest case:** the vendor's claim, if true, makes it the cheapest: £30,600 hire plus about £7,500 fuel (a 55% cut from £16,660 saves roughly £9,200) is about £38,100, some £3,800 under last year, with quieter running everywhere.
**Arithmetic:** at a 30% fuel saving rather than 55% the total is about £42,300, level with last year. The night arithmetic is the problem: two packs at about 96 kWh cannot carry 45 kW for 8 hours.
**Killing modes:** the campsite load stays at about 45 kW, the packs empty around 03:00 and an engine starts within 150 m, so the condition is breached (~30%, because the arithmetic gives about 4 hours of cover); the fuel saving is far below 55% because arena units run hard all day and gain little from a battery (~20%, because the saving is mostly from low-load hours and is a vendor claim); six unfamiliar units are commissioned in the same week and two have teething faults on opening night (~15%, because nobody on your crew has run this kit).
**Chance it misses the objective:** ~40%, reference class: first-time adoption of vendor-pitched hybrid kit at a mid-size event, where partial fuel savings and one commissioning fault are normal. Disanalogy: Gantry will have engineers on call, though that is unpriced.
**Most dangerous:** the night shortfall, since it ends in a licence breach at the worst moment. **Hidden assumption:** that "engine off when the battery can carry the load" means all night. **Cheapest falsifier:** ask Gantry for the usable kWh per pack and run one pack against a 20 to 25 kW load bank or the real campsite load for 8 hours at their yard; about £0 to £500, 5 days; an engine start before 07:00 kills it.

### Mixed fleet (the staged option)
**Strongest case:** puts the new kit exactly where the condition bites and keeps proven diesels where it does not. £10,200 for two hybrids plus £16,800 for four diesels is £27,000 hire. Fuel I estimate at around 85% of last year (about £14,200, my assumption), so a total near £41,200: level with last year and well inside the ceiling.
**Killing modes:** the night load cannot be held to about 24 kW, so the two packs run flat and an engine restarts at the campsite (~20%, because the cut from 45 kW is untested and the showers are the likely big draw); commissioning of two unfamiliar units slips or one faults (~15%, because of ordinary new-kit risk, limited to two units); the council reads "running" as "present and energised" and does not accept an engine-off hybrid sitting at the campsite (~10%, because the wording is unclear and nobody has asked).
**Chance it misses the objective:** ~20%, reference class: staged adoption of a new technology on a minority of units, which commonly delivers the headline benefit with fewer faults than a full roll-out. Disanalogy: the load cut touches paying campers, which most such pilots did not.
**Most dangerous:** the night shortfall. **Hidden assumption:** that a metered 20 to 24 kW overnight load is achievable with showers off. **Cheapest falsifier:** meter last year's campsite circuits or, failing that, add up the showers, lighting and medical tent from their nameplates; 1 day, about £0; a load above 30 kW that cannot be cut kills it.

### Managed service
**Strongest case:** a specialist already runs hybrid and battery sites and carries the commissioning risk contractually.
**Arithmetic:** none possible, since there is no quote. I have not scored it.
**Killing modes:** no capacity at 11 weeks' notice (~20%, because specialists book peak-season events early); a price that breaks the ceiling (~15%, because managed pricing typically adds a service margin); a handover gap when a new contractor learns your site in days (~10%, because of ordinary mobilisation risk).
**Chance it misses the objective:** ~30%, weakly sourced, reference class: switching power contractors on short notice.
**Most dangerous:** the late handover. **Hidden assumption:** that a better supplier exists at a similar price. **Cheapest falsifier:** two quotes requested this week; £0, 5 days; no quote under £52,000 with the night condition met kills it.

### Dark campsite
**Strongest case:** cheapest and quietest. If only the medical tent and safety lighting run at night (about 15 kW), one rented battery of about 120 kWh holds for 8 hours and every diesel can be switched off at 23:00.
**Killing modes:** the council or the medical contractor treats a dark campsite as a safety or welfare breach (~30%, because campsite lighting and toilets are normally licence expectations); camper complaints about no showers or phone charging (~15%, because the campsite is where the complaints came from last year); the battery unit is not available or the single unit is a single point of failure for the medical tent (~10%, because one unit with no spare).
**Chance it misses the objective:** ~30%, reference class: events that cut services overnight to meet noise conditions, which usually keep the licence but take a hit on reviews.
**Most dangerous:** the single battery unit under the medical tent. **Hidden assumption:** that 15 kW is enough for medical and safety lighting. **Cheapest falsifier:** ask the medical contractor for their night load in writing and ask the council licensing officer what lighting they expect; £0, 3 days; a requirement above about 25 kW kills it.

**What the whole field assumes:** that last year's two numbers, 520 kW peak and 45 kW overnight, are right and that the council will read "running" as an engine that is turned on. Every option is sized from the first and every night-time design from the second. The council's reading is untested for every hybrid option.

## The decision rule
**IF** by the end of the month the council confirms in writing that an engine-off hybrid at the campsite is not "running" under the condition, **AND** Gantry confirms in writing the usable kWh per pack, zero-break transfer for the medical tent and a fuel-use estimate from your load profile, **AND** the 8-hour test ends with no engine start, **THEN** sign the mixed fleet (two hybrids at the campsite, four diesels at the arena). **ELSE** sign Relocate: order the cable, distribution and a medical battery that same week, and keep the hybrids for next year. If the test passes only with a load above 24 kW, add the remote top-up set before signing.
**Cheapest decisive experiment:** run one hybrid pack against the measured campsite load for 8 hours at Gantry's yard, with the council question and two other supplier quotes in parallel. About £0 to £500 and 5 days; an engine start before 07:00 flips the call to Relocate.
**Runner-up:** Relocate; switch if the council will not accept an engine-off hybrid or the test fails.
**If passing over Relocate was the mistake:** by the horizon, the packs ran flat on night two, an engine started at 03:00 and the council cited the new condition; the grid conversation was then harder because the licence was already in question. That resolves into the ELSE branch, which is why the council letter and the test are conditions of signing and not follow-ups.

For the grid: do not commit Gwen's half-share (£19,000) until the network operator confirms the quote's validity, whether a deposit is due, and what 400 A really covers. Take the decision at the post-festival readout, with the hybrid results in hand, and include whether the hybrid packs can be kept to cover the peaks the grid cannot.
