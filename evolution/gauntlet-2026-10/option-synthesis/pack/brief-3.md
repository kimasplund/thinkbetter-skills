I run Wexcombe Joinery, a nine-person furniture workshop, and we've won the fit-out of the Casa Liria, a 40-room boutique hotel in the old harbour quarter of Velaro, across the water. It's our biggest job ever, and I need to book the freight this week.

The load is beds, desks, wardrobes and the reception joinery: about 52 m³ and 9 tonnes once crated. We build our own crates from offcuts, as we always have. Everything will be finished and crated by 3 March.

The hotel's contractor wants it all on site between 14 and 18 April. Before the 14th there's nowhere to put it, because they're sealing the floors; after the 18th we pay £1,200 a day in delay charges. They also asked us to warn our drivers that the only bridge into the harbour quarter has a 7.5-tonne limit.

Our forwarder, Quintell, has provisionally booked us on groupage, sharing a container with other loads: £96 per m³ door to door, 5 to 7 weeks in transit, consolidations leaving weekly. They also quoted a full 40 ft container (67 m³) at £4,150 door to door, 4 to 5 weeks, sailing 5, 12 or 19 March. And they sent their standard checklist for Velaro: mostly customs paperwork, plus a line saying wooden packaging must be heat-treated and stamped.

My partner, Joss, wants to take it over ourselves in our two 7.5-tonne box vans on the ferry: two round trips with both vans, about £2,400 per van per trip, five days per round trip. He says at least we'd be in control.

Which way should we ship it?
