# Options: shipping the Casa Liria fit-out to Velaro
**Decide by:** this week (Quintell's groupage booking is only provisional) · **Objective:** all 52 m³ / 9 t on site at Casa Liria between 14 and 18 April, packaging accepted at the destination, at the lowest total landed cost · **Horizon:** 18 April
**Assumptions I'm working from:** the 4 to 5 weeks on the container quote is counted from the sailing date, not from collection (if it is counted from collection, the dates below move earlier and the container looks better still). The £1,200 a day delay charge runs from 19 April. Your crated cargo is ready on 3 March and misses that week's groupage cut-off, so groupage realistically leaves on 10 March. Joss's £2,400 per van per trip covers ferry, fuel and driver time. The "7.5-tonne" bridge limit is a gross vehicle weight limit. Quintell's prices exclude destination duties and taxes (they say nothing either way). Anything I could not source from your message is marked weakly sourced.

## The recommendation
**Adopt the full 40 ft container on the 12 March sailing (£4,150), but only after Quintell confirms three things in writing this week, and replace your offcut crates before 3 March. Keep the groupage booking provisional until the container booking is confirmed in writing, then cancel it. Do not take the 5 or 19 March sailings.**

- Groupage has the wrong shape for this job. Its transit spread, 5 to 7 weeks, is two weeks wide. Your delivery window is five days wide. Leaving 10 March, it lands anywhere from 14 to 28 April, so about two chances in three it is late, at £1,200 a day. It also costs more than the container: 52 m³ × £96 = £4,992 against £4,150.
- The 12 March container lands 9 to 16 April on the quoted 4 to 5 weeks: at worst two days inside the end of your window, and mostly a few days early, which is cheap to hold and far cheaper than late.
- Joss's plan buys control at £9,600 (four loads at £2,400) against £4,150, with both vans and at least two of your nine people away for about ten days, and with no slack at the end of the window. His instinct about control has value, but only as a rescue for the most critical items, not as the main plan (see the combination below).
- Chance this misses the objective by 18 April: **~20%**, because a container being rolled to a later sailing or slipping past the quoted range is the ordinary way these go wrong, and you have only two days of margin after the slowest quoted transit. Reference class: sole-use container sailings booked a month ahead (my judgment, weakly sourced). Disanalogy: I know nothing of the route or the season, and rolling is more common at peak times.
- This was compared in one context, not blind, with no separate reader for the final check.
- I proposed and favour this recommendation, and the check on it was done by me rather than by a fresh reader. Treat that as a reason to send the three questions below, which are the independent check.

**Two things in your message matter more than the price difference, and both apply to every option.**

1. **Your crates.** Quintell's checklist says wooden packaging must be heat-treated and stamped. That is the international wood-packaging standard (ISPM 15, IPPC/FAO, 2002, revised 2009): solid wood packaging must be heat-treated or fumigated and carry the registered stamp, applied by an authorised marker. Crates you build from offcuts cannot carry that stamp. Processed sheet material such as plywood is normally exempt from the standard, but check that with Quintell and Velaro. If crates are refused at the border, the cargo can be held, re-packed at your cost or sent back, and the 18 April date is gone whichever way you ship.
2. **The bridge.** The only bridge into the harbour quarter is limited to 7.5 tonnes. A loaded 40 ft container on a lorry or a groupage delivery truck is several times that. Whichever sea option you choose, the last leg must be done by 7.5 tonne or smaller vehicles, probably at least two loads, and "door to door" may not include that. Your own vans are fine on the bridge only if loaded weight, van plus cargo, stays at or under the plate weight. At about 2.25 t per load across four loads they do.

## What the choice had to satisfy
Written down before I assessed any option. I could not commit or hash this here (no shell), so the seal is this message: the text below is the full pre-registration and nothing was added or loosened afterwards.

- **Must all be true (violation removes an option):** goods on site between 14 and 18 April, any early arrival held off site by the carrier; all wooden packaging heat-treated and stamped, or non-wood, or exempt sheet material; every vehicle crossing the bridge at or under 7.5 tonnes gross; cargo not available before 3 March.
- **Guards (a challenger must be at least as good as groupage):** chance of arriving by 18 April; expected delay charges plus holding cost; how well the destination requirements (packaging, customs, bridge, operator permissions) are confirmed in writing before committing.
- **Value (scored 1, 3, 5 or 7, anchored):** total landed cost (freight plus expected delay and holding: under £5,000 = 7, £5,000 to £6,500 = 5, £6,500 to £9,000 = 3, over £9,000 = 1); slack between the slowest likely arrival and 18 April (3 or more days = 7, 2 days = 5, 0 to 1 day = 3, negative = 1); disruption to the workshop (none = 7, a few hours of a few people = 5, days of one or two people = 3, days of the vans and two or more people = 1); ability to change course after booking (free until days before = 7, a fee = 5, most of the cost lost = 3, none = 1); visibility and control of where the cargo is (own people at every handover = 7, live tracking and one named contact = 5, milestone updates = 3, none = 1).
- **Adoption rule:** a challenger replaces the current groupage booking only if all must-be-true items pass, every guard is at least equal, the value total is at least 4 points higher, no single value is more than two rungs (4 points) lower, and its failure analysis leaves no fatal flaw that has no fix.
- **Noise band:** totals within 2 points of each other are not separated, and no winner is forced.
- **Combination rule:** a combination is adopted only after its own test and only if it beats each of its parents.
- **What would change the call:** Quintell cannot confirm the last leg on 7.5 tonne vehicles or hold early arrivals until 14 April; the proposed packaging is refused and no compliant alternative can be ready by 3 March; or a haulier quote comes in under the container's landed cost.

## How each finalist dies
### Groupage (current booking)
**Strongest case:** cheapest to book today, already provisionally arranged, the consolidator carries the paperwork, and you pay only for the volume you ship. Weekly departures give choice.
**Arithmetic first:** £4,992 for 52 m³. Leaving 10 March, 5 to 7 weeks lands 14 to 28 April; the window is 14 to 18 April, so about one chance in three of landing inside it, none early, about two in three late by an average of about 5 days, an expected delay cost of roughly £4,000. This option is dominated by a single risk: the transit spread is nearly three times the window.
**Killing modes:** the arithmetic above (more than half of all failures) · cargo held because another shipper's goods in the shared box fail inspection (~15%, because a hold on any one load can stop the whole consolidation) · a delivery truck that cannot cross the bridge, so a re-handling stop that nobody priced (~10%, because the bridge limit is far below any normal delivery truck).
**Chance it misses the objective:** ~70%, reference class: consolidated sea freight to a fixed five-day window. Disanalogy: the weekly consolidation could run toward the quick end of its range, which I cannot see from here.
**Most dangerous:** the date spread. It is silent until the cargo is already at sea, and nothing can be done then. **Hidden assumption:** that "5 to 7 weeks" is a promise rather than a range. **Cheapest falsifier:** ask Quintell for the last six departures' actual transit times on this route, an email, free, one day; if the spread is under 10 days it still misses a five-day window, so it cannot kill the finding, only soften it.

### Full 40 ft container, 12 March sailing
**Strongest case:** £842 cheaper than groupage, a week or two faster, sole use so no one else's cargo can hold yours, 15 m³ spare, and a sailing date that lets you plan.
**Arithmetic first:** 52 m³ in 67 m³ is 78% full; 9 t is far below any container's weight limit; £4,150 ÷ 52 m³ = £80/m³. 12 March plus 4 to 5 weeks is 9 to 16 April. That is up to 5 days before the window and at most 2 days inside it, with two days of margin before 19 April. It does not fall short on its own numbers.
**Killing modes:** the container is rolled to the 19 March sailing or slips beyond the quoted 5 weeks, landing 16 to 23 April (~40% of failures, because you have only two days of margin and the weekly sailing means a roll costs a full week) · the quoted "door to door" does not include devanning and delivery over the bridge by 7.5 tonne vehicles, adding cost and a day or two at the worst moment (~20%, because the limit makes a normal container lorry impossible and the quote doesn't mention it) · the cargo arrives on time but the contractor's floor sealing overruns, so it sits on a depot you now pay for (~10%, because floor-sealing schedules slip on most sites; this one is a success that still costs).
**Chance it misses the objective:** ~20%, reference class: sole-use container sailings booked about a month ahead (weakly sourced). Disanalogy: the route and season are unknown; peak weeks roll more.
**Most dangerous:** the last-leg gap. It is silent until delivery day and irreversible by then. **Hidden assumption:** that Quintell's "door to door" covers a bridge-compliant delivery and a hold until 14 April. **Cheapest falsifier:** email Quintell three questions (below); free, one working day; the result that kills the cheap version is "last leg not included" with an add-on over about £1,500, or "no hold available".

### Own vans on the ferry (Joss)
**Strongest case:** you decide when each load goes, you are the ones at every handover, the vans are legal on the bridge when loaded under their plate weight, and nothing waits on a sailing date.
**Arithmetic first:** 4 loads × £2,400 = £9,600, £5,450 (131%) more than the container, before the two or more people away for about ten days and the vans out of service. Because 9 t is too heavy for two van loads, the work needs two round trips. To deliver inside 14 to 18 April, the first trip must arrive at about 14 April and return by 16 April, and the second must leave immediately and arrive on 18 April. There is no slack: one ferry disruption puts you at £1,200 a day.
**Killing modes:** the second trip arrives after 18 April, from a ferry cancellation, breakdown or customs queue on a schedule with no spare day (~40% of failures) · a key person or van is lost: a driver is ill, or a van fails, in the busiest month (~20%, because with two vans and two trips every unit is single-point) · permissions you do not yet know you need: operator licensing for international haulage with 7.5 t goods vehicles, driver hours recording, the vehicle's own entry and exit paperwork, and whether the wood-packaging rule bites on road imports too (~15%, because none of it is in your message, so the planning risk is unknown rather than low).
**Chance it misses the objective:** ~30%, reference class: small firms doing their own cross-water haulage on a fixed deadline (weakly sourced). Disanalogy: Joss may have done this before and know the ferry and paperwork well.
**Most dangerous:** the permissions, which stay silent until a customs officer or a roadside check raises them, with the cargo already loaded. **Hidden assumption:** that you are legally entitled to carry your own goods abroad in these vehicles. **Cheapest falsifier:** ask Joss for the operator licence and what the vans' last international trip needed; free, one day; the result that kills it is "we have never done this and don't know".

### Hired road haulier
**Strongest case:** professional drivers, their own vehicles and paperwork, a delivery date you choose within the window, 7.5 t rigid vehicles that fit the bridge.
**Arithmetic first:** unpriced. Your own vans' £2,400 per load suggests a haulier would charge at least similar per load, so 3 to 4 loads imply roughly £7,000 to £10,000 (weakly sourced). If so, it costs more than the container by a wide margin; it competes only if a quote lands under about £5,500.
**Killing modes:** no vehicles free in the peak April weeks, or a price far above the container (~40%, because small-vehicle capacity is booked early in spring and I assume the price) · the same permission and packaging checks as Joss's plan, passed to the haulier but still your cargo and your crates (~20%) · damage from re-handling in a groupage-style multi-drop (~10%).
**Chance it misses the objective:** ~20% on delivery date, higher on cost; the number is unreliable until a quote exists.
**Most dangerous:** price shock after you've lost the container sailing. **Hidden assumption:** a haulier exists at a price near the container's. **Cheapest falsifier:** request two quotes today; free, one to two days; the kill is a price over £6,500.

**What the whole field assumes:** that your offcut crates will be accepted at Velaro. Every option carries them, and none of the quotes checks them. As the facts stand, solid-wood crates with no registered stamp fail the stated rule, so this is untested and probably false. It must be settled before choosing between the others.

## The decision rule
**IF** by the end of this week Quintell confirms in writing that (a) the proposed packaging is acceptable at Velaro, (b) the container is devanned and delivered in vehicles of 7.5 t or less with the add-on priced at about £1,500 or less, and (c) an early arrival is held until 14 April, **THEN** book the 12 March container, order the compliant packaging the same day, and cancel groupage only once the container booking is confirmed. **ELSE** if (a) fails and no compliant packaging can be ready by 3 March, no option works unchanged: pad-and-strap with plywood or non-wood frames and ask Quintell to accept that, and do not book anything irreversible until it does. **ELSE** if (b) or (c) fails, take the hired haulier's quote and book the cheaper of the container with the full add-on and the haulier, if the haulier is under about £5,500.
**Cheapest decisive experiment:** one email to Quintell with the three questions, plus two haulier quote requests. Cost £0, one to two working days. The readout that flips the call: any "no" on the last leg or the hold, or a haulier quote under about £5,500.
**Runner-up:** the hired haulier; switch if its quote is under about £5,500 or if Quintell cannot give a written last-leg and hold arrangement.
**If passing over the runner-up was the mistake:** by 18 April the container was rolled to the 19 March sailing and arrived on 22 April, four days of delay charges (£4,800), while a haulier with vehicles free in the window would have delivered on 16 April. That resolves into the standby vans: the 2 April check on the vessel's ETA sends the reception joinery by van, and the rest of the call stands.
