# Options: wintering 600 colonies to deliver 420 graded colonies to Senna Vale on March 1
**Decide by:** Oct 24 (before the late-October haul window) · **Objective:** at least 420 colonies graded (8+ frames) on March 1, at the best net cash after wintering costs · **Horizon:** March 31 (co-op payment)
**Assumptions I'm working from:**
- Your outdoor colonies grade at about 66% of the 600 (range 63-69%). That is your 34-40% losses, plus last spring's 104 of 150 (69%). The cellar grades at 80% and Dunmere at 90%, as reported. Both are other people's figures, not yours.
- "Holds about 500" includes Agnete's 300, so about 200 spaces are free. Unconfirmed.
- Haul, rent, feed and cellar are all paid in October or November out of the $15,000. I wanted to keep at least $4,000 unspent for surprises, so I treated that as a floor.
- Colonies graded beyond 420 earn nothing from this contract. They still have value as colonies. I used Rafe's $150 as a rough replacement price, which is my guess.
- Rafe's colonies would be accepted by the co-op as meeting the grade. Check this.

## The recommendation
**Put about 200 colonies in Agnete's cellar, keep about 400 outdoors, and sign Rafe's offer in writing as a backstop for up to 80 colonies. Don't haul everything to Dunmere, because you can't pay for it.** Spend about $4,800 up front and keep about $10,200 in the bank. Do this week: visit the cellar, get Agnete's space and records, and get Rafe's terms on paper. His offer costs nothing unless you use it.

Why:
- **The haul can't be paid for.** Hauling all 600 costs $37,800 up front against $15,000 of credit. Even the part you could afford is poor value. Each hauled colony costs about $60 more than leaving it outdoors and adds roughly 0.24 graded colonies (90% against about 66%). That is about $250 per extra graded colony.
- **The cellar is the cheapest extra graded colony you can buy.** It costs about $15 more per colony and adds about 0.14 graded colonies, so about $107 per extra graded colony. Rafe's colonies cost $150 each and the haul about $250.
- **Rafe's offer is worth taking, and your reasoning against it is the one thing I'd change.** When you are short, a missing colony costs you the $190 you don't earn and a $40 charge. A $150 colony therefore nets you +$80, not -$40. Buy only to the size of the gap, because colonies past 420 earn nothing. Because it is paid when the co-op pays you, it uses none of your $15,000. It is the only option that needs no cash now.
- **The two cover different failures.** In a normal year the cellar plus outdoors lands about at 424, right on the line, and Rafe tops up the gap. In a bad winter (45% outdoor losses) the cellar softens the blow and Rafe covers the rest. Rafe alone is hurt most when the whole valley is short and he has no colonies to sell.

Expected net on the contract after wintering costs, using the assumptions above (rough):

| | Normal year | Bad winter (45% outdoor loss) |
|---|---|---|
| Current plan | $71.8k | $53.2k |
| Current plan + Rafe | $74.0k | $59.6k |
| **Cellar 200 + Rafe (recommended)** | **$73.7k** | **$64.8k** |

The recommended plan is only about $2k ahead of the current plan in a normal year and about $11.6k ahead in a bad one. Most of its value is protection against the bad winter. It also leaves you with roughly 40 more living colonies at the end (survivors 84% against 63% on the cellar block), worth about $6k at Rafe's price. That is my estimate, not a figure from you.

**Chance it misses the objective by March 31: about 20%.** The usual path is a hard winter that leaves you short and Rafe unable to deliver (about 11%), plus a cellar failure (about 5-10%). The reference class is your own three winters, which all fell short of 420, and Agnete's four years; neither includes a cellar run by you. Your current plan misses about 90% of the time on your own record.

**How it was compared:** in one context, not blind. I generated this plan and favour it, so I am the interested party. I could not start a separate context here, so the final check of this recommendation ran in the same context that chose it. Treat it as weaker than an independent check would be.

## What the choice had to satisfy
Written before any option was generated. I could not hash it here, so this records the order it was written in, not a tamper-proof seal.

**Hard constraints** (breaking one removes the option):
- Up-front cash from the line at most $15,000.
- No new borrowing.
- Cellar space at most what is actually free (assumed 200).
- Broker volume at most 80 at $150, paid at the co-op payment.
- 420 colonies on the trucks on February 20.

**Guards** (a challenger must be at least as good as the current plan):
- Cash left after up-front costs at least $4,000.
- Shortfall in a bad winter no worse than the current plan's.
- No single failure that can wipe out more than about 200 colonies.

**Value** (where options compete):
- Expected net on the contract.
- Net in a bad winter.
- Colonies alive beyond 420.
- Cash left.
- What the winter teaches you.

**Adoption rule.** A challenger replaces the current plan only if it passes every hard constraint and guard, is at least $1,500 better in expected net, is not materially worse in a bad winter, and its failure analysis leaves no unmitigated fatal flaw. A combination is adopted only if it also beats each of its parts. If the leader is within about $1,500 of the runner-up, no pick is forced and the deliverable is the cheapest experiment that separates them.

## How each finalist dies
### Current plan (outdoors in wraps)
**Strongest case:** Known, cheap at $1,800, and you have three winters of experience with it.
**Own arithmetic:** 600 at about 66% gives about 396, under 420 even with a good winter.
**Killing modes:**
- The arithmetic itself: your record puts you short every year (~40%, because even the best winter, 34% loss, leaves under 420 graded).
- A fourth hard winter with losses of 40% or more leaves about 360 graded and a loss of about $6k+ in revenue and charges (~30%, because two of your last three winters were at or near 40%).
- Colonies alive but under 8 frames on March 1 after a cold February (~15%, because survival is not the same as grading).
**Chance it misses the objective:** ~90%. Reference class: your own last three winters. Disanalogy: none, it is your own record.
**Most dangerous:** the arithmetic, since it is silent until February.
**Hidden assumption:** that this year's winter is milder than the last three. **Cheapest falsifier:** none worth buying.

### Outdoors + Rafe
**Strongest case:** No cash up front, a built-in cap on the shortfall, and no change to how you winter.
**Own arithmetic:** expected shortfall about 27 colonies at $150, about $4,000 paid in March.
**Killing modes:**
- Rafe can't supply in a hard winter, when every operator in the valley is short (~30%, because the shortfall that makes you buy is the shortfall that empties his supply).
- Terms change or the co-op won't accept his colonies as meeting the grade (~15%, because the offer is verbal and I haven't seen the co-op's rules).
- The shortfall exceeds 80 (~10%, because a 45% loss leaves about 108 missing).
**Chance it misses the objective:** ~20%. Reference class: your own three winters plus a verbal offer. Disanalogy: the offer is untested.
**Most dangerous:** Rafe's supply failing, because you only learn in February with no time to react.
**Hidden assumption:** Rafe has the colonies when you need them. **Cheapest falsifier:** ask Rafe for the offer in writing, with a named source and a delivery date by February 15. Cost $0, one day. If he won't, this option is dead.

### Cellar for ~200
**Strongest case:** The cheapest extra graded colony ($107), real evidence from four years of Agnete's operation, and independent of the haul.
**Own arithmetic:** 200 at 80% plus 400 at 66% gives about 424, on the line; it still misses in about 4 years of 10.
**Killing modes:**
- Arithmetic: it lands on 420 with no cushion, so any normal variation makes it short (~40%, because the margin over 420 is about 4 colonies and the spread between winters is about 25).
- Cellar failure through poor ventilation, heat or a CO2 build-up killing a block of colonies (~20%, because you have never run a cellar and one operator's four years is a small sample).
- The cellar is not what it was described to be: fewer spaces free, or her figures include colonies that were stronger going in (~15%, because the 16% loss and 80% grade are hers).
**Chance it misses the objective:** ~50%. Reference class: Agnete's four years, 16% loss and 80% grade. Disanalogy: her colonies, her cellar management.
**Most dangerous:** a cellar failure, because it is silent until you open the doors and hits 200 colonies at once.
**Hidden assumption:** your colonies winter as well as hers. **Cheapest falsifier:** visit the cellar and ask for her records. Cost: a morning. The result that kills it: fewer than 150 free spaces, no way to control temperature or ventilation, or records that don't match your grading standard.

### Partial haul (~150 colonies)
**Strongest case:** The lowest loss rate you have heard of, and what most of the valley does.
**Own arithmetic:** the cash limit caps it at about 153 colonies, $9,600 for the haul and rent plus feed. Expected graded about 433, which is not enough cushion.
**Killing modes:**
- The neighbours' 90% does not transfer to your colonies, which are weaker going in (~30%, because their numbers are self-reported and the benefit depends on colony condition).
- A cash squeeze: with $4,000 left, a feed price increase or a truck repair forces a bad choice (~15%, because the margin is thin).
- Dunmere availability or pricing changes, or yards fill if more operators move south (~10%, because a haul that works for half the valley can crowd its own yards).
**Chance it misses the objective:** ~30%. Reference class: neighbours' reported 15% loss and 90% grade. Disanalogy: not your colonies.
**Most dangerous:** the neighbours' figures failing to transfer, because you learn it in February.
**Hidden assumption:** the same as the cellar. **Cheapest falsifier:** ask two neighbours for their loss and grading figures for last year's start-of-winter strength.

**What the whole field assumes:** that the survival and grading rates reported by other operators carry over to your colonies. If your colonies are weak going into winter (mites, old queens, light weights), every option underdelivers together. Test this before you commit: wash-count mites on about 20 colonies this week and weigh a few. If mite loads are high, treat first. I have no source for the thresholds, so use the figures your own beekeeping advisers use.

## The decision rule
**IF** by Oct 24 the cellar has at least 200 free spaces confirmed, you have seen its temperature and ventilation controls, and Rafe has signed a backstop (up to 80 colonies, $150 each, checked against the co-op's 8-frame grade, delivered by Feb 15, paid when the co-op pays you) **THEN** do the recommended plan: move 200 colonies to the cellar when Agnete moves hers, winter 400 outdoors, and keep about $10,000.
**ELSE IF** Rafe won't commit in writing **THEN** use the cash he would have replaced: put the 200 in the cellar and haul about 100 more to Dunmere (about $6,000 more, keeping the $4,000 floor), and expect about 449 graded.
**ELSE IF** the cellar falls through (fewer than 100 spaces, or no controls) **THEN** haul about 150 to Dunmere, winter the rest outdoors, and take Rafe's backstop if he signs.
Don't split the difference by putting a small number into each: with three weak spots at once you inherit all of them.

**Cheapest decisive experiment:** visit the cellar and ask Agnete for her four years of loss and grade figures, and wash-count 20 of your own colonies. About $0, 2 days. It flips the call if free spaces are under 150, there is no way to control temperature or ventilation, or your mite counts are high.
**Runner-up:** outdoors + Rafe; switch if the cellar fails the visit.
**If passing over the runner-up was the mistake:** by March 1 the cellar will have lost colonies, say 30% of the 200, and the 400 outdoors will have come through a mild winter. Rafe's colonies at $150 would have cost less than the $3,600 for the cellar. This resolves into the ELSE branch: in a mild winter outdoors + Rafe is the cheaper path, and the call to take the cellar costs you about $1k.
