Congratulations on the contract. My advice is to skip the southern haul as the main plan. Put as many colonies as the cellar will hold into Agnete's cellar, keep the rest outdoors, and sign Rafe's offer as insurance. You turned the broker down, and I think that was the one wrong call so far.

**Assumptions I'm working from**
- The co-op pays $190 only for colonies that grade, up to 420. I assumed nothing extra is paid above 420.
- The $15,000 has to cover every up-front cost. The broker is paid in March, out of the co-op's payment.
- You haven't told me what share of your outdoor survivors grade. I assumed about 55% of all colonies put out, which is 63% surviving and most of those reaching 8 frames. Please replace this with your own records. It is the number that matters most.
- The cellar's "about 500" may include Agnete's 300, which would leave you about 200 places.

**Why the haul south is the weakest buy**

The neighbours' figures are self-reported and come from people who chose to do it. Your own trip last spring graded 104 of 150, which is 69%, not 90%. The haul also costs $63 a colony, so the full 600 would be $37,800 against $15,000 of credit. You can't afford it.

The table compares each option by what it costs for every extra colony that grades, compared with leaving that colony outdoors:

| Option | Extra cost per colony | Extra graded per colony | Cost per extra graded colony |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cellar | $15 | about 0.25 (80% vs 55%) | about $60 |
| Broker | $150 (paid in March) | 1.0 | $150 |
| South, at your own 69% | $60 | about 0.15 | about $400 |
| South, at the neighbours' 90% | $60 | about 0.35 | about $170 |

The cellar is far cheaper than anything else. The broker comes next, and it needs no cash now. The south is the most expensive route even if the neighbours' numbers are right.

**Why the broker's offer is a good deal**

You pay $150 and the colony earns $190, so you gain $40. Every colony you are short also costs you $40 in penalty, so each colony you buy is worth about $80 against being short. It is also the only option that guarantees the grade rather than hoping for it. Your 420 target against 600 colonies at 55% to 80% grading is close, and 80 guaranteed colonies is about the size of the likely gap.

**What could go wrong**
- **The cellar fails to hold its figures.** The risks are damp, mould, heat build-up or a power cut, and any of them can ruin a winter's colonies in a week without warning. Her 16% loss comes from four years and 300 colonies, which is a sound base. Doubling her stocking is a change she has no record for. Ask her how it is ventilated, and move in 200 rather than all 500 if you can.
- **Rafe doesn't deliver, or the co-op won't take his colonies.** Before you sign, ask the co-op in writing whether substitute colonies count toward the 420 and how they are graded. Get Rafe's offer in writing too, with delivery before February 20, a stated grade standard, and a refund if they fail it. The offer is only worth $150 a colony if both of those hold.
- **Cash.** Don't spend the line down to nothing. Keep about $3,000 for feed top-ups, repairs and a truck breakdown. The plan above leaves about $10,000 unspent before the broker is paid.
- **The 55% outdoor figure is wrong.** If it is 45%, you are 80 short and Rafe covers it exactly. If it is lower, buy the extra from the south (see below).

**Where the south could still fit**

If the cellar holds fewer than 200 colonies, send the leftover colonies south, up to about 100 ($6,300) and no more. That also gives you your own figures for a year when the valley is all doing it. Don't send more until your own trip shows the neighbours' rate.

**Decision rule**
- If Agnete confirms 200 places and the co-op accepts substitutes, take the cellar and the broker, and keep the rest outdoors.
- If the cellar has fewer than 200 places, send the difference south, up to 100.
- If the co-op won't take substitutes, the cellar plus a south group of up to about 100 becomes the plan, and you should expect a shortfall of around 40 to 70. At $40 a colony, that is $1,600 to $2,800 in penalties, which you can absorb.

**The cheapest check before you commit**

Count your colonies this week and note how many are already at 10 frames or more. A colony that goes into winter at 8 frames is unlikely to grade in March. This costs a day and tells you your real outdoor rate better than my 55% does.
