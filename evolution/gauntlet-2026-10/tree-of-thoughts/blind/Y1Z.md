**Short answer:** Neither side of the split can go ahead as currently described. The bike park is the better bet, but only if you first renegotiate the upper-slope lease. Make that negotiation the main item in November, and don't vote to spend money on either favourite yet.

## Why snowmaking doesn't work as proposed

- **Water.** Nine hectares at 4,500 m³ per hectare is about 40,500 m³ a season. That is more than your whole 40,000 m³ licence, before counting the 31,000 m³ the beginner-area guns already use. You have about 9,000 m³ of headroom, which covers roughly 2 hectares. The €2.1M plan would need a licence increase that you can't count on getting. (I'm assuming the beginner guns draw on the same licence.)
- **Climate.** Your lower runs (650–900 m) got 18 skiable days against 71 above. Snowmaking only helps when it is cold enough to make snow. If the lower slopes are too warm for natural snow, they will often be too warm for guns, so you could spend €2.1M and still have an unreliable product.
- **Funding.** The land is yours, so the fund would probably pay 50% (about €1.05M). That is the best funding case of any option, but it doesn't fix the water and temperature problems.

A small snowmaking scheme of about 2 hectares, to link or extend the beginner area, might be worth pricing. The full scheme should not go to a vote.

## Why the bike park can't proceed yet

- **The lease bans it.** The upper-slope lease limits you to winter sports between 1 December and 15 April, and the 2025 renewal kept that clause. Summer lift-served biking is outside it. You need a written variation from the Grazing Commons Trust. The clause probably exists to protect summer grazing, so expect the Trust to want something in return, such as seasonal limits, trail routing, fencing and a fee.
- **Funding.** The lease was renewed for ten years in 2025, so about nine remain. The fund needs at least 15 years left on leased land. As things stand, the €850k bike park gets no 50% grant, and the Summit chairlift work wouldn't qualify either. With the grant it costs you about €425k. Without it, €850k.
- **The revenue claim.** "Could double annual revenue" is the consultant's view. Ask for the assumptions: visitor numbers, ticket price, season length, and the chairlift's summer running costs and condition.

Even so, the bike park has what the others lack. It uses the upper slopes where the snow is, it earns in the months you currently earn nothing, and it spreads your risk across two seasons, which a snow-only strategy can't do.

## The other three options

- **Upper-only operation (€600k refit plus shuttle).** This matches where the snow is, since 71 days against 18. It has the same lease problem, though: a refit of a lift on leased land with nine years left won't get the grant. It also shrinks the business and leaves the lower land unused. It works as a fallback, not a lead.
- **Dry slope and tubing (€1.4M).** This is on land you own, so it is probably eligible for 50% (about €700k net), and it doesn't depend on snow or on the Trust. It is the one option you could start without anyone's permission. The weakness is demand: ask for visitor and revenue figures before committing. It is a solid second phase, or the main plan if the Trust says no.
- **Selling to Ardane.** Check the terms of the €1.2M buyout grant, which must be repaid if the land is sold within ten years of 2019. That runs to 2029, so a sale before then means repaying it. Whether a sale of the company, rather than the land, triggers repayment depends on the grant agreement, so get legal advice. A buyer will also face the same lease limits. Keep talking informally, but don't let it drive the vote.

## What I'd put to the board in November

1. **Authorise negotiation with the Trust.** Ask for two things together: a lease extension so at least 15 years remain (to about 2041 or later), and a variation allowing summer use for the bike park. A longer lease is also what makes the chairlift refit and bike park fundable. Offer what they are likely to care about, such as grazing protection and trail routing.
2. **Make the bike park conditional on that outcome,** and ask the consultant for a costed, evidence-based revenue case, including the chairlift's condition.
3. **Start the fund application for the dry slope and tubing** on your own land, as the fallback and possible second phase.
4. **Do not approve the full snowmaking scheme.** Commission a water and temperature study on a small scheme of about 2 hectares at most, and ask the regulator whether a licence increase is realistic.
5. **Park the Ardane approach** until you know the grant repayment position.

## Fallbacks

- **If the Trust agrees:** bike park first, dry slope and tubing second, small-scale snowmaking if the study supports it.
- **If the Trust refuses summer use but extends the lease:** upper-only operation with a grant-funded refit, plus the dry slope and tubing on your own land.
- **If the Trust refuses everything:** the dry slope and tubing and a smaller winter operation are your realistic choices, and the Ardane conversation becomes more important after 2029.

## What I'm unsure about

I'm working from your figures only. The main unknowns are the Trust's attitude, the chairlift's condition, the real demand behind the consultant's numbers, and the wording of the buyout grant. The Trust's answer decides which of these plans is open to you, so I would ask them first.
