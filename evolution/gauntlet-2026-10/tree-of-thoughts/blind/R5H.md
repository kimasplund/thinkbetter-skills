**Short answer:** Neither camp is right as the proposals stand. Two facts in your own brief break both of the leading options. The best chance of being here in ten years is to make the bike park viable by fixing the lease, and to do the dry slope and tubing lane on your own land in the meantime.

**Why full snowmaking doesn't work as scoped**
- The consultant's own figure is 4,500 m³ per hectare, and 9 ha comes to about 40,500 m³ a season.
- Your licence allows 40,000 m³ a season in total, and the beginner-area guns already used 31,000 m³.
- That leaves about 9,000 m³ of headroom, which covers roughly 2 ha, or about a fifth of the lower runs.
- Covering all 9 ha would need a licence increase, which is uncertain and may not be granted. It would also need a larger water source.
- The lower runs got 18 skiable days at 650–900 m, so they are marginal. Snowmaking only works in cold enough hours, and a warming trend cuts those hours.
- Spending €2.1M on the most climate-exposed part of the hill is the riskiest bet on the table. I would not do it unless the licence is secured first.

**Why the bike park is attractive but blocked**
- The upper slopes are leased, and the lease limits you to winter sports between 1 December and 15 April. A summer bike park breaches that clause as it stands.
- You renewed for ten years in 2025, so about 8 years remain. That is under the 15 years the tourism fund requires for assets on leased land, so the 50% grant would not apply.
- Without the grant the cost is €850k. With it, and with a lease that allows it, the cost would be about €425k.
- The "double annual revenue" claim is the consultant's estimate. Ask for the assumptions behind it, such as visitor numbers, price and season length, before you rely on it.
- It is still the strongest idea strategically. It uses an asset you already have, the chairlift, and it earns in months when you earn nothing now. It also doesn't depend on snow.

**The other options**
- **Dry slope and tubing lane (€1.4M):** This is on land you own, so it qualifies for the 50% grant, which brings it to about €700k. It doesn't need snow or much water. It is the only option that is fully fundable today. It is also less proven as a revenue earner, so ask for the visitor and revenue case.
- **Upper-only with shuttle (€600k refit):** The chairlift is on leased land, so the same grant problem applies. This is a sensible fallback that cuts costs, but it shrinks the offer while pass sales are already falling.
- **Ardane:** The €1.2M buyout grant is repayable if the land is sold within ten years of 2019, which means before 2029. A sale now would trigger that repayment, and it would reduce what the community receives. Check whether a sale of the company, rather than the land, would also trigger it. Treat the approach as a source of leverage and as a last resort.

**What I would recommend**
1. **Open lease talks with the Grazing Commons Trust now.** Ask for two changes: permission for summer recreational use, and an extension to at least 15 years remaining. This unlocks the bike park and the 50% grant, so it is the most valuable thing the board can do before November. Check with the fund whether an extension signed before you apply counts.
2. **Make the November vote conditional.** Approve the bike park in principle, subject to the lease amendment and a revenue case you can test. If the Trust refuses, you will know before committing money.
3. **Proceed with the dry slope and tubing lane**, ideally in a smaller first phase, with a grant application. It diversifies income, it sits on land you control, and it doesn't depend on the lease.
4. **Drop full snowmaking.** If members want snow on the lower hill, consider a limited scheme of about 2 ha within the existing licence, and only after a proper check of how many cold hours the site gets.
5. **Keep upper-only as the fallback** if the Trust won't move, and keep the Ardane conversation open but don't pursue it before 2029 unless things get much worse.

**Assumptions:** I took "ten years from 2025" to mean the lease ends in 2035. I assumed the lease terms aren't open to negotiation unless you ask, and that the fund's rules are as you describe them. I haven't seen the consultant's financial model. A few things would change my view. These are a lease refusal, a licence increase that is confirmed, or evidence that the bike park figures are well below the consultant's claim.

**On the split between members:** You can tell them that both sides are partly right, and that the deciding issue is the lease. The bike park supporters have the better strategy. The snowmaking supporters are right that the lower hill is worth saving, and the dry slope and tubing lane is the fundable way to do that.
