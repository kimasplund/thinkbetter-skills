**Short answer:** Grow through a Hollis deal, but not the one on the table and not by signing it as it stands before December. Three of your own constraints stop it working as offered. Go back to Hollis with a reshaped deal, buy the 15 kg roaster, and keep web subscriptions as a smaller second leg. Don't open a café or take the Varnland distributor.

## Why the offer doesn't work as written

**1. The importer cap rules out 18 tonnes of Alto Merida on top of what you sell now.**
Roasting loses about 15%, so every roasted tonne needs about 1.18 tonnes of green coffee.
- Your current 21 tonnes already uses roughly 24.7 tonnes of green, against a 26 tonne cap.
- Hollis's 18 tonnes would need about 21.2 tonnes of green on top of that.
- The cap allows about 1.3 tonnes of extra green, or roughly 1.1 tonnes of extra roasted coffee. That is about 6% of what Hollis wants.
- The only way to supply Hollis the Alto Merida blend is to drop most of your café and web business. Total roasted output could not exceed about 22 tonnes, so Hollis would leave you about 4 tonnes for everyone else. That swaps 40 customers for one buyer, and the buyer is leaving in December.

If your 21 tonnes is green weight, the picture is a little less tight but the conclusion is the same.

**2. Your roaster can't do it either.**
- With a 5 kg batch every 20 minutes and a 10 hour weekday window, the theoretical ceiling is 30 batches a day, or about 37 tonnes of green a year. That assumes no cleaning, loading gaps, holidays or breakdowns.
- Realistically you can do about 28 to 30 tonnes. Your current 24.7 tonnes already uses most of that.
- Adding Hollis would take you to about 46 tonnes of green. The lease also stops you running evenings or weekends to catch up.

**3. The Hollis stores in your county would breach your café agreements.**
Your café contracts promise you won't sell the Alto Merida blend through supermarkets in the county, and six Hollis stores are in the county. A private label with the same coffee inside it is very likely to count. You would risk 40 customers, who are your core business, to win one. Have a solicitor read those clauses, because the wording about what counts as "the Alto Merida blend" matters. Don't assume private label gets around it.

**4. The timing doesn't fit.**
A 15 kg roaster has a seven month lead time. Order it in November and it arrives in June, so a February start can't be met in-house.

## The options compared

## What I'd do instead

**Counter Hollis before their buyer leaves in December, with these changes:**
- **A different organic blend, not Alto Merida.** The cap applies only to Alto Merida green coffee. A second organic origin from a different importer, or an additional supply deal with your current importer, takes you out of the cap and probably out of the café clause as well. This is the central change. Have the solicitor confirm the clause wording before you rely on it.
- **A ramp in volume.** For example 5 to 6 tonnes in year one, 12 tonnes in year two and the full 18 tonnes in year three, with a start after the new roaster is running (about June to September, not February). If they want a February start, use a certified organic contract roaster for the first few months only.
- **Keep the six county stores out** unless your café customers agree in writing.
- **Protect your margin.** Link the price to green coffee costs, and limit the volume commitment so a bad harvest or a late machine delivery is not a default.
- **A way out.** Notice and exit terms if Hollis's volumes or payment slip, and some protection against a single supermarket buyer changing the terms after the person who signed it has gone.

**Place the 15 kg roaster order once you have a signed term sheet or letter of intent.** Don't wait for the full contract, because the lead time is long, but don't commit £68,000 on a verbal promise either. One 15 kg machine roughly triples your hourly output, so it would cover Hollis plus everything you have now inside the lease hours. Check three things first: the landlord's consent, power or gas supply, and that your organic certificate can be extended to cover the new machine.

**In parallel,** grow the web subscriptions modestly with a different origin or two, so that you don't depend on any one importer or any one buyer.

## What I'm assuming

- The 21 tonnes is roasted weight sold, and the 5 kg batch is green coffee loaded.
- The price Hollis offered gives a decent margin per kg. I can't check that without your numbers, but "roughly double our profit" at full volume may be much less at a ramped volume.
- You can find a second organic origin with an importer willing to supply that volume.
