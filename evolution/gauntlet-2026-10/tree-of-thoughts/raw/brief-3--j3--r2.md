**Short answer:** Don't sign the Hollis deal as offered. Four separate constraints each block it, and any one of them would be enough on its own. Growth is still possible, though, and Hollis may be worth a counter-offer before December.

I'm assuming your 21 tonnes is roasted weight, so about 24.7 t of green coffee. If it's green weight, the problems below get a little smaller but don't go away.

**Why the deal doesn't work as written**

1. **Your café agreements.** You promised the cafés you wouldn't sell Alto Merida through supermarkets in the county. Six Hollis stores are in the county. Signing would breach those agreements, and those 40 cafés are your core business. This is the hardest blocker, because it's a legal and relationship risk rather than a logistics one.

2. **The importer cap.** Your Alto Merida green coffee is capped at 26 t a year until 2028. You already use about 24.7 t. Hollis needs 18 t roasted, which is about 21.2 t green. That would take you to roughly 46 t, nearly double the cap, for most of the three-year term.

3. **Roasting capacity.** Roasting 39 t a year (21 t plus 18 t) means about 46 t green, or roughly 9,200 batches of 5 kg. At 20 minutes each, that's about 3,060 machine-hours. The lease allows 10 hours a day, five days a week, which is about 2,500 hours a year at most, with no cleaning, cooling, holidays or breakdowns. You're probably already running at 75 to 90% of what's realistic. The machine can't do this.

4. **Timing.** A 15 kg roaster has a seven-month lead time. Ordered today (October), it arrives in May at the earliest, so it can't support a February start. It also costs £68,000 up front against a deal you can't yet fulfil.

The contract roaster option fails for the same reasons. It fixes capacity but not the cap or the café promise. It would also need to hold its own organic certification, because your certificate covers only your unit. Check this before you rely on it.

**What I'd do instead**

*Before December, go back to Hollis with a counter-offer:*
- Exclude the six county stores, and offer the remaining 24 stores. Check that this works with the café agreements, and ideally ask your cafés whether they'd accept a differently named private-label product.
- Offer a lower volume, perhaps 6 to 8 t, ramping up. This is the amount that could fit under the cap if you free up green coffee.
- Propose a later start, such as the second half of the year, so any new equipment can arrive.
- Offer a different origin or blend for their organic range. That sidesteps the Alto Merida cap and the café promise together. Ask your importer about extra volume or a second organic origin.
- Use a contract roaster only if it's certified organic and the product isn't Alto Merida.

Faye's urgency over the buyer leaving is real, but a signed contract you can't deliver, or that breaches your café agreements, costs far more than a lost buyer. A well-prepared counter-offer to the buyer now, or to their successor later, is better than a rushed yes.

*Growth paths, ranked:*
1. **Web subscriptions.** Best fit. High margin, no new infrastructure, and modest volume that fits within the cap and your hours. Grow it steadily with the capacity you have.
2. **A reduced, restructured Hollis deal.** Worth pursuing if the terms above can be met. It could still add meaningful profit without breaking anything.
3. **A 15 kg roaster.** Think of this as a decision you take once a deal is secured, not a response to one. It removes the batch-count limit, though not the lease hours or the green-coffee cap.
4. **The Varnland distributor.** This has the same cap problem for Alto Merida, plus export and organic paperwork. Defer it.
5. **Your own café.** It's a different business, with a lease, staff and capital. It doesn't use your strengths and would distract from the roasting business. I'd leave it out for now.

**Things to check this week**
- The exact wording of the café agreements, ideally with a solicitor. Does the promise cover only Alto Merida and only the county? Can you get a waiver or amendment?
- Whether your importer will raise the cap, or supply a second organic origin.
- Whether your lease allows any extension of roasting hours. This is unlikely, but worth asking.
- Whether your 21 t is roasted or green weight. It changes how tight things are.
- Hollis's real margin at the lower volume. "Double the profit" at 18 t may be much less at 7 t.

I'm working from your numbers only, so please check the figures against your actual batch logs.
