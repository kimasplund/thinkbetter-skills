**Short answer:** Neither side of the split is right as the proposals stand. The best chance of being here in ten years is an upper-mountain strategy: refit the Summit chairlift, and run winter plus a summer bike park on it. That only works if you first renegotiate the Grazing Commons Trust lease. Make that negotiation the condition of the November vote. Don't vote for the big lower-run snowmaking scheme.

**Why snowmaking across the lower runs doesn't work**

- At the consultant's own figure, 9 ha x 4,500 m³ is about 40,500 m³ a season. Your licence allows 40,000 m³, and the beginner-area guns already used 31,000 last winter. That leaves about 9,000 m³ of headroom, which covers roughly 2 ha of the 9. You would have to win a larger abstraction licence before the €2.1M makes sense. That is a regulatory risk you don't control, and water licences are rarely extended for a business that needs more every year.
- The lower runs managed 18 skiable days at 650-900 m. Snowmaking helps only when it is cold enough to make snow. As winters warm, the lower slopes are where that window shrinks first.
- It is the most expensive option, and you would be putting the most capital into the least reliable ground.

**Why the bike park is better but is blocked as it stands**

- The lease limits you to winter sports between 1 December and 15 April, and you renewed it on those terms in 2025. A summer bike park on the upper slopes would breach it. The consultant's "double annual revenue" figure doesn't mention this. I'd treat it as an unverified upper bound until she shows the visitor, spend and operating-cost assumptions.
- Funding also fails. The tourism fund excludes assets on leased land unless at least 15 years remain, and you have about nine years left. So the €850k bike park would not get the 50% grant as things stand. The €600k chairlift refit has the same problem, because the lift sits on the leased upper slopes.
- Both problems have the same fix. If the Trust agrees to (a) allow summer recreation and (b) extend the lease to at least 15 years remaining from the date you apply, the bike park and the refit both become eligible. That would cut roughly €425k from the bike park and €300k from the refit, if the fund treats them as eligible capital, which you should confirm with it in writing.

**The other options**

- **Upper-only winter operation:** It is sound, because the upper runs had 71 days against 18. But if it is your whole plan, you are a smaller ski area with one lift and the same falling pass sales. A refit that serves winter and summer is easier to justify than one that serves winter alone.
- **Dry slope and tubing on the lower land:** This is the one capital project that is clearly fundable today. You own the land, so the fund should cover half of the €1.4M, leaving about €700k. It also earns on days when the lower runs have no snow. I'd keep it as a second phase, not the headline. Its revenue case needs checking, and it should not absorb money the lift refit needs.
- **Ardane:** Selling gives up the goal of still being here, so it should be a fallback rather than a plan. It also has a cost. Selling the land before the tenth anniversary of the 2019 purchase means repaying the €1.2M buyout grant. If the board ever wants to sell, it matters whether the sale happens before or after that date, and whether a buyer would take on the repayment.

**What I'd put to the board in November**

1. Authorise the chair to open talks with the Trust now. Ask for summer recreational use and a lease extension to 15+ years remaining. The Trust gains a more stable tenant, and the park will use land it already grazes only lightly, but check for grazing conflicts and offer to deal with them.
2. Ask the tourism fund in writing whether the refit and the bike park qualify once the lease is amended, and whether the 15 years is measured at application or at award.
3. Make the €600k refit and the bike park conditional on those two answers. If the Trust refuses, go to the fallback below.
4. Limit snowmaking to what the licence supports, about 2 ha of the beginner and link areas, as a small, cheap addition rather than a €2.1M scheme. Do not commit to the big scheme unless a larger licence is granted.
5. Treat the dry slope and tubing as phase two, funded with the grant, once the upper-mountain plan is secure.
6. Ask the consultant to rework her figures for the bike park, including lease and funding constraints, and for snowmaking within the real water limit.

**If the Trust says no**

Without summer use or a longer lease, the bike park is out and the refit gets no grant. In that case the best option is a cheaper, leaner base: run the upper-only winter operation with a basic refit funded from reserves or a smaller grant, build the dry slope and tubing on your own land with the 50% grant, and keep selling as the last resort, timed with the €1.2M repayment in mind.

**What I'm unsure of**

- I'm assuming the lease clause and the 15-year rule are as you described. If the Trust or the fund has any flexibility already, the case is stronger.
- I don't have visitor numbers, operating costs or the bike park's margin. The recommendation rests on the access and funding constraints, not on revenue forecasts, and the board should see real numbers before committing.
- The 2 ha snowmaking figure is a rough division of the licence headroom. Your engineer should confirm it, including how much water the beginner-area guns need in a colder winter.
- Whether the lift refit counts as eligible for the grant if the lift itself is owned by you but stands on leased land is for the fund to decide, so ask them.
