**Short answer:** Neither side of the split can go ahead as proposed. The best chance of being here in ten years is a plan built on the upper slopes, with a bike park as the second leg, and the lease with the Grazing Commons Trust fixed first. The lower land should get the tubing and dry slope, not snowmaking. Your own numbers rule out two of the five options and put a condition on a third.

**What the figures say**

1. **Full lower-run snowmaking doesn't fit your licence.**
   - 9 ha at about 4,500 m³/ha is roughly 40,500 m³ a season. That alone exceeds the 40,000 m³ licence.
   - The beginner-area guns already use 31,000 m³, so the total would be about 71,500 m³. That is nearly 80% over the licence.
   - Headroom is about 9,000 m³, which covers about 2 ha at the consultant's rate.
   - The runs sit at 650–900 m and had 18 skiable days. Snowmaking only helps if it is cold enough to make and hold snow, and that is least reliable at the altitude where you need it most. I would not put €2.1M into the lowest, warmest part of the hill.
   - Ask the consultant how she reconciled her water figure with the licence. A licence increase is a regulatory outcome you can't count on.

2. **The bike park as proposed conflicts with your lease.**
   - The upper slopes are leased with a clause limiting you to winter sports between 1 December and 15 April. A summer bike park on those slopes appears to be outside that clause, so you would need the Trust to agree a variation.
   - The lease has about nine years left (ten years from 2025). The tourism fund needs at least 15 years remaining on leased land, so the park and the chairlift refit would probably not qualify for the 50% grant on the current lease.
   - So the €850k is likely to be a full €850k, not €425k, and it is built on land where you have nine years of tenure and no summer rights.
   - "Could double annual revenue" is the consultant's view. Ask her for visitor numbers, pricing, operating costs and comparable parks. Revenue is not margin, and a bike park also brings staffing, insurance and maintenance.

3. **The upper mountain is your business.** It had 71 skiable days against 18 below, and the Summit chairlift is the one asset that serves winter and a possible summer product. Upper-only with a shuttle is the most realistic winter core. Its weakness is the same as the bike park's: the €600k refit is on leased land with nine years left, so it is likely ineligible for the grant on the current lease.

4. **The dry slope and tubing lane is the one proposal that is clean on funding.**
   - It sits on land you own, so the tourism fund's lease rule doesn't apply. If it is otherwise eligible, you would pay about €700k of the €1.4M.
   - It earns in weeks the snow doesn't come, and it gives the vacated lower land a use.
   - Check demand and operating costs before you commit. Dry slopes and tubing lanes are often modest earners, so get a business case rather than relying on the capital grant.

5. **Selling to Ardane is the worst moment to sell.** The €1.2M buyout grant is repayable if the land is sold within ten years of 2019, so before 2029. A sale now would probably trigger repayment before the community sees any value. It would also end community ownership, which is presumably why you hold the hill. Treat it as a fallback to test, not a plan. If the board wants to explore it, take legal advice first on whether a share or business sale triggers repayment as well as a land sale.

**Recommended direction**

1. **Make the Trust negotiation the first move, before or alongside the November vote.** Ask for two things:
   - An extension so that at least 15 years remain at the time you apply for funding, which means a term running to about 2041 or later.
   - A variation to the seasonal clause allowing summer recreation use, including a bike park.
   
   Everything on the upper slopes depends on this. An extension plus the variation unlocks 50% grants on the chairlift refit and the bike park, and it gives the community tenure that justifies investing in them. Find out early whether the Trust will do this and what it would want in return, such as rent changes, liability terms or environmental conditions. The renewal on unchanged terms in 2025 suggests it may not be easy, so don't assume yes.

2. **Commit to the upper-only winter operation with a refit of the Summit chairlift**, conditional on the lease outcome. If the Trust refuses an extension, you need to know before spending €600k of your own money. At that point the question becomes whether upper-only can pay back in nine years.

3. **Use the lower land for the tubing lane and dry slope**, subject to a proper business case and the grant. Consider small-scale snowmaking on the beginner area only within the 9,000 m³ headroom, and only if it is cheap and the engineering supports it.

4. **Put the bike park second, as a conditional phase.** It stays attractive for diversifying away from winter, but only if the lease variation comes through and the revenue claim survives scrutiny. Run the member debate on those terms, because it turns "snowmaking versus bike park" into "what does the Trust allow".

5. **Keep Ardane in reserve.** Don't discuss price or terms until you have lawyers' advice on the grant repayment and a clear sense of the minimum the community would accept.

**Before the vote**

- Get the Trust's position on extension and the summer-use clause.
- Get the consultant's water calculation and her bike-park revenue model.
- Confirm with the tourism fund which of the projects qualify and whether the lease term is measured at application or award.
- Check the lease for any other limits on construction, trails or lift works on the upper slopes.
- Check the licence terms for any seasonal or rate limits and whether an increase is possible.
- Have a lawyer confirm whether the €1.2M clawback depends on the form of any sale.

**Assumptions:** I've taken the lease clause at face value as ruling out summer commercial use without consent, and the fund's 15-year test as applying at the time of application. I haven't seen the lease or the fund's rules, so these are the first things to verify.

**Confidence:** High that snowmaking across the whole lower area can't work under the licence and that the lease blocks the bike park and the grant as things stand. Moderate that upper-only plus a conditional bike park is the best path, because it turns on the Trust's answer and on unverified revenue estimates.
