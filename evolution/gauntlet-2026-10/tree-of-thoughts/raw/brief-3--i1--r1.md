**Short answer:** Don't sign the Hollis deal as written. As offered, you can't legally or physically deliver it. Don't walk away either. Counter with a version you can deliver, and send it in the next two weeks, while the current buyer is still there. ## Why the deal fails as offered

Three of your own constraints block it, and any one of them is enough.

1. **Green coffee cap.** 21 tonnes roasted at about 15% weight loss needs roughly 24.7 tonnes of green. Your importer's cap is 26 tonnes a year until 2028. That caps you at about 22 tonnes of roasted Alto Merida, so you have about 1 tonne of headroom. Hollis wants 18 tonnes roasted on top of your 21, which is about 46 tonnes of green and nearly double the cap. Faster roasting doesn't fix this. Only a higher cap or a different coffee does.

2. **Café agreements.** You promised the cafés you wouldn't sell the Alto Merida blend through supermarkets in the county, and six Hollis stores are in the county. A Hollis-labelled bag of the same blend is very likely a breach. Have a solicitor read the exact wording, but plan on the basis that it is. Your 40 cafés are your base, and they'd have good reason to be angry.

3. **Roasting capacity.** I'm assuming the 5 kg is green coffee per batch. That gives 3 batches an hour. Your lease allows about 10 hours a day on roughly 250 weekdays, so the ceiling is about 2,500 roasting hours a year. Your current 21 tonnes already uses about 1,650 of them. Hollis would add about 1,400 more, which takes you to about 3,050 and well past the ceiling. That's before cleaning, loading, cooling and the odd breakdown. The lease rules out evening or weekend shifts.

The 15 kg roaster doesn't rescue the February start either. With a seven-month lead time, an order placed today arrives in May at best, and then you need to install and commission it. It also does nothing about points 1 and 2.

Two further risks. Hollis would be nearly half your volume on a three-year contract, with a buyer who is leaving. And "doubles our profit" needs checking against what you give up. If any café volume is lost to make room, or the price has no room for green coffee price rises, the real gain is smaller.

## What I'd do instead

**1. Counter-offer Hollis with a different coffee.** The cap and the café promise both attach to the Alto Merida blend. A Hollis organic private-label blend from a different origin, bought from a second importer, avoids both. It also lets you grow volume without touching your existing business. Offer it on these terms:
- Start smaller, for example 5 to 8 tonnes in year one, rising in steps as capacity arrives.
- Put volume bands and a minimum notice period into the contract, so you aren't forced to supply more than you can roast.
- Include a price-adjustment clause for green coffee costs.
- Ask for a letter of intent now, so the commitment survives the buyer leaving in December.

If Hollis insists on Alto Merida, the alternatives are: stores outside the county only (24 of the 30), a written waiver from the cafés, or a higher cap from your importer. Each is slow and uncertain, so I'd treat them as fallbacks.

**2. Use a contract roaster to bridge the gap.** This is the right use of one, but only if the roaster holds its own organic certification, or you extend your certificate to cover the arrangement. Check that with your certifier before you promise Hollis anything, because losing certification would put all your sales at risk. Use the contract roaster until Hollis volumes are proven, then decide whether to buy the 15 kg machine. If Hollis is at 10 or more tonnes and renewed, order it then. If not, you haven't spent £68,000 on a bet.

**3. Grow web subscriptions in parallel.** It uses little extra capacity per pound of profit, margins are the best you have, and it doesn't depend on any one buyer. It also fits inside the Alto Merida cap if you grow it gradually, and it gives you cash flow while the Hollis deal is being worked out.

**4. Keep the Varnland distributor for later.** It's a reasonable second channel, but it needs the same extra capacity and coffee as Hollis, so don't run both at once. Revisit it once the Hollis volumes and the roaster decision are settled.

**5. Don't open a café now.** It adds lease, staff and fit-out costs, and it would take your attention away from the one opportunity that has a deadline.

## Why this order

Hollis is the only option on the list with a fixed deadline and a large sum of money behind it, so it deserves the effort. But it only works if you can remove the legal and sourcing blockers first. Subscriptions are the safest way to grow without taking on those risks. The contract roaster lets you test demand without spending £68,000 on a machine. The distributor and the café can both wait.
