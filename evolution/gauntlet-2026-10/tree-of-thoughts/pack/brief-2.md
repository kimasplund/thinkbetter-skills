I chair the board of Corrie Fell Snowsports, a community-owned ski area. Season-pass sales are down a third in four years, and the board votes on a long-term plan in November.

Our lower runs (650–900 m, 9 hectares) had 18 skiable days last winter. The upper runs (900–1,300 m), served by the Summit chairlift, had 71.

The proposals:

- Snowmaking across the lower runs, our consultant's favourite: €2.1M. Her figures assume about 4,500 m³ of water per hectare per season.
- A summer bike park using the Summit chairlift and new trails on the upper slopes: €850k. The consultant thinks it could double annual revenue.
- A dry slope and tubing lane on the lower land: €1.4M.
- Give up the lower runs and run a smaller upper-only operation, with a shuttle from the car park and a €600k refit of the Summit chairlift.
- Accept an informal approach from Ardane Resorts to buy us.

Funding: the regional tourism fund pays 50% of capital costs for eligible projects, but excludes assets on leased land unless at least 15 years remain on the lease. We own the lower land. The upper slopes are leased from the Grazing Commons Trust; we renewed for ten years in 2025 on the same terms as before, including the usual clause limiting us to winter sports between 1 December and 15 April.

Other bits: our water abstraction licence allows 40,000 m³ a season, and the existing snow guns on the beginner area used 31,000 m³ last winter. The community bought the hill in 2019 with a €1.2M buyout grant that has to be repaid if the land is sold within ten years.

Members are split between snowmaking and the bike park. Which direction gives us the best chance of still being here in ten years?
