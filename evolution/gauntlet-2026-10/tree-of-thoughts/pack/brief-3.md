I own Kittiwake Lane Roasters with my business partner, Faye. We roast about 21 tonnes a year, nearly all of it our Alto Merida house blend, sold wholesale to 40 cafés around Kelby and through our web shop.

Hollis Markets, a regional supermarket chain with 30 stores (six of them in our county), has offered us a three-year private-label deal: 18 tonnes a year of the Alto Merida blend for their organic range, starting in February. At their price it would roughly double our profit. Faye wants to sign before their buyer moves on in December.

Other paths we've talked about: growing our web subscriptions, opening our own café, supplying a distributor in Varnland, or using a contract roaster for the Hollis volume so we don't have to buy a bigger machine.

Background:

- We roast on one 5 kg machine. A batch takes 20 minutes and loses about 15% of its weight.
- Our unit is underneath flats, so the lease only allows roasting from 8 am to 6 pm on weekdays.
- A 15 kg roaster costs £68,000, with a seven-month lead time.
- Everything we sell is certified organic. The certificate covers our unit.
- Our importer's contract caps our Alto Merida green coffee at 26 tonnes a year until 2028.
- Our café supply agreements, which we wrote when we started out, promise we won't sell the Alto Merida blend through supermarkets in the county.

What's the best way for us to grow over the next three years? And should we sign with Hollis?
