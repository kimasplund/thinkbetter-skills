Notes (J-B, brief-3)

R0F: Arithmetic on cap and hours checks out (24.7 t green, ~3,060 h vs ~2,500 h). But "6 to 8 t could fit under the cap if you free up green coffee" is muddled, because headroom is ~1.3 t and that volume means cutting café supply. It also says any one of the four blockers is enough on its own, which overstates timing since a contract roaster could bridge it. It says a 15 kg roaster doesn't remove the lease-hours limit, which is confused. Web subs are ranked first without noting they are also capped. The different-origin idea is buried as one option among several.
Q7L: Sound and accurate. Good catches: "Hollis alone leaves ~4 t for everyone else", the cap/deal overlap across years, and the certifier's sign-off on private-label labelling. It names a different blend as the central counter and gives concrete protections. Modest redundancy in the bottom line, and little on what would change the advice.
S9M: Hours arithmetic is correct (1,650 h now, +1,400 h). Also good: the LOI to survive the buyer leaving, the concentration risk, and naming the largest open question. Its assumption note is backwards: if 5 kg is roasted output, capacity per hour is higher, not lower. "Renewed" in the roaster decision rule doesn't fit a three-year term. It also misses that subscriptions sit under the ~1 t cap headroom.
R7C: Strongest framing: the cap is the real ceiling, so grow profit per kilo, and a second uncapped coffee is its own path. It notes Hollis may prefer their own blend, gives clear certifier and subcontractor steps, and has a sensible three-year shape including the 2028 renegotiation. "Retail earns far more per kilo" is stated as fact without support, and the reply is slightly long.
F4Z: Most accurate capacity picture: ~20 batches/day now (~6.5 h), comfortable, with 46 t green needing ~12 h/day. It also shows a 4-6 t second-blend start fits the current machine without spending £68k. It gets the subtle points right: Alto Merida can grow only ~5% until 2028, Varnland isn't hit by the café clause, and shifting café volume to subscriptions raises margin. Its explicit "what would change this" section adds to that. "Saturday-free scheduling" is a muddled phrase.
A2K: A bold, defensible line (reshaped Hollis on a second origin as the main growth lever, ramp 5-6/12/18 t, order the roaster on LOI), with good assumptions and a check list. It rejects a café because "a unit above flats probably doesn't suit it", which is a non sequitur since a café would be in other premises. It also rejects Varnland on the cap without applying the same second-origin logic it uses for Hollis.
