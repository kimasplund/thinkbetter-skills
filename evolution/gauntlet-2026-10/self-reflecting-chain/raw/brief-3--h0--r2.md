Short version: Dara's arithmetic is mostly right, but the reasoning doesn't support the conclusion. Lantern's measurable effect is closer to 2 to 3 points per pupil than 9, and expanding it to the bottom 30% would not come close to the 2-point goal. I would not vote on this summary as it stands.

Here is each step in order.

**Step 1: "12-point gain, so Lantern added about 9 points per pupil"**

There are two problems, and they stack.

*The groups don't match.* The September 82 includes all 180 pupils. The June 94 covers only the 150 who finished. The 30 who left averaged 74 in September, so the 150 finishers averaged (180 x 82 - 30 x 74) / 150 = 83.6 in September. The like-for-like gain is 94 - 83.6 = **10.4 points, not 12**.

*"Everyone else" is the wrong comparison.* The lowest scorers in September tend to score higher on any retest, whatever is done for them. Last year, with no Lantern, the bottom 15% gained 8 points while everyone else gained 3. So the fair benchmark for Lantern pupils is 8, not 3.

- Corrected estimate: 10.4 - 8 = **about 2.4 points** for pupils who finished.
- Even Dara's uncorrected 12 would give only 4 against the right benchmark.
- The 9 comes from comparing 12 with 3, which credits Lantern with the ordinary bounce-back of low scorers.

The 8-point benchmark is itself uncertain. It comes from one earlier cohort, and the cohort may differ from this year's. Treat 2.4 as a rough figure, perhaps anywhere from 1 to 4. Even the optimistic end is far from 9.

We also know nothing about how the 30 who left did in June. If the finishers were the more motivated pupils, 2.4 flatters the programme. The 2.4 applies to finishers only.

**Step 2: "The gap narrowed from 26 to 17, a 35% reduction"**

The arithmetic is correct: 108 - 82 = 26, 111 - 94 = 17, and 9 / 26 is about 35%. The inference is the problem. The gap was going to narrow anyway. Last year's pattern (8 against 3) would have taken it from 26 to about 21, roughly a 19% reduction with no programme.

- On the matched group, the gap went from 24.4 (108 - 83.6) to 17, a narrowing of 7.4 points.
- The narrowing you could credit to Lantern is therefore about 2.4 points, not 9.

**Step 3: "Form B was harder, so the true gain is larger than 12"**

This doesn't follow. The scores are already on an equated scale, and equating exists to remove differences in form difficulty. Adding a further allowance for the harder form double-counts. Everyone else sat Form B too, so any residual form effect also shows up in their 3-point gain and cancels in a comparison. The claim should be dropped. If the committee has doubts about the equating, ask for the equating report. Don't assume the bias runs in Lantern's favour.

**Step 4: "15% x 9 = 1.35, so the bottom 30% gives 2.7, beating the goal"**

The multiplication is right, but it is built on the 9, and doubling it needs two more assumptions.

- *With the corrected effect:* 2.4 x 15% is about 0.36 points on the district average. If you also allow that only 150 of 180 finished, it is about 0.3.
- *Doubling is unlikely to hold.* The extra 15% would be pupils who scored higher in September. They have less room to gain and less bounce-back to absorb, so the per-pupil effect is likely to be smaller than for the lowest group. Even at the same 2.4, the bottom 30% would add about 0.7 points.
- *Attrition would scale too.* If a fifth of a larger cohort also leaves, the real effect shrinks further.
- *The goal itself needs defining.* Everyone gained something this year without Lantern, so "raise the district average by 2 points" needs to be pinned down. Is it above the usual year-to-year growth, or 2 points on top of whatever the cohort would score anyway? Lantern as designed, even doubled, does not clearly reach either reading.

**What I would tell the board**

- The data show a modest positive effect of roughly 2 to 3 points for pupils who complete Lantern. That is not 9, and it is not proven causal.
- Expanding to the bottom 30% would likely add well under 1 point to the district average, not 2.7. Lantern alone won't deliver the target.
- Ask Dara to redo the table on matched pupils (the 150 finishers in both months), to benchmark against last year's bottom-15% gain of 8, to remove the Form B claim, and to report what is known about the 30 who left.
- A better test before spending on a full expansion is a pilot on part of the next group, with a comparison group of similar pupils who aren't enrolled.

The weakest link in my own check is the 8-point benchmark. It rests on a single earlier year, so the 2.4 estimate could move in either direction. The overall conclusion holds either way: the claimed 9-point effect isn't supported, and the 2.7 projection depends on it.
