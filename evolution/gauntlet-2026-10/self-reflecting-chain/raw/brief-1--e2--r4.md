## Step by step

**Step 1: capacity (72 loaves × 9 loads = 648, up 216). This is the biggest error.**
The oven can take 72 loaves, but the proofing cabinet holds only 60 and proofs in the same 40 minutes. Dough goes straight from proofer to oven, so you can only bake 60 per load. That gives 60 × 9 = 540 loaves a day, up 108, not 216. Today you sell 432 and turn away about 150, so demand is about 582. The D9 as things stand would cover only 108 of those 150.

**Step 2: €900 a day (150 × €6.00). Wrong on volume, and wrong on what is being counted.**
- Volume: 108 extra loaves, not 150.
- €6.00 includes 10% VAT, which you pass on to the tax authority. Net revenue is €6.00 ÷ 1.10 = €5.45.
- €6.00 is revenue, not profit. Ingredients and packaging are €1.70 a loaf, so the margin is €5.45 − €1.70 = €3.75 a loaf.
- Correct figure: 108 × €3.75 = about €405 a day, not €900.

**Step 3: the energy "saving" of about €7 a day. It is actually a cost.**
Her per-loaf numbers are right only at 72 loaves per load. €4.00 ÷ 48 = 8.3 cents now. €5.20 ÷ 72 = 7.2 cents. At the 60 loaves you can actually fill, €5.20 ÷ 60 = 8.7 cents, which is worse than today. Per-loaf cost is also the wrong comparison. What matters is total spend. The proofer is the bottleneck, not the oven. If the proofing cabinet could hold about 65 loaves or more per load, you'd have 585 or more a day and could meet the full 150 loaves of unmet demand.
