Short version: Dara's arithmetic is mostly right, but the reasoning has three problems. Corrected, Lantern looks like it adds roughly 2 to 3 points per pupil who finishes, not 9. Expanding it to the bottom 30% would lift the district average by well under 1 point, not 2.7. I would not use the 2.7 figure to justify Monday's vote.

**Step 1: "Lantern added about 9 points per pupil" (not supported)**

There are two separate errors.

*The two months aren't measured on the same pupils.* The September 82 includes all 180 pupils. The June 94 covers only the 150 who finished. The 30 who left averaged 74 in September, so the 150 finishers started higher than 82:

- (180 x 82 - 30 x 74) / 150 = 12,540 / 150 = 83.6

On a like-for-like basis the finishers' gain is 94 - 83.6 = 10.4 points, not 12. Dropping the weakest pupils from the June figure flatters the result.

*"Everyone else gained 3" is the wrong comparison.* The Lantern pupils were picked because they scored lowest in September. Pupils selected that way tend to bounce back somewhat even with no help, because some of a very low score is bad luck on the day. You have direct evidence of this from last year, before Lantern existed: the bottom 15% gained 8 points by June, while everyone else gained 3. The right yardstick for Lantern is the 8, not the 3:

- 10.4 - 8 = about 2.4 points per finisher

Two things support using last year's 8. Everyone else gained 3 again this year, so the baseline looks stable. And the selection rule, the bottom 15% in September, is the same.

If Dara's 12 is used with the 8 baseline, the answer would be 4. Even that is less than half of 9.

**Step 2: "The gap narrowed 35%" (arithmetic correct, conclusion misleading)**

The numbers check out: 108 - 82 = 26, 111 - 94 = 17, and 9/26 is about 35%. But the gap would have narrowed without Lantern. At last year's rates, the gap would have gone from 26 to 21 (8 versus 3), a reduction of about 19%. On the like-for-like start of 83.6, the gap went from 24.4 to 17, a narrowing of 7.4 points. About 5 of those are what you would expect anyway, which leaves roughly 2.4 again. The 35% figure should not be quoted as Lantern's effect.

**Step 3: "Form B was harder, so the true gain is larger" (does not follow)**

The facts say June is reported on the same equated scale as September. Equating exists to remove the difficulty difference between forms, so adjusting again would count the same correction twice. "If anything larger" is unjustified. It is also possible that equating is slightly imperfect, in which case the error could go either way. It would be worth asking whether last year's June test used Form B, since the 8-point baseline is only comparable if the scaling was handled the same way. Until then, treat the scores as they are and don't add a bonus.

**Step 4: "Expanding to the bottom 30% gives 2.7" (arithmetic right, inputs wrong, and the scaling assumption is weak)**

The multiplication is consistent with Dara's inputs: 0.15 x 9 = 1.35, doubled is 2.7. Using the corrected effect:

- Effect on pupils who finish: about 2.4 points. If all 180 were enrolled and the 30 leavers got no benefit, that is about 2.4 x 150/180 = 2.0 points per enrolled pupil.
- Current district lift: 0.15 x 2.4 = about 0.36 points (about 0.30 if you count only the 150 finishers against all 1,200 pupils).
- Doubling to the bottom 30%, if the new group benefits equally: about 0.7 points. That is roughly a third of the 2-point goal.

Even this is optimistic. The extra 15% are higher scorers, so they have less room to improve, and less natural bounce-back to separate from a true effect. There is no reason to expect the same gain from them as from the lowest group. The leavers are also an open question. The 30 who left may differ from those who stayed in ways that affect the result. Their June scores, if they stayed in the district, are the missing piece.

**One more caution on the goal itself**

Last year's within-year gains alone would raise the district average by about 0.15 x 8 + 0.85 x 3 = 3.75 points between September and June without Lantern. So it matters what "raise the average by 2 points next year" is measured against. If the target is the Year-4 average compared with last year's cohort, a within-year gain tells you little about it. The committee should pin down the baseline before judging any programme against that target.

**What this means for Monday**

- Lantern may well be worth keeping. A gain of roughly 2 to 3 points per pupil beyond what would have happened anyway is plausible, but it comes from one cohort and one year of baseline data, and I can't tell how much of it is noise.
- Expansion will not by itself meet the 2-point goal. On these figures it contributes well under 1 point.
- Before the vote, ask Dara for: (1) June scores for the 30 leavers if available, (2) a restated comparison using the same pupils in both months and last year's bottom 15% as the baseline, (3) the cost per point gained, and (4) confirmation of how Form B scores were equated and which form last year's June test used.
- If the board wants stronger evidence, the sharp cutoff at the bottom 15% gives a natural comparison. Pupils just below the line and just above it are very similar, so tracking both groups would give a cleaner estimate than any year-to-year comparison. A phased expansion that keeps some similar pupils out for one year would do the same job.
