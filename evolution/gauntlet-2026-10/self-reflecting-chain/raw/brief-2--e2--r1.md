**Short answer: do not send the invitations on this schedule.** Owen's plan does not have two weeks of slack. Done correctly, the last case check falls on Saturday 1 May, two days after the 29 April sign-off, so the slack is about minus two days. It can probably be recovered, but only with decisions that haven't been made yet.

I checked each of Owen's four steps in order. Each later step depends on the one before it, so the first error carries through.

**Step 1: when objects can enter (Owen: 31 March). This is wrong.**
The policy counts 30 days from the end of the last wet trade in the room. The silicone seals on the cases are a wet trade, and the fitters finish them on Friday 12 March. Owen counted from 1 March, when the HVAC stabilised, and so ignored the silicone.
- 30 days from 12 March ends on Sunday 11 April.
- The first mounting day is therefore Monday 12 April, not Wednesday 31 March. That is 12 days later than Owen assumed.
- This assumes readings stay stable throughout, including while the fitters work. If the silicone work upsets the climate, the count restarts.

**Step 2: pest freezing (Owen: 5 loads, ready 31 March). The load count is wrong, but this step is not the problem.**
- The freezer has 12 slots. 52 standard crates take 52 slots, and the 8 oversized crates take 16. That is 68 slots in total.
- 68 slots over 12 per load needs 6 loads, not 5. Five loads give only 60 slots.
- Start Monday 15 March and run loads back to back, 72 hours each. The sixth load comes out around Friday 2 April.
- The 24-hour sealed rest happens outside the freezer, so it doesn't hold up the next load. The last crates are ready around Saturday 3 April.
- That is still about a week before mounting can start on 12 April, so freezing has room to spare. Two cautions:
  - Back-to-back loads mean crates come out on weekend days (for example Sunday 21 March and Saturday 27 March), so someone has to be on site.
  - Any slip of more than about a week in the freezing start would begin to matter.

**Step 3: mounting (Owen: 14 days, finishing 13 April). This is wrong.**
The team installs 126 objects at 9 a day, which is 14 working days. Owen added 14 calendar days to 31 March, and the team doesn't work weekends.
- Starting Monday 12 April, 14 weekday shifts run 12–16 April, 19–23 April, then 26–29 April.
- The last object goes in on Thursday 29 April.
- Even Owen's own start date of 31 March would have finished around 19 April, not 13 April.
- I haven't allowed for public holidays. If your team's calendar has any between 12 and 29 April, the finish moves later.

**Step 4: final check (Owen: 14 April, two weeks of slack). This is wrong.**
Each case must stay closed for 48 hours after its last object is mounted. Owen's own dates break this rule, because a check on 14 April is only 24 hours after a 13 April finish.
- With the last object mounted on 29 April, the earliest check is Saturday 1 May. That is opening day, and two days after the conservator's sign-off.
- The last mounting day that still works:

  - A final mount on Tuesday 27 April gives a check on Thursday 29 April, with no margin.
  - A final mount on Wednesday 28 April or later misses sign-off.

**What would close the gap**
You need to finish mounting by Tuesday 27 April at the latest, and by Monday 26 April to leave a day of margin. That is 18 objects short at 9 a day, or about two working days.
1. Add Saturday mounting. Two Saturdays (17 and 24 April) would finish on 27 April with no margin. Three weekend days would give a day of margin.
2. Raise the daily rate. Finishing by 27 April means 126 objects in 12 working days, or about 10.5 a day. Adding a second mounting crew or staggering cases would do this.
3. Check the cases in sequence. The 48-hour hold applies per case, so mount and close cases one at a time. Only the last case sets the end date, and the conservator can start checking earlier cases while mounting continues.
4. Ask the fitters whether the silicone work can be done earlier than 8–12 March. Each day it moves earlier would move the whole schedule back by a day. This is the only option that adds real slack, but it depends on the fitters' availability.
