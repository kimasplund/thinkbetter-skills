**Short version:** Dara's arithmetic is mostly right, but the reasoning doesn't support the conclusion. Her own figures point to an effect of roughly 2 to 3 points per pupil, not 9. Expanding to the bottom 30% would add well under 1 point to the district average, not 2.7. I would not vote on the 2-point claim as written.

**Step 1: "Lantern added about 9 points per pupil" (not supported)**

Two problems.

- *The September and June groups are different.* The 82 includes all 180 pupils. The 94 covers only the 150 who finished. The 30 who left averaged 74 in September, so the 150 finishers averaged (180 x 82 - 30 x 74) / 150 = 83.6 in September. Like for like, the finishers gained 94 - 83.6 = 10.4 points, not 12. About 1.6 points of the headline gain comes from the lowest scorers dropping out of the June figure.
- *The wrong comparison group.* Dara subtracts the 3 points gained by everyone else. But pupils who score in the bottom 15% in September tend to score higher on any retest, even with no help, because some of them had an unlucky day. Last year, with no Lantern, the bottom 15% gained 8 points while everyone else gained 3. The right baseline for Lantern pupils is therefore about 8, not 3.

That gives 10.4 - 8 = about 2.4 points. That is the best estimate from these facts, and it's modest. It assumes this year's cohort would have behaved like last year's. One year of data is a thin baseline, so treat the true figure as roughly 1 to 4 points. It also applies to pupils who finished. We don't know how the 30 leavers did, so the effect across all 180 enrolled is likely smaller.

**Step 2: "The gap narrowed from 26 to 17, a 35% reduction" (arithmetic right, meaning wrong)**

The sums check out: 108 - 82 = 26, 111 - 94 = 17, and 9/26 is about 35%. But the same two problems apply.

- On the 150 finishers, the September gap is 108 - 83.6 = 24.4, so it narrowed by 7.4 points (about 30%), not 9.
- Last year's pattern (8 against 3) would have narrowed the gap by 5 points with no programme at all. Only about 2.4 points of the narrowing can reasonably be credited to Lantern. A gap that closes is what you'd expect for a group selected for low scores, so it isn't evidence of impact.

**Step 3: "Form B was harder, so the true gain is, if anything, larger" (does not follow)**

The facts say June scores are on the same equated scale as September. Equating exists to remove differences in form difficulty, so the adjustment is already made. Adding a further credit for the harder form double-counts it. Without a reason to doubt the equating, there is no basis for "larger than 12". If you suspect the equating is imperfect, ask Dara or the test provider how it was done. The direction of any error isn't known. This step shouldn't count in Lantern's favour.

**Step 4: "Expanding to the bottom 30% gives 2.7, beating the 2-point goal" (fails on three counts)**

1. *The input is inflated.* The 9-point effect is wrong. With about 2.4 points, Lantern's current lift to the district average is 0.15 x 2.4 = about 0.36, not 1.35.
2. *Doubling the coverage doesn't double the benefit.* The pupils added in the 15% to 30% band score higher. They have less room to rebound and may have a different need for tutoring, so the effect on them could be smaller or just different. Nothing here tells us.
3. *The target is next year's change.* Lantern already operates. The pupils currently enrolled are already in this year's results. Only the added 15% would shift next year's average. Even on Dara's own numbers, that increment is 1.35, below the 2-point goal. On the corrected number it is about 0.36. Taking Dara's 2.7 as the total instead, the corrected equivalent is about 0.7, still well short.

**What this means for Monday**

- Lantern may well help. A gain of about 2 to 3 points over the no-programme baseline is plausible, but this data can't tell it apart from noise with any confidence.
- Expanding Lantern is very unlikely to reach the 2-point district goal on its own. The expected district-wide gain is under 1 point.
- If the board wants to decide on evidence, ask Dara for:
  1. the June results of the 30 leavers, if any exist;
  2. the same comparison for last year's bottom-15% pupils broken down by the same finishers-only rule;
  3. a comparison group, such as pupils just above the 15% cut-off, who weren't enrolled;
  4. how Form B was equated to Form A.

If the vote can wait, a short pilot at the 15 to 30% band with a comparison group would answer the expansion question directly.
