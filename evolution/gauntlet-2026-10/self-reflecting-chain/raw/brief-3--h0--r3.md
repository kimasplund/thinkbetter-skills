Short version: Dara's arithmetic is mostly right, but the inputs are wrong in two places and the comparison is wrong in a third. Lantern's effect looks like roughly 2 to 3 points per pupil, not 9. Expanding it to the bottom 30% would lift the district average by well under 1 point, not 2.7. I would not vote on the basis of this summary.

**Step 1: "Lantern added about 9 points per pupil" (does not hold)**

There are two separate problems.

*The two averages cover different pupils.* The 82 includes all 180 pupils. The 94 covers only the 150 who finished. The 30 who left averaged 74 in September, so the finishers' September average was (180 x 82 - 30 x 74) / 150 = 83.6. The like-for-like gain for the finishers is 94 - 83.6 = 10.4, not 12. Part of the 12 was just the weakest pupils dropping out of the data.

*"Everyone else gained 3" is the wrong baseline.* Last year, with no Lantern, the bottom 15% gained 8 points by June. Pupils selected for low scores tend to rise anyway, partly through statistical bounce-back and partly through ordinary growth. Subtracting the 8 from the finishers' 10.4 leaves an estimated Lantern effect of about 2.4 points per finisher. The 9 came from comparing a low-scoring group against a high-scoring group that behaves differently.

How far to trust the 2.4:
- It rests on last year's cohort behaving like this year's. That is a reasonable assumption but an untested one.
- It says nothing about the 30 leavers, whose June scores we don't have. It is an estimate for pupils who completed the programme, and it may be flattered if the pupils who finished were the ones more likely to improve anyway.
- It is a rough estimate, probably somewhere between 1 and 4 points. I would not quote it more precisely than that.

**Step 2: "The gap narrowed 35%, from 26 to 17" (arithmetic right, meaning overstated)**

The numbers check out: 26 to 17 is a 9-point narrowing, which is 34.6%. But the 26 includes the 30 leavers and the 17 doesn't. On the finishers alone the gap went from 108 - 83.6 = 24.4 to 17, a narrowing of 7.4 points, or 30%.

More importantly, the gap would have narrowed without Lantern. Last year's pattern (8 vs 3) closes the gap by about 5 points on its own. So the narrowing attributable to Lantern is about 2.4 points, which matches Step 1. The narrowing is real but mostly isn't Lantern.

**Step 3: "Form B was harder, so the true gain is larger" (not supported)**

The facts say June is reported on the same equated scale as September. Equating exists to remove exactly this difficulty difference, so a harder form should not depress scores on that scale. The "if anything larger" adjustment counts the difficulty twice. Everyone sat Form B, so the 3-point gain for other pupils is also on that scale and any residual form effect would cancel in a comparison. I'd drop this point. The one fair caveat is that equating is never perfect. Ask Dara whether the test publisher reports an equating error, but there is no basis for adjusting the gain upward.

**Step 4: "Expanding to the bottom 30% gives 2.7 points" (fails)**

The multiplication is correct: 15% x 9 = 1.35, and 30% x 9 = 2.7. The problems are the inputs and the doubling.
- With a realistic effect of about 2.4, the current programme lifts the district average by roughly 0.15 x 2.4 = 0.36 points. Allowing for the 30 who left, it is closer to 0.3.
- Doubling coverage does not double the effect per pupil. The second 15% scored higher in September, so they have less room to bounce back, and they may gain less from tutoring. Even if they gained the same 2.4, the district lift would be about 0.7 points, or about 0.6 after typical attrition.
- To reach 2 points from Lantern at 2.4 per pupil, it would have to cover about 83% of Year-4 pupils.

**The goal itself needs defining**

Using last year's pattern, the district average rises by about 0.15 x 8 + 0.85 x 3 = 3.75 points with no programme at all. If the board means "average up 2 points on this September", that may happen with or without Lantern, and Lantern is not what decides it. If the board means "2 points above what would have happened anyway", Lantern at its likely scale cannot deliver that. The committee should settle which one it means before Monday.

**What I would put to the committee**

1. Lantern probably helps, by a modest amount of roughly 2 to 3 points for pupils who finish. That may well be worth its cost per pupil, but the figures offered are not the case for it.
2. The 2.7-point forecast should be withdrawn. A defensible range for the bottom-30% expansion is about 0.5 to 1 point on the district average.
3. Ask Dara for these before the vote: June scores for the 30 leavers if any exist, the cost per pupil, and whether last year's 8-point figure came from a comparable cohort.
4. If the committee wants a cleaner answer, it could expand Lantern next year to a randomly chosen part of the eligible group and compare it with the rest, rather than relying on last year as the comparison.
