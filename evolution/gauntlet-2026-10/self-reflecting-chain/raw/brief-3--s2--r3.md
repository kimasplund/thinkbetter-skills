Short version: Dara's arithmetic is mostly fine, but the reasoning has four problems, and together they change the conclusion. On the evidence given, Lantern's effect looks like roughly 2 to 3 points per pupil, not 9. Doubling it would lift the district average by well under 1 point, not 2.7. I would not vote on the basis of the 2.7 figure.

**Step 1: "Lantern added about 9 points per pupil" (not supported)**

Two separate errors.

*Mismatched groups.* The 82 in September covers all 180 pupils. The 94 in June covers only the 150 who finished. The 30 leavers averaged 74, so the 150 finishers started higher than 82:
- (180 x 82 - 30 x 74) / 150 = 83.6
- The like-for-like gain is 94 - 83.6 = **10.4**, not 12. Attrition made the gain look 1.6 points bigger.

*Wrong comparison group.* Dara subtracts the 3-point gain of "everyone else". But the pupils who were lowest in September are not like everyone else. Last year, with no Lantern, the bottom 15% gained 8 points by June. That is the natural counterfactual, probably because very low scores are partly bad luck on the day and bounce back (regression to the mean), and low scorers have more room to grow. So:
- 10.4 - 8 = **about 2.4 points** attributable to Lantern, per finisher.

That is a rough estimate. It rests on one prior year's cohort, and nothing here gives a margin of error. A range of about 1 to 4 points is a sensible way to hold it.

**Step 2: "The gap narrowed 35%" (arithmetic right, meaning misleading)**

- 108 - 82 = 26 and 111 - 94 = 17. The 9-point narrowing is 35% of 26, so the sums are correct.
- But without Lantern, last year's pattern (8 vs 3) would already have narrowed the gap by 5 points, from 26 to 21, about 19%. Most of the "35%" is what you'd expect anyway.
- Using only finishers adds a further distortion, as in step 1.

**Step 3: "Form B is harder, so the true gain is larger" (does not follow)**

The facts say June is reported on the same equated scale as September. Equating exists precisely to make scores comparable across forms of different difficulty. So the difficulty has, in principle, already been adjusted for, and adding more on top double counts. The reasoning would only hold if the equating were known to be imperfect, and then the direction of any error is unknown. I would also check that last year's 8-point and 3-point baselines used the same form pairing. If they didn't, the comparison in step 1 needs a caveat. Treat the claim as unsupported rather than as a reason to be more optimistic.

**Step 4: "Expanding to the bottom 30% doubles the effect to 2.7" (fails on several counts)**

- Even taking Dara's inputs, 15% x 9 = 1.35 is right, but the input is wrong.
- With the corrected figure, the district effect is about 0.15 x 2.4, which is **about 0.36**. If you count only the 150 finishers, it's 150/1,200 x 2.4 = 0.3. Both are far below 2.
- Doubling the reach does not double the effect. The pupils from the 15th to 30th percentile score higher, so there is less room to gain, and less regression-to-the-mean effect to confuse things. Their benefit from the tutoring is unknown and plausibly smaller.
- Expansion at the same effect size gives about 0.7 points, roughly a third of the 2-point goal.
- Attrition will probably recur. If 1 in 6 leave, the effect on the district is smaller again.
- The goal is a rise in the district average "next year". Some of the district's natural movement (for example, the 3-point gain for everyone else) is not Lantern's doing and shouldn't be counted toward the goal or against it without a clear baseline.

**What I would tell the board**

1. Lantern probably helps, but the evidence supports a modest effect (about 2 to 3 points per pupil), not 9.
2. Expanding to the bottom 30% is unlikely to reach the 2-point district goal by itself. Dara's 2.7 should be withdrawn.
3. If the board wants a firmer number before committing money, one option is to expand in a way that allows comparison, for example by assigning places among eligible pupils by lottery or by school, so next year's results show the real effect.
4. Ask Dara for: June scores of the 30 leavers if they remain in the district (to avoid dropping them silently), results by school or class, and confirmation of how the equating was done.

Assumptions: I treated "everyone else" as the 1,020 non-Lantern pupils with the figures Dara gave, took last year's cohort as comparable to this year's, and assumed the 8-point baseline applies to the pupils who stayed in Lantern.
