I went through Petra's case line by line, and I would not present it on Thursday as written. The oven may still be a good buy, but the numbers as they stand do not clear the board's four-month test. There are four errors, and all of them push the payback in the flattering direction.

**1. The proofing cabinet caps output at 60 loaves a load, not 72.**
The proofer holds 60 loaves and proofs in the same 40 minutes as the bake, so dough can only reach the oven 60 at a time. The D9's 72-loaf capacity can't be used.
- Output is 60 x 9 = 540 loaves a day, up 108 from today's 432. Petra's figure of 648 (up 216) is out of reach.
- Her "150 extra loaves" doesn't hold either. You can add at most 108, even though demand is there for 150.

**2. €6.00 is revenue, not profit.**
- The €6.00 includes 10% VAT, which goes to the tax office. Net revenue is 6.00 / 1.10 = €5.45.
- Ingredients and packaging are €1.70, so the margin is about €3.75 a loaf before energy. Petra's €900 a day treats the full shelf price as profit.

**3. The oven increases energy cost. It doesn't save any.**
- Energy per load goes from €4.00 to €5.20 (30% more). Over 9 loads that is €46.80 a day against €36.00 now, so +€10.80 a day.
- The per-loaf comparison of 7.2 cents versus 8.3 cents only works at 72 loaves a load. At the 60 you can actually fill, it is 5.20 / 60 = 8.7 cents, which is worse than today's 8.3.
- The "€7 a day saving" is really a cost of about €11 a day.

**4. The financing arithmetic is wrong, and it is mixed into the payback test inconsistently.**
- 6% x €54,000 x 5 charges interest on the full balance for all five years. On a standard monthly repayment loan the balance shrinks, so the payment is about €1,044 a month. Total interest is roughly €8,600 and total repayment about €62,600, not €70,200.
- The board's rule is almost certainly payback on the purchase price. Petra's version adds the interest but the daily profit takes no account of financing, so the two sides don't match. Either compare the €54,000 price against operating profit, or compare the full loan cost against the same profit. Don't blend the two.

**Corrected daily gain**
- Extra margin: 108 loaves x €3.75 = about €405
- Less extra energy: about €11
- Net: about **€395 a day**, against Petra's €907

**Corrected payback (6 trading days a week, about 26 a month)**

| Basis | Cost | Days | Months |
|---|---|---|---|
| Purchase price | €54,000 | about 137 | about 5.3 |
| Price plus corrected loan interest | about €62,600 | about 159 | about 6.1 |

Both are outside four months, which is about 104 trading days. To pass, the oven would need to earn about €520 a day on the purchase price.

**There may be a way to make it work.**
Everything above is driven by the proofer. If a third rack or a larger cabinet lifted capacity to 72, you could capture the full 150 loaves of unmet demand:
- 150 x €3.75 = about €563, less about €11 energy, is roughly €552 a day.
- €54,000 / €552 is about 98 days, or about 3.8 months, which squeaks inside the rule on the purchase price.
- That only holds if the extra proofing capacity costs under roughly €3,000 to €3,500. Get a quote before Thursday.

**What I would check before presenting**
- Whether the "roughly 150 loaves turned away" is measured or estimated. It drives everything, and the margin is thin even in the best case.
- Whether you can actually sell 108 more loaves a day across the three shops, and whether the bakehouse can handle the extra labour and delivery.
- Whether the old oven can be sold or kept as backup, and what the D9's installation and servicing cost. None of that is in the case yet.
- Which basis the board uses for payback: price only, or including financing.

**For Thursday**
Don't pitch "three months, pure upside". A more credible pitch is that the D9 as costed pays back in about five to six months, and that with a proofer upgrade it could get inside four. Ask the board to approve it conditional on a proofer quote. The opportunity is real, with 150 loaves a day of unmet demand, but it is smaller and slower than Petra's draft suggests. It is better to find that out now than to have a board member find it in the room.
