Short version: Dara's arithmetic is mostly right, but the reasoning doesn't hold. Lantern's measurable effect looks like roughly 2 to 3 points per pupil, not 9. Expanding it would raise the district average by well under 1 point, not 2.7. I would not vote on the basis that expansion meets the 2-point goal.

**Step 1: "Lantern added about 9 points per pupil" (not supported)**

There are two problems.

*The two Lantern figures cover different pupils.* The September 82 includes all 180 pupils. The June 94 covers only the 150 who finished. The 30 who left averaged 74 in September, so the finishers started higher than 82:

- All 180 pupils: 180 x 82 = 14,760 points
- The 30 leavers: 30 x 74 = 2,220 points
- The 150 finishers: 14,760 - 2,220 = 12,540, which is 83.6 on average

The like-for-like gain is 94 - 83.6 = **10.4 points**, not 12. Part of Dara's 12 comes from the weakest pupils dropping out of the June figure.

*"Everyone else" is the wrong comparison.* Pupils chosen because they scored in the bottom 15% tend to score higher on a retest even with no help, because some of their low September scores were bad days. Last year, with no Lantern, that same group gained 8 points by June, against 3 for everyone else. So the expected gain without Lantern is about 8, not 3.

The estimate of Lantern's effect is therefore about 10.4 - 8 = **2.4 points per pupil**. Using Dara's unadjusted 12, it would be 4. Her 9 compares against the wrong baseline (12 - 3), and 6 of those 9 points are the ordinary rebound that happens anyway.

This 2.4 is a rough estimate, not a measured effect. The 8-point baseline comes from one year. The 150 finishers may differ from the 30 leavers in ways that affect their gains. Treat it as "probably a small positive effect", not as a precise number.

**Step 2: "The gap narrowed from 26 to 17, a 35% reduction" (arithmetic right, meaning overstated)**

The numbers check out: 108 - 82 = 26, 111 - 94 = 17, and 9 / 26 is about 35%. But:

- Using the like-for-like September figure, the starting gap is 108 - 83.6 = 24.4, and the reduction is 7.4 / 24.4, about **30%**.
- Without Lantern, the gap would have narrowed anyway. Last year's gains of 8 vs 3 imply 26 shrinking to about 21, a reduction of roughly 19%. Only the difference beyond that can be credited to Lantern.

**Step 3: "Form B was harder, so the true gain is larger than 12" (does not follow)**

Dara says the June scores are on the same equated scale as September's. Equating exists to adjust for differences in difficulty between forms, so a 94 on Form B is already comparable to an 82 on the September form. Adding a further credit for difficulty would count it twice. At most, the harder form adds a little measurement noise. There is no basis for saying the gain is "if anything" larger. If the committee wants reassurance here, ask whoever ran the equating how well it works at the low end of the scale, where the Lantern pupils sit.

**Step 4: "Expanding to the bottom 30% beats the 2-point goal" (does not hold)**

The arithmetic is right: 15% x 9 = 1.35. The input is wrong. With the corrected effect:

- Current Lantern: 0.15 x 2.4 is about **0.36 points** on the district average.
- Doubling coverage to 30%: about **0.7 points** at best, if the extra pupils benefit as much as the first group.

That assumption probably fails. Pupils ranked 181 to 360 score higher, have less room to improve, and have less rebound to ride on. Their effect is likely smaller than the first group's. So 0.7 is more likely a ceiling than a central estimate. Even on Dara's own 9 points, doubling gives 2.7 only under that same unchecked assumption.

**A point about the goal itself**

The target is a 2-point rise in next year's Year-4 average, and that is a different cohort from this year's. Last year's pattern, with no Lantern, implies the district average rose by about 0.15 x 8 + 0.85 x 3 = 3.75 points between September and June. That is a within-year gain by the same pupils, not a year-on-year change in cohort average, so the two are not directly comparable. But it shows that "raise the average by 2" needs a clear definition (September to June for the same pupils, or this cohort against last year's) before any programme can be judged against it.

**What I would tell the committee on Monday**

1. The evidence supports "Lantern probably helps, by a modest amount", not "Lantern adds 9 points".
2. Expansion on this evidence would deliver well under 1 point of district gain. It will not reach the 2-point goal by itself.
3. Before voting, ask Dara for:
   - the finishers-only comparison (83.6 to 94)
   - this year's results set against last year's bottom-15% gain of 8
   - how the 30 leavers' scores changed, if they were retested
   - the equating documentation for the two test forms
4. If the board wants a firmer estimate before spending more, a cleaner test is to expand to part of the next group and compare with similar pupils who are not enrolled, rather than relying on last year as the baseline.

Assumptions I made: "everyone else" is the other 1,020 pupils; last year's pupils and this year's are comparable; and the 8-point gain last year reflects what would happen to this group without Lantern.
