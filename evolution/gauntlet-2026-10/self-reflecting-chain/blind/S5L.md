Short version: Dara's arithmetic is mostly correct, but the reasoning has four problems. Together they shrink the estimated effect of Lantern from about 9 points to roughly 2 to 4 points. They also mean the case for beating the 2-point goal by expanding to the bottom 30% does not hold. Here is each step.

**Step 1: "Lantern added about 9 points per pupil" (not supported)**

The 12-point gain compares two different groups. September includes all 180 pupils, but June covers only the 150 who finished.
- The 30 leavers averaged 74 in September. So the 150 finishers averaged (180 x 82 - 30 x 74) / 150 = 83.6 in September.
- The like-for-like gain is 94 - 83.6 = 10.4 points, not 12. The 1.6 points of difference is purely the weaker pupils dropping out of the June figure.

The comparison with "everyone else gained 3" is also the wrong benchmark. Last year, with no Lantern, the bottom 15% gained 8 points by June while everyone else gained 3. Low scorers tend to rise on retest anyway, because part of a very low score is bad luck on the day (regression to the mean). Lantern is also selected on exactly that low score.
- Against the 8-point baseline, the effect is about 10.4 - 8 = 2.4 points.
- Even with Dara's uncorrected 12, it would be 12 - 8 = 4 points.
- A realistic range is therefore about 2 to 4 points, not 9.

Caveats on the baseline. It comes from one earlier cohort, so it is noisy. It may also have used a different test form. Treat 2.4 as a rough estimate, not a precise one. A pupil-level comparison of Lantern pupils against similar non-Lantern pupils would be much stronger. The 30 leavers are also a question in themselves. They may have moved away, or they may have withdrawn because the programme was not working for them.

**Step 2: "The gap narrowed 35%, from 26 to 17" (arithmetic right, meaning overstated)**

108 - 82 = 26, 111 - 94 = 17, and 9/26 is about 35%. But this has the same two flaws as step 1.
- The June group is missing the leavers. On a like-for-like basis the starting gap is 108 - 83.6 = 24.4, so the narrowing is 7.4 points, about 30%.
- The gap would have narrowed without Lantern. Last year's pattern (8 against 3) shrinks it by 5 points on its own. That is roughly 20% of 26, with no programme at all.

So the gap narrowing is not good evidence of Lantern's impact. Only the part beyond the usual narrowing is.

**Step 3: "Form B is harder, so the true gain is larger" (does not follow)**

There are two separate problems.
- The scores are already on an equated scale. Equating exists to remove the difficulty difference between forms. Adding a further "harder test" credit double-counts it. If equating is reliable, there is nothing to add.
- Even if Form B did depress scores, every pupil sat it, including the comparison group. A difference between groups is not inflated or deflated by it.

The claim is unjustified, and the honest position is "no adjustment needed". Dara might ask the test provider how reliable the equating is for low scorers in particular. Equating is often least precise at the ends of the scale.

**Step 4: "1.35 now, 2.7 if doubled, beats the goal" (this fails)**

The multiplication is correct: 15% x 9 = 1.35. But the 9 is the wrong input.
- With the corrected effect of about 2.4, Lantern's contribution to the district average is 0.15 x 2.4, about 0.36 points. Even with 4 points, it is 0.6.
- Doubling to the bottom 30% assumes the new pupils benefit as much as the first group. They are higher scorers, with less room to improve and less regression-to-the-mean effect. The effect is more likely smaller. Delivery capacity and dropout (17% left this year) could also change as the programme grows.
- At best, expanding gives about 0.7 to 1.2 points on the district average. That is well short of 2.7, and short of the 2-point goal.
- There is also a framing issue. Next year's Year-4 average is for a new cohort, not these pupils. The goal depends on the next cohort's starting level, not only on what Lantern does.

**What I would tell the board on Monday**
1. Lantern looks beneficial, but the evidence supports a modest effect, roughly 2 to 4 points per participating pupil. It does not support 9.
2. On current evidence, expansion alone will not meet the 2-point district goal. It would contribute a fraction of it.
3. Before the vote, ask Dara for these:
   - the like-for-like figures (finishers only, September and June);
   - a comparison with similar non-participants, if any exist, such as pupils just above the 15% cutoff;
   - the evidence behind the 8-point baseline;
   - why the 30 pupils left.
4. If the board wants a pilot expansion, consider staging it and measuring it properly. That would show whether the effect holds for higher-scoring pupils.

**Assumptions:** I took the figures as given. I assumed the 8-point and 3-point baselines are on the same scale as this year's scores, and that the 150 finishers' June scores are the full June data for Lantern pupils.
