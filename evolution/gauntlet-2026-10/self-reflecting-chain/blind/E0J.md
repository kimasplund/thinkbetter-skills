Short version: Dara's arithmetic is mostly right, but the reasoning doesn't hold up. The evidence supports an effect of roughly 2 to 3 points for the pupils who finished Lantern, not 9. On that evidence, expanding to the bottom 30% would lift the district average by well under 1 point, not 2.7. I would not vote on the basis of this summary as written.

Here is each step in order.

**Step 1: "Lantern added about 9 points per pupil" (12 minus 3). This is not supported, for two reasons.**

*The before and after groups are different.* The September 82 includes all 180 pupils. The June 94 covers only the 150 who finished. The 30 who left averaged 74 in September, so the 150 finishers averaged (180 x 82 - 30 x 74) / 150 = 83.6 in September. Their like-for-like gain is 94 - 83.6 = 10.4, not 12. About 1.6 points of the headline gain comes from the lowest scorers dropping out of the June figure.

*The comparison group is wrong.* "Everyone else" gained 3, but pupils picked for being at the bottom don't behave like everyone else. Last year, with no Lantern, the bottom 15% gained 8 points by June, while everyone else gained 3. Much of that is the usual bounce-back when you select the lowest scorers on one sitting. That 8 is the fair baseline. Against it, the finishers' gain is 10.4 - 8 = about 2.4 points. Dara's 9 therefore mostly reflects the bounce-back that would have happened anyway.

This estimate is rough. It rests on one prior year, and it assumes this year's cohort would have behaved like last year's. It also covers only finishers. Pupils who stay in a programme may differ from those who leave, for example in motivation or stability at home, and we can't see what the 30 leavers would have scored. The true effect could be somewhat higher or lower than 2.4, but 9 is not a defensible figure.

**Step 2: "The gap narrowed from 26 to 17, a 35% reduction." The arithmetic is correct, but the conclusion drawn from it isn't.**

26 to 17 is a 9-point narrowing, and 9/26 is 34.6%. But on the like-for-like finisher group, the starting gap is 108 - 83.6 = 24.4, so the narrowing is 7.4 points, about 30%. And last year's pattern (8 against 3) means the gap would have narrowed by about 5 points with no programme at all. Only the part beyond that, about 2.4 points, can be credited to Lantern. The gap narrowing mostly repeats Step 1's problem.

**Step 3: "Form B is harder, so the true gain is, if anything, larger." This does not follow.**

The June scores are on the same equated scale as September's. Equating exists to put different forms on a common scale, so differences in question difficulty have already been adjusted for. Adding a further allowance for the harder form double-counts the adjustment. Without evidence that the equating is poor, there is no reason to push the gain above the measured figure. The honest statement is that the form difference is already accounted for. If anyone doubts the equating, ask whoever produced it for evidence, such as a link-sample check. Don't assume the bias runs in Lantern's favour.

**Step 4: "9 points x 15% = 1.35, and doubling coverage gives 2.7, beating the 2-point goal." The multiplication is right, but the inputs and the doubling don't hold.**

- *Wrong effect size.* Replace 9 with about 2.4. Lantern covers 150 finishing pupils out of 1,200, which is 12.5%. That gives 150 x 2.4 / 1,200 = about 0.3 points on the district average. If the leavers gained nothing from it, that figure stands. If the effect is larger than my estimate, the district gain scales up with it.
- *Doubling assumes the next 15% benefit equally.* The pupils ranked 181 to 360 score higher, have less room to gain, and are less likely to bounce back. Their benefit is unknown and plausibly smaller. Even at the full 2.4 points for 360 pupils with the same completion rate, the lift would be about 0.7 points, far short of 2.
- *Dropout.* One in six pupils left. If an expanded programme loses a similar share, that further dilutes the lift unless retention improves.

**Goal check.** The board wants the district average up by 2 points. On the evidence given, expanding Lantern alone is very unlikely to deliver that. Also, check what "raise the average by 2 points next year" means. It could be next year's cohort against this year's, or growth within a year. Last year, with no Lantern, the district's within-year gain was about 0.15 x 8 + 0.85 x 3 = 3.75 points. Within-year growth of that size would make a 2-point goal trivial, so the goal is probably meant to be cohort against cohort. That needs to be settled before anyone claims Lantern would meet it.

**What I would tell the committee on Monday**

1. The like-for-like gain for finishers is 10.4 points, not 12.
2. Compared with what similar low scorers gained last year, Lantern's measurable contribution is about 2.4 points per finishing pupil, with real uncertainty.
3. No Form B adjustment is needed.
4. Expansion to 30% would likely lift the district average by well under 1 point, not 2.7. It is a modest gain that may still be worth the cost, but it won't meet the 2-point goal by itself.

**Data that would firm this up:** June scores for the 30 who left, if they stayed in the district. Results from more than one prior year for the bottom 15%. A comparison group of similar low scorers who were not enrolled, if any exist. Any evidence on how Lantern would work for pupils above the current cut-off.
