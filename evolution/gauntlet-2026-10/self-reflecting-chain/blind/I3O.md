**Short answer:** Dara's arithmetic is mostly right, but the reasoning has three problems, and together they overstate Lantern's effect by a factor of three or four. Expansion to the bottom 30% would not come close to the 2-point goal. I would not take the "2.7" figure into Monday's vote.

**Step 1: "Lantern added about 9 points per pupil." Not supported.**

There are two errors here.

- *The two averages cover different pupils.* The September 82 includes the 30 who left. Their September average was 74, so the 150 who finished averaged (180 x 82 - 30 x 74) / 150 = 83.6 in September. Their like-for-like gain is 94 - 83.6 = **10.4**, not 12. About 1.6 points of the headline gain comes from the weakest pupils dropping out of the June figure.
- *"Everyone else" is the wrong comparison group.* Lantern pupils were chosen because they scored lowest, and the lowest scorers tend to move back toward the middle on a retest, whatever is done for them. Last year, with no Lantern, the bottom 15% gained 8 points while everyone else gained 3. The right baseline is therefore 8, not 3.

Corrected estimate: 10.4 - 8 = **about 2.4 points per pupil who finished**. Even on Dara's uncorrected 12, the figure would be 4, not 9. The 2.4 rests on last year's cohort being a fair stand-in for this year's. That is the weakest link in the chain, but it is far better than comparing against a group that was never expected to rebound.

**Step 2: "The gap narrowed 35%, from 26 to 17." The arithmetic is right, but the meaning is not.**

108 - 82 = 26 and 111 - 94 = 17, and 9/26 is about 35%. But a gap between the lowest scorers and everyone else was always going to narrow. Last year it narrowed by 5 points (8 minus 3) with no programme. On Dara's own numbers, 5 of the 9 points of narrowing is the expected rebound. On like-for-like numbers (24.4 down to 17, so 7.4 points), only about 2.4 is Lantern. The 35% figure should not be quoted as a Lantern result.

**Step 3: "Form B was harder, so the true gain is larger than 12." This double-counts.**

The June scores are on the same equated scale as September's. Equating exists to remove exactly this difficulty difference, so the scores are already comparable and no further upward adjustment is warranted. The "larger than 12" claim should be dropped. The only reasonable caveat is that equating is never perfect, and nothing in the facts says which way any residual error would run. Separately, 12 is not the right starting figure anyway (see Step 1).

**Step 4: "The district average rises 1.35, and 2.7 at 30%." The arithmetic is right, but the input is wrong and the extrapolation is shaky.**

0.15 x 9 = 1.35 and doubling gives 2.7, but 9 is not Lantern's effect. Using 2.4:

- Current programme: only the 150 finishers benefit. 150/1,200 x 2.4 = **about 0.3 points** on the district average, not 1.35.
- Doubled to the bottom 30%: about **0.6 to 0.7 points**, if the effect held. It might not. The second 15% are higher scorers, so they have less room to rebound and may gain less from tutoring. Keeping 150 of every 180 enrolled would also need to hold.
- Even with Dara's inflated gain after correcting only the baseline (4 points), 30% coverage gives 0.3 x 4 = 1.2, still short of 2. Reaching 2 would need roughly 6.7 points of true effect per pupil across 360 pupils, nearly three times what the evidence shows.

**The goal itself needs defining.** Last year, with no programme, the district average rose by about 0.15 x 8 + 0.85 x 3 = 3.75 points. If "raise the average by 2 points" means September to June, the district may reach it with no change. If it means 2 points above what would happen anyway, Lantern at any scale on this evidence will fall well short. The committee should settle which meaning the board intends.

**What I would tell the board**

- Lantern probably helps, by about 2 to 2.5 points per pupil who finishes, but this comes from a single-year comparison and is uncertain.
- Expansion to 30% would likely add well under 1 point to the district average, not 2.7.
- 30 of 180 pupils (one in six) left the programme. That is worth understanding in its own right.

**To firm this up, ask Dara for:**

- June scores for the 30 leavers, where they stayed in the district.
- Last year's bottom-15% figures broken down more finely, ideally by score band.
- Whether last year's gain of 8 came from a comparable test form.

**Assumptions I made:** last year's cohort is a fair guide to the expected rebound this year; the "everyone else" groups are the same size and composition across years; and the equated scale works as described.
