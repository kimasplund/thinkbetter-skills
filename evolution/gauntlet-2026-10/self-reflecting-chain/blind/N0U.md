Short version: Dara's arithmetic is mostly right, but the reasoning has four problems, and together they change the conclusion. On the figures you gave, Lantern's effect looks like about 2.4 points per pupil, not 9. Expanding it to the bottom 30% would lift the district average by well under 1 point, not 2.7. I would not vote on the basis that expansion meets the 2-point goal.

**Step 1: "Lantern added about 9 points per pupil" (not supported)**

There are two errors here.

a) The September and June groups don't match. The September 82 covers all 180 pupils, but the June 94 covers only the 150 who finished. The 30 leavers averaged 74, so the 150 finishers averaged (180 x 82 - 30 x 74) / 150 = 83.6 in September. The like-for-like gain is 94 - 83.6 = 10.4, not 12. (The leavers were weaker, so dropping them flatters the June average.)

b) The wrong comparison group. Dara subtracts the 3-point gain of "everyone else". The pupils who score lowest in September tend to gain more anyway, partly because of regression to the mean. Last year, with no Lantern, the bottom 15% gained 8. That is the right baseline. So Lantern's estimated effect is about 10.4 - 8 = 2.4 points per finishing pupil.

Caveats on the 2.4 figure. It assumes this year's cohort would have behaved like last year's. Pupils who finished a programme may differ from those who left in ways beyond their September score, such as motivation or stability at home. The estimate is rough, but it is much better grounded than 9.

**Step 2: "Gap narrowed from 26 to 17, a 35% reduction" (arithmetic right, interpretation misleading)**

The numbers check out: 108 - 82 = 26, 111 - 94 = 17, and 9/26 is about 35%. But the gap narrowed partly because of the groups' different starting points. Last year the gap narrowed by 5 points (8 vs 3) with no programme. Using the matched finisher group, the gap went from 108 - 83.6 = 24.4 to 17, a narrowing of 7.4. About 5 of that would have happened anyway, which leaves roughly 2.4 attributable to Lantern. That matches Step 1. The 35% figure should not be quoted as Lantern's impact.

**Step 3: "Harder Form B means the true gain is larger than 12" (not justified)**

You say June scores are reported on the same equated scale as September's. Equating exists to remove the effect of form difficulty, so a Form B score is already adjusted. Adding a further correction for difficulty counts it twice. The claim also applies only to the 12 figure, which was wrong anyway (see Step 1). I'd drop the claim unless Dara can show the equating is imperfect. If last year's comparison used a different form pairing, the 8-point baseline is worth checking for comparability, but that is a question to ask rather than a reason to inflate the gain.

**Step 4: "15% x 9 = 1.35, and doubling to 30% gives 2.7" (arithmetic right, inputs wrong)**

- 0.15 x 9 = 1.35 is correct, but the 9 is not. With 2.4, the district-wide lift is about 0.15 x 2.4 = 0.36. Because 30 of the 180 left, and presumably gained nothing from the programme, the realised figure may be closer to 0.3.
- Doubling the coverage doubles the effect only if the next 15% of pupils benefit as much as the lowest 15%. Pupils scoring higher have less room to improve, and may need a different kind of help. Capacity, tutor quality and dropout rates may also change at twice the scale.
- Even if the effect held at 2.4, the bottom 30% would give about 0.72 points, far short of 2. It would take roughly 2.4 x 0.30 = 0.72 at best, so even tripling the coverage would fall short.

**What I would tell the committee**

- The best estimate is a gain of roughly 2 to 3 points for Lantern pupils beyond what they would have gained anyway. That is a modest, real benefit, not a 9-point one.
- Expanding to 30% is unlikely to meet the board's 2-point goal on its own. It might still be worth doing for its own sake, but it should be sold as a smaller improvement.
- Another point: if the goal compares next year's cohort average with this year's, then ordinary cohort variation could swamp an effect of 0.3 to 0.7 points. Be clear about how success will be measured.

**Questions to put to Dara before Monday**

1. Can you confirm the 83.6 September average for finishers, and show the like-for-like gain?
2. Is last year's 8-point and 3-point comparison on the same test forms and scale as this year's?
3. Why did the 30 pupils leave? Did they leave for reasons related to the programme?
4. Is there any evidence of how pupils just above the 15% cut-off respond to tutoring?
5. What would expansion cost per point of average gain compared with other options?

The assumptions I've made: the figures are as stated, last year's cohort is a fair baseline for this year's, and "raise the average by 2 points" means the district-wide mean.
