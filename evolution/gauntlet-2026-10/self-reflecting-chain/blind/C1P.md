Short answer: no, it doesn't hold up. Petra's arithmetic is fine in places, but four of the five steps have problems, and corrected for them the payback is roughly 23 weeks (about 5.3 months), not 13. On the board's four-month rule, the D9 as specified fails. There is a route to a pass, covered at the end.

I assumed four months is about 104 trading days (26 days a month at six days a week). I also assumed no extra labour cost, and that the €54,000 is the all-in price. Please check those.

**Step 1: capacity (72 loaves x 9 = 648, up 216)**

The multiplication is right, but the oven isn't the only limit. The proofer holds 60 loaves per 40-minute cycle, and dough goes straight from proofer to oven. The D9 could never be fed 72 loaves per load. Today the 48-loaf oven is the bottleneck, but with the D9 the proofer becomes the bottleneck.

- Real output is 60 x 9 = 540 loaves a day, not 648.
- That is 108 more than today's 432, not 216.
- It falls short of the 150 loaves of turned-away demand.

So the "comfortably covers" claim fails. The D9 covers about 108 of the 150.

**Step 2: €900 a day "profit"**

This is revenue, not profit, and it has two errors.

- The €6.00 shelf price includes 10% VAT, which isn't ours to keep. Net revenue is 6.00 / 1.10 = €5.45 per loaf.
- Ingredients and packaging of €1.70 per loaf come off that, leaving about €3.75 per loaf.

On the corrected 108 extra loaves, the contribution is 108 x 3.75 = about €405 a day. Petra's 150 loaves would give about €563 a day, still well short of €900.

**Step 3: the energy "saving" of €7 a day**

Her per-loaf figures are right. Today €4.00 / 48 = 8.3c, and the D9 at €5.20 / 72 = 7.2c. Two things are wrong.

- The 7.2c only applies if every load is a full 72. With the proofer capping loads at 60, the D9 costs €5.20 / 60 = 8.7c per loaf, which is worse than today.
- A per-loaf rate can't turn a 30% rise in energy per load into a saving. We run the same 9 loads a day, so energy goes from €36 to €46.80 a day. That is an extra cost of €10.80 a day, not a €7 gain. The sign is wrong.

**Step 4: financing cost (€16,200 interest, €70,200 total)**

Two problems here, one about method and one about numbers.

- Method: payback is normally measured on the purchase price, not price plus financing. Adding interest to the cost makes the oven look worse, so this error goes against Petra. If the board wants financing included, use the figure below.
- Numbers: 6% x €54,000 x 5 is simple interest on the full balance for five years. A standard monthly repayment loan reduces the balance as you pay. The payment is about €1,044 a month, total repaid about €62,600, and interest about €8,600, not €16,200.

**Step 5: €70,200 / €907 = 77 days**

The division is correct, but both the numerator and denominator are wrong. Redoing it with the corrected figures:

- Daily gain: €405 contribution minus €10.80 extra energy = about €395 a day.
- On the purchase price: €54,000 / €395 = about 137 trading days, roughly 23 weeks or 5.3 months. This fails the four-month test.
- With financing included: about €62,600 / €395 = about 159 days, roughly 6.1 months. This also fails.

**Where this leaves Thursday**

The weakest part of my numbers is the assumption that nothing else changes (labour, loading time, and whether demand really is a steady 150 a day). Even so, the conclusion is not close. To pass, the oven needs to earn about €519 a day (€54,000 / 104 days), and as specified it earns about €395.

The fix is the proofer. If the proofer is expanded to hold 72 loaves, the D9 can run full:

- That covers the full 150 extra loaves: 150 x 3.75 = €563 contribution, less €10.80 energy, is about €552 a day.
- Payback is then €54,000 / €552 = about 98 days, or 3.8 months. That passes, narrowly.

The headroom is thin. A four-month limit is about 104 days, so the proofer upgrade would have to cost less than roughly €3,400 for the combined package to stay inside it. Before Thursday, get a quote for a third proofer rack or a larger cabinet.

My suggestion is to present the corrected numbers openly: the D9 alone fails the rule at about 5.3 months, and the D9 plus a proofer upgrade is a close call that depends on that quote and on the demand figure holding up.
