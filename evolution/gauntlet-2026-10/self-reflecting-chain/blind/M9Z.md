**Short answer: Owen's plan does not hold. Instead of two weeks of slack, the plan as written misses the 29 April sign-off by about two days. I would not tell the director to send the invitations on the strength of this schedule.**

I went through his four steps in order, because each one feeds the next.

**Step 1: when objects can go in. Owen is wrong; the earliest date is Monday 12 April, not 31 March.**
The policy counts 30 days from the end of the last wet trade in the room, not from when the HVAC stabilised. Silicone sealant is a wet trade, and the case fitters finish on Friday 12 March. Counting from there:
- If 12 March is day 1, day 30 is Saturday 10 April.
- If 13 March is day 1, day 30 is Sunday 11 April.

Either way, the first working day for the mounting team is Monday 12 April. That is 12 days later than Owen's date.

This assumes the climate readings stay stable while the fitters are in the room. If the silicone work knocks the readings out of range, the count restarts. Please ask facilities to confirm.

**Step 2: freezing. Owen is wrong on the count, but it doesn't matter.**
Eight oversized crates take two slots each, so the load is 52 + 16 = 68 slots. The freezer holds 12, so that is 6 loads, not 5. At 72 hours each, back to back from Monday 15 March, the last load comes out on Friday 2 April. With the 24-hour sealed rest it is ready on Saturday 3 April. This assumes someone can load and unload at weekends, because the 72-hour cycles land on Sundays and Saturdays. Even with that, every crate is ready more than a week before the 12 April gate in Step 1. Freezing is not the constraint, and starting earlier than 15 March would add margin.

**Step 3: mounting. Owen is wrong; he counted calendar days, but the team works weekdays only.**
126 objects at 9 a day is 14 working days. Starting Monday 12 April:
- 12–16 April: 5 days (45 objects)
- 19–23 April: 10 days (90 objects)
- 26–29 April: 14 days (126 objects)

Mounting finishes on Thursday 29 April, not 13 April. (Even from Owen's own 31 March start, 14 working days would end on 19 April, not 13 April.)

**Step 4: final check. Owen leaves out the 48-hour closed period.**
Each case must stay closed for 48 hours after its last object is mounted. If the last objects go in on 29 April, the earliest final check is Saturday 1 May. That is opening day, and two days after the conservator's sign-off is due. The plan has about two days of negative slack, not two weeks of positive slack.

**What it would take to recover**
For the final check to fall on or before Thursday 29 April, the last object must be mounted by about Tuesday 27 April. Between 12 and 27 April there are 12 working days, which is 108 objects at the current rate. You are 18 objects short, which is two days of work. Options:
- Raise the installation rate to about 10.5 objects a day (11 or 12 with a bigger team or a second crew).
- Add two extra working days, for example two weekend days, at the current rate.
- Get the mounting team access to the room on the earliest legitimate day, with crates and cases staged beforehand, so no weekday is lost.

Even with these, the margin is close to zero. If the last case closes on Tuesday 27 April, its 48 hours run out on Thursday 29 April, the day of sign-off itself. A target of finishing on Monday 26 April, which needs about 12 objects a day, would give one day of buffer. Closing cases one at a time as they fill, and having the conservator check them as they become ready, rather than all at the end, also helps.
