Short version: Dara's arithmetic is mostly right, but the conclusion is not. Three of the four steps have problems, and together they overstate Lantern's effect by a large margin. On the figures you gave, expanding Lantern to the bottom 30% would not come close to the 2-point goal. Here is each step.

**Step 1: "12-point gain, so Lantern added about 9 points per pupil"**

There are two errors.

*a) The September and June groups don't match.* September (82) includes all 180 pupils. June (94) covers only the 150 who finished. The 30 who left averaged 74 in September, so the finishers' September average was:

(180 x 82 - 30 x 74) / 150 = 12,540 / 150 = 83.6

The like-for-like gain for the finishers is 94 - 83.6 = 10.4 points, not 12.

*b) Comparing with "everyone else" ignores what these pupils would have gained anyway.* The pupils selected were the lowest scorers. Last year, before Lantern, the bottom 15% gained 8 points by June, while everyone else gained 3. Part of that is regression to the mean, because very low scores tend to drift up on retest. Part may be catch-up growth. So the right comparison for Lantern pupils is 8, not 3:

- Using Dara's 12: 12 - 8 = 4 points.
- Using the corrected 10.4: 10.4 - 8 = 2.4 points per finisher.

So the best estimate of Lantern's effect is roughly 2 to 4 points, not 9. The corrected figure (about 2.4) is the better one. It is also uncertain, because it relies on last year's cohort being comparable to this year's.

**Step 2: "The gap narrowed from 26 to 17, a 35% reduction"**

The arithmetic is right: 108 - 82 = 26, 111 - 94 = 17, and 9/26 is about 35%. But the figure doesn't show what Dara wants it to show.

- It has the same group mismatch as Step 1. The September gap uses all 180 pupils and the June gap uses only the 150 finishers.
- Without Lantern, the gap would probably have narrowed anyway. Last year's pattern (8 vs 3) implies a 5-point narrowing, from 26 to about 21. Lantern's contribution is the difference between the 17 and that 21, not the whole narrowing.

I'd drop this as evidence of impact.

**Step 3: "Form B is harder, so the true gain is, if anything, larger"**

This doesn't follow.

- June scores are on the same equated scale as September's. Equating exists to adjust for form difficulty, so the harder questions should already be accounted for.
- If the equating were imperfect, the error could go in either direction, so "if anything larger" is unsupported.
- Any form effect would hit Lantern pupils and everyone else alike. It would therefore largely cancel in a comparison between the two groups.

I'd remove this step, or replace it with a note that the equating should be confirmed with whoever ran the test.

**Step 4: "9 points x 15% = 1.35, so doubling to 30% gives 2.7, beating the goal"**

The multiplication is correct, but the inputs are not.

- *Wrong per-pupil effect.* Replace 9 with the corrected figure. If the 2.4-point effect applies only to the 150 finishers, and the 30 leavers got nothing, the effect per enrolled pupil is 2.4 x 150/180 = 2.0. The district-wide lift is then 0.15 x 2.0 = about 0.3 points. Even using the generous 4-point figure, it is 0.15 x 4 = 0.6.
- *Doubling is not safe.* Pupils ranked 16th to 30th percentile start higher, so they have less room to improve and less regression-to-the-mean effect to build on. The effect per pupil would probably be smaller than in the current group. Delivery capacity and quality may also fall when a programme doubles in size.
- *Even the generous case falls short.* At 30%, the generous 4-point figure gives 0.3 x 4 = 1.2, and the corrected figure gives about 0.6. Both are below 2.
- *The goal is about a different comparison.* The goal is to raise the district's Year-4 average next year. Dara's figures are September-to-June gains for one cohort. The goal needs a view of next year's cohort against this year's, which also depends on normal growth, cohort differences and test form. No step here connects the two.

**Bottom line for Monday**

| Claim | Dara | Corrected |
|---|---|---|
| Lantern gain (finishers) | 12 | 10.4 |
| Lantern effect per pupil | 9 | about 2.4 (4 at most) |
| District lift at 15% | 1.35 | about 0.3 (0.6 at most) |
| District lift at 30% | 2.7 | about 0.6 (1.2 at most), likely less |

My reading: the data suggest Lantern probably helps, modestly. They do not support the claim that expanding it would reach the 2-point goal. If the board wants to reach 2 points, Lantern alone is unlikely to do it.

**Suggestions before the vote**

1. Ask Dara to redo the comparison on the 150 finishers only, using the September-to-June gain against last year's 8-point baseline.
2. Ask what is known about the 30 leavers and whether any had June data. Also ask whether pupils who finished differ systematically from those who left, for example in motivation or attendance.
3. Ask whether last year's bottom-15% cohort is comparable, and whether the same selection cutoff and test conditions applied.
4. If the expansion goes ahead, consider a design that gives a cleaner comparison, such as a waiting list or a staggered rollout. Pupils just above and just below the 15% cutoff would be a natural comparison group.

Assumptions: I took the 108-to-111 figures for everyone else as correct, and assumed the leavers got no benefit from Lantern. I also assumed last year's cohort is a fair baseline. If any of these is wrong, the numbers above will shift, but the overall conclusion should hold.
