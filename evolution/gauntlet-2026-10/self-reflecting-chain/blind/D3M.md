I went through his four steps in order, because each one feeds the next.

**Step 1: when objects can go in. Owen is wrong; it's 12 April, not 31 March.**
- The policy counts 30 days from the end of the last wet trade in the room. Sealant is named as a wet trade.
- The case fitters run silicone seals 8-12 March, so the clock starts after 12 March, not on 1 March.
- Counting 13 March as day 1 (the same way Owen counted 1 March as day 1), day 30 is Sunday 11 April. Objects can go in from Monday 12 April.
- That is 12 days later than Owen's date.
- This assumes the HVAC readings stayed stable during the fitters' week. If they didn't, the clock restarts and everything below moves later. Please check the logs for 8-12 March.

**Step 2: freezing. This part works, but his arithmetic is wrong.**
- The 60 crates need 52 slots for the standard crates plus 16 for the 8 oversized ones. That is 68 slots, and the freezer holds 12.
- 68 divided by 12 is 5.7, so you need 6 loads, not 5.
- Starting Monday 15 March with loads back to back, the last load comes out Friday 2 April.
- Add the 24 hours at room temperature and the last crates are ready on Saturday 3 April.
- That is still more than a week before objects can go in on 12 April, so freezing isn't the constraint. It does assume someone can load and unload at weekends. If not, the finish slips by a few days but should still clear 12 April.
- Owen also left out the 24-hour room-temperature hold. Fix both so the numbers are right, but neither changes the outcome.

**Step 3: mounting. This is where the plan breaks.**
- 126 objects at 9 a day is 14 working days. The team works Monday to Friday, and Owen counted 14 calendar days.
- Starting Monday 12 April, the 14 working days run 12-16 April, 19-23 April, then 26-29 April.
- The last object goes in on Thursday 29 April, not 13 April.

**Step 4: final check. This follows from Step 3 and fails.**
- Each case stays closed for 48 hours after its last object is mounted.
- If the last object goes in on 29 April, the final check can't happen before Saturday 1 May, which is opening day.
- The conservator's sign-off is due Thursday 29 April. So the plan misses it by about two days, instead of beating it by two weeks.

**Weakest points**

- The 9 objects a day rate, which is Owen's figure. Any lost day pushes the end date later.
