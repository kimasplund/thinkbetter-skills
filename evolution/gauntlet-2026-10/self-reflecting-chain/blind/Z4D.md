**Step 1: when objects can go in. Owen's date is wrong.**
The policy counts 30 days from the end of the last wet trade in the room, not from when the HVAC stabilised. Silicone on the cases is a sealant, so the fitters' return on 8-12 March is a wet trade. The clock therefore starts after Friday 12 March. Day 30 is Sunday 11 April, and the first day objects can enter is **Monday 12 April**, not Wednesday 31 March. That is 12 days later than Owen has it.
This assumes the climate stays stable through the fitters' visit. If it dips, the clock restarts.

**Step 2: freezing. Owen's load count is wrong, but it doesn't matter.**
The 8 oversized crates take 16 slots and the other 52 take one each, so the total is 68 slots. The freezer holds 12 per load, so that is 6 loads, not 5. Five loads only hold 60 slots.

**Step 3: mounting. Owen counted calendar days, not working days.**

**Step 4: the final check. Owen skipped the 48 hours.**
Each case stays closed for 48 hours after its last object goes in, so a case finished on 29 April can't be checked until **Saturday 1 May**, which is opening day. That is two days after the sign-off. **What it takes to hit 29 April**
The last object has to be mounted by **Tuesday 27 April**. Working back from the earliest start of 12 April, that leaves 12 working days, which is 108 objects. You are 18 objects (two days of work) short. Options:
1. **Two extra Saturday sessions** (for example 17 and 24 April) at 9 objects a day. Mounting then finishes Saturday 24 April, the last cases can be checked on Monday 26 April, and you have about three days in hand. This is the most robust option.
