Notes (J-A, brief-1)

My own check of the case: the proofer limits output to 540 loaves a day (+108). Contribution is (6/1.1 - 1.70) = about EUR 3.75 a loaf, so about EUR 405 a day. Energy adds EUR 10.80 a day, so the net gain is about EUR 395 a day. Payback is about 137 trading days on EUR 54,000, which fails the 104-day limit. A real amortised loan costs about EUR 8,640 in interest. With a 72-loaf proofer the gain is about EUR 552 a day and payback about 98 days, which leaves about EUR 3,400 of headroom. All twelve replies get these core corrections right, so they differ mainly in extras and precision.

- X1P: All core corrections, with a useful proofer threshold (65 loaves) and headroom. "A proofer expansion would very likely exceed that" is a guess stated with some confidence. Good Thursday bullets.
- W0S: All core corrections, plus the only reply that raises the strongest counter-case: the loan repayment of EUR 1,044 a month is easy to carry against a gain of about EUR 10k a month. It keeps that apart from the board's payback rule. The headroom must also cover labour and the old oven's disposal. Clear assumptions. Best overall.
- A9R: All core corrections, with good sensitivity work (needs about 141 loaves, about a 64-loaf proofer, +18 days per EUR 10k, and fails if demand is about 100). Broad checklist. One muddled sentence about energy being "double counted", and the bottom line is slightly optimistic.
