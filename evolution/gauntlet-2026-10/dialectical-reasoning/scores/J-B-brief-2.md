# J-B scores, brief-2

Notes:
- C3U: Strong on the works-vs-concerts point, the four questions for the council and a measurement plan. But it states that "the 2019 decline came while you were programming mostly familiar work". That is not in the brief, and the reply uses it to argue against the greatest-hits option. The "why this satisfies both sides" section mostly repeats earlier points.
- Z6X: Careful with evidence. It says plainly that "classics refill Saturdays" is untested, and it lists clear "what would change my view" conditions and cheap first steps. One unsupported claim: that €70k is a "small fraction" of a budget the brief never gives.
- C5N: Very actionable. Counting last season's living-composer works is a cheap check that is easy to miss, and it gives a dated action list and a split of decision roles. It names outside composers with confidence. It overreaches with "the board's split has no real basis" and "about four works" from the film concert.
- M1S: Well calibrated. It flags rights fees for film scores, works out Unbound's real capacity (about 1,000 seats) and asks for side-by-side comparison measures. It assumes without support that Fridays are currently a Beethoven/Tchaikovsky core.
- E2V: Concise and sound, with assumptions stated explicitly. It is thinner than the others on what to do first, and it says existing living-composer works "cost nothing extra", which ignores rental and rights costs.
- P5I: Decision rules for each slot and a 60% review trigger are useful. Its confidence statement separates the grant reading from the sales impact well. It is a little long, and the "used up the one lever" claim is speculative.
- H9Z: Covers the ground but runs long, and the board-presentation section repeats earlier points. It calls the film-concert cost "low" without basis, and its argument that Ilse's grant risk rests on a stricter council reading is muddled.
- C4F: Tight and usable. Checking past Saturday results by programme type is good. But it states as fact that whole contemporary programmes "are the weakest sellers in the main hall", which the brief does not support, and it builds part of the argument on that.
