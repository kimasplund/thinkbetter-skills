I'm general manager of the Varnholt Philharmonic, a 62-player regional orchestra. Single-ticket sales are down 22% since 2019, and the board wants a programming direction for the 2027–28 season by March.

Our chair, Gerrit Lund, wants a season built on popular classics: Beethoven, Tchaikovsky, the big Romantic concertos. Our music director, Ilse Raadt, says that would gut the orchestra's identity and cost us the regional arts council grant, which is 38% of our income. She reads the grant as requiring a fifth of our concerts to be contemporary programmes. She has also warned that several principal players will leave if we turn into a greatest-hits band.

What I have:

- The grant condition reads: "At least 20% of works programmed across the season must be by living composers."
- Last season we gave 16 main-stage concerts, each with three or four works. Friday subscription concerts averaged 81% full; Saturday single-ticket concerts averaged 48%.
- Our only main-stage sell-out was a concert of film and video-game scores, all by living composers. Ilse called it a one-off that isn't real repertoire.
- Players run a late-night chamber series, Unbound, in the 180-seat foyer. All six nights sold out last season, with a median audience age of 31 against 64 in the main hall. It's mostly new music.
- Commissioning a new 20-minute orchestral work costs about €35,000.

Gerrit and Ilse both have a point, and the board is split down the middle. Which way should we go?
