Short answer: neither is fully right, and the vote on 28 November doesn't have to be a choice between closing and carrying on. Each partner has one claim that holds up and one that your own facts undercut.

**Dr Osei's dairy argument is weaker than it looks**

- Your facts say calvings are attended on the farm by farm-animal vets taking calls from home, and that they have never used the Kilbride clinic at night. Closing overnight small-animal cover therefore doesn't remove the round-the-clock calving cover the contracts require.
- Check the contract wording before relying on this. If a contract requires access to a hospital for surgical referrals, such as caesareans needing theatre, the picture changes. If it only requires 24-hour farm call-out, the €260,000 isn't tied to Kilbride.
- Some risk remains. The dairy clients may care about the practice's overall reputation, or about a wider out-of-hours offer. Ask them directly before you vote.

**Dr Osei's abandonment point is still strong**

- Kilbride is the only overnight emergency service within 70 km. Brackley is 70 km away and its director says its overnight service is already stretched.
- About a fifth of your 1,950 overnight patients (roughly 390) are Thornby clients. Thornby has no out-of-hours service of its own. Closing leaves those animals and owners with nowhere close, and some emergencies won't survive a 70 km trip.
- This is an animal-welfare and professional-duty issue as well as a commercial one. Check your regulator's expectations on emergency cover for your registered clients. Thornby's clients aren't yours, but your clients are.

**Dr Halloran's retention argument is right about the cause, but closing is not the only fix**

- Three vets left in 18 months and all three named nights. That is strong evidence. If nothing changes, you probably lose more.
- The cause is how nights are staffed, not the fact that nights exist. All 11 small-animal vets do one or two shifts a month on top of a full day rota. That is fair on paper, but it is why people leave: they're tired and unrewarded for the burden.
- Last spring you turned down an experienced emergency vet because she would only work nights. That is the clearest sign the policy is the problem. A dedicated night vet would have taken the load off the other 11.

**Options between the two positions**

1. **Dedicated night staffing.** Hire one or two emergency-focused vets, or a night-only rota, and take day-rota vets off nights or down to occasional cover. Pay a proper night premium and give protected rest. This addresses the exit-interview complaint directly.
2. **Shorter hours.** 71% of patients arrive before 1 am, and after 1 am you average under two a night. Run 7 pm to about 1 am staffed on site, with a telephone triage and on-call arrangement after that. This cuts the heaviest burden while keeping the service. Under two patients a night after 1 am is a small load, so the savings in vet hours are large and the clinical cost is small.
3. **Charge for what it costs.** Introduce or raise an out-of-hours fee. Open a conversation with Thornby about a per-patient or annual contribution, since they depend on you for about 390 patients a year.
4. **Phased decision.** If you can't resolve it by 28 November, vote for a time-limited trial (say six months from January) of option 1 or 2 with set measures: vet retention, overnight caseload, loss per month, and dairy client feedback.

**On the €190,000 loss**

- That figure needs context. Part of it is probably the cost of day-rota vets' time, which you pay anyway. Work out the true marginal cost of overnight against the revenue it brings. Also count the referral value of overnight clients who stay with you for daytime care.
- Against that, losing three vets costs a lot in recruitment, locums and lost capacity. Put a number on it.
- Even so, a loss of that size can't continue indefinitely. Options 2 and 3 both narrow it.

**What I'd recommend for the vote**

Don't close outright in January. Vote to end the everyone-does-nights model, move to shortened staffed hours with a paid, voluntary or dedicated night team, and set a review date. Before the vote, do three things:

1. Read the four dairy contracts and, if possible, speak to those clients.
2. Talk to Brackley and Thornby about capacity and cost-sharing.
3. Check the regulatory position on emergency cover.

**Assumptions I've made**

- The €190,000 loss is the clinic's full cost against revenue, and I don't know how it is built up.
- The farm-animal vets' call-out arrangement meets the contract terms.
- I can't judge Brackley's real capacity beyond what its director told you.

If the dairy contracts turn out to depend on the Kilbride clinic, or Brackley can't absorb your overnight cases, the case for keeping some overnight service gets stronger. If neither is true and you can't recruit dedicated night staff, Dr Halloran's closure becomes the more defensible outcome, but then it should come with a proper referral plan for Thornby's clients and your own.
