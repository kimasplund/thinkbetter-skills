Short answer: neither is fully right, and your own facts suggest a third option that beats both. Each partner has the diagnosis half right and the remedy wrong.

**Where Dr Halloran is right**

- Losing three vets in 18 months, all citing nights, is a real retention problem. A €190,000 annual loss on top of that is hard to defend as the status quo.
- But her remedy treats "nights" as one lump. Your facts say otherwise.

**Where Dr Osei is right, and where she is not**

- Closing outright abandons a service nobody else within 70 km provides. Brackley says it is already stretched, so the referral plan may not work in practice. Some animals would be seen late or not at all.
- Her dairy argument, though, looks weak on your facts. Calvings are attended on the farm by the farm-animal vets, on their own home-call rota, and they have never used the Kilbride clinic at night. Closing the clinic's overnight service does not obviously remove the calving cover the contracts require. Check the contract wording before you rely on this. If the contracts only require farm call-out cover, the €260,000 is not at risk from this decision. If they require a hospital to be open for referred cases, such as a caesarean that needs surgery, that is a different matter.

**What the facts point to**

1. **The problem is the rota design, not nights as such.** All 11 vets do one or two nights a month on top of a full day rota. That is "fair", but it means everyone gets disrupted sleep and has no dedicated recovery. You also turned down an experienced emergency vet who wanted only nights. That is a likely fix you declined.
2. **Demand is front-loaded.** 71% of the 1,950 patients arrive before 1 am. After 1 am the average is under two a night. A full 7 pm to 8 am service staffs many hours for very little work.
3. **A fifth of your overnight patients, about 390, come from Thornby**, which has no out-of-hours service. That is both a referral pressure and a possible source of income. Thornby could pay for access or contribute to the cost.

**Options to compare before 28 November**

- **A. Dedicated night staffing.** Hire one or two emergency-focused vets, or a small night team, on night-only or block contracts with proper time off in lieu. Day vets come off the night rota. This answers the exit-interview complaint directly.
- **B. Shorten the hours.** For example, run staffed 7 pm to about 1 am, then an on-call arrangement or telephone triage for later. This keeps most of the caseload (71%) and cuts the heaviest burden. Check the clinical and reputational risk of the late-night gap, and consider a triage line with a clear escalation route.
- **C. Cost-share with Thornby** and any others who send patients. Even a modest annual fee or per-case surcharge would narrow the €190,000 loss.
- **D. Review fees.** Check overnight pricing against the real cost of the service.
- **E. Close entirely**, only if A to D cannot be made to work, and only after confirming Brackley's capacity and the dairy contract terms.

A and B together, with C, are the most promising. They respond to Dr Halloran's retention point without giving up the service Dr Osei wants to keep.

**Things to verify first**

- The exact wording of the four dairy contracts. This is the single fact that most changes the decision.
- Whether Brackley can take roughly 1,950 cases a year, which is unlikely if they are already stretched. Ask for numbers, not impressions.
- What a night-only vet or small team would cost against the €190,000 loss. A shortened service may cost less than it appears.
- Whether Thornby would contribute or would move its clients elsewhere.
- Whether the three departed vets would have stayed under a different rota. Their exit interviews may say.

**Assumptions I made**: that the €190,000 is the net loss after overnight revenue, and that the farm-animal team's home rota is currently adequate for the contracts.

**For the vote**: I would not put "close or keep" to the partners. I would put it as "keep a redesigned service, with a fixed review date, and close only if specific conditions are not met by then." Setting those conditions in advance, such as a recruitment target and a Thornby agreement, gives both partners something concrete to hold the plan to, and it should break the deadlock.
