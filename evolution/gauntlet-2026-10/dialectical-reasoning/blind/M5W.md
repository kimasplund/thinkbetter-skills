Short answer: neither plan is right as stated, and I'd hold off on a 28 November vote on "close or stay open". Your own numbers point to a third option that gives each partner what they actually care about. Some of your figures also don't support the story you've been told, so I'll start there.

**Two assumptions to check first**

1. **The dairy contracts probably aren't at risk from closing Kilbride.** Dr Osei's case rests on the €260,000 of dairy work needing round-the-clock calving cover. But you say calvings are attended on the farm by your farm-animal vets, taking calls from home, and that they have never used the Kilbride clinic at night. If so, the contracted cover doesn't depend on the clinic. Before the vote, read the four contracts and confirm what "round-the-clock" commits you to. If it means farm-visit cover, closing Kilbride leaves it untouched. If it names Kilbride as backup for hospitalised cattle, that changes things. I'd settle this with the contract text, not with opinions.

2. **The exit interviews say nights are the problem. They don't say the service is.** All 11 small-animal vets do one or two night shifts a month on top of a full day rota. That is equal, but it isn't the same as fair. A vet who works a day, then a night, then a day has no recovery. You also turned down an experienced emergency vet last spring because she would only work nights. Under your current policy, the most direct fix for the burnout was available and you declined it. So "nights drive vets out" is probably true, but "closing overnight is the only way to fix that" isn't established. Dr Halloran's diagnosis may be right while her remedy is bigger than it needs to be.

**Where each partner is right**

- **Dr Halloran** is right that €190,000 a year plus three resignations in 18 months is not sustainable, and that the current rota is part of the cause. Replacing a vet costs far more than the vet's notice period, and each exit adds load on the rest.
- **Dr Osei** is right that closing hands your clients' emergencies to someone else. That concern holds even if the contracts turn out to be safe. The 70 km referral hospital is a real gap for a rural valley, and Brackley's director has already told you they are stretched. Referring everything there risks sending sick animals on a long drive to a service that may not have room. It also risks reputational damage to you when it goes badly.

**What your data suggests**

- About 1,950 patients last year. At 71% before 1 am, roughly 1,385 arrive between 7 pm and 1 am. The remaining ~565 spread over 1 am to 8 am is about 1.5 a night. The workload is concentrated in the first six hours. The small hours are mostly staffed waiting.
- About a fifth, roughly 390 patients, are Thornby clients. Thornby has no out-of-hours service and relies on you. That is revenue you are not charging for, and also a reason to be careful about closing, since those clients would have nowhere nearer than 70 km.

**A third option to put to the vote**

1. **Shorten the clinic's hours** to roughly 7 pm to 1 am, which captures about 70% of the demand. After 1 am, move to a phone triage line with one vet on call from home and a clear escalation route into the clinic for true emergencies. That turns most of the 1-to-8 am shift from sitting in the building into being reachable, which is a very different burden.
2. **Hire or contract dedicated emergency vets** to carry most of the night work, and take the 11 day vets off the rota or down to an occasional backup. The vet you turned down is the kind of person this needs. The aim is to remove nights from the people who are leaving because of them.
3. **Charge Thornby for the service.** Offer a retainer or per-case arrangement so that the 20% of your night work that benefits another practice helps pay for it. Thornby has a strong reason to agree, since without you they have no cover.
4. **Agree with Brackley now**, not in January, what they can absorb. If they can take the post-1 am overflow reliably, you have a fallback. If they can't, you know closing outright is not viable.

**Does the maths work?** I can't tell without costs, and it's the main thing missing. Whether a shortened service with dedicated staff cuts the €190,000 loss enough depends on what a dedicated emergency vet costs, what you save on overtime and replacement hiring, and what Thornby would pay. I'd want a one-page estimate of that before 28 November. Even if the loss only halves, retaining two or three vets who would otherwise leave is worth a large amount against that.

**What I'd do before the vote**

- Read the dairy contracts and confirm what cover they require.
- Ask the three leavers, or the current vets, whether it was the volume of night work, the stacking onto day shifts, or the lack of any choice. That decides which of the options above would actually have kept them.
- Get a written answer from Brackley on capacity.
- Approach Thornby.

If the contracts really are independent of Kilbride and Brackley can't absorb the load, I'd expect the shortened-hours, dedicated-staff model to beat both of the original proposals. If the contracts do depend on the clinic, Dr Osei's position gets stronger and the case is for keeping full hours with a restructured rota. Either way, the choice isn't between abandoning clients and losing vets.
