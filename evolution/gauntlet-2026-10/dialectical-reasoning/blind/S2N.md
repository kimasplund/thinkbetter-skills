She says closing overnight would cost you the four dairy contracts (about €260,000 a year) because they need round-the-clock calving cover. Her weaker arguments still count for something.

**Dr Halloran has the right diagnosis and the wrong remedy.**
Three of three leavers named nights, and I wouldn't dismiss that because the sample is small. When everyone who leaves gives the same reason, it is a pattern. Losing vets is also expensive, and it is probably the biggest hidden cost in your €190,000.

But look at what exactly they are objecting to. Nights are layered on top of a full day rota for all 11 vets, every month. That structure is what is driving people out, and closing the clinic is only one way to fix it. You also turned down an experienced emergency vet last spring because she would only do nights. That is the opposite of what your problem needs. Your policy of "nobody carries more than their share" kept the rota fair, and it also blocked the obvious fix.

Closing completely would also leave you with the same retention problem if the day rota is the real cause, and I can't tell from three exit interviews which it is. Ask the vets who are still there.

**What your numbers say about a better design.**
- 71% of overnight patients arrive before 1 am. That is roughly 1,385 of 1,950. After 1 am you see under two a night.
- So the expensive part of the service, a vet awake and on site from 1 am to 8 am, serves very few animals. The part that justifies the clinic is 7 pm to about 1 am.
- A model that follows this: staffed clinic from 7 pm to 1 am, then a phone triage line and an on-call vet at home for the rest of the night, with a vet going in only when a case needs it. This is the same arrangement your farm-animal team already works. Your own people have shown it can run.
- Hire the night-only emergency vet you turned away, or someone like her, to take most of the 7 pm to 1 am shifts. Then the day vets are doing nights only occasionally, as backup, and the thing the leavers complained about largely goes away.
- Charge properly. A fifth of the overnight caseload is Thornby's. Agree a referral fee or a retainer with them, and review your out-of-hours pricing. I don't know your current fees, so I can't say how much that closes.
