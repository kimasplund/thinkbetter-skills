**Short answer:** Don't pick either side. Build the 2027-28 season around popular classics, which is Gerrit's instinct, and meet the grant through living composers' works, which is Ilse's constraint. The two don't conflict as much as the board thinks, because the grant condition is looser than Ilse has been reading it.

## The grant condition is about works, not concerts

The clause says "at least 20% of works programmed across the season must be by living composers." Ilse describes it as a fifth of concerts being contemporary programmes. Those are very different requirements.

- 16 concerts with three or four works each is roughly 48 to 64 works, so about 56 on average.
- 20% of that is 10 to 13 works by living composers. Aim for 14 to leave a margin.
- That is one short piece in most concerts, or a few concerts with more. It does not need 3 or 4 contemporary programmes.

Two other points about the wording:

- "Living composers" is not limited to new-music-festival composers. John Williams, Joe Hisaishi, Nobuo Uematsu, Jennifer Higdon, Anna Clyne, Caroline Shaw and Mason Bates all count, as do Arvo Pärt and others whose work audiences already like. Your sell-out concert of film and video-game scores, all by living composers, therefore counts toward the quota. Ilse called it not "real repertoire", but for the grant it is compliant, and it was your best-selling night.
- Whether works played in the Unbound foyer series count as "programmed across the season" is not clear from the clause. Ask the arts council in writing this month. If they count, Unbound alone could cover much of the quota. Until you have that answer in writing, plan as if they don't.

Before the board meets, send the council a draft season and ask them to confirm it meets the condition. A 38% income line depends on one sentence, and both sides are arguing from a paraphrase of it.

## What each side has right

**Gerrit** is right that the box office needs attention. Single tickets are down 22%. Saturday single-ticket concerts averaged 48% full while Friday subscription concerts averaged 81%. Familiar titles are the most dependable way to bring in people who aren't already committed.

**Ilse** is right on two counts. Grant income is 38% of the budget, so any plan that risks the condition is a poor bet however many extra tickets it sells. And a principal leaving is costly and slow to repair. If the players hear "greatest hits band", the auditions and the retention conversations that follow will cost more than the programming change saves.

Ilse's premise is the weak part. She is arguing against a quota that the grant doesn't impose, and she is dismissing evidence from your own sales that contradicts her.

## Suggested shape for 2027-28

1. **Put the popular classics where the empty seats are.** Use the Saturday single-ticket slots for the big Romantic and Classical names. Those are the 48% nights. Keep the Friday subscription programmes closer to what your regular audience expects, since 81% suggests it is working.
2. **Put one living composer in nearly every concert.** A 10-to-15-minute piece before the Tchaikovsky is the standard way to do this. Choose accessible, well-regarded works rather than the hardest available. Players tend to accept this more readily than a whole contemporary programme, because the main course is still the repertoire they trained on.
3. **Repeat the film and game concert, and treat it as the programming it was.** Do it once or twice, with a different programme each time and Ilse or a guest conducting. It is a sold-out evening and about four of your works toward the quota. Present it as the orchestra's new-audience concert, not a stunt.
4. **Use Unbound as the pipeline.** Six sell-outs with a median age of 31, against 64 in the main hall, is the audience the orchestra most needs and does not have. It is also where players can do the work that makes them want to stay. Look at expanding it to more nights or a bigger room, and invite Unbound audiences to a main-stage concert with a ticket offer.
5. **Commission one or two works, not eleven.** At about €35,000 each, a single commission fits the budget as the season's artistic statement, which could be a good outlet for Ilse. Everything else in the quota should come from existing works, which cost rehearsal time but not commissioning fees.

## How the decision splits between the two sides

- Ilse leads on which living composers and which works. She has the standing to say what is good, and she is the one the players need to trust.
- Gerrit leads on the Saturday and subscription mix, and on the marketing of the classics.
- Both of them sign off on a single document showing the quota counted work by work, so no one has to take the claim on trust.

## What I'm unsure about

- **The Saturday gap may not be about repertoire.** It could come from the day of the week, pricing, or who buys single tickets. I'd test it by putting a few of the proposed classics-led Saturdays into the season and reviewing the numbers after the first quarter, not committing every date at once.
- **One sell-out is one data point.** The film and game concert might have drawn a one-time crowd. It is encouraging, but it shouldn't carry a whole strategy. Programme it twice and see whether it repeats.
- **I don't know hall capacity or the budget.** If the classics-led Saturdays fill, the arithmetic will be favourable. Check it against your actual seat counts and fees before the board vote in March.
- **The players' reaction is unknown.** Ilse's warning is a claim about her principals. Ask them directly what would make them stay, rather than guessing from either camp's position.

## What to do before March

1. Write to the arts council this month. Ask whether the 20% is counted by works, whether Unbound counts, and whether they will review a draft season.
2. Count the works in last season's 16 concerts and see how many were by living composers. You may be closer than you think.
3. Draft the season with the 14-work target, then take it to Gerrit and Ilse as one document.

If the council confirms the works-based reading, the board's split has no real basis, because the season can meet Gerrit's commercial case and Ilse's grant condition at once.
