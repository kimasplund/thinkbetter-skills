**Recommendation: don't choose between Gerrit and Ilse. Build a season that is mostly familiar repertoire where single tickets are weak, and meets the grant through living-composer works placed so they help sales instead of hurting them.** The grant wording makes this possible, because it doesn't say what Ilse thinks it says.

## The grant is a count of works, not a share of concerts

The condition is "at least 20% of works programmed across the season must be by living composers." Ilse reads it as a fifth of concerts being contemporary programmes. The text asks for something much smaller and more flexible.

- 16 concerts with three or four works each is roughly 48 to 64 works. 20% is about 10 to 13 works, and 12 is a safe target.
- Those 12 works can be spread across the season. A 7-minute piece by a living composer opening a Beethoven or Tchaikovsky programme counts the same as a full contemporary concert.
- Film and video-game scores by living composers are, on the literal wording, works by living composers. Your only sell-out counts toward the quota.

This turns the dispute from "popular or contemporary" into a question of where the 12 works go. The wording is ambiguous in places, so get the council's confirmation in writing before March. Ask four things:
1. Is the count per work or per minute?
2. Do film and game scores count?
3. Do Unbound programmes count, or only main-stage?
4. Do arrangements by living arrangers count?

If the answer to the last two is yes, Unbound alone could cover a large part of the quota. Plan as if it doesn't, so the plan survives a strict reading.

## What each side has right

**Gerrit.** The audience data supports him. Saturday single-ticket concerts averaged 48% full, against 81% for Friday subscription nights. The 22% single-ticket decline since 2019 is where the money is leaking, and familiar titles are the standard remedy. The sell-out was a living-composer concert, but it was a film and game concert. It doesn't show demand for a conventional contemporary programme.

**Ilse.** She is right about the stakes. The grant is 38% of income, so losing it is a far bigger risk than a soft Saturday. Player retention is also a real cost. Losing principals would damage quality, and quality is what draws audiences. She is also partly right about the sell-out. Selling out once does not make film music a replacement for the core repertoire. Her mistake is the inference that popular classics put the grant at risk. They don't, if the 20% is planned.

**The evidence neither side has used.** Unbound sold out all six nights, mostly with new music, to an audience with a median age of 31 against 64 in the main hall. That counters both positions. New music is not inherently a box-office problem, and the younger audience you will need in ten years is already coming to it. The problem is the main hall's format and packaging.

## Why a plain compromise fails

"Half classics, half contemporary" would put contemporary programmes on Saturdays, your weakest nights, and you would probably miss on both sales and morale. Pure greatest-hits spends the identity and the players on a bet that hasn't been tested. It wouldn't fix the problem anyway, since the 2019 decline came while you were programming mostly familiar work. The useful question is which slot each kind of work belongs in.

## Proposed structure for 2027-28

1. **Saturdays carry the popular repertoire.** Put the big Romantic concertos and the Beethoven and Tchaikovsky programmes here, with a strong soloist where the budget allows. This is the direct answer to the 48%.
2. **Friday subscription concerts stay artistically ambitious.** An 81% house shows the subscribers trust the programming. Keep Ilse's judgement here, and give the principals substantive repertoire.
3. **Living-composer works fill the quota through short pieces.** Put about 8 to 9 short works, 5 to 12 minutes each, as openers or interludes across both series, paired with the popular pieces. Pick accessible, rhythmically vivid work to begin with. Audiences tend to accept short new pieces next to familiar ones.
4. **Two or three film and game concerts.** They are the one format with proven demand, and they add several works to the count. Repeating the sell-out twice is a reasonable test. Call them what they are, a different audience-building product, and keep them separate from the "repertoire" argument Ilse is having.
5. **Two commissions.** Two 20-minute works cost about €70,000 and add two to the count. Choose composers with a link to the region or to a soloist so the premieres can be marketed. Put them on Friday programmes, not Saturdays.
6. **Keep and expand Unbound.** It is sold out and young, and the cost is low. Consider adding nights, subject to the council ruling on whether it counts. Treat it as the pipeline for new single-ticket buyers, and invite its audience to the main hall with a ticket offer.

## Why this should satisfy both sides

- **For Gerrit:** the weakest nights get the most familiar programming, and there is a visible plan to fill them.
- **For Ilse:** the grant is protected with margin, and the Friday programming and Unbound stay intact. Players keep serious repertoire and new-music work. Consult the principals on the Friday programmes and the commissioning shortlist as a retention measure.
- **For the board:** the plan is built from checkable pieces, with a cost you can state (roughly €70,000 for commissions, plus the film-concert production costs).

## What to measure, and when to adjust

- Track Saturday occupancy against 48% and single-ticket revenue per concert. Check after the first third of the season.
- Track how short new works on Saturdays affect sales compared with Saturdays without them. If there is a real drop, move the new pieces to Fridays and use more film and game works to meet the count.
- Count works every quarter against the quota, with a buffer of two works, so a cancellation can't take you under 20%.
- Record how many Unbound attendees buy a main-hall ticket.

## Risks and open questions

- **Sample size.** One sell-out and six Unbound nights are thin evidence. Treat the film concerts as an experiment with a review point, not a settled fact.
- **Grant interpretation.** Everything above depends on the council reading "works" as you do. If it measures by concert or by minutes, the plan has to change, with more of the quota in dedicated contemporary slots. Settle this first.
- **Why single tickets fell.** I don't know whether the cause is repertoire, pricing, marketing, day of the week or habits after the pandemic. The 22% decline may not be fixed by programming alone. Look at what the Saturday no-shows had in common before committing the whole season.
- **Player morale.** A season that looks like a pure greatest-hits list could still cost you principals. Even with the quota met, the Friday programming is what Ilse needs for the retention argument.

**Confidence: about 75%** that this structure meets the grant and improves Saturdays. It is high on the grant arithmetic and lower on how much the audience response will improve, since that is what I can't see from the numbers you've given me.
