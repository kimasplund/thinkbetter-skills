**Short answer:** Neither side's framing is right. Recommend a hybrid: a Saturday-focused popular-anchor season that meets the grant with room to spare, with living-composer work placed deliberately inside it. Don't choose "greatest hits" or "contemporary-heavy".

**Where each side is right, and where each is wrong**

- **Gerrit is right** that the problem sits in single-ticket sales. Fridays are at 81% and Saturdays at 48%. The gap is on the Saturday single-ticket side, so that is where programming should change. Subscribers are mostly fine, so rebuilding the whole season around popular classics risks fixing a problem you don't have on Fridays.
- **Ilse is right** about the grant's importance (38% of income) and about player retention risk. But her reading of the condition is wrong, and that matters. The clause says "at least 20% of **works** programmed" must be by living composers. It does not say 20% of concerts, and it does not require "contemporary programmes". With 16 concerts of 3 to 4 works each, you have roughly 48 to 64 works. 20% is about 10 to 13 works. That can be met with one living-composer piece on most concerts, including otherwise classical programmes. (Confirm the wording and definition of "programmed" with the arts council in writing, since that is the single most important fact here.)

**What the evidence suggests**

- The sell-out was film and game scores, all by living composers. It is evidence of demand for living-composer work that audiences find accessible, not a one-off to dismiss. It also counts toward the grant if the composers are living, which is worth checking.
- Unbound sold out all six nights, with a median audience age of 31 versus 64 in the main hall. That is your strongest evidence of a younger audience for new music. Caveat: 180 seats and six nights is small, so it doesn't prove main-hall demand. It does show the players have credibility with that audience.

**Recommended direction for 2027-28**

1. **Popular anchor on Saturdays.** Put well-known works (Beethoven, Tchaikovsky, a big concerto) on the Saturday single-ticket slots, where you are at 48%.
2. **Living-composer works woven in** at about 25% of works, giving a buffer over the 20% floor. Pair a short, accessible living-composer piece with a classic on the same programme, rather than segregating "contemporary nights" that can be avoided.
3. **Repeat the film and game programme** or a variant, perhaps once or twice, as a deliberate acquisition product. Ilse's objection that it isn't real repertoire is fair artistically, but it fills the hall and meets the quota.
4. **Grow Unbound** (more nights, possibly a larger space if the numbers hold) and use it as the new-music laboratory the players own. This is also the best answer to the retention worry, because it gives principals meaningful artistic ownership.
5. **Commission selectively.** At about €35,000 each, two or three commissions (roughly €70,000 to €105,000) are affordable only if tied to a clear audience or funder story. Many of the quota works can be existing pieces by living composers, which cost nothing extra.
6. **Keep Fridays largely as they are**, with the subscribers who are already loyal.

**Risks and what to check**

- Confirm the grant definition and the counting method (per work, per performance, or by duration).
- Test the retention concern directly: ask the principals what they would accept. Popular repertoire with strong guest soloists and some new work may be fine for them. Their objection may be to a lack of artistic ambition, not to Beethoven.
- Set a measure for the board: for example Saturday fill rate rising from 48% to a target you agree in advance, grant compliance at 20% or more, and no loss of principals. Review after the first half-season.
- I don't have budget or revenue per seat figures. If popular programmes need higher guest fees, check the net effect and not just attendance.

**Assumptions:** the 16 concerts and 3 to 4 works each hold for next season, and film and game composers in the programme are living.

**For the board:** the choice is not "identity versus survival". The grant is easier to meet than Ilse fears, and the audience evidence supports a mix. That should let both Gerrit and Ilse back one plan.
