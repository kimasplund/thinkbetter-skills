# Premortem: Saltmarsh Clay second space (beginner wheel courses)
**Horizon:** Dec 25 · **Success was defined as:** "the new space covering its own costs by Christmas without pulling money from the original studio"
**Note on the percentages:** shares of failure ("if this dies, this is what killed it"), not the probability the plan fails overall. They sum to ~100. **Overall chance this misses its success definition by Dec 25, as written:** ~85%, because it misses on its own numbers before any risk materializes.
**Assumptions I'm working from:** you gave me no past comparable project, member fee, kiln loan size or fit-out budget, so I filled gaps. "Covering its costs" means monthly tuition at or above monthly costs in December. Courses run back to back, 8 weeks each, with tuition paid up front. The $9,400 a month excludes clay, glaze, wheels and fit-out. The lease starts June 2 and rent begins about Aug 2. You have not yet asked members whether they will ferry work across town. Your own numbers are used wherever you gave them.

## The Autopsy
**The strongest case for this plan:** You have 30 people who already pay monthly, which proves people in Corvel Bay will pay for clay. Of the 140 sign-ups, each one stood at a wheel and chose to leave their name, which is better evidence than a survey. Beginner courses are the standard feeder into membership, and course graduates are the cheapest members you will ever acquire. That upside is not in your numbers at all. Two rent-free months cut the cost of fitting out. A gas kiln you own is a resalable asset. Your cohorts run in the evenings, when the studio would otherwise be idle. A smart operator looking at this would call it a measured expansion by someone who knows the trade.

Six ways this died, ranked by probability.

### 1. The $13,600 was a per-course figure read as a monthly one, and the new space never covered $9,400 a month
**Probability:** ~40% (the plan misses even at 100% fill; its revenue ceiling is about $7,400 a month against $9,400 in costs)
**Existing guard:** none. The "comfortably in the black" line is the thing that went unchecked.
**Cause of death:** Four full cohorts bring in $13,600 every 8 weeks, which is about $7,400 a month. Costs ran $9,400 a month before clay, glaze and wheels. At 75% fill the figure is about $5,500 a month, and the December run-rate gap was roughly $4,000 a month. Break-even needed about 51 paid seats per 8-week cycle, and the room had 40.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (June): lease signed; the rent-free period went on fit-out and kiln deposits. The $13,600 vs $9,400 comparison was never re-run per month.
- Month 2 (July): the kiln loan, insurance and instructor retainer ran about $5,300 a month with zero income. Wheels, sinks and shelving came out of the $22,000.
- Month 3 (August): rent began Aug 2. Cohort 1 opened about 75% full, so $10,200 was collected up front. August looked profitable, which made the plan feel validated.
- Month 4 (September): no tuition arrived until cohort 2 on Sept 29. The month showed roughly -$9,400, and the August "profit" turned out to be a prepayment.
- Month 5 to 6 (Oct to Dec): cohort 3 started Nov 24. Cumulative tuition by Dec 25 was about $30,600 against about $57,600 of costs since June. The $22,000 was gone by mid-December, and the last rent payment came out of the original studio's account.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that tuition per course is comparable to costs per month.
**First warning sign:** a monthly spreadsheet today. In flight, it was the early-September close showing no tuition coming in.
**Reference class:** second locations of small membership studios usually break even later than the first one (weakly sourced; this is a pattern, not a published base rate). Disanalogy: you have a ready waitlist and a feeder membership, which pushes the number down; the shortfall at full fill pushes it back up, so ~40% holds.

### 2. The gas meter upgrade landed after the Aug 4 start, and every cohort began without a working kiln
**Probability:** ~20% (the quoted 10 to 14 weeks from June 2 ends Aug 11 to Sept 8, and Aug 4 sits before the best case)
**Existing guard:** none. Applying the day the lease is signed starts the clock; it does not shorten it.
**Cause of death:** The utility's quote was a lower bound. Lead times over two weeks run 2 to 3 times the stated figure until evidence says otherwise (weakly sourced rule of thumb), which put energization between mid-October and late December. The kiln could not fire until the meter was live, and installation and inspection came after that.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (June): application filed June 2; the utility needed a landlord authorization letter, which took two weeks to obtain.
- Month 2 (July): a site survey found the service line undersized and moved the job to the construction queue. The kiln was delivered and sat in boxes.
- Month 3 (August): rent started Aug 2. Aug 4 cohorts met with no way to bisque. You offered 8 weeks of throwing and no fired pieces, and the first refund requests arrived in week 3.
- Month 4 (September): cohorts 2 and 3 were pushed; the waitlist cooled; no revenue arrived for 6 weeks.
- Month 5 to 6 (Oct to Dec): the kiln fired for the first time in November, so only one cycle's tuition landed before Christmas.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that a utility's range is a commitment, and that Aug 4 only has to be reachable in the optimistic case.
**First warning sign:** no written energization date by July 7.
**Reference class:** a utility's quoted range as a floor (weakly sourced). Disanalogy: applying immediately helps; the application's dependence on a signed lease and landlord paperwork pushes it back.

### 3. The cohorts filled and the single kiln could not keep up
**Probability:** ~15% (the pieces math does not close even if everything else works)
**Existing guard:** the plan counted firings (three a week) but not that each piece needs two of them.
**Cause of death:** Members put through about 60 pieces a week. Forty beginners at 2 to 3 pieces a week each add 80 to 120, for 140 to 180 pieces a week. Three loads of 70 give 210 slots, but each finished piece needs a bisque and a glaze slot, so the kiln finishes about 105 pieces a week. Demand ran 133% to 171% of capacity.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 to 2 (June to July): fit-out went smoothly; the kiln arrived.
- Month 3 (August, assuming the meter arrived on time): the first full cohorts produced about 100 pieces a week by week 2. Bisque and glaze loads alternated, and the backlog grew by 35 to 75 pieces a week.
- Month 4 (September): by week 8 of cohort 1 the backlog was 280 to 600 pieces. Students finished the course with unglazed bisque.
- Month 5 (October): you gave beginners priority and members complained; or you gave members priority and students asked for refunds. Cohort 2 re-enrollment fell.
- Month 6 (December): with a firing backlog, no one signed up for the holiday cohort, and the owner worked weekends running firings.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that firings per week measure capacity, rather than finished pieces per week.
**First warning sign:** more than 40 pieces waiting at Friday close, by week 3 of the first cohort.
**Reference class:** kiln throughput is arithmetic, not a base rate. Disanalogy: none beyond the cap on beginner pieces, which you can control.

### 4. The 140 names turned out to be a list of people who liked a free session
**Probability:** ~10% (a free booth is a low-intent source; filling 40 seats needs 29% of the list to pay $340)
**Existing guard:** none. Names were collected, with no deposits.
**Cause of death:** Free try-it lists convert at a small fraction of the list (weakly sourced; the real figure was found only by asking for money). The list was also a month old at the lease signing and three months old at the first class.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (June): the email went out; 14 people replied.
- Month 2 (July): 22 paid. Cohorts were consolidated to two.
- Month 3 to 4: cohort 1 started at 55% fill, and word of mouth was too thin to refill the next one.
- Month 5 to 6: you discounted to $249 to fill seats, which cut revenue and anchored the price.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that interest at a booth predicts payment at $340.
**First warning sign:** fewer than 25 paid deposits by May 28.
**Tie-break:** #4 and #5 are both ~10%. #4 sits first because it fires on its own in the first cohort, while #5 needs two independent events to coincide.

### 5. The compound failure: the dying electric kiln failed while the new kiln was not live, and members left
**Probability:** ~10% (it needs #2's slip plus a failure of a kiln already described as dying)
**Existing guard:** none. The plan says "I won't replace it."
**Cause of death:** For six or more weeks, neither kiln could fire. The 30 members, who pay for firing, had nowhere to fire. Their commitment to the plan is "accept": they have not been asked, and a pocket veto is how members leave.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (June): the old kiln limped through its last good firings.
- Month 2 (July): the old kiln failed. Members' greenware piled up on shelves. The new kiln was waiting on a meter that had not arrived.
- Month 3 (August): six members froze their memberships. New rent began.
- Month 4 (September): the gas kiln finally fired. Reduction firing changed the glaze colors members had developed for an electric kiln, and a second round of cancellations followed.
- Month 5 to 6 (Oct to Dec): membership fell from 30 to about 20, so the original studio lost roughly a third of its income. The new site's shortfall was paid out of the original studio's account. The success definition broke, with a personal guarantee still running.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that members will tolerate being a delivery service for a kiln that is not yet live, and that the dying kiln will last until the new one is ready.
**First warning sign:** a missed or aborted firing at the original studio before the new kiln has fired once.

### 6. The fit-out, compliance and firing labor were never priced
**Probability:** ~5% (the exposure is real but small next to the others; the cost is a cash drain rather than a single cause of death)
**Existing guard:** none. Fit-out appears in the plan only as the reason for the rent-free months.
**Cause of death:** Ten wheels need electrical circuits, sinks and clay traps, and a gas kiln needs ventilation and inspection. Three attended firings a week need a person's hours, and the part-time instructor was already teaching four nights. The building itself, including floor loading, landlord rules on clay waste and the fire marshal, is the blind spot I cannot see from here.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 to 2: fit-out ran about 30% over an unwritten budget.
- Month 3: the inspection failed once on ventilation; a re-inspection took three weeks.
- Month 4 to 6: firing labor fell to the owner, who was then also running two sites.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that "fit out" is a cost you can absorb inside the cushion.
**First warning sign:** fit-out spend over 110% of a written budget by July 15.

**Considered and cut:** wheel shortage (folded into #3), glaze chemistry change (folded into #5), no-shows after payment (folded into #4).
**Downgraded — guarded for now:**

| The instructor leaves or is poached | A part-time instructor is budgeted and you can cover classes | Two missed or reshuffled sessions, or no signed fall agreement by June 30 |
| Gas safety or inspection failure | Inspection and insurance are in the $9,400 | No inspection booked 2 weeks before kiln delivery |

## The Rebuild
**The revised plan:** Do not sign June 2 on these terms. Sign only if the lease starts rent when the gas meter is energized (or Sept 1, whichever is later) and the personal guarantee is capped. Apply to the utility before signing, with the landlord's authorization letter in hand. Repair or replace the dying electric kiln rather than abandoning it, so there are two kilns. If the utility will not commit to an energization date by Sept 8, buy electric instead of gas. Run five cohorts (add Saturday morning) at $395 rather than four at $340, starting the week of Sept 15 or two weeks after the first successful test fire, whichever is later. Redefine success: December tuition at or above monthly costs, and no more than $12,000 drawn from savings.

**Pre-launch checklist:**
1. **Monthly math.** Rebuild the model monthly with clay, glaze, wheels and fit-out included. Walk away from the current terms if the plan at 75% fill loses more than $2,000 a month.
2. **Utility.** Get a written energization estimate. Walk away if it is later than Sept 8. Falsifier of the "10 to 14 weeks" assumption: no written estimate by May 28.
3. **Deposits.** Offer the 140 names a refundable $100 deposit for the September cohorts. Walk away if fewer than 25 deposits arrive by May 28. Falsifier of "demand is 140": under 25 paid in two weeks.
4. **Members.** Ask all 30 in writing whether they will drive greenware across town and test a new glaze atmosphere. Walk away if fewer than 21 say yes. Falsifier of "members will go along": fewer than 21 written yes answers.
5. **Lease.** Get the rent-commencement and guarantee-cap terms in writing. Walk away if the landlord refuses both.

**Decision rule:** Sign IF a written energization date no later than Sept 8 AND at least 25 deposits AND at least 21 member yes answers AND a capped guarantee with rent tied to energization are all in hand by May 28, ELSE do not sign on June 2; extend to June 16 and re-test. Sequence the riskiest test first: the utility and the deposits come before any signature.
**If the decision rule lands on delay, reduce, or don't, the reverse autopsy:** We delayed and it was a mistake. The landlord leased the space to someone else in June. The 140-name list cooled by September, and half the interest was gone. A rival ran a fall beginner series before you did.
**What tips the balance:** a landlord who agrees in writing to energization-based rent and a capped guarantee, plus 25 deposits; if both arrive, sign by June 16, ELSE the caution stands.
**Consciously accepted:** site surprises beyond the budgeted 15% contingency (the building's unseen condition). Priced at a $2,500 reserve; anything beyond that is paid from savings.
**How the rebuilt plan dies:** the landlord refuses the rent concession and the space is gone; the deposit test is self-selected and flatters demand; the electric kiln repair fails and the second-kiln money doubles the loan. At five cohorts the plan needs 90% fill, which is itself thin.
**Residual risk:** even rebuilt, Christmas break-even needs about 45 of 50 seats filled at $395. A more realistic outcome is break-even in January.

## The Adversary
**Playing:** the nearest beginner-class competitor, such as a community-ed program or another studio, which gains most from your delay. Assumed to be well resourced, wary of fixed costs, and unable to sign a five-year lease on short notice. Their best alternative is to simply run their own fall series at a lower price.
**The kill chain:**
- Jun 2 to Jun 30 (probe): they see your Harbor Days booth and your lease signing, and note the Aug 4 start.
- July 1 (position): they open early-bird registration for a Sept start at $295, before your kiln is live.
- Aug 4 (strike): your first cohorts slip or are weak; they email the same fair attendees.
- Sept to Oct (entrench): they sign referral arrangements with local employers.
- Nov (holiday): they sell gift certificates for beginner courses while you are unable to deliver finished pieces.
**Detection opportunities:** their registration page appears by July 1; their email lands in the week of Aug 4; their gift-certificate promotion starts by Nov 1.
**The move you'd never see coming:** they hire your part-time instructor, whose commitment to you is only "comply," and offer your stranded members free firing.
**What this means you should change:** put the instructor on paper (done in the rebuild) and offer members firing access while the new kiln is down.
