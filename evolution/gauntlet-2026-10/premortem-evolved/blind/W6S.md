# Premortem: Saltmarsh Clay second space (beginner wheel courses)
**Horizon:** Dec 25 · **Success was defined as:** "the new space covering its own costs by Christmas without pulling money from the original studio"
**Note on the percentages:** shares of failure ("if this dies, this is what killed it"), not the probability the plan fails overall. They sum to ~100. **Overall chance this misses its success definition by Dec 25, as written:** ~90%. The full-house case alone misses it.
**Assumptions I'm working from:**
- You are reading this at least two weeks before June 2. If not, the signing date moves, not the gates below.
- Courses run back to back in eight-week blocks. Block 1 starts Aug 4, block 2 Oct 6, block 3 Dec 1. Tuition is collected at enrolment, one to three weeks before each block starts. Costs hit monthly from Aug 2, when the rent-free window ends.
- The $9,400 a month is exactly the five lines you listed: rent, kiln loan, insurance, utilities, part-time instructor. Wheels, shelving, ventilation, clay, glaze, kiln gas or power, payment fees and marketing are not in it.
- The utility's 10-14 weeks counts from a complete application. I allowed 1-2 more weeks after the meter for install, inspection and a test firing.
- Every piece is fired twice (bisque, then glaze), and every load is a full 70 pieces. Both assumptions are generous.
- The 140 names are a paper sheet from a free booth, with no deposit and no price shown. You have not expanded before, so the reference classes below are general ones, not your own track record.

## The Autopsy
**The strongest case for this plan:** The studio already has 30 people paying monthly, which proves local demand for clay. 140 people raised a hand at a free booth. Beginner courses are the classic feeder into memberships, so the second space grows the funnel for both sites. The electric kiln is dying and must be replaced anyway, so part of the kiln spend is not new. The first two months are rent-free, the kiln is financed, and the instructor is part-time. A full house grosses $13,600 per block, and a smart operator could believe the rest is execution.

Six ways this died, ranked by probability.

### 1. The $13,600 was an eight-week number set against a one-month cost, and the space lost money every month even with every seat sold
**Probability:** ~40% — the plan's own inputs fall short by about $2,030 a month at a full house, and the Christmas target needs 128% fill.
**Cause of death:** Four cohorts of 10 at $340 gross $13,600 per eight-week block, which is about $7,370 a month. Costs were $9,400 a month. At $340 break-even needs 51 paid seats in a room with 40. Reference class: second sites of small membership and class businesses are usually underwritten on full-utilisation numbers and then miss on ramp. That is my judgment, not a measured rate (weakly sourced). Disanalogy: your room has a seat ceiling you could raise with off-peak cohorts, which would push the number down, but the plan adds none.
**How it unfolded:**
- July: Block 1 sold out and $13,600 arrived in one deposit. It looked like the model working, and wheels and shelving were bought from it.
- Aug-Sep: $18,800 went out in two months, with no income in either. The gap was read as fit-out timing.
- Oct: Block 2 sold out and its deposit arrived in late September. By Oct 31, $27,200 had come in against $28,200 out.
- Nov: Block 3 deposit arrived. On Nov 30 the account showed $40,800 in against $37,600 out, and the space was declared "in the black". The wheel and fit-out invoices had been paid from savings and were not in the comparison.
- Dec: No deposit arrived, because block 4 enrols in January. December's $9,400 went out. On Dec 25 the figures were $40,800 in against about $47,000 out, a $6,200 hole at 100% fill before any equipment. January's rent was covered from the original studio's takings, and the success definition failed.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that "$13,600 a course against $9,400 a month" compares like with like. It was the most confidently stated line in your message, so I attacked it first. Divided by the 1.85 months an eight-week block spans, $13,600 is $7,370.
**First warning sign:** the first 30 seconds of arithmetic before signing. After that, the August P&L: $0 in, $9,400 out.

### 2. The meter arrived after the first cohort had started throwing, and neither site had a working kiln
**Probability:** ~20% — the earliest quoted meter date (Aug 11) is already after the Aug 4 start, and the electric kiln at the original studio is already dying.
**Cause of death:** Filed June 2, 10-14 weeks lands Aug 11 to Sep 8, plus 1-2 weeks to install, inspect and test-fire. The first usable firing is Aug 18 at best and late September at the long end. Quoted utility ranges are usually met at the long end or missed (weakly sourced). Reference class: infrastructure connections scheduled to the optimistic end of the quote. Disanalogy: you file the day the lease signs, which pushes the number down. The plan also sets the opening before even the earliest quoted date, which pushes it up. The dated constraints compound: rent-free ends Aug 2, classes start Aug 4, the earliest meter is Aug 11, and the Christmas target does not move.
**How it unfolded:**
- June: Application filed June 2. The utility asked for the appliance specification and piping drawings that the landlord's contractor had not produced. The clock restarted on the corrected resubmission in late June.
- July: Fit-out finished and Block 1 sold 36 of 40 seats. Rent-free ended Aug 2 with no energised meter.
- Aug: Classes started. Beginners threw about 90 pieces a week and the greenware shelves filled. The original electric kiln failed in mid-August. Your 30 members' 60 pieces a week had nowhere to fire.
- Sep: The meter connected Sep 15 and the kiln was commissioned Sep 26. About 1,000 pieces sat waiting, roughly 14 loads of backlog from seven weeks of beginner work plus the members' queue.
- Oct: The first cohort ended with no finished piece. Refund requests came in, five members cancelled, and Block 2 enrolled 24.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that a quoted range from a utility is a schedule, and that a date in the middle of the plan could be fixed before its prerequisite was in writing.
**First warning sign:** June 16, with no written energisation date from the utility.

### 3. The compound: the thin waitlist met the late kiln, and the guarantee was called with no cash left
**Probability:** ~15% — it needs both #2's delay and a weak conversion of the waitlist, but each is individually plausible and they feed each other.
**Cause of death:** The two modes most likely to co-occur were treated as independent. A free-booth sheet is a curiosity list. I would expect roughly 15-20% to ever pay (weakly sourced, my estimate). The late kiln then removed the referrals that would have filled the next block.
**How it unfolded:**
- June-July: The 140 names were emailed and 22 paid. Two cohorts ran. The other two were cancelled or merged.
- Aug: $7,480 came in against $9,400 out per month. The cushion started to be spent.
- Sep: The meter slipped to Sep 8 and then later. Block 1 students had no fired work. Block 2 enrolled 14 seats ($4,760), almost all referrals from before the delay.
- Oct-Nov: By Oct 31, $12,240 had come in against $28,200 out, a $15,960 gap before any fit-out. The $22,000 cushion was gone by mid-November. The kiln loan payment bounced.
- Dec: Block 3 sold 12 seats. At Christmas the figures were $16,320 in against about $47,000 out. The landlord called the personal guarantee on about 53 remaining months of rent, roughly $217,000.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that booth sign-ups are customers, and that the downside was capped at $22,000.
**First warning sign:** the deposit count on May 26, before the signature. If the booth names do not put down a refundable $100 in the next two weeks, they will not put down $340 in July.

### 4. Every cohort filled, and the kiln became the product's bottleneck
**Probability:** ~10% — it needs the demand to arrive and the kiln to run on time, a narrow path.
**Cause of death:** Three firings a week of 70 pieces, with each piece needing two firings, finishes 105 pieces a week. Demand is 60 from members plus 80-120 from 40 beginners at 2-3 pieces each, so 140-180. The plan is 33-71% over capacity before a single glaze fails. In practice glaze loads pack looser than bisque, so capacity is lower still.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 after commissioning: the queue cleared in a week and looked fine.
- Month 2: bisque waited 8-10 days and glaze 10-14 days. Students stopped trimming new work because the old work was not back.
- Month 3: Nov. Members' holiday gifts sat in the queue until after Dec 10. Several of the members' regular glazes, tuned for the old electric kiln, came out different in the gas atmosphere.
- Month 4: Block 3 enrolment fell and members' goodwill, the original studio's income, absorbed the cost.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that one kiln serves two sites' output and a full house of beginners.
**First warning sign:** more than one full load of greenware waiting longer than 7 days, usually in the second week of regular firing.

### 5. The $9,400 left out the costs and hours that mattered
**Probability:** ~10% — the plan lists five cost lines and none of the capital or labour of running a second site.
**Cause of death:** Ten wheels, shelving, ventilation and a clay trap are plausibly $12,000-$25,000 of fit-out depending on new versus used (weakly sourced estimate; get quotes). Then there is clay, glaze, kiln gas, payment fees and marketing. The labour was never scheduled: three kiln loads a week, a drive across town for members' work, and Mon-Thu evenings with a single instructor.
**How it unfolded:**
- June-July: Fit-out came out of savings, not rent-free time. Spend passed $12,000, more than half the cushion, before the first student paid.
- Aug-Sep: You loaded kilns, drove work and covered the evenings the instructor could not. The original studio's open hours thinned.
- Oct: The instructor, who had no written agreement, took a better offer. You taught the Mon-Thu cohorts yourself.
- Nov: Members at the original studio, the stable income, began to leave over the thinner service.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that fixed costs equal the lines you could name.
**First warning sign:** a one-page ledger on Jul 1 with more than six lines unpriced.

**Tie-break between #4 and #5:** both sit at ~10%. #4 ranks first because its damage lands on the original studio's paying members and the Christmas period; #5 is recoverable with savings and a second teacher.

### 6. The frame's blind spot: nothing in the plan says what problem the second space fixes
**Probability:** ~5% — a judgment call. I can't turn this into a mechanism from what you've written.
**Cause of death:** The plan gives no constraint at the original studio that the second space relieves. It states no member waitlist, no wheel shortage and no evening shortage. Its only demand evidence is a free booth, and its only schedule is the lease. Whatever the real reason for this space is, it is carrying weight that does not appear on the page. Treat that as unresolved. The adversary section tests part of it.
**How it unfolded:**
- By November you could not name which constraint the second space had removed, and the original studio had spare evening wheel time the whole time.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that a second site is the next step because it is the visible one.
**First warning sign:** May 26, when the original studio's unfilled evening wheel seats are counted.

**Considered and cut:** the instructor quitting mid-block (folded into #5), the landlord refusing the fuel line or venting (folded into #2), members leaving over the drive across town (folded into #2), a rival undercutting price (folded into #3 and the adversary section).

## The Rebuild
**The revised plan:** Do not sign on June 2 as written. First pre-sell the autumn blocks to the waitlist with $100 refundable deposits at $425. Convert Block 1 to six cohorts: Mon-Thu evenings plus two weekend cohorts. Keep the original electric kiln running until the new one has fired three clean loads of members' work. Sign only with a capped guarantee and a rent start tied to the kiln's energisation. Set the first cohort's start four weeks after the kiln's confirmed date, not on Aug 4.

**Pre-launch checklist:**
1. **Demand.** Send the deposit offer to the 140 by May 12. Check how many pay by May 26. Walk away from the June 2 signing if fewer than 30 pay. Falsifier for "the waitlist is demand": fewer than 30 deposits at $425 by May 26.
2. **Kiln date.** Ask the utility, or an electrician for an electric kiln, for a written energisation date, and ask the landlord to authorise the application before signing. Walk away from signing without a rent-start clause if no date is in writing, or if the date is after Aug 25. Falsifier for "the meter holds": no written date by June 16.
3. **Lease.** Send the four edits above. Walk away if the guarantee is uncapped and the rent start is fixed on the calendar. Also confirm with the city that a ten-person class and a fired kiln are permitted in the unit.
4. **Members.** Ask the 30 whether they would drive work across town twice per piece, and what firing at the new site would cost them. These are the people the plan treats as endorsing but are really at "accept" (nobody has asked them). Walk away from relying on one kiln if more than 6 would leave or cut back. Falsifier for "members follow the kiln": 6 or more of 30 say they would leave, by May 26.
5. **Original studio capacity.** Count free evening wheel seats at the original. If 10 or more are free on two or more evenings, run the first beginner cohorts there. This is the cheapest test of whether the second site is needed.

**Decision rule:** IF (a) 30 or more refundable deposits at $425 are in by May 26, AND (b) the lease has the guarantee cap, the rent-start clause and the fuel-line consent, AND (c) the original kiln is repaired or a second electric kiln is secured, THEN sign June 2 and file the utility application that day. ELSE do not sign. Run two evening cohorts at the original studio from Aug 4 as a pilot, then decide by Oct 1 for a January opening. IF the missing conditions are met by June 12, sign then, accepting a shorter rent-free window.
**If the decision rule lands on delay — the reverse autopsy:** The horizon passed. We didn't sign June 2 — and it was a mistake.
- The unit was let to another tenant on June 9 and the two rent-free months went with it.
- The 140 names cooled. The pilot filled 14 seats and a rival's autumn course took the rest.
- The old kiln failed in August anyway, and the pilot ran on borrowed firing.
**What tips the balance:** 36 or more deposits by June 12 and a written energisation date. That reverses the call, and it is written into the rule above as the June 12 branch.
**Consciously accepted:** a rival undercutting the price. No discount response is built. Priced: if a rival runs $300 courses and takes 10-15% of seats, that is 4-6 seats a block, about $1,700-$2,550. The 68% break-even fill absorbs it.
**How the rebuilt plan dies:** deposits measure intent, not attendance. The $425 price and the weekend cohorts are untested, and six cohorts add instructor and owner hours the plan does not yet cover.
**Residual risk:** a capped guarantee of $49,200 is still real money. The catchment could be smaller than six cohorts a block need. The gas or electric supply could still slip.

## The Adversary
**Playing:** the nearest rival provider of beginner courses. I assume there is one: another studio or the rec department. They gain the autumn beginner pipeline. Their kilns work today, and their registration page is established. They can discount $40-$100 and promise a fired-by date. They cannot build a second site by August. Their best alternative, ignoring you, costs them a few seats a block.
**The kill chain:**
1. **Probe (Jun 2-20):** they see your booth, your signage and the $340 price.
2. **Position (Jun 20-Jul 15):** they open autumn registration early at $290-$299 with a promise of fired pieces by week 6.
3. **Strike (Jul 15-Aug 4):** they email your deposit holders' circle. Refundable deposits are the easiest to peel.
4. **Entrench (Sep 15-Nov 30):** your late kiln becomes their sales line. Block 2 sells at 60%, and they open January cohorts before you do.
**Detection opportunities:**
1. Their registration page opens before June 30 or at under $300 (check Jun 20, Jul 1).
2. Four or more refund requests between Jul 1 and Aug 4.
3. Three or more of your first 10 lost leads cite price or firing time (Sep 1).
4. Block 3 under 25 paid seats on Nov 10.
**The move you'd never see coming:** the part-time instructor, the only person who meets all 40 students every week, with no written agreement. A rival's easiest move is to hire them, or they start private classes.
**What this means you should change:** hold the student contact list in the studio's name, sign the instructor agreement by Jul 1, and promise a fired-by date only after the kiln has run.
