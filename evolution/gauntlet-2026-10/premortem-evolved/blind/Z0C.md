## The Autopsy
**The strongest case for this plan:** You are not guessing at the product. You run a studio with 30 people who pay monthly, you know how to fire and teach, and beginner wheel courses are the standard entry point for a pottery studio. Six ways this died, ranked by probability.

### 1. The course revenue was compared to a month of costs, and the gap never closed
**Probability:** ~30% — arithmetic from your own figures, partly rescuable by repricing.
**Guard in the plan today:** none. The "comfortably in the black" line compares unlike units.
**Cause of death:** $13,600 is what one 8-week course brings in. $9,400 is what one month costs. Eight weeks of costs is about $17,350, so a completely full set of four cohorts lost about $3,750 a course. Break-even needed 51 students per cycle; you have 40 seats. **How it unfolded:**

- August: cohorts one through four started and $13,600 came in against $9,400 out. It looked like "in the black" because eight weeks of cash was read as one month of income.
- September: no new enrollments until October 6. The second month of costs ran against cash already spent, and the account crossed zero before the next cohorts paid.
- October–November: the second cycle added $13,600. By November 30 cash in was $27,200 against $37,600 of costs.

**First warning sign:** visible today. Restate each cohort as $1,700 of monthly revenue against $9,400. Operationally, the account balance on September 30.

### 3. The courses filled, and the kiln could not finish both students' and members' pieces

## The Adversary
**Playing:** the nearest competing beginner wheel program, an established local studio or community-ed class. They gain most from your waitlist, because it is the same pool as their fall enrolment. They have existing kilns, an existing student base and a brand. They can't open a new site by September. Their best alternative is to do nothing, which costs them nothing, so any move they make is cheap.
**The kill chain:**
- June 2–15, probe: they watch your signage, your lease and your fair booth, and see your price.
- June 16–July 15, position: they list an early-bird fall beginner series at about $290 and email past students.
- August 4–September 8, strike: your kiln delay becomes public. They advertise "pieces fired in a week" to your waitlist.
- October, entrench: they offer your members firing at a per-piece price just as your electric kiln dies, aimed at the 30 members who are your real asset.
**Detection opportunities:**
- Probe: the competitor's booking page shows a fall series by June 15; check weekly.
- Position: refund requests from deposit holders reach 15 percent or more by July 15.
- Strike: first-week attendance under 36 of 40, or more than four refund requests, by August 11.
- Entrench: three members ask about another studio's firing price by October 15.
**The move you'd never see coming:** the competitor never touches the beginner market. It offers your members firing and a free month, which takes your recurring revenue at the moment your kiln dies.
**What this means you should change:** replace the old kiln before the competitor can offer firing, and ask members now about firing before they ask elsewhere.
