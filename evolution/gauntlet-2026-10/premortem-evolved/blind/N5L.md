# Premortem: Ledgerwood's 10-school expansion under the Arbelle grant
**Horizon:** July 31, 2027 (the summer when the second-year $600k is decided) · **Success was defined as:** "all 14 schools running in January, and the second-year money released next summer."
**Note on the percentages:** shares of failure ("if this dies, this is what killed it"), not the probability the plan fails overall. They sum to ~100. **Overall chance this misses its success definition by the horizon:** ~90% as written. The January start and the year-two test each fail on your own numbers, before any outside surprise.
**Assumptions I'm working from:** the 10 new schools are all inside Fennick Unified, so one MOU process governs them. You have not yet filed the MOU requests for the new schools. The $50k a month is the full cost of the expansion, and the $150k reserve is otherwise uncommitted (it is also the cushion under the existing four schools). Tutor ratio at the new schools matches today's (about 2.3 children per tutor, which is how 400 children turn into roughly 175 new tutors). Arbelle pays on the usual 45-day lag. You vote October 8, so every date below that falls before then is a task for this week.

## The Autopsy
**The strongest case for this plan:** Ledgerwood has a measured result, not a promise: about 12 points over comparison children after a full year, sustained at four schools with about 70 tutors. A funder with $1.2M has looked at that record and chosen to scale it. All ten principals want the program. The tutor model draws on a pipeline (Harmon State education students who need practicum hours) that already works at the current size, and the money covers the coordinator costs that have been the binding constraint. Scaling a working model into schools that are asking for it, with the money in hand, is the best position a small nonprofit gets.
Six ways this died, ranked by probability.

### 1. The 15-point year-two bar was out of reach on Ledgerwood's own numbers, and the $600k was never released
**Probability:** ~40% — the arithmetic already fails: the current program earns 12 points in a full year, and the new schools get less than half a year.
**Cause of death:** the release test asks new schools to beat the existing four schools' full-year result by 25% in about 40% of the dose. Even in a world where every school opened January 12, mid-April is 13 weeks of twice-weekly sessions (roughly 22 sessions after spring break and test weeks) against about 28 weeks (roughly 50 sessions) behind the 12-point figure. Scaled by dose, the expected gap is about 5 to 6 points. (Linear scaling by dose is a simplification and weakly sourced, but it would need to be badly wrong to reach 15.) Reference class: your own track record, 12 points after a full year at schools you run well. Disanalogy: new sites in their first semester, with brand-new coordinators and first-time tutors, almost always run below a mature site, which pushes the number down, not up.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (Nov): coordinators started and the board treated the 15-point condition as a stretch goal, not a gate.
- Month 2 (Dec): tutors were trained; nobody asked what the existing sites' effect looked like at week 13 of their own year.
- Month 3 (Jan): tutoring began, with the first weeks lost to onboarding, snow days and the Jan 20 tutor start.
- Months 4 to 5 (Feb to Mar): sessions per child ran well below the existing schools' pace; children missed sessions to testing prep and the new coordinators were still learning the schools.
- Month 6 (Apr): the benchmark came in. New-school children outscored the comparison group by about 5 points.
- Months 8 to 9 (Jun to Jul): Arbelle declined the second-year release. Ten coordinators were on payroll with no funding behind them, and the board was choosing between layoffs and reserves it no longer had.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that 15 was a number to execute toward, not a number to test against your own data. Guard already in the plan: none.
**First warning sign:** the existing four schools' January benchmark from last year, which you can pull this week. If the effect at about week 13 was under 8 points, the bar was never reachable. Second sign: fewer than 14 sessions delivered per child by February 27.

### 2. The district MOU did not clear in time, and most of the 10 schools missed January 12
**Probability:** ~20% — the last MOU took five months and nothing says this one has been started.
**Cause of death:** Fennick requires a district-office MOU before any outside program uses school space or student data. The first one took five months; a vote on October 8 plus five months is early March. Procurement and legal timelines for ten sites at once, treated as 2 to 3 times the stated figure until evidence says otherwise, make a January start for most schools implausible. Principal enthusiasm is a below-Endorse commitment: they said yes, but no one has put a room, a day and a time on paper, and a principal cannot sign a district MOU. The district office is also graded Accept at best: "we know the people there now" describes familiarity, not a champion who spends capital. Reference class: your own five-month MOU. Disanalogy: you now have relationships, which pushes the time for each school shorter, but you are asking for ten at once instead of four, which pushes it back; net, treat it as no faster than last time.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (Nov): the MOU requests went in three weeks after the vote, and coordinators started November 1 anyway.
- Month 2 (Dec): the district asked for revised data-sharing and background-check language; the coordinators had no school to work in and spent the month on recruiting events.
- Month 3 (Jan): three schools were cleared. Seven were not. Tutors, trained and waiting, drifted off to other practicum placements.
- Months 4 to 5 (Feb to Mar): schools cleared one by one; each opened with whatever tutors were still available.
- Month 6 (Apr): the schools that opened in March had weeks of dose, not months, at the benchmark.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that the principals' excitement was the same thing as access. Guard already in the plan: none.
**First warning sign:** by Dec 1, fewer than 5 of 10 MOUs signed or in final legal review. Earlier: any principal who cannot name a room, days and a time in writing by Oct 31.

### 3. Compound: the MOU slip met the arrears payment schedule, and the reserve was gone before the first check arrived
**Probability:** ~15% — each piece is likely; the interaction is what takes cash to zero while the grant is still paying nothing.
**Cause of death:** costs start November 1 at $50k a month, and Arbelle pays in arrears about 45 days after each quarter closes. The first quarter (Nov to Jan) closes Jan 31, so the first payment lands about March 17. By March 16 you have spent about $225k against a $150k reserve: a $75k hole, and the reserve is gone on January 31. The second payment (Feb to Apr costs) lands about June 14, and the float is back to about $70k below zero. This is the case where everything goes right. If the MOU slips (#2), coordinators draw salary against schools that are not open, and Arbelle may question reimbursing idle months.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (Nov): ten coordinators started; $50k out.
- Month 2 (Dec): MOU delay meant no tutoring; $100k out in total, reserve down to $50k.
- Month 3 (Jan): reserve hit zero when the first quarter closed; the expense report was assembled by the executive director and a bookkeeper, and was two weeks late.
- Month 4 (Feb): payroll for the existing four schools was funded by delaying vendor and stipend payments.
- Month 5 (Mar): the check came about March 17, but it was $150k against $225k spent, and Arbelle disallowed part of the idle-coordinator cost.
- Month 6 (Apr): the board approved an emergency loan on bad terms.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that a reimbursement grant behaves like cash in hand. Guard already in the plan: the $150k reserve, which covers three months, not four and a half.
**First warning sign:** unrestricted cash under $75k at any month-end, or no advance or credit facility signed by Dec 1.

### 4. The Harmon tutor supply was smaller and later than the plan, so schools opened half-staffed
**Probability:** ~10% — the plan needs about 245 tutors from one university, 3.5 times today's pool, and an unconfirmed calendar.
**Cause of death:** about 175 new tutors have to come from the same Harmon education cohort that supplies today's 70. Harmon's spring semester starts January 20, eight days after the January 12 start, so no practicum student can be enrolled or credited until after the first week of tutoring; and recruiting in November and December collides with finals and winter break. Reference class: tutoring programs that depend on a single university's practicum students lose a share of recruits between sign-up and first session (weakly sourced). Disanalogy: your 70 tutors are proof the credit incentive works, which pushes the number down for the first 70 and not for the next 175.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (Nov): recruiting events drew sign-ups, but practicum registration for spring had not opened to the new sites.
- Month 2 (Dec): finals and break; training sessions ran at about half attendance.
- Month 3 (Jan): tutoring ran with fewer than 120 tutors, so each school served about 25 children, not 40.
- Months 4 to 6 (Feb to Apr): tutors dropped out at the semester's midpoint, and coordinators spent their time on rescheduling and recruiting.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that "Harmon students" is a pool with the capacity you need, with a faculty approver who agreed to it. Guard already in the plan: none beyond the existing relationship.
**First warning sign:** fewer than 120 practicum seats confirmed in writing by Oct 31, or fewer than 120 trained tutors by Dec 12.

### 5. The plan on schedule: 14 schools opened with no one to run them, and the original four schools got worse
**Probability:** ~10% — Tie-break with #4 below.
**Cause of death:** the plan prices ten coordinators and nothing above them. No program manager, no data owner for the comparison-group data, no one who builds the quarterly expense reports Arbelle pays on. The executive director supervises ten new coordinators on top of four existing sites. A small organization tripling its footprint in two months diverts its best coordinators and its director from the sites whose 12-point result earned the grant. Reference class: nonprofits that triple in a year commonly see their original sites' quality slip first (weakly sourced). Disanalogy: you have a tested program model and written materials, which lowers the chance of drift; you have no named deputy, which raises it.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (Nov): the director interviewed and hired ten coordinators.
- Month 2 (Dec): the original four coordinators were pulled in to train the new ten.
- Month 3 (Jan): all schools opened; the director spent two days a week on the district and the data-sharing questions.
- Months 4 to 5 (Feb to Mar): the session completion rate at the original four dropped; the first expense report went out late and was bounced for missing documentation.
- Month 6 (Apr): the original schools' April benchmark came in below last year's, and the comparison data Arbelle would use was the weakest the program had ever produced.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that coordinators are the only management layer scale needs. Guard already in the plan: none.
**First warning sign:** the first quarterly expense report not submitted by Feb 14, or the original four schools' completed-session rate falling 5 points below last year's in January or February.
**Tie-break (#4, #5):** both sit at ~10%. #4 ranks above #5 because the supply gap shows up in a single month, December, and the evidence is available now, while #5 needs a second condition (the schedule holding) before it can fire.

### 6. The frame's blind spot: the district or the funder changed the rules after the plan was fixed
**Probability:** ~5% — a judgment call; there is no reference class for this, and I cannot yet name its mechanism.
**Cause of death:** this plan is exposed to two bodies it does not control, and the analysis treats their rules as fixed. The district can change the benchmark instrument or the comparison-group definition, reassign the office that signs MOUs, or put the program under budget review. Arbelle can reinterpret how "outscore a comparison group" is computed, or who builds the group. The adversary section tries to resolve this.
**How it unfolded:**
- Months 3 to 5 (Jan to Mar): a new district reading lead arrived and reopened the vendor list.
- Month 6 (Apr): the benchmark's scoring changed, and the old comparison group no longer existed.
- Month 8 (Jun): Arbelle's program officer reset the test to a pooled district average, and the release was judged on that.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that the rules on the page would still be the rules in April. Guard already in the plan: none.
**First warning sign:** any announcement from the district office before March 1 about a benchmark change, a new MOU template or a leadership change.

**Considered and cut:** principals withdraw after the MOU stalls (folded into #2); coordinator hiring slips past November 1 (folded into #3); Arbelle rescinds after the vote (<5%, recoverable, the offer letter stands).
**Downgraded — guarded for now:**
| Mode | Guard that holds it down | Signal that re-upgrades it |
|---|---|---|
| Safeguarding or background-check failure across 245 tutors | The existing clearance process has worked for 70 tutors under the current MOU | The new MOUs add fingerprinting or longer clearance, or a clearance takes more than 3 weeks |
| Arbelle changes terms between the offer and the signed agreement | The written offer letter | The draft grant agreement adds clawback, new reporting or a different payment schedule |

## The Verdict

| 3 | 5 | 4 | 20 |

**Most likely:** #1 — it has the highest feasibility and the top I×F, and it is the only mode that is arithmetic on your own figures, not a forecast.
**Most dangerous:** #3 — highest impact. The first warning arrives January 31, when the reserve reaches zero, and the point of no return is the missed payroll for the existing four schools in February. That is a short silent period (no sign until the money is gone), and the reserve cannot be rebuilt in-year. It is a different mode from the most likely one, because #1 costs you $600k and a reputation while #3 puts the 160 children you already serve at risk.
**The hidden assumption:** every external clock will run on your calendar. Compare the mechanisms: #1 works only if the new schools get a full dose by April; #2 only if the district's five-month process takes ten weeks; #3 only if Arbelle's 45-day lag does not matter; #4 only if Harmon's January 20 start and seat count line up with January 12. These four modes die unless the funder, the district, the university and the benchmark calendar all bend to a plan that a nonprofit set on its own timeline. That convergence is stronger evidence than any single probability.
**Fatal flaw:** the year-two release test as written. It is a 15-point bar on a mid-April benchmark for schools that cannot have more than 13 weeks of tutoring, set against a program that earns 12 points in 28 weeks. No execution fixes that; only the terms can. The plan also fails the cash arithmetic on the base case, but that one is repairable with money, not with a negotiation.

## The Rebuild
**The revised plan:** accept the grant in principle on October 8, conditional on a signed amendment by November 6. File MOU requests for all 10 schools on October 9. Hire coordinators school by school, 30 days before each site's MOU is expected, not all on November 1. Start tutoring on January 20 to align with Harmon, not January 12. Open only the schools that have a signed MOU, written principal letters and confirmed tutor seats; schools not ready by December 15 move to an October 2027 wave. Ask Arbelle for a first-quarter advance (or a $100k bridge line of credit), and ask that release of the second-year money be split: half on implementation milestones (sessions per child, tutor retention) in summer 2027, half on a full-year benchmark in April 2028. Close the management gap with a program and data manager by January 31.
**What changed and why:**

|---|---|---|---|

Decision date: signed by Nov 6 or the board moves to the decision rule's ELSE | Launch-Blocking |
| First-quarter advance from Arbelle or a $100k bridge line of credit; freeze hiring if month-end cash falls under $75k | #3 (limit) | A few thousand dollars in fees and interest, against a roughly $75k hole under the existing schools. Decision date: signed by Dec 1 or hiring halves | Launch-Blocking |
| File MOU requests for all 10 on Oct 9 and gate each school on a signed MOU; hire coordinators only after (or 30 days before) | #2, #3 (prevent) | The Jan start slips for some sites; saves up to $50k for each idle month. Decision date: sites without a signed MOU on Dec 15 move to the October 2027 wave | Launch-Blocking |
| Written Harmon commitment (practicum sections, seat count) and a Jan 20 start; second tutor source (another college or paid part-time tutors) | #4 | Dozens of hours of director time; a paid tutor pool costs more per child. Decision date: seats confirmed by Oct 31 | Launch-Blocking |
| Hire a program and data manager and a part-time finance/reporting lead | #5 | About $40k for the first half-year (an assumption), inside the grant's admin line if Arbelle allows it; against the loss of the original 4 schools' quality and a bounced expense report | Fast-Follow, by Jan 31 |
| Name a deputy and cap the executive director's direct reports; ring-fence the original four coordinators from onboarding duty | #5 | Slower onboarding of new sites | Fast-Follow |
| Watch the district calendar and benchmark announcements; write the rule into the grant file | #6 | An hour a month | Track |

For the most dangerous mode (#3) the response is prevent (the advance closes the entry path) and limit (the $75k freeze). For #1 it is prevent (rewrite the test) and limit (the milestone split). #6 is the one mode accepted.
**Pre-launch checklist:**
1. **The 15-point bar.** Check: pull the existing four schools' winter benchmark from last year and compare the effect at about week 13 with the 15-point bar. Do it this week, before the vote. Walk away if the effect at week 13 was under 8 points and Arbelle will not change the test. Falsifier: any figure under 8 means the bar is unreachable in 13 weeks.
2. **The MOUs.** Check: call the district contact, ask whether new schools amend the existing MOU or need a new one, and what the review clock is per school; file the requests on Oct 9. Walk away from a January start at any school where the district's own estimate runs past Dec 18. Falsifier for "the principals are ready": fewer than 8 of 10 principals sign a one-page letter naming room, days and time by Oct 31.
3. **The cash model.** Check: build the month-by-month float using Arbelle's actual payment dates (ask for the date of its last three payments to other grantees), and get a term sheet for the bridge. Walk away from the hiring schedule if neither an advance nor a facility is signed by Dec 1.
4. **The tutors.** Check: meet Harmon's practicum director and get the number of spring practicum seats in writing; at 2.3 children per tutor, 120 seats supports about 280 children. Walk away from a 400-child launch if fewer than 120 seats are confirmed by Oct 31. Falsifier for "Harmon can supply 175 more": fewer than 120 confirmed seats on that date.
**Decision rule:** accept the full expansion IF by Nov 6 (a) Arbelle signs an amended year-two test that is measured on a dose-adjusted or full-year basis, AND (b) Arbelle advances the first quarter or you hold a signed $100k facility, AND (c) the district confirms in writing that review has started on all 10 MOUs; THEN hire coordinators site by site from Nov 16. ELSE take the first-year money only for the schools with signed MOUs and confirmed tutors (a smaller first wave), and decline the year-two contingency as written. Do the risky capability test (the MOU and tutor seats) before the irreversible step (the coordinator offers).
**If the decision rule lands on delay, reduce, or don't — the reverse autopsy:** The horizon passed and we narrowed the launch, and it was a mistake. Arbelle would not renew the offer in the form it was made, and the foundation funded another organization. The district cleared all 10 MOUs in six weeks because the office knew us. Harmon delivered 200 tutors and the 15 points was reachable because the comparison group was weak.
**What tips the balance:** Arbelle's written confirmation that the year-two test is measured pro-rated for dose or on a full year, plus a district MOU commitment of ten weeks or less. With both, the ELSE branch becomes: accept all 10 schools in the waves above.
**Consciously accepted (at most one):** #6, the district or funder changing the rules. Priced at roughly 5% of the $600k, about $30k expected, with no available way to close it; it is documented in the grant file.
**How the rebuilt plan dies:** the weakest joint is the amendment itself. It depends on the same funder whose terms created the problem, and a counter-offer at this scale may be read as a refusal. Even if it lands, "all 14 schools running in January" is knowingly given up, so the success definition as you wrote it is not met.
**Residual risk:** the linear-dose assumption could be wrong in either direction; the original four schools' quality under a thin management layer; and a district-level shock that no clause can prevent.

## The Adversary
**Playing:** a rival literacy provider already contracted at several of the 10 target schools (assumed to exist; I do not know your districts's vendors). Motivation: its slot, its per-student fee and its relationship with the principals are what Ledgerwood's grant-funded, free program threatens. Resources: existing contracts and a standing relationship with the district curriculum office. Constraints: it cannot be seen attacking a nonprofit with a published result, and it does not control the benchmark. Best alternative: lose some placements gradually, so it prefers delay over confrontation.
**The kill chain:**
- Oct to Nov (probe): ask principals about scheduling and room constraints; offer to extend its own hours.
- Dec (position): tell the district office that a volunteer-based model at 245 tutors needs a fuller vetting review before any MOU.
- Jan (strike): the MOU queue stalls behind that review; schools on the fence fill their afternoon slots.
- Feb to Apr (entrench): a test-prep package crowds out the tutoring hours; sessions drop just before the benchmark.
**Detection opportunities:**
- Oct to Nov: two or more principals cannot confirm a room and time in writing by Nov 15.
- Dec: the district asks for new vetting or policy documents after Dec 1, beyond what the first MOU needed.
- Mar: sessions canceled at any school exceed 15% in a month for scheduling reasons.
**The move you'd never see coming:** whoever builds the comparison group decides the outcome. If the group is chosen from schools that run another intensive program, or picked after the results are in, 15 points can become unreachable, or free, without anyone touching the tutoring.
**What this means you should change:** fix the comparison group, instrument and calculation in the grant agreement before January, and get principal letters naming room and time on paper.
