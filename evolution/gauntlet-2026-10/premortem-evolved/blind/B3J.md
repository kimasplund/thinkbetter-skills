# Premortem: Ledgerwood Reading Partners, 10-school expansion on the Arbelle grant

**Horizon:** August 31, 2027 (when the second-year release is decided) · **Success was defined as:** "all 14 schools running in January, and the second-year money released next summer."
**Note on the percentages:** shares of failure, meaning "if this dies, this is what killed it". They are not the probability the plan fails overall. They sum to about 100. **Overall chance this misses its success definition by the horizon:** ~90% as written, because the January start and the 15-point bar each fail on your own numbers (see #1 and #2).
**Assumptions I'm working from:**
- This is the 2026-27 school year, so January 12 is 2027 and the benchmark is mid-April 2027.
- No MOU work for the ten new schools has started, and nothing is hired. The board vote comes first.
- Arbelle reimburses the full $50k a month, including coordinator payroll and the November-December months before any child is tutored. The grant agreement says nothing different.
- The $150k reserve is your only cushion. It also backs the existing four-school program.
- Winter break ends in early January, there is one spring break in late March or early April, and there are federal holidays in January and February.
- The 15 points is measured the same way as your current 12: pooled new-school students against a comparison group, on the district spring benchmark.
- The district requires some background clearance for school volunteers. I don't know the turnaround.

## The Autopsy

**The strongest case for this plan:** Ledgerwood has run one-on-one tutoring for a full year at four schools, and its students beat comparison students by about 12 points. Few small nonprofits can show that. A serious funder has put up $1.2M, which is almost exactly the $50k a month the expansion costs over 24 months, so the plan is fully funded on paper. Ten principals want the program. Harmon State's education school sends a steady supply of students who need practicum credit, and 70 of them already tutor for you. You have an existing MOU with the district office and people there who know you. The $150k reserve covers the first quarter of costs to the dollar. A smart funder looking at that record would say this is the organization to bet on. Every mode below has to beat this case, not a weaker one.

Seven ways this died, ranked by probability.

### 1. The new schools got roughly twenty sessions per child against the roughly forty-eight that produced your 12-point lead. They finished near 5 points against a 15-point bar, and the second-year $600k was never released.
**Probability:** ~30% — on the plan's own arithmetic the miss is close to certain if the program reaches April intact. The share is held to 30% because #2 and #3 can kill the plan before the test is taken.
**Reference class:** your own four schools: about 12 points after a full October-April year. Disanalogy: the new schools get about 40% of the dose, with brand-new coordinators and first-semester tutors. That pushes the number down. Early gains might be steeper than later ones, which pushes it up slightly. The net is still well short of 15.
**Cause of death:** a funder bar set above your proven full-year effect and applied to a program that has a quarter-year of runway before the test.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (Nov): coordinators start. Everyone treats January 12 as launch day. No one converts it into a count of sessions before the mid-April test.
- Month 2 (Dec): training runs through Harmon's finals and winter break. Practicum credit does not start until January 20, so the January 12 week runs on whichever tutors showed up.
- Month 3 (Jan): after onboarding, baseline testing and the first partial weeks, regular two-a-week sessions start around January 20 to 26.
- Months 4-5 (Feb-Mar): holidays, spring break and the district's own test-prep windows take about three weeks of sessions out of the semester.
- Month 6 (Apr): by the mid-April benchmark the average child has had about 20 sessions. Your current schools run about 24 weeks times 2, roughly 48. At your own rate of about 0.25 points per session, 20 sessions is about 5 points. Even the full-year program at its best is 3 short of the bar.
- August: Arbelle declines the release. Ten coordinators are on payroll through October with no funding behind them.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that a 13-week program produces what a 28-week program did, and that a proven 12 becomes 15 at ten new schools.
**First warning sign:** now. The bar in the grant letter exceeds your demonstrated effect. In flight, an average under 8 completed sessions per child on February 15 means April's dose is under 20.

### 2. Compound: the MOUs for most of the ten schools were not signed in January, and coordinators were paid $50k a month into schools they couldn't enter until the reserve hit zero on January 31.
**Probability:** ~20% — your own MOU took five months, and the float arithmetic leaves no slack.
**Reference class:** your MOU for the current four schools, about five months. Disanalogy: you now know the people, which shortens it. This is ten schools, a much larger data-sharing footprint (about 400 students) and a $1.2M program that will draw more review, which lengthens it. A five-month review started in October ends in March. Even if it takes half as long, the signature lands in late December, not in time for a January 12 start.
**Cause of death:** a district approval treated as a formality, plus a payment schedule that makes the organization the lender.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (Nov): the board accepts, and ten coordinators start on November 1. The district office puts the MOU request in its queue and asks which data-sharing terms apply to the new schools.
- Month 2 (Dec): coordinators can't recruit on campus or fix schedules, because no MOU means no authorized use of school space or student data. About $100k has been spent with no child served.
- Month 3 (Jan): January 12 passes with two or three schools authorized. On January 31 the cumulative $150k equals the whole reserve, and cash is zero.
- Month 4 (Feb): the first report can't be filed until the quarter closes on January 31. Arbelle pays about March 17. February payroll and vendors are carried by delaying bills, a bridge loan or layoffs, and the shortfall is about $75k. Nothing is left for the existing four schools if anything else slips.
- Month 5 (Mar): the $150k lands on March 17. MOUs for the remaining schools arrive in March and April, leaving them five weeks of tutoring before the benchmark (this is what feeds #1).
- Month 6 (Apr): the second quarter's $150k is again spent before Arbelle's June 14 payment, so the same $75k hole reopens. The board has spent a year of reserves and still has no signed schools.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that "we know the people" turns five months into five weeks, and that $150k in reserves covers the lag. It covers one quarter, not the 4.5 months between the first dollar spent and the first dollar received.
**First warning sign:** no written district timeline or countersigned MOU by November 20.

### 3. The 175 additional tutors didn't exist on January 12 and trickled in after the semester began.
**Probability:** ~15% — the pool has to grow from 70 to roughly 245, 3.5 times today's size, and the credit mechanism doesn't start until January 20.
**Cause of death:** a tutor supply tied to a university calendar and practicum registration that the January 12 date ignored.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (Nov): recruiting competes with fall finals and with spring registration. Students can pledge but can't yet earn credit.
- Month 2 (Dec): training sessions run during finals and winter break, and attendance is thin. Pledges stall around 110.
- Month 3 (Jan): sessions start on January 12 with the original 70 stretched across more sites, or with no one at some schools. The new cohort arrives January 20 and needs clearance (#4).
- Month 4 (Feb): about 120 tutors are working by mid-February. Each tutor now carries three or four children or sessions drop to once a week.
- Month 5 (Mar): attrition among first-semester practicum students begins as coursework builds. Make-up sessions are not staffed.
- Month 6 (Apr): practicum ends in May; the benchmark finds a second shortfall layered onto #1.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that Harmon's education school has 175 more students who need practicum hours this spring and can begin before the semester does. I don't know either number, so this is unverified.
**First warning sign:** fewer than 90 written spring placements from Harmon's practicum office by November 20.

### 4. Ten principals said yes. The plan never scheduled the clearances, consent forms or baseline testing that put 400 children in chairs.
**Probability:** ~10% — silent until January, and not in the plan at all. The principals' enthusiasm is the assumption you state most firmly, and everything attached to it is verbal.
**Cause of death:** a yes that costs the principal nothing, treated as a site that is ready.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (Nov): coordinators meet principals, who agree in principle. No one assigns a room, a staff contact or a way to select 40 children.
- Month 2 (Dec): teacher referrals are collected, and consent forms go home before the break. Returns are slow over the holiday.
- Month 3 (Jan): fingerprinting or background clearance for 175 tutors and 10 coordinators takes weeks (the length is my assumption). Tutors approved in early February arrive after the start date.
- Month 4 (Feb): three or four schools have fewer than 25 consented children. Cafeteria and library space is contested by other after-school programs.
- Month 5 (Mar): principal attention moves to spring testing. The yes is revealed as acceptance, and calls go unreturned.
- Month 6 (Apr): four schools run at partial rosters. The 40-per-school target was never met.
**The assumption that allowed it:** the principals' excitement. They are at best Accept on the commitment scale: they agree, and silence does the rest. They don't control the MOU, they carry no cost, and the only evidence is your report of what they said. That is not paper. It survives only as a signal of willingness.
**First warning sign:** fewer than 7 of the 10 schools have a named contact, a room and a roster by December 15.

### 5. Ten coordinators were hired in three weeks, and the executive director became the only thread holding it together.
**Probability:** ~10% — a November 1 start gives about 23 days from the vote, and most hires carry two weeks' notice.
**Cause of death:** an unbudgeted hiring timeline and a single point of failure.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (Nov): perhaps five or six coordinators start November 1. The rest are filled over November from weaker pools. You run recruiting, the MOU, Arbelle and the existing four schools yourself.
- Month 2 (Dec): coordinators are trained in a hurry. Several don't know the model well enough to train tutors.
- Month 3 (Jan): coordinators at blocked schools (#2) have nothing to do. Two resign.
- Month 4 (Feb): the first expense report to Arbelle is late and incomplete, because no one besides you holds the records. Payment slips past March 17.
- Month 5 (Mar): you are managing the cash gap, the MOUs and two coordinator vacancies alone.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that the organization has the management layer to absorb ten hires and a tripled program with the current leadership.
**First warning sign:** fewer than 8 of 10 offers accepted by October 25.

### 6. The launch worked where it opened, and it thinned the original four.
**Probability:** ~10% — it needs the launch to succeed first, which makes it later and rarer than the failures above.
**Cause of death:** a model whose results depended on a stable core of 70 tutors and a coordinator watching 40 children, then drained to staff the expansion.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 3 (Jan): new sites need experienced hands, so the strongest of the 70 are asked to mentor new tutors. You take a day a week for the new sites.
- Month 4 (Feb): the original four schools' session completion falls as senior tutors and coordinator time leave.
- Month 5 (Mar): the same four schools have more cancelled and short sessions.
- Month 6 (Apr): the original schools' lead drops from 12 to about 8. Arbelle sees the flagship results slip, and the track record that earned the grant is the thing that weakened.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that the model is scale-independent and that growth costs only money.
**First warning sign:** completion rate at the original four schools drops more than 10 points below the October-December baseline for two consecutive weeks.

### 7. The frame's blind spot: something in Fennick Unified's own calendar or data moved the bar, and nothing in the plan could see it.
**Probability:** ~5% — I cannot give this a mechanism, which is the point.
**Cause of death:** a plan that treats the district as a fixed counterparty and the benchmark as a stable ruler.
**How it unfolded:**
- Months 1-5: nothing visible. The plan assumes the 12 points and the 15 points are measured on the same instrument, the same window and the same type of comparison group.
- Month 6 (Apr): the benchmark is administered in a different form or window, or the comparison group is defined by someone else after the fact. The grant letter does not say which counts.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that the ruler stays still. The adversary section is where I try to resolve this.
**First warning sign:** no written confirmation of the instrument, window and comparison-group definition by January 31.

**Tie-break within the 10% rung:**
- #4 outranks #5: the clearance and consent gap stays silent until January, while a hiring slip shows by mid-November and can be corrected.
- #4 outranks #6: it is in the plan today as a gap, whereas thinning needs the launch to succeed first.
- #5 outranks #6: a hiring shortfall is near-term and mundane, while #6 only matters if sites open.

**Considered and cut:** principal turnover at one or two schools (folded into #4), Harmon dropping practicum credit (folded into #3), coordinator payroll running above $50k a month (folded into #2).

**Downgraded — guarded for now:**
| Mode | Guard that holds it down | Signal that re-upgrades it |
|---|---|---|
| Safeguarding incident involving a new tutor | District clearance rules, assumed to apply, plus the existing four schools' clean record | Any tutor in a building before clearance completes |
| Arbelle disallows part of the first expense report | The expense categories in the grant budget | Any query or documentation request on the first report |
| Funding cliff when the $1.2M ends in October 2028 | It is beyond the horizon, and year two is unfunded until the bar is met | No second-funder conversation opened by June 30, 2027 |

**Most likely:** #1, highest feasibility among the high scorers. The 15-point bar is above your proven effect, and the calendar leaves a fraction of the dose. This is arithmetic, not a risk.
**Most dangerous:** also #1, for a different reason. It is the highest-impact mode and the quietest: the dose is set in January when the start date is missed, but the verdict arrives in April and August. After that, nothing can be done. #2 is loud and bridgeable, so it ranks second.
**The hidden assumption:** modes #1, #2, #3, #4 and #5 all die unless January 12 is achievable. The date looks like it was set by the school and university calendar and the grant cycle, not by the lead times of the three institutions that must say yes first: the district (five months last time), Harmon (credit starts January 20) and the labor market (23 days to hire ten people). Five independent paths ending at one belief is stronger evidence than any single mode's probability.
**Fatal flaw:** yes, one. The second-year condition, as written, requires a 15-point lead on a fraction of your dose, when your proven effect is 12 after a full year. Accepting it unamended commits your reserves and ten coordinators to a $600k release that the arithmetic says is unlikely. The binding constraint behind it is that the grant pays you back after you've spent it.

## The Rebuild

**The revised plan:** do not accept the grant as written. Ask Arbelle to amend the test and the payment terms before the board signs. Start in two waves: up to five schools on January 12 where the MOU is signed, tutors are committed and sites are ready, and the other five on a fall start that gives them a full October-April year. Hire coordinators only as their schools clear. Before you spend anything, get written dates from the district and a written count from Harmon, and have a credit line in hand.

| **Launch-Blocking.** File one consolidated MOU request this week. Get a written timeline by November 15. Any school without a signed MOU by December 15 moves to the fall wave. | #2 | About 8 hours of your time and some goodwill. |

Cost check: each change costs less than the mode it closes. The reserve-at-risk and the $600k release dwarf the credit-line interest and the delay to the fall wave. The one over-spend, giving up a January start for five schools, is justified because the full-year dose is the only route to the bar.

**Pre-launch checklist:**
1. **Arbelle's terms.** *Check:* how "outscore a comparison group by 15 points" is defined (test, comparison group, who computes it, pooled or per school, and how your 12 was measured), and whether November-December costs are reimbursable. *How:* read the agreement and ask the program officer in writing. *Falsifier for "15 is reachable":* your own session records. Divide 12 points by completed sessions per child at your current schools, multiply by about 20. If the projection is below 10 points, the assumption is false. *Walk away:* Arbelle refuses to move the test date or the bar in writing by October 29.
2. **The district MOU.** *Check:* whether one amendment covers all ten schools and what the review steps are. *How:* one written request to the district office. *Falsifier for "we know the people":* no written committed date for a school by December 15. *Walk away:* the district's earliest realistic date for even half the schools is after January 31.
3. **Harmon.** *Check:* the number of spring practicum placements, the start date, the credit mechanism. *How:* the practicum office, in writing. *Falsifier:* fewer than 90 committed by November 20. *Walk away:* under 60 for wave 1.
4. **Clearances and consent.** *Check:* the district's volunteer-clearance requirement and turnaround, and the consent process for 40 children per school. *How:* ask the district HR or volunteer office. *Walk away:* turnaround above four weeks with no pre-clearance route, so no tutor can start before February.
5. **Cash.** *Check:* a credit line or advance for at least $100k. *How:* your bank and Arbelle's finance contact. *Walk away:* nothing in writing by November 1, so no more than five coordinators start.

**Decision rule:** IF by October 29 Arbelle has agreed in writing to a full-year test (or a bar at or below 12) AND a payment fix (monthly, advance, or the $100k credit line is in hand) AND the district has given a written signing date by December 15 for at least five schools, THEN hire wave-1 coordinators (up to five) for November 15 and plan the fall wave. ELSE accept year one only for wave 1, hire nothing beyond five coordinators, and treat year two as unfunded. Splitting the difference here does not inherit the worst of both: five schools at about $25k a month needs about $112k of float against $150k of reserves, so it fits inside the cushion, and the fall wave gets a full-year dose.

**If the decision rule lands on delay, reduce, or don't — the reverse autopsy:** the horizon passed, we took wave 1 only, and that was a mistake. (1) Arbelle withdrew the offer when asked to amend, and no other funder appeared. (2) The district signed a single MOU in November, so ten schools could have opened on time. (3) Early gains at fresh schools were steeper than the dose arithmetic suggested, and the 15-point bar was met at ten schools we didn't open.
**What tips the balance:** IF the district countersigns one MOU covering all ten by November 15 AND Harmon confirms 175 placements in writing, THEN add wave-2 coordinators from December 1 even if Arbelle holds the bar, with year two treated as unbudgeted upside.
**Consciously accepted:** that Arbelle may refuse the amendments. The cost, priced: the loss of an offer that on current terms reimburses costs only, with no margin, and releases year two only if an unlikely bar is met. Declining the amendments would put roughly $75k of recurring float and a $600k release on the line.
**How the rebuilt plan dies:** the new weakest joint is Arbelle's willingness. You are asking a funder to rewrite its offer in the three weeks before the vote while you hire, and a risk-averse program officer may read the ask as doubt. The wave-1 dose is still about 20 sessions, so unless the test moves, the bar remains hard.
**Residual risk:** an unamended bar, a district that signs late anyway, and the thin management layer (#5) that no change removes.

## The Adversary

**Playing:** a rival literacy provider already working in Fennick, such as a larger nonprofit or a paid tutoring vendor. It wants the same Arbelle dollars and the same after-school slots. It has paid staff that don't depend on a university calendar, and it has its own district contact. It can't copy your Harmon pipeline or your four-school track record, and it needs the same MOU to enter a building. Its best alternative is other schools or other districts, which makes Fennick optional and lets it wait for you to stumble.
**The kill chain:**
1. **Probe (October-November).** The board vote and Arbelle announcement are public. The rival calls the district office and the ten principals: "we can start in January with paid staff."
2. **Position (December).** It offers the district a faster path through its own existing MOU and pre-cleared staff, aimed at three or four of your ten.
3. **Strike (January 12).** Your launch slips at those schools. The rival is in the building with a room and a roster, and principals who said yes to you have no obligation to refuse a second provider. Space is finite.
4. **Entrench (February-March).** It updates Arbelle's program officer and offers to take over any stalled school within the remaining funds.
5. **Finish (April).** Your new-school results arrive late and short, and the rival's are already on the table.
**Detection opportunities:**
- Probe: a principal or district staff member mentions another provider, by November 30.
- Position: room assignments stay "TBD" past December 15 at three or more schools.
- Strike: a school that answered within a day goes silent for five school days between January 5 and January 12.
- Entrench: the program officer asks for a recovery plan or shortens the check-in cadence, by March 31.
**The move you'd never see coming:** the district itself consolidates. The superintendent's office signs one district-wide literacy contract in spring, and the outside-program MOU queue stops. The ten yeses, and your signed MOUs, become irrelevant overnight.
**What this means you should change:** get signed site agreements from the principals, which turn a yes into paper and make exclusivity of the room a point of discussion. Ask the district directly about any plan for a district-wide provider before you hire.
