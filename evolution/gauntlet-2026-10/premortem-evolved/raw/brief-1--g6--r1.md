# Premortem: Saltmarsh Clay second space

**Horizon:** Dec 25 · **Success was defined as:** "the new space covering its own costs by Christmas without pulling money from the original studio"
**Note on the percentages:** shares of failure, meaning "if this dies, this is what killed it". They are not the probability the plan fails overall. They sum to ~100. **Overall chance this misses its success definition by Dec 25:** ~90%. A five-year lease with your personal guarantee is $246,000 of committed rent, $237,800 of it after the free months, so the plan is judged at that weight.
**Assumptions I'm working from:**
- You gave no past second site or course run to compare against, so the reference classes below are my general read of small studio expansions. They are marked weakly sourced, not measured.
- Fit-out is not inside the $9,400: ten wheels, gas piping from the new meter, a ventilation hood, kiln installation, shelving, clay and glaze, marketing. I assumed you own none of it already.
- I assumed about $3,500 a month of non-rent carrying cost (kiln loan, insurance, utilities) in June and July, then the full $9,400 from August once rent starts.
- Courses run back to back, 8 weeks each, so at most 6.5 cycles a year. I don't know what your 30 members pay, so the original studio's economics are unsized.

## The Autopsy

**The strongest case for this plan:** You have a working studio with 30 people paying monthly, so you know how to run a room of potters, fire work and keep people coming back. 140 people stood at a booth, tried a wheel, and wrote down their contact details, which is a warmer lead list than most new classes ever get. $340 for eight weeks of instruction is a normal price for beginner wheel classes. The first two months are free, so the fit-out costs no rent. A gas kiln that can hold 70 pieces gives the studio fired capacity it lacks today, and it replaces a dying electric kiln with something that serves both sites. If the meter, the waitlist and the firing schedule behave, this is a sensible second site run by someone who has done the first one.

Seven ways this died, ranked by probability.

### 1. Four full cohorts could never cover $9,400 a month, and the plan priced eight-week courses as if they were months
**Probability:** ~30% — the plan's own numbers, with every seat sold, land below its own cost line.
**Cause of death:** $13,600 is what the four cohorts bring in per eight-week course, not per month. Spread over the 8 weeks, a full house is about $7,370 a month ($13,600 x 6.5 cycles / 12) against $9,400 of costs: a $2,030 monthly loss at 100% fill. Break-even needs about 51 paid seats per cycle (9,400 x 12 / 6.5 / 340) and you have 40 seats. At the $340 price, no fill rate reaches the target. Through Christmas the picture is the same: about $53,000 of costs against at most $40,800 of course revenue (three cycles starting Aug 4, Sept 29 and Nov 24), so about $12,000 short even if every seat sells. Below roughly 75% average fill the $22,000 is gone before Christmas, and that is before a dollar of fit-out. Guard already in the plan: none, because the plan's own arithmetic says "comfortably in the black".
**Reference class (weakly sourced, you offered none):** expansions sized on the full-house month tend to miss on fill and fit-out, not on teaching quality. **Where you differ:** you have a recurring member base most second sites lack, which would help, but the new site earns only one-off eight-week fees and has a hard ceiling of 40 seats, so the number goes up, not down.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (June): Lease signed June 2, rent-free. You apply for the meter, spend on the kiln deposit and fit-out, and carry about $3,500 a month with no revenue.
- Month 2 (July): Fit-out finishes. Rent starts in August. Nothing has been checked against the monthly line because the plan never states one.
- Month 3 (Aug): Cohort one opens full and $13,600 lands in the account. It reads as proof that the plan works, because $13,600 looks larger than $9,400, which is the sentence the plan was built on.
- Month 4 (Sept): Cycle two sells and is paid up front on about Sept 29. Cumulative cash sits near break-even on Sept 30 (about $27,200 collected against about $25,800 spent). The cash picture is the best it will ever look.
- Month 5 (Oct): Rent, loan, insurance and instructor go out with no new money coming in until cycle three. The dip reads as "between cycles", not as a run-rate problem.
- Month 6 (Nov): Cycle three collects around Nov 24 and spans Christmas week, when attendance is soft. Run-rate revenue is still $7,370 a month against $9,400.
- Month 7 (Dec): At Christmas you are about $12,000 short even at 100% fill, and the monthly loss will repeat every month until something changes. The guarantee means the lease cannot be walked away from.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that a number per course and a number per month are the same kind of number.
**First warning sign:** Sept 30, when cumulative cash looks level. The real signal is the run-rate line you can compute today: $7,370 a month at best against $9,400.

### 2. The kiln finished 105 pieces a week against 140 to 180 coming in, and the bottleneck grew with enrollment
**Probability:** ~20% — it binds once beginners fill about half the seats, and the kiln math in the plan is a ceiling, not a rate.
**Cause of death:** Every piece needs two firings, bisque and then glaze. Three firings a week at 70 pieces gives 210 piece-slots, which is 105 finished pieces a week. Members alone need 60 a week, which is 57% of that. Forty beginners at 2 to 3 pieces a week add 80 to 120, so total demand is 140 to 180 against 105: 33% to 71% over capacity. The kiln saturates at only about 15 to 22 beginners, or 40% to 55% of the seats. The 2-day cycle is also optimistic for a gas kiln of this size once cooling is counted (my read, not verified). If it slows to 3 days, two firings a week finish 70 pieces, which barely carries the members. Guard already in the plan: none, because the plan assumes a full kiln is a good kiln.
**Reference class (weakly sourced):** firing-for-hire studios with a single kiln run out of kiln time before they run out of students. **Where you differ:** you have one kiln for two audiences, with members' work driven across town, so the ceiling is lower than for a studio that fires only its own students.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (Aug): The meter lands on the best date, Aug 11, the kiln is commissioned and cohort one is throwing. The first firing is two weeks behind the first class.
- Month 2 (Sept): 40 students throw 80 to 120 pieces a week while members send 60. The kiln finishes 105. The unfired backlog grows 35 to 75 pieces every week.
- Month 3 (Oct): The backlog reaches 280 to 600 pieces after eight weeks, which is 3 to 6 weeks of the kiln's full output. Cohort one graduates with one or two glazed pots.
- Month 4 (Nov): Members' bisque waits three weeks and they stop bringing work across town. Graduates do not re-enroll for cycle three and say why in reviews.
- Month 5 (Dec): Gift season arrives as the peak of glazed-work demand. The kiln is still behind on September's work.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that a kiln's stated capacity is its throughput, and that members and beginners can share one without queueing.
**First warning sign:** the second Monday after the first firing, when the pieces waiting for bisque exceed 100.

### 3. The meter slipped past Aug 4 and the old kiln died first, so students had no fired work and members had no firing at all
**Probability:** ~15% — the best date the utility quoted falls a week after the first class, and the members' kiln has no replacement date.
**Cause of death:** You apply June 2. The utility's 10 to 14 weeks puts energization between Aug 11 and Sept 8, so even the best case lands after the Aug 4 start. The kiln still has to be delivered, vented, inspected and test-fired, none of which is scheduled. Meanwhile the electric kiln at the original studio is dying and you will not replace it. Whichever kiln fails first, the other is not ready. Cohort one finishes its eight weeks (about Sept 22) with no fired work, and members' 60 pieces a week have no firing site for the gap. Guard already in the plan: the utility's range, which is a quote, not a commitment. Commitment grades: the utility is Comply (no written date), the 30 members are Accept (nobody has been asked to wait), and the 140 are Comply (a sign-up sheet cost them nothing).
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (June): Lease signed, meter requested June 2. No written date comes back.
- Month 2 (July): The electric kiln fails or limps. Members' pieces stack up; you start driving what can be fired to a studio you have no agreement with, or hold it.
- Month 3 (Aug): Cohort one opens Aug 4 with no gas kiln. Students make pots that sit on shelves for weeks. Members who pay monthly for firing are paying for a service you cannot deliver.
- Month 4 (Sept): The meter arrives near Sept 8, followed by installation and inspection. Cohort one ends without fired work and asks for refunds. Members start cancelling.
- Month 5 (Oct): The first firing happens. Members who left do not come back, which shrinks the original studio's income.
- Month 6 (Nov): The new site's losses now come out of an original studio that has fewer members, which is exactly what the success definition ruled out.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that the utility's range is a promise, and that monthly members will wait out a firing gap because they are already paying.
**First warning sign:** June 19, about 2.5 weeks after the application, with no written energization date from the utility.

### 4. The 140 names were a one-time pool of free-booth curiosity, and the plan has no source of buyers after them
**Probability:** ~10% — signing a sheet at a free tryout costs nothing, and the plan contains no marketing line at all.
**Cause of death:** The 140 names came from a free try-the-wheel booth. Signing a sheet is a Comply-grade commitment, not a purchase. If 30% pay, that fills one cycle with 42 seats. If 15% pay, it fills 21 seats, a bit over half of one. Either way the pool is spent by cycle two, and cycles two and three need 80 more buyers by Sept 29 and Nov 24 with no channel or budget behind them. Guard already in the plan: none.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (June): You email the 140 and leave the booth conversations unconfirmed.
- Month 2 (July): Registrations arrive slowly. Cohorts on two evenings fill; the others sit at a handful.
- Month 3 (Aug): Cohort one runs at about half capacity. The revenue figure never reaches $13,600.
- Month 4 (Sept): Cycle two has no waitlist left to draw on and no ad, referral or partner channel.
- Month 5 (Oct): You cut cohorts to two evenings, which leaves fixed costs unchanged.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that a name on a sign-up sheet is a customer with a date.
**First warning sign:** the first email to the 140, answered by fewer than 28 paid deposits by late May.

Add gas piping from the meter, a ventilation hood, shelving, sinks and clay traps, clay and glaze, and marketing. The $22,000 meant to carry operating losses was spent on getting the room ready. ### 6. You were the only person who could run both sites, and the schedule needed you everywhere

**Cause of death:** No one else is named for loading and unloading the kiln, driving the members' work, opening the original studio or covering the instructor. The unspoken fact the plan avoids is that the original studio's owner is now at another address four evenings a week. Guard already in the plan: a part-time instructor, who covers classes, not the kiln or the drive.

**The assumption that allowed it:** that the instructor hire solves capacity, when it solves only the classroom.

## The Rebuild

**The revised plan:**
1. Do not sign June 2 as drafted. Sign only after the five gates in the decision rule are met, and only with a lease rider: rent starts when gas is energized (not on a calendar date), the personal guarantee is capped at 12 months of rent ($49,200) and burns off after 12 on-time months, with written landlord consent to a vented gas kiln and a right to sublet or assign.
2. Ask the landlord for a letter that lets you apply to the utility now, before signing, so the meter clock starts earlier.
3. Move the first cohort to the week of Sept 15, one week after the worst-case meter date, or to one week after the first successful test firing if that is earlier. Sell seats only once the utility has given an energization date in writing.
4. Price at $420 and add a fifth cohort (Saturday morning) for 50 seats. Break-even is 41 paid seats per cycle (9,400 x 12 / 6.5 / 420).
5. Include four fired pieces per beginner in the course fee. Extra firing is charged per piece, and the rest is reclaimed. That puts beginners at about 25 pieces a week, and with members' 60 the kiln runs at about 80% of 105.
6. Do not retire the electric kiln until the gas kiln has fired two good loads. Get a repair quote now, or arrange a paid per-piece bridge with a nearby studio for the gap.
7. Success moves from Christmas to the January cycle (week of Jan 5): monthly revenue at or above monthly cost at 41 or more paid seats of 50. Christmas cannot be the date: with two cycles collected by Dec 25 (starting Sept 15 and Nov 10), about $42,000 at full fill meets about $45,000 of cost.

Response labels for the most dangerous modes: #1 is **prevent** (price, seat count and the deposit gate close the entry path) and **limit** (the capped, burning-off guarantee bounds the loss at $49,200). #7 is **limit** (written consent before signing; if it is refused afterward the capped guarantee contains the loss). All eight changes cost less than the modes they close: the largest, the lease rider, is a negotiation, against a $237,800 uncapped exposure.

**Pre-launch checklist:**
1. **Utility date.** Ask in writing for the energization date and whether street-side work is needed, before signing. Walk away if the best written date is later than Sept 8 or the landlord cannot authorize an application before signing. Falsifier: a written date after Aug 4 proves the Aug 4 start impossible.
2. **Deposits.** Send the $75 deposit email to all 140 and count payments by May 29. Walk away if fewer than 28 paid. Falsifier: fewer than 28 paid deposits out of 140 proves the waitlist is not demand at $420.
3. **Kiln cycle.** Ask the kiln dealer for the full firing-plus-cooling hours of a 24 cu ft load at your cone. Walk away if the cycle exceeds 72 hours, because two firings a week finish about 70 pieces, which barely covers the members. Falsifier: any cycle over 48 hours proves the three-a-week assumption false.
4. **Fit-out and permits.** Collect three written quotes and the written landlord, insurer and fire-authority confirmations. Walk away if fit-out plus kiln deposit leaves less than $11,000 of the $22,000 (about 1.2 months of fixed costs), or any of the three refuses in writing.
5. **Members.** Ask all 30 in writing whether they would keep paying if firing is delayed to Oct 15. Walk away if 6 or more of the 30 say no or do not answer. Falsifier: 6 or more refusals proves members will not wait out the gap.

**Decision rule:** Sign June 2 IF all five hold by May 29: at least 28 paid deposits at $420, the lease rider granted in writing, a written utility date, at least $11,000 of savings left after quoted fit-out, and no more than 5 of the 30 members saying they would leave over a firing gap. ELSE do not sign, and run one deposit-funded eight-week pilot cohort of ten at the original studio, then revisit at its close. ELSE IF 36 or more deposits are paid and the full rider is granted by May 29, sign June 2 even without the written utility date, because the rent clock waits for gas and the capped guarantee bounds the loss at $49,200.
**If the decision rule lands on delay, reduce, or don't — the reverse autopsy:** The horizon passed. We didn't sign, and it was a mistake. The landlord let the unit to a tenant who took the free months. The 140 drifted to the competitor's fall catalogue before the pilot ran. The electric kiln died anyway in August and, with no second site, members left the original studio.
**What tips the balance:** the ELSE IF branch above. Thirty-six deposits plus the full rider is the evidence that the caution costs more than it saves.
**How the rebuilt plan dies:** its weakest joint is the $420 price. A seat count of 41 of 50 has to be sold to people the waitlist cannot supply past the first cycle, and a higher price lowers conversion in exactly the place where the plan has no pipeline. The Saturday cohort adds a fifth cohort of fixed teaching hours without an extra channel.
**Residual risk:** even rebuilt, break-even needs 82% of 50 seats every cycle, and Christmas cannot be reached. If the gate is passed on thin evidence and fill sits near 70%, the monthly loss is about $2,000 to $3,000 with a capped but real guarantee still in force.

## The Adversary

**Playing:** the nearest established provider of beginner wheel classes. I don't know who that is, so I assumed a studio and the town's community-ceramics or recreation program. Motivation: they lose fall enrollments if you succeed. Resources: existing kilns, a published catalogue and a mailing list. What they cannot do: match a purpose-built new room, and they will not spend heavily to fight a newcomer. Best alternative: do nothing and keep their existing fill, which costs them nothing.
**The kill chain:**
1. **Probe (June 2 to July 15):** They watch the lease signing and the booth list. They publish fall dates and an early-bird price before you can sell seats.
2. **Position (July 15 to Sept 1):** They offer a "firing included" bundle and a Saturday slot at or below $300, using the gap you have not yet closed.
3. **Strike (Sept 8 to Oct 15):** They see that your electric kiln is failing and your gas kiln is late. They offer your 30 members a per-piece firing arrangement.
4. **Entrench (Nov 1 to Dec 15):** They sell holiday gift certificates for classes starting in January, which takes the gift-buying peak that your Nov 10 cycle never touches.
**Detection opportunities:**
- Move 1: their fall catalogue or early-bird announcement is visible by July 15.
- Move 2: a "firing included" listing or a price at or below $300 appears by Sept 1.
- Move 3: two or more of your members mention another studio's firing, or request a release, between Sept 8 and Oct 15.
- Move 4: gift-certificate listings appear by Nov 1 while your cycle-two registrations are under 36 on Nov 10.
**The move you'd never see coming:** a subsidized public or community-college ceramics program whose fall catalogue, published in July, sets a town-wide price for an eight-week wheel course well below $340. It isn't targeting you, can't be negotiated with, and anchors what every beginner thinks the class is worth.
**What this means you should change:** lead with a written firing guarantee in every sale and every member email. Check public fall catalogues by July 15 before fixing the price. Put a firing promise to the 30 members in writing before September.
