## The Autopsy
**The strongest case for this plan:** You already run a studio where 30 people pay every month and say they love it, so you can operate wheels, a kiln and a community. 140 strangers wrote their names down after a free try-the-wheel booth, which is real warm interest, not a survey answer. Six ways this died, ranked by probability.

### 1. The new room lost about $2,000 a month at 100% fill, and the personal guarantee made the loss yours
**Probability:** ~30% — the plan's own revenue is per 8-week cycle, its costs are per month, and nobody put them in the same unit
**Reference class and disanalogy:** Second locations of small service businesses commonly under-run the first (weakly sourced, no dataset I can cite). Where you differ: your first studio sells recurring membership, while this one sells a one-off course that must find 40 new buyers every 8 weeks. That pushes the number up, not down.
**Cause of death:** $13,600 is the revenue of four full cohorts over 8 weeks (4 × 10 × $340), not per month. 8 weeks is about 1.85 months, so full capacity earns about $7,390 a month against $9,400 of costs. Per cycle: $13,600 in, about $17,300 out. Break-even at $340 is 51 paying students per cycle, and the room has 40 seats.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (June): Lease signed June 2 with the guarantee. Fit-out takes about $12,000 and the pre-opening costs about $6,600 more, leaving roughly $3,400 of the $22,000 before one student has paid. Rent-free hides the burn.

**Guard the plan already has:** none. The cushion is the only guard, and it is spent before the first break-even month.
**The assumption that allowed it:** "$13,600 a course against $9,400 a month. Comfortably in the black."
**First warning sign:** Today. Divide $9,400 by $340 per 1.85 months and you have 51 students, not 40. The next signal is cycle 1 deposits below 24 by May 30.

### 2. The meter was not live on August 4, so the first cohorts threw clay with nothing to fire it in

**Reference class and disanalogy:** Utility service upgrades and equipment commissioning commonly run past the quoted window (weakly sourced). **How it unfolded:**

### 3. The kiln was too small for the room's own success

**How it unfolded:**

**Guard the plan already has:** none. The plan rules out replacing the dying electric kiln.
**The assumption that allowed it:** "A load holds about 70 pieces and a cycle takes two days, so three firings a week" counted loads, not finished pieces.
**First warning sign:** Any load calendar booked a week ahead, or a bone-dry piece waiting more than five days for a bisque slot.

## The Rebuild

## The Adversary

It has working kilns, an instructor, and marketing already paid for. It can't match your community or your Harbor Days relationships. **The kill chain:**

2. **Aug 11 to Sep 8, strike.** While your meter is pending, they offer your members open bisque and glaze firing at a promotional per-piece rate.

**Detection opportunities:** Move 1 shows as deposits flattening below pace and waitlist replies saying "I signed up elsewhere" (by July 7). Move 2 shows as members asking what a piece costs to fire elsewhere and fewer members bringing work (by Aug 31). Move 3 shows as fewer than 40% of cycle 1 graduates enrolling in cycle 2 or joining (by Oct 6).
**The move you'd never see coming:** They never touch your students. They offer your uncontracted part-time instructor a full-time job in August, and you lose the person who runs the room.
**What this means you should change:** Put the instructor on paper with a notice period, offer your own "firing included" with the course, and give members a reason to stay that has nothing to do with the new kiln.
