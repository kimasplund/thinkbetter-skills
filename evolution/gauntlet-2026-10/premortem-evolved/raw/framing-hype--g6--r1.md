# Premortem: Saltmarsh Clay second space (beginner wheel courses)

**Horizon:** Dec 25 · **Success was defined as:** "the new space covering its own costs by Christmas without pulling money from the original studio"
**Note on the percentages:** shares of failure ("if this dies, this is what killed it"), not the probability the plan fails overall. They sum to ~100. **Overall chance this misses its success definition by the horizon:** ~90%.
**Assumptions I'm working from:** You gave no past project to compare against, no fit-out budget, no member dues and no weekly hours for yourself, so I used general knowledge of small studio and retail expansions for the comparisons, and I priced fit-out as unknown rather than guessing a figure. I assumed the $9,400 a month starts when you sign, except the $4,100 rent, which starts Aug 2 when the free period ends. I assumed the 10-14 week meter estimate covers the meter only, and that piping, ventilation and inspection come after it. I assumed tuition is paid up front for each 8-week course and that the four evening cohorts run on the same 10 wheels back to back with no gap weeks. I did not run a dated search, so no outside statistic is quoted. Where I lean on a base rate it is marked as weakly sourced. All dates hang off your June 2 and August 4.

## The Autopsy

**The strongest case for this plan:** You already run a studio where 30 people pay every month, so you have shown that Corvel Bay will pay for clay time and that you can run the place. Beginner courses are how most studios grow that base, and a second room dedicated to them keeps newcomers out of members' wheel time. 140 people wrote their name down at the fair after trying the wheel, which is a warm list that cost nothing to build. Tuition is paid up front, so eight weeks of cash arrives in the first weeks of each run. Two rent-free months soften the fit-out. A gas kiln fires bigger and cheaper per piece than an electric one, and the utility has given you a real range for the meter. A smart advocate would say you are expanding while the demand is warm, with cash in the bank and a fallback in the original studio.

Seven ways this died, ranked by probability.

### 1. The "comfortably in the black" sum compared eight weeks of tuition to one month of costs, and the new space lost money even with every seat sold
**Probability:** ~30% — the shortfall sits inside your own numbers, before any miss on demand or timing
**Cause of death:** $13,600 is eight weeks of tuition, $1,700 a week. $9,400 is one month of costs, about $2,170 a week. With all 40 seats full on a continuous schedule, the space lost about $470 a week, roughly $2,000 a month. Break-even needed 51 students on 40 wheels, or a price near $435 a seat. By Christmas the costs ran about $55,700 (two months at $5,300 while the rent was free, then about 4.8 months at $9,400). Two full courses brought in $27,200. That left a $28,500 hole against a $22,000 cushion, before any fit-out. At half full, the hole was about $42,000.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (Jun): rent-free, so the loan, insurance, utilities and instructor drew the cushion down by about $5,300 with no income.
- Month 2 (Jul): fit-out spending and a second $5,300 month. The cushion was gone before a single student had paid.
- Month 3 (Aug): the first course's tuition landed up front and made August look healthy. Rent started Aug 2, so costs went to $9,400.
- Month 4 (Sep): no tuition arrived, since the next course started Oct 6. Cash fell by the full $9,400, and the August high was spent.
- Month 5 (Oct): the second course's tuition arrived and was spent within weeks. The cushion crossed zero around Dec 1 on a full house, and weeks earlier at any lower fill.
- Month 6 (Nov-Dec): you pulled money from the original studio to cover rent, which by your own definition is the failure.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that a per-course figure and a per-month figure can sit on opposite sides of a ">" sign. This was the claim you stated with the most certainty, and it did not survive the first division.
**First warning sign:** the September bank statement, with $9,400 going out and no tuition coming in, about Sep 1. That is after the lease was signed.
**Reference class:** a second site that opens on owner savings usually ends up funded by the first site's income for longer than planned (weakly sourced; general pattern, no study cited). **Disanalogy:** your tuition is prepaid, which hides the monthly loss for roughly the first 3 months, so it is found later than usual. That pushes this share up, not down.
**Existing guard:** none. The $22,000 cushion pays for the loss; it does not remove it.

### 2. Classes opened Aug 4 with rent running and a kiln that could not be lit
**Probability:** ~20% — the utility's own range already lands after your start date, and lead times over two weeks usually run 2-3x until someone confirms otherwise
**Cause of death:** The meter application goes in Jun 2. Ten to fourteen weeks lands Aug 11 to Sep 8, so even the best case misses Aug 4. At 2-3x that range, the meter arrives between Oct 20 and Mar 23. Piping and inspection follow it. Rent-free ends Aug 1, so the first rent bill arrives before the earliest possible gas. The kiln loan starts on delivery whether the gas is on or not.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (Jun): you applied Jun 2. The utility acknowledged the application and gave a queue, not a date. Fit-out went ahead, and the kiln arrived and started its loan payments.
- Month 2 (Jul): still no written date. You enrolled 40 students for Aug 4 anyway, because the schedule was announced.
- Month 3 (Aug): Aug 2 rent started and Aug 4 classes began. Aug 11, the earliest the utility had said, passed without a meter. Students threw pots that could not be fired.
- Month 4 (Sep): the utility said late October. The first course ended Sep 26 with about 100 pieces unfired. Refund requests and discounts came in.
- Month 5 (Oct): the second course filled on a weaker word of mouth. The old studio's electric kiln was still limping (see #4).
**The assumption that allowed it:** that "10-14 weeks" was a promise rather than a queue estimate, and that the first class could wait on the kiln.
**First warning sign:** no written energization date by Jun 30, four weeks after applying.
**Reference class:** utility and trade lead times that slip past their stated window (weakly sourced). **Disanalogy:** you have a range from the utility itself rather than a guess, which lowers the share a little. But your own start date sits inside the early end of that range, which raises it more.
**Existing guard:** applying the day of signing. That guard is inadequate, because it starts the clock only after you are committed.

### 3. Both rooms filled, and the one kiln could fire about 105 pieces a week at each stage against 140-180 needed
**Probability:** ~15% — it needs demand to arrive (see #5), but the kiln arithmetic itself is firm
**Cause of death:** Every piece needs a bisque slot and a glaze slot. Three firings a week, alternating, give about 1.5 bisque and 1.5 glaze loads, so roughly 105 pieces a week through each. Need: 60 member pieces plus 40 students at 2-3 pieces each, so 140-180 a week. Short by 35-75 pieces a week, a backlog of 280-600 pieces after 8 weeks. Members take 60 of the 105 slots, which leaves 45 for students who need 80-120. The kiln supports roughly 15-22 beginners, not 40. Three two-day cycles run the kiln 6 days out of 7 with no slack. One failed element or valve stopped both studios at once, since the electric kiln is not being replaced.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (Aug-Sep): firings barely started, so nothing looked wrong. Pieces piled on shelves.
- Month 2 (Sep-Oct): bisque shelves filled. The instructor skipped glaze lessons because nothing was ready.
- Month 3 (Oct): members' work waited three weeks. Loads were packed tighter to catch up, and glaze loads suffered cracks and runs.
- Month 4 (Nov): the first ruined load cost a week and some goodwill. Students asked for refunds, and members asked about the old studio's kiln.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that 70 pieces a load, three loads a week, was enough because 210 is bigger than 180. It counts a piece once, and a piece goes through the kiln twice.
**First warning sign:** more than one full load of unfired pieces waiting over a week, about the third week of the first course.
**Existing guard:** none. Note the bind: if demand is thin (#5), this mode relaxes. If demand is full, this one bites. There is no enrollment level where both hold.

### 4. Compound: the gas delay and the dying electric kiln landed together, and the members who carry the original studio left
**Probability:** ~10% — it needs the slip in #2 (likely) and the old kiln failing before the new one lights (plausible)
**Cause of death:** The old kiln is dying and will not be replaced. Member work is meant to move to the new kiln. When #2 delays the new kiln, members have nowhere to fire. Their dues are the only income the plan assumes is untouched.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (Aug-Sep): the electric kiln failed or slowed. Members' work waited. The new kiln was not lit.
- Month 2 (Oct): the first member asked when their pieces would be fired. A few froze their dues.
- Month 3 (Nov): fewer dues came in at the same time that the new site was drawing savings. Cash moved from the original studio to the new space's rent.
- Month 4 (Dec): the original studio was losing members, so "without pulling money from the original studio" was already false.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that members will accept a cross-town drive and a firing gap. Members are at best "accept": nobody has asked them, and a pocket veto costs nothing until they stop paying.
**First warning sign:** a member asking where their pieces are, or a freeze, in the first half of September.
**Existing guard:** none.

### 5. The 140 names were curiosity at a free booth, and far fewer paid $340 for a particular weeknight
**Probability:** ~10% — free sign-up sheets measure interest, not willingness to pay; conversion to paid is often low double digits at best (weakly sourced)
**Cause of death:** 29% of the list had to buy, and in the right pattern: ten per night on four specific nights. Fair visitors choose one night, if any. At 15% conversion, about 21 students enrolled. Two cohorts then ran at a revenue of $7,140 per course against costs of $17,400 for the same eight weeks.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (Jun): you emailed the list. Replies came slowly, and many of the "sure" people were not available on Mondays or Tuesdays.
- Month 2 (Jul): two nights filled, two did not, and you cut to two cohorts.
- Month 3 (Aug): the fixed costs stayed the same while revenue per course halved.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that people who write their name down for free will pay $340, and will do it on a specific weeknight. The list was gathered at one summer event.
**First warning sign:** fewer than 20 paid seats by Jul 1.
**Existing guard:** the 140-name list is a partial guard, since it is a real set of leads. It does not hold because no one has paid anything.

### 6. The $22,000 cushion was spent once on costs nobody priced, and again on the gap
**Probability:** ~10% — the $9,400 holds only run-rate items, and nothing in the plan prices opening the doors
**Cause of death:** Fit-out and consumables are absent from the plan. That means wheels, wedging tables, shelving, glaze, clay for 40 students at 2-3 pieces each, venting, the gas line from the meter to the kiln, and your own pay. The cushion paid for them before the first monthly loss started.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1 (Jun): quotes arrived above what you pictured, but the rent-free months made it feel cheap to wait.
- Month 2 (Jul): you paid from savings to open on time.
- Month 3 (Aug): classes opened with a smaller cushion, and clay and glaze for 40 students came out of cash.
- Month 4 (Sep): the cushion was already drawn down when #1's monthly loss began, so the date it ran out moved earlier by the size of the fit-out.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that the monthly cost list was the cost of the business.
**First warning sign:** the first fit-out quote, due in June.
**Existing guard:** the $22,000 cushion. It is the thing being spent, not a guard against spending.

### 7. The blind spot: an inspector, insurer or landlord asked a question this plan never contained
**Probability:** ~5% — judgment call; no permit, occupancy limit, ventilation rule or inspection appears anywhere in the plan, and I cannot see Corvel Bay's rules from here
**Cause of death:** A gas kiln, a new meter and 40 evening students arrived without a written sign-off for gas appliances or assembly use. The landlord's insurer asked for one.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 2 (Jul): the kiln and piping went in on the landlord's say-so.
- Month 3 (Aug): the insurer or inspector asked for sign-off. Without it, classes could not run on the premises.
- Month 4 (Sep): rent continued while students waited.
**The assumption that allowed it:** that if the landlord let you fit out, the use was allowed.
**First warning sign:** no written permit or insurer confirmation for gas appliances in hand by Jul 15.
**Existing guard:** none. The adversary section tests this slot.

**Tie-breaks at 10%.** #4 over #5: #4 is triggered by an event already on the calendar (the utility range against Aug 4), while #5 depends on behaviour I cannot see from here. #4 over #6: if the slip happens, #4 follows from a failing kiln the plan has already decided not to fix, while #6 is a cost estimate that you control. #5 over #6: the first sales email tests #5 within weeks, while #6 is bounded by what you choose to spend.
**Considered and cut:** gas price swings (folded into #1), a shortage of beginner supplies (<2%, recoverable).

## The Rebuild
**The revised plan:** Do not sign on June 2 on these terms. Spend the next three weeks turning the unknowns into paper. Get the utility's written energization date. Sell paid seats. Negotiate the lease so rent starts only once the gas is live and the guarantee is capped. Keep a firing source for the 30 members until the new kiln has run a month. Start with two evening cohorts until the kiln proves it can carry more. Reprice or cut fixed costs until the model works at 30 students instead of 51. If the answers come back right, sign. If not, run a small beginner pilot at the original studio in September and revisit the second space with proof.
**What changed and why:**

|---|---|---|
| Launch-Blocking: Sign only with rent starting at gas energization plus 30 days, the guarantee capped at 6 months' rent ($24,600) and burning off after 12 on-time months, and a break option at month 18. Decide by May 26. | #1, #2, #4 | The landlord may refuse and re-let the space |
| Launch-Blocking: Pre-sell paid seats from the list. Need 28 paid by May 26 or no signature. | #5, #1 | A delayed launch, and asking leads for money |

| Launch-Blocking: Keep a firing source for members, either by repairing or replacing the dying electric kiln or by contract firing, until the gas kiln has run 4 clean weeks. Quote by May 26, in place by Jul 15. | #4 | A repair or per-piece fee I cannot price from here |

No change costs more than the loss it closes. The largest cost, a landlord who walks, is small against a $246,000 nominal guarantee. Responses for the most dangerous modes: #1 prevent (rows 1, 2, 5), #4 limit (row 4, because the old kiln's failure date cannot be moved), #2 prevent (row 3, via the electric option) or limit (row 1, the rent trigger).
**Pre-launch checklist:**
1. **Gas date.** Ask the utility what the application requires, whether you can apply before signing, and for an energization date in writing. Walk away if the written date is after Sep 15 and the electric route does not work. Falsifier for "the meter will be live by Aug 4": it is already false on the utility's own range (Aug 11 earliest), and any answer of "20+ weeks" or no written date by Jun 30 confirms it.
2. **Paid demand.** Offer the 140 names a paid seat, deposit or full, with a May 26 close. Walk away with fewer than 28 paid seats, or fewer than 8 on any one night, which means that night does not run. Falsifier for "the demand is already there": fewer than 28 paid seats by May 26.
3. **Lease and permits.** Ask the landlord for the rent trigger, the guarantee cap and the month-18 break, and have a lawyer check cross-default between lease and loan. Get the permit and insurer answers in writing. Walk away if the landlord refuses all three terms, or if any permit is unresolved by Jul 15.
4. **Cash.** Get itemized fit-out and consumables quotes. Walk away if fit-out plus six months of projected losses at 60% fill exceeds $22,000. On your own numbers six months at 60% fill already loses about $30,000, before any fit-out.
5. **Members and firing.** Ask the 30 members about the drive and a firing gap, and ask a ceramics supplier for realistic glaze-load counts for beginner work. Walk away if more than 5 members say they would leave, or if the old kiln fails before a firing source is in place.
**Decision rule:** Sign IF (a) the written energization date is on or before Sep 15, (b) the rent starts at energization plus 30 days and the guarantee is capped, (c) 28 or more seats are paid by May 26 with at least 8 on each night that will run, AND (d) the model breaks even at 30 paid students (a price near $580, or fixed costs near $5,500 a month at $340). ELSE do not sign; run one beginner cohort at the original studio in September and reopen the question on Nov 1 with that evidence.
**If the decision rule lands on delay, reduce, or don't — the reverse autopsy:** We did not sign, and it was a mistake. The space was let in the first week and nothing as good appeared. The list went cold over the summer. A rival ran the autumn beginner courses.
**What tips the balance:** If the landlord accepts the energization trigger and the cap, the utility commits to a date by Sep 15 in writing, and 28 seats are paid, the decision reverses to sign. That is written into the rule above as the condition for signing.
**Consciously accepted:** the landlord may let the space to someone else while you gather deposits. Priced cost: about 2 months of delay and possibly the space, against a nominal $246,000 guaranteed exposure.
**How the rebuilt plan dies:** The new weakest joint is the three-week sprint itself. Deposits may not arrive in the window, the landlord may not hold the space, and a September pilot at the original studio takes wheel time from members and leans on the same dying kiln. If the lease concessions fail, the rebuild comes down to a choice between walking away and signing the original terms.
**Residual risk:** Even rebuilt, a second site that depends on selling a $340-$580 beginner course in a town of unknown size may never cover $9,400 a month. The honest result may be that the second site is the wrong way to grow, and growing the member base is the better one.

## The Adversary

**Playing:** your landlord, with the equipment lender behind them. The landlord gains a five-year rent stream of about $246,000 backed by your person, and the free months are a discount against that guarantee, not a gift. They have a lawyer-drafted lease, time, and no need to be your friend. They cannot force you to sign or accelerate before a default, and their best alternative is an empty unit that costs them rent. That is why the free months were offered.
**The kill chain:**
1. **Jun 2, position:** you sign, and the guarantee becomes personal. Rent is fixed to start Aug 2, whatever the meter does.
2. **Aug 2, probe:** the first rent invoice arrives with no gas and no kiln. The landlord is passive and the clock runs.
3. **Late Oct to Nov, strike:** the cushion runs dry. At the first missed rent a notice arrives, and the cure period starts to run.
4. **Dec, entrench:** the landlord demands the remaining rent under the guarantee, net of re-letting. If the original studio is not a separate company, its assets are personal assets.
**Detection opportunities:**
1. Jun 2: lease says rent starts on a date, not on gas. Seen before signing if you read it.
2. Aug 2: a rent invoice with no energization letter. Seen on the invoice.
3. Oct 15: cushion under $10,000. Seen on the bank balance, three to five weeks before a missed payment.
4. Any day: a notice, which comes after the damage.
**The move you'd never see coming:** a cross-default, if either document has one, so a missed loan payment on the kiln triggers a default on the lease or the other way round. Neither document has been read for it.
**What this means you should change:** Read both documents for cross-default and rent triggers before signing, and open the rent conversation at the first sign of trouble, not after the first missed payment.
