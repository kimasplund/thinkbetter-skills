Short answer: not as written. I wouldn't sign on June 2. Three problems, any one of which could sink the plan, and the numbers that make it look safe don't hold up.

**1. The math is per course, not per month**

$13,600 is what four full cohorts bring in over an 8-week course. Your costs are $9,400 per month. Over the same 8 weeks, costs are roughly $17,400 (about 1.85 months). So even with every seat sold and a new course starting the moment the last one ends, you lose about $3,800 per 8-week cycle. That's roughly $2,000 a month. Put another way, full cohorts bring in about $7,350 a month against $9,400 in costs.

Break-even at these prices would take about 51 students per 8 weeks. You only have 40 seats (4 cohorts x 10 wheels). The plan can't cover its costs at full capacity. To work, you'd need some mix of these:
- More seats: two sessions per evening, daytime or weekend cohorts, or open-studio memberships on top.
- A higher price: roughly $430 or more per student at 40 seats.
- Lower fixed costs: a cheaper space, or a shorter or smaller commitment.

**2. The timeline doesn't work**

- Meter: 10-14 weeks from June 2 means gas arrives between about August 11 and September 8. That's after the August 4 course start. Utility schedules also tend to slip, and the kiln still needs installation and inspection after the meter.
- Rent: the two free months end around August 2. You'd pay rent, the kiln loan and the instructor from August, with no kiln and probably little income yet.
- Students: beginners would throw pieces for weeks with no way to fire them. That's a poor first impression, and you'd probably have to refund or delay.
- Members: your old electric kiln is dying and you won't replace it. If the new kiln isn't running, your 30 paying members have nowhere to fire. That puts your existing income at risk, which is the thing you said you must not touch.

**3. Kiln capacity is tighter than it looks**

You have three firings a week at 70 pieces each, which is 210 slots. But every piece needs two firings, a bisque and then a glaze. The real throughput is about 105 finished pieces a week.

- Demand: members 60 + beginners (40 x 2-3 = 80-120) = about 140-180 finished pieces a week.
- That's 35-70% more than the kiln can handle, before breakage, refires or uneven loads.

Expect a backlog that grows every week, with students waiting a month or more for finished work. You'd need a second kiln, a larger one, or a cap on member and student volume.

**Demand is softer than 140 names**

A free try-the-wheel booth signup is a low-commitment signal. Typical conversion to a paid $340 course might be 5-15%. That's roughly 7-21 students, not 40. Test it before you commit: email the list now and ask for deposits, or pre-sell a pilot. If you get 25 or more paid deposits, the demand is real.

**Cash and risk**

- A five-year lease at $4,100/month is about $246,000, and you'd guarantee it personally.
- Your $22,000 cushion covers perhaps 8-10 months of the monthly gap if nothing else goes wrong. But fit-out, wheels, deposits, kiln installation and the meter upgrade aren't in your $9,400 and would eat much of it first. A delayed start or half-full cohorts could use it up by autumn.
- "Covering its own costs by Christmas" is unlikely on these numbers, and a failure would reach back to the original studio.

**What I'd do instead**

1. Don't sign yet. Apply for the meter upgrade first if the utility will take the application without a lease, or ask the landlord for a lease contingent on utility approval.
2. Negotiate the lease: rent-free months that start when gas is live, a 1-2 year term or a break clause, and a cap on or removal of the personal guarantee.
3. Keep the old kiln running or repaired until the new one is fired and proven. Don't leave members without a kiln.
4. Move the course start to October or later, after the meter is installed and the kiln is tested.
5. Fix the economics: more seats, a higher price, or a revenue add-on such as open-studio memberships at the new space.
6. Pre-sell deposits from the waitlist, then size the number of cohorts to actual demand.
7. Plan kiln capacity for two firings per piece, or plan a second kiln.

The idea of beginner courses is reasonable, and a waitlist does show interest. But the plan, as it stands, loses money even when everything goes right, depends on a utility date that falls after your start, and overloads the kiln. Fix those three things before you put your name on the lease.

I assumed 4.33 weeks per month, that the cohorts run once a week, and that your $9,400 doesn't include fit-out or equipment purchases. If any of those are wrong, tell me and I'll rerun the numbers.
