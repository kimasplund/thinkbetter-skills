Notes (J-B, skeptical reviewer):

- A1J: The most rigorous arithmetic of the six. I checked the $7,370 a month, break-even at 51 seats, about $53k against $40.8k by Christmas, 105 finished pieces a week, the kiln saturating at 15 to 22 beginners, and the $420 / 41-seat rebuild, and all of them hold. Estimates are flagged. But it is buried in heavy ceremony: commitment grades, an I×F table, tie-breaks, tiers, falsifiers and a calibration row. The decision rule's ELSE / ELSE IF order is muddled.
- L9X: Accurate, short, and ranks the key issues well: timeline, unit economics, waitlist, kiln, and the guarantee. "Probably underpriced" is unsupported, and the conversion range is unsourced but hedged. It has a stop rule and a pilot. It does not cover permits or owner bandwidth.
- S3V: Thorough, and the math is correct. It makes the sharp point that moving members' firing earns the new space nothing, and that a rival poaching members is the real threat. Weaknesses: the "19 weeks = 2x slip" claim is sloppy, the 55-75% coverage figure doesn't match its own numbers, mode #7 is filler, and machinery is everywhere ("Accept-level", tiers, falsifiers).
- P1O: Opens with the answer, which helps. It makes the good point that firing is the binding constraint and proposes a used kiln for the original studio. But its gates are internally inconsistent (20 vs 30 deposits; 40 deposits / Aug 4 vs 44 / Aug 11), the $50-100 member fee is invented and unflagged, and "the account crossed zero in September" doesn't follow from its own figures.
- G1D: The best domain insight: a gas reduction kiln changes members' glaze colours, and members' 60 pieces leave room for only about 18 students. But it calls the utility quote a "lower bound" and applies a 2-3x rule it then misapplies. Machinery ("accept", "comply") and length are heavy.
- P5K: Clean, correct math ($17.4k per cycle, $430 price needed, 35-70% kiln overload), and it keeps the members' kiln in view. The recommendations are generic and have no thresholds. A 25-deposit "demand is real" bar doesn't connect to the 51-seat break-even.
