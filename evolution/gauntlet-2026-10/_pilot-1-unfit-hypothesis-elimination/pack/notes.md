# Pack notes

**Brief 1 (craft brewery, lager off-flavor).** True cause: the inline density meters on FV3/FV4 trigger the automatic cold crash too early (9-10 days vs 13-15), so fermentation stops before the yeast has reabsorbed diacetyl. The batch table separates cleanly by tank and by days-to-crash. Batch 9 (FV3 in manual mode, 15 days, clean) is the built-in control. Red herring 1 is the new yeast lot L22, which the head brewer pushes and which lines up with the timing, but batch 2 on the old lot is buttery and seven L22 batches are clean. Red herring 2 is the new brewer Pia, who brewed 3 of the 5 bad batches. Her FV1/FV2 batches are clean, and other brewers' FV3/FV4 batches are bad. The mill gap, the sanitizer change, chiller load and infection are extra noise that the temperature logs, the plating and the timeline rule out. The hype and skeptical framings repeat every fact and number exactly and change only the tone.

**Brief 2 (municipal water utility, discolored water).** True cause: a valve left partly or fully closed after the September 4 Ardent Avenue main repair. It reroutes and speeds up flow into Linden Heights and scours iron from the old unlined cast-iron mains. The evidence is the next-day onset, 54 of 63 complaints in that zone, a drop of more than 10 psi only there, and iron-dominant samples. Red herring 1 is Well 7. Its high raw manganese and its September 2 restart give it timing, but it feeds the whole system, effluent manganese is unchanged, and the problem is iron, which the well barely carries. Red herring 2 is the Hollis Park flushing, a well-known cause of discoloration. It started four days after the complaints and was in a different zone, where pressure did not change. The lead service line work explains at most Hollis Park's handful of complaints.

**Brief 3 (subscription practice app, trial conversion drop).** True cause: the August 31 Android release with the required billing library upgrade broke purchase handling, so Android conversions are being refunded or never granted. The signal is a roughly 15x jump in Play Console refunds with App Store refunds flat, on a platform carrying about 60% of trials. The user's team has already folded this into the price story. Red herring 1 is the price rise, backed by the timing, the billing tickets and the CEO's instinct. Regions with no price change dropped just as hard, and iOS refunds did not move despite the same price rise. Red herring 2 is the onboarding test, backed by a real drop in day-3 engagement. Conversion is the same in both arms, so it cannot explain the overall fall.
