Yes, this is normal. Buttery flavor is diacetyl. Yeast makes it early in fermentation and then reabsorbs it. At 10°C that reabsorption is slow, so a lager on day 10 often still tastes buttery. A diacetyl rest is the standard fix.

**When to start the rest**
Start when fermentation is about 70-80% done, or when gravity is within a few points of your expected final gravity. Don't wait until it's completely finished, because you need active yeast to clean up the diacetyl. Check gravity first. If it's still high, give it a few more days before you raise the temperature.

**Temperature and time**
- Raise the beer to 15-18°C (60-65°F). Around 16-18°C is common.
- Hold it for 2-3 days. Some brewers go up to 4-5 days at the lower end of that range.
- Raise it gradually, a few degrees per day, so you don't stress the yeast or throw off other flavors.

**Check that it worked**
- Taste a sample after 2 days. Warm the sample to about room temperature first, because cold hides diacetyl.
- Better still, use the forced diacetyl test. Heat a sample in a sealed jar to about 60°C for 15-30 minutes, then cool it and smell it. This converts the diacetyl precursor, so it shows what the beer would taste like if you stopped now. If it's clean, the rest is done.

**After the rest**
Cool the beer slowly, about 2-3°C per day, down to lagering temperature (0-4°C). Lager it for several weeks. The yeast will keep cleaning up some remaining flavors during this time.

**If the butter flavor doesn't go away**
- Check for infection. A persistent, slick, buttery or butterscotch note, especially with a sour or off smell, can mean bacterial contamination (Pediococcus). Rest alone won't fix that.
- Low yeast health or too little pitched yeast can also leave more diacetyl behind.
