I'm the operations supervisor at the Brenmoor Water Authority, about 40,000 connections. Since September 5 we've logged 63 discolored-water complaints: brown or rusty water, worst first thing in the morning. A normal month is 8 to 10. The council wants an explanation at Thursday's meeting.

Where the complaints are: 54 in the Linden Heights pressure zone, 6 in Hollis Park, 3 scattered elsewhere.

What happened recently:
- Sept 1 onward: a contractor is replacing lead service lines in Hollis Park, about 10 a day.
- Sept 2: Well 7 came back online after rehab and now supplies about 15% of the whole system. Its raw water runs high in manganese (0.04 mg/L). Plant effluent manganese has stayed at 0.010 to 0.014 mg/L, same as before.
- Sept 4: a crew repaired a main break on Ardent Avenue, on the 12-inch main between the Calder Road tank and Linden Heights. They isolated it with four valves and reopened them that evening.
- Sept 9 to 12: we ran unidirectional flushing in Hollis Park.

Lab results on complaint samples: iron 0.6 to 1.1 mg/L (we normally see under 0.1), manganese 0.02 to 0.03 mg/L, chlorine residual normal. Well 7's iron is 0.04 mg/L.

Routine hydrant pressure checks on September 15 read 49 to 53 psi in Linden Heights, against 60 to 64 in June. Hollis Park read 71, unchanged.

Linden Heights mains are mostly 1950s unlined cast iron.

Our water quality manager thinks it's Well 7 and wants to take it back offline. Our engineer thinks the Hollis Park flushing stirred up sediment that travelled. What's causing this, and what should I tell the council on Thursday?
