**Short answer:** The evidence points to a hydraulic disturbance in Linden Heights that started with the September 4 Ardent Avenue repair, most likely a valve that did not go back fully open. That disturbance is stirring up iron scale from the old unlined cast iron mains. It is not Well 7, and it is probably not the Hollis Park flushing. This is the best-supported hypothesis, not a confirmed finding, and one field check can confirm or kill it.

**Why not Well 7**
- The complaint water is high in iron (0.6 to 1.1 mg/L) with only modest manganese (0.02 to 0.03 mg/L). A manganese problem from Well 7 would show up as manganese, usually black or dark brown.
- Well 7's iron is 0.04 mg/L, and plant effluent manganese is unchanged at 0.010 to 0.014 mg/L. Well 7 water can't be the source of the iron.
- Well 7 could still matter indirectly. A new 15% supply source can change flow directions in the network. Taking it offline would be a big, costly step on weak evidence.

**Why not the Hollis Park flushing**
- Hollis Park pressure is unchanged at 71 psi, and it has only 6 of the 63 complaints.
- Linden Heights has 54 of 63 complaints, and its pressure is down about 10 to 12 psi from June. Sediment from a flushed area doesn't explain a pressure loss in a different zone.
- The flushing ran September 9 to 12, but the complaints began September 5, before it started.

**Why the Ardent Avenue repair fits best**
- The timing matches. The repair was September 4 and the complaints start September 5.
- The location matches. The 12-inch main runs between the Calder Road tank and Linden Heights, so it is a main feed to the problem zone.
- A lower pressure plus a concentrated complaint pattern is what a partly closed valve on that feed would produce. Flow is restricted, the system reroutes it through smaller or older mains, and velocities and flow directions change. Unlined 1950s cast iron holds a lot of corrosion scale, and sudden flow changes release it. That gives high iron, normal chlorine and worst-in-the-morning complaints, because water has sat in the pipes overnight and then demand ramps up.
- Four valves were closed and reopened in one evening. One that is not fully open, or a wrong valve left closed, is a common and easy mistake.

**Other contributors**
- The 6 Hollis Park complaints are likely local. Lead line replacement involves shutoffs and disturbance, and flushing can briefly cause discoloration.
- The 3 scattered complaints are within normal range.
- I'm assuming the Linden Heights pressure drop is recent. The only comparison is June versus September 15, so I can't date it. If it had been gradual, I'd look at other causes such as tank levels or demand.

**What to do before Thursday (in this order)**
1. Check the position of all four Ardent Avenue valves, counting turns and comparing to the records. Fully open any that aren't. Do this first, because it is cheap.
2. Put pressure loggers on Linden Heights hydrants and at the Calder Road tank outlet. See whether pressure recovers after the valve fix.
3. Check the Calder Road tank level and its fill/draw pattern, and any pressure-reducing or booster settings.
4. Check how Well 7 operation changes flows in Linden Heights, for example by comparing flow direction or velocities with the well on and off in your model.
5. After the pressure is restored, do targeted flushing of Linden Heights, starting nearest the source, until the water clears. Don't flush before fixing the cause, because the scale will keep coming back.
6. Keep Well 7 online unless the test above points to it. Keep monitoring manganese at the plant and in the system.

**What to tell the council**
- "Since September 5 we've had 63 discolored-water complaints, versus 8 to 10 in a normal month. Most are in Linden Heights. The water is safe to use as far as our testing shows. The discoloration is iron from old cast iron pipes, and chlorine is normal." Only say it is safe if your own sampling supports that. Iron at these levels is an aesthetic issue, not a health one.
- "The data rules out Well 7 as the source. Its iron is very low and plant manganese hasn't changed."
- "The leading cause is a change in flow and pressure in Linden Heights following the September 4 main repair, which disturbed deposits in aging pipes. We're verifying the valves and pressure this week."
- "Next steps: restore normal pressure and flow, then flush Linden Heights. We'll report results and set up a longer-term plan for the unlined cast iron mains."
- Be upfront that the cause is not yet confirmed. Saying "most likely, and here's the check we're running" is safer than a firm claim you may have to retract.

**If the valve check finds nothing wrong**
Then the cause is still hydraulic, so look for a closed or partly closed valve elsewhere, a leak or unaccounted demand, and the Well 7 flow changes. Hold off on the Well 7 shutdown until you've seen evidence it matters.
