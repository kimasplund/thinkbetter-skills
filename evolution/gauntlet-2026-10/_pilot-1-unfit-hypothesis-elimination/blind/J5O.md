**Short answer:** The most likely cause is the Sept 4 Ardent Avenue repair. One or more of the four isolation valves was probably not fully reopened, or the main is still restricted. That would explain the pressure loss and the discoloration in Linden Heights. The data doesn't support Well 7 or the Hollis Park flushing as the main cause. This is a strong working hypothesis, not a confirmed finding, and one field check can settle it.

**Why Linden Heights and the Ardent Avenue work**
- 54 of 63 complaints (86%) are in Linden Heights. That is the zone fed by the 12-inch main that was isolated and reopened on Sept 4. Complaints began the next day, Sept 5.
- Pressure in Linden Heights is down about 10 to 12 psi from June (49 to 53 vs 60 to 64). Hollis Park is unchanged at 71. A real, zone-specific pressure loss points to a hydraulic change. A partly closed valve on the feed main is the simplest explanation.
- A restricted feed forces the zone to draw water differently. Velocities rise or flow direction changes in the old unlined cast iron mains. That scours the iron corrosion deposits off the pipe walls.
- The complaint chemistry fits this. Iron is 0.6 to 1.1 mg/L, against a normal level under 0.1. Manganese is only 0.02 to 0.03. That is a corrosion-scale signature, and the worst complaints are first thing in the morning, which is when demand shifts after the overnight lull.

**Why not Well 7**
- Well 7's iron is 0.04 mg/L, so it cannot be the source of 0.6 to 1.1 mg/L iron.
- Plant effluent manganese is unchanged at 0.010 to 0.014 mg/L, so the well's manganese is being removed. Complaint-sample manganese is low.
- Well 7 supplies about 15% of the whole system, yet the complaints are concentrated in one zone.
- A caveat: the well's higher manganese could build up in the system over time. That is a longer-term thing to monitor, not an explanation for this event.

**Why Hollis Park flushing is not the main cause**
- Complaints started Sept 5. Flushing ran Sept 9 to 12, after the problem began.
- Hollis Park has only 6 complaints and normal pressure. Sediment from there would not reach Linden Heights and leave Hollis Park mostly clean.
- The Hollis Park complaints are plausibly local. The lead service line replacements (about 10 a day) and the flushing both disturb pipes and can cause short-term discoloration. That is a small, separate effect.

**What to do now**
1. Send a crew to confirm that all four Ardent Avenue valves are fully open. Count turns against the valve records. This is the highest-value check.
2. Put pressure loggers on the Ardent main and in Linden Heights, and do a hydrant flow test. Check whether pressure recovers once the valves are corrected.
3. If a valve is found partly closed, open it fully. Then flush Linden Heights in a controlled way, starting nearest the source. Do not flush aggressively before the hydraulics are fixed.
4. Keep Well 7 online for now. Keep monitoring iron and manganese at the entry point and in the distribution system.
5. If the valves are all open and pressure is still low, look for other causes. These include a damaged or collapsed section of the repaired main, a leak, or a tank or booster issue at Calder Road.
6. Sample a few Linden Heights complaint sites again after the fix, to confirm iron drops.

**What to tell the council on Thursday**
- Brenmoor had 63 discolored-water complaints since Sept 5, against a normal 8 to 10 a month. 54 are in Linden Heights.
- The water is safe to drink in the sense that chlorine is normal and iron and manganese are low health-wise. The issue is mainly aesthetic: rust-colored water from iron deposits in old mains. Confirm this with your water quality manager and the regulator before you say it.
- The leading cause is a pressure and flow change in Linden Heights after the Sept 4 main repair. This stirred up deposits in the 1950s unlined cast iron mains.
- Well 7 and the Hollis Park flushing were checked and don't fit the data.
- The next steps are the valve and pressure verification, controlled flushing, and follow-up sampling. Give a date for when you will report back.
- Avoid saying the cause is certain until the field check is done. If the check is done before Thursday, report the result.

**Assumptions:** I'm assuming the Sept 15 pressure readings are comparable to June (same hydrants, similar demand), and that no other work happened in Linden Heights.
