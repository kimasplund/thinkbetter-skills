Yes, that's normal. Buttery flavor at day 10 of a cold lager fermentation is expected. Yeast produces diacetyl early in fermentation, and at 10°C they reabsorb and clean it up slowly. A diacetyl rest is the standard way to finish that job, so you're at the right point to do it.

**When to start:** Wait until fermentation is about 70-80% done, or until gravity is within roughly 4-6 points (about 1 °P) of your expected final gravity. If you're still well above that, let it ferment further at 10°C first. Don't wait for it to finish completely, because you want active yeast for the cleanup.

**Temperature:** Raise it to 16-18°C (60-65°F). Go up to about 18°C if you want it faster. Don't go much past that, or you risk off-flavors from the warmer ferment. You can raise it in one step, or let it climb 2-3°C per day if you prefer to be gentle.

**How long:** Hold it for 2-3 days. Many brewers go up to 4 days for a larger or higher-gravity lager. Fermentation will often pick up a little again, which is a good sign.

**How to check it's done:** Taste a sample after the rest. A quick forced-diacetyl test is more reliable than tasting alone. Warm a sample in a sealed jar to about 60°C for 15-30 minutes, then cool it and smell and taste it next to a plain sample. If the heated one isn't buttery or slick-tasting, you're clear. If it is, give it another day or two warm.

**After the rest:** Cool it down gradually, about 2-3°C per day, to near 0-2°C for lagering. Lager for several weeks. Longer cold storage also helps clean up any trace that remains.

**Things that can keep diacetyl lingering:**
- Cooling too early, before the yeast has reabsorbed it
- Too little healthy yeast, from under-pitching or old yeast
- Oxygen exposure or an infection (a persistent buttery or slick taste that doesn't fade after the rest can point to bacterial contamination)

If the butter note is still there after a proper rest and a few more days, check for those. For a normal, clean ferment, though, the rest should handle it.
