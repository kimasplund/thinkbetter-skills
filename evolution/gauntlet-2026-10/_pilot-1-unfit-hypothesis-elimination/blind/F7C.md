**Short answer:** the evidence points to a hydraulic change in Linden Heights that began with the September 4 Ardent Avenue repair, most likely a valve that was not fully reopened. It is not Well 7, and it is not the Hollis Park flushing. This is a strong inference, not yet a confirmed finding. One field check this week can confirm it.

**Why this fits**

- **Pressure.** Linden Heights reads 49 to 53 psi, down from 60 to 64 in June, a loss of about 10 to 12 psi. Hollis Park is unchanged at 71. A pressure loss in one zone, in the same area as 54 of the 63 complaints, is the strongest clue you have.
- **Location.** The Ardent Avenue main is the 12-inch line between the Calder Road tank and Linden Heights. A partly closed valve, or four valves reopened out of sequence, would restrict the main feeding that zone. That would explain the lower pressure. It would also push more flow through the old side mains, at different velocities and in different directions.
- **Pipe condition.** Linden Heights is mostly 1950s unlined cast iron. These mains carry iron scale and tubercles built up over decades. A change in flow direction or velocity can shear that scale off. The result is rusty water, worst in the early morning, when the flow pattern is changing from low night demand to the morning peak.
- **Timing.** Complaints started September 5, the day after the repair. Nothing else on your list happened that close to the start.

**What the other explanations don't fit**

| Explanation | Why it doesn't hold up |
|---|---|
| **Well 7** | The complaint samples are high in iron (0.6 to 1.1 mg/L) but only 0.02 to 0.03 mg/L in manganese. Well 7's problem is manganese, and its iron is only 0.04 mg/L. Plant effluent manganese is unchanged at 0.010 to 0.014 mg/L, so Well 7's manganese is being removed. Well 7 cannot supply the iron. Also, 15% of supply at 0.04 mg/L manganese, even unremoved, could not produce these numbers. |
| **Hollis Park flushing sending sediment to Linden Heights** | Flushing ran September 9 to 12, but Linden Heights complaints began September 5. The effect would have to come before its cause. Hollis Park pressure is unchanged, and it is a separate zone with only 6 complaints. |
| **Lead line replacement** | It is plausible for some of the 6 Hollis Park complaints, because shutting off and reconnecting services disturbs scale locally. It cannot explain 54 complaints in another zone or a pressure drop there. |
| **Treatment or chlorine problem** | Chlorine residual is normal and plant effluent is unchanged. |

**What is still open**

- I haven't ruled out that Well 7 changed the flow pattern. Adding 15% of supply from a different point can shift where water flows in the system. That could contribute to the Linden Heights pressure change. It would be a hydraulic effect, not a water-chemistry one.
- The pressure readings are one set of hydrant checks, from a single day. Confirm them with a logger before you rely on the numbers.
- The 6 Hollis Park complaints probably have a separate, local cause, such as the service line work. The 3 scattered complaints are within normal background.

**Checks to do before Thursday (a day or two of work)**

1. **Inspect all four valves isolated on September 4.** Confirm each is fully open and count the turns against the valve records. This is the most important check, and it is quick.
2. **Put pressure loggers in Linden Heights** and at the Calder Road tank outlet. Check tank level and fill/drain cycles against the SCADA history since September 4. Look for a step change on September 4.
3. **Run a hydrant flow test** on the Ardent Avenue main. Low flow with high pressure loss across the repair section would point to a restriction.
4. **Test a few complaint locations for turbidity and iron over a morning cycle**, and note the flow direction if you can.

If the valves turn out to be fully open, the restriction may be elsewhere, such as damage from the repair or a different valve. The pressure drop is still the thing to chase. Don't blame the chemistry until the hydraulics are cleared.

**What to tell the council**

- The discolored water is iron from old cast iron mains, not a contaminant. Iron at these levels is an aesthetic issue and a nuisance, not a health hazard. Check this with your state primacy agency and your health officials, and don't state it more strongly than they will.
- The pattern points to a hydraulic change in Linden Heights following the September 4 main repair. You have a specific check under way to confirm it.
- Well 7 does not fit the lab results, so there is no basis to take it offline. Taking it offline would remove 15% of supply and would probably not fix the complaints. Keep it running unless the checks change the picture.
- Hollis Park flushing is not the cause of the Linden Heights complaints, based on timing and on pressure data.
- Next steps are to correct any valve problem, then flush Linden Heights in a controlled, planned way, working from the source outward, and to track complaints against a baseline of 8 to 10 a month.
- Longer term, the unlined cast iron in Linden Heights will keep producing episodes like this whenever flow changes. Cleaning, relining or replacement of those mains, and pressure monitoring, belong in the capital plan.

I'd present this as "most likely cause identified, confirmation under way", and give the council a date for the confirmation results. That is more defensible than naming a cause you haven't verified, and more useful than saying you don't know.
