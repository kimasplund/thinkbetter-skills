I manage the employee giving program at Tolvane Engineering (3,800 staff). We relaunch our donation match on January 1 with a $1.2M annual budget, and finance has asked me to find the ways it could be abused first. Here's the design:

- Employees log a donation to any registered charity in our portal and upload the receipt. We pull the national charity register nightly, so every registered charity is eligible. OCR checks the amount, date and charity name on the receipt.
- We match 2:1, up to $5,000 in matches per employee per calendar year.
- If five or more employees donate to the same charity in a calendar month, each of their matches for that charity that month rises to 3:1 (still within the $5,000 cap).
- Receipts from online giving platforms such as Givelark are accepted, as are bank-transfer confirmations.
- Matches are paid straight to the charity in the quarter after a receipt is approved. HR manually reviews any single match over $2,000; everything else is approved automatically.
- Volunteer grants: every 20 hours an employee volunteers with a charity earns that charity $500, up to $2,500 a year. Hours are confirmed by a contact at the charity, whose email the employee enters.

Because the money only ever goes to registered charities, never to the employee, I'm not too worried about people pocketing it; I'm more worried about an embarrassing headline. Where would someone smart attack this, and what controls would you add?
