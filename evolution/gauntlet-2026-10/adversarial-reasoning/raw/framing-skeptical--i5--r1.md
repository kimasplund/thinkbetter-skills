Your doubts are justified. As described, this setup can't deliver what your bylaws require, and on a contentious $1.9M vote a few of these problems would be easy to exploit. I wouldn't send links Monday unless the first four items below are fixed. That doesn't mean going back to paper for good. These are mostly fixable, but not by volunteers in a weekend, so be ready to delay a week or to run the roof assessment on paper while the board election goes online.

## The serious problems

**1. The ballot is not secret.**
Each submission is logged with the token ID and the choices. Every token belongs to one unit, and Varrow emailed it, so Varrow can link every unit to its vote. So can anyone who gets the log or the CSV. Your bylaws require a secret ballot, so this alone could make the result challengeable. The practical risk is worse: on a roof assessment that people are angry about, members will know their neighbours' votes are visible to the management company, and some won't vote honestly or at all.
Fix: confirm with the vendor that votes are stored separately from voter identity. The log should show who voted, never what they chose. If the system can't do that, it can't meet your bylaws.

**2. The lost-link process is an open door.**
Someone calls, gives a unit number and the leaseholder's full name, and a fresh link goes to any address they name. Both facts are easy to find, because names are on mailboxes, in the directory and in old minutes. Anyone motivated can take over a unit's vote. Here is how it plays out:
- An attacker, or an overeager neighbour, calls and gets a new link sent to their own address.
- They vote, and the real member's original link is dead.
- Because the last submission counts, they can also overwrite a vote the member already cast.
- The member finds out only when their own link stops working, if they notice at all. Nobody is told.
Fix: send replacement links only to the address on file, or verify through a call back to the phone number on file or in person at the office. Notify the original address whenever a link is reissued. Keep a list of every reissue for the committee to review. The attack leaves no trace at all today.

**3. Proxies are approved by one person with no checks.**
A scanned PDF goes in, the office manager approves it, and that unit's ballot moves to someone else's page. Nothing in your description checks that the signature is genuine, that the member actually granted it, or how many proxies one person can hold. Forged or pressured proxies are the cheapest way to move a close vote. Because the approver is a Varrow employee, you also have no independent check on the person doing the approving.
Fix: have two people approve each proxy, one from the committee and one from Varrow. Confirm each with the grantor by phone. Cap proxies per holder if your bylaws allow it. Publish the proxy list to the committee before voting closes.

**4. One party can see everything and change everything, and it isn't neutral.**
Varrow runs the system, sees the logs, approves proxies, reissues links, exports the results and emails you the CSV. You then certify what they send you. Nobody independent can check the count. Varrow also administers the building where a $1.9M roof contract will be awarded. I'm not saying they would cheat. But if the result is disputed, you will have no way to prove they didn't. Dorota Weiss makes this worse. She is a candidate with full admin rights, and for an election she should have none. Even if she never touches anything, the appearance alone will be used against the result. She should step back from admin access until the election is certified, and a non-candidate should take over as liaison.
Fix: a non-candidate committee member should receive the export directly from the system or at least verify it. Retain the raw log so a second person can recount it. Reduce admin access to the minimum and record who holds it.

## Other problems

**5. The live dashboard influences the vote.**
Running totals for each question are visible to the board and the office manager while voting is open. Board members can then target reminder calls at people likely to vote their way, or hold back when they're ahead. Turnout by floor can also show how particular groups are voting if a floor is small. Use turnout counts only, with no running totals on the questions. Better still, call only people who haven't voted without showing anything about how others voted.

**6. The links themselves are fragile.**
A token in a URL, emailed for 10 days, will be forwarded, sit in shared inboxes, be opened by mail scanners and end up in browser history and server logs. The email address on file may be wrong, belong to a former occupant, or be shared by a couple who are on opposite sides of the roof question. Members without email, or who aren't comfortable online, get left out. Offer a paper or in-office option for them, and send a reminder asking members to confirm their address before Monday.

## Smaller things worth checking

- **Quorum:** 50% of units online may be harder or easier to reach than on paper. Decide in advance what happens if turnout is low, and whether the assessment needs more than a simple majority under your bylaws.
- **Close time:** state it with a timezone. A few minutes either way will become an argument if the margin is thin.
- **Joint leaseholders:** if two names are on a unit, who can vote, and what if they submit different choices? The last submission wins, which means one of them can overwrite the other.
- **Changing votes:** this is fine for convenience, but combined with problem 2 it means any stolen link can erase a legitimate vote. Send a confirmation email to the address on file for every submission and every change.
- **No receipt:** a voter can't confirm their vote was recorded, and you can't prove it either. Give each voter a confirmation code and publish a list of codes after close.
- **Disputes:** write down now who can challenge the result, how, and what evidence will be kept.
