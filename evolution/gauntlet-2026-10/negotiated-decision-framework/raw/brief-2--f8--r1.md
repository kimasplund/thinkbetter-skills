**Short version:** don't try to get "everyone signed up" to the plan as drafted. Go to the CEO on Friday with a plan that is conditionally agreed. Each group that can stop it should have said what it needs, and the plan should be built around those needs. At the moment the surgeons are the only group you know is on board, and they are the one group that doesn't limit your capacity. Anaesthesia, sterile processing, physio and ward beds are the real limits, and the nurses are your biggest risk. The proposal has gaps you should fix before anyone else finds them.

## What the numbers say

I've assumed about 21 weeks from November to March 31, minus 2 to 3 weeks lost to the holidays and cancellations. That leaves about 18 to 19 working weeks. Hip and knee cases take about 2 to 2.5 hours of room time, including turnover. Replace these with your own figures.

- To reach 450 you need about **24 extra cases a week**.
- Extending two rooms to 7:30pm Monday to Thursday probably adds one to two cases per room per evening, so 8 to 14 a week.
- A Saturday list in both rooms adds about 6 to 8 a week.
- That comes to roughly **14 to 22 a week, or 260 to 410 over the period**. It is probably short of 450 even if everything goes well, and "if we don't lose many days" is doing a lot of work.

Check this against your own list lengths before Friday. If it holds, you will want a third lever. Options are a Friday-evening list, a weekend list in a third room, or asking the region to confirm in writing how it will treat a near miss (for example, funding paid per case rather than all or nothing). I would ask the region that question this week whatever the answer.

The bed arithmetic is the bigger problem. 450 patients at two nights each is about 900 extra bed-nights, or around 6 beds a day over the period. A ward already at 95% can't absorb that. Saturday cases also mean Sunday and Monday discharges, and your physios work only Monday to Friday. A Saturday joint patient who can't be walked safely until Monday stays an extra night. This lowers throughput and uses up more beds.

## Who can stop this, and what each group needs

**Surgeons (five, paid per case).** They want volume and they are already in favour, so they are not the problem. The risk is how they behave once the plan is running. Per-case pay can push them toward easy primaries and away from complex revisions, and it can cause friction over who gets the premium Saturday slots. Agree the allocation rule up front, such as fair sharing of lists and a case-mix expectation. Ask them to commit to being present for all of their sessions. It costs them nothing and you need it.

**OR nurses and the union local.** This is the most important relationship. They have said they will "consider" voluntary shifts. That is a signal they are leaving themselves room, not a commitment. Their underlying interests are probably these:
- Last year you suspended the specialty premium, so there is a trust deficit. Asking for goodwill now, when you want something, will be remembered at the April table.
- Voluntary extra shifts at time-and-a-half are a good deal for individuals, but they can look like a bad precedent for the union ahead of bargaining.
- Fatigue and safety on 12-hour days and Saturdays are real concerns.

What I would do:
1. Meet the local leadership this week, before the CEO meeting. Ask them directly what would get a yes, and how many shifts per week they could fill.
2. Offer something that doesn't depend on the April outcome: a fixed premium for these shifts, paid from the one-time funding, written up as a short letter of understanding that sets no precedent. Consider whether restoring the specialty premium, even temporarily, is on the table. That is a CEO decision, so find out now whether you have any room.
3. Keep this agreement out of the bargaining table. Say in writing that nothing agreed here prejudges April.
4. Offer predictable, advance-posted rosters, an agreed cap on consecutive extended shifts, and a way to stand down without penalty.

If the union won't sign, the plan has no fallback. Agency or casual staff are the backup, so cost that now.

**Anaesthesia (Corvane group).** Don't treat "contract renewed in June for three years" as settled. That contract covers the current scope. Eight anaesthetists who already cover labour and delivery and overnight call will not automatically add four evening lists and two Saturday lists. Ask them how many extra sessions per week they can staff, at what rate, and whether it is within the contract or needs an amendment. This is probably your hardest constraint, and also the one you have the least leverage over, because they have a three-year contract and other obligations. A group whose evening and weekend hours increase will want a fair per-session rate and a guarantee that obstetrics coverage is protected. Offer both.

**Sterile Processing (reports to Support Services, 7am to 6pm weekdays).** Evening lists need instruments. Sets used from about 3pm onward cannot be reprocessed after 6pm if nobody is there. Saturday lists have no reprocessing at all. Their manager is not in your reporting line, so this needs the Support Services director, and probably the CEO, to agree. Options include extending SPD to 9pm Monday to Thursday, a Saturday shift, or buying more loaner sets and implant trays so the lists run on fewer reprocessing cycles. The loaner-set route may be the cheapest. Costing it is part of the plan.

**Physiotherapy (Monday to Friday).** They need either weekend cover, or a different discharge pathway for Saturday cases (for example, day-of-surgery mobilisation by Saturday-shift physio, or Sunday cover). A modest weekend rota, funded from the money, is probably enough. Ask them what they need.

**Ward and bed management.** At 95% you need a deliberate answer: a temporary extra-capacity area, a step-down or discharge lounge, earlier discharge targets, or moving some day-surgery or low-acuity cases elsewhere. Involve the nursing leader for the ward early, since they will bear the cost and are often missing from these conversations.

**CEO and the region.** The CEO wants a credible plan and no surprises. The region wants volume. Both of them will want to know what happens on April 1, when the money is gone and the staff and patients are still there.

## Where people really agree

Everyone in this list wants the wait to come down, and everyone is paid or rewarded in some way by this plan. Nobody disagrees about the goal. Most of the disagreements are about who bears the extra hours and who pays. That means they are mostly solvable with money and scheduling, not with persuasion. Open every conversation with the shared point that 14 months is unacceptable and the funding is a one-time chance. Then ask each group what it would take.

## What the plan should look like

1. **Fund the people before you fund the volume.** Break the $2.1M into surgeon fees, nursing premium shifts, anaesthesia sessions, SPD hours, physio and ward cover, and loaner instruments. If the sum of what the groups need is more than the funding, you will know that now and not in January.
2. **Start with a pilot, not the whole schedule.** Run one extended room for 3 to 4 weeks from November. Measure cases per week, overruns, cancellations, discharge delays and staff willingness. Then scale to the second room and Saturdays. This tests the weakest links cheaply and gives the union and anaesthesia a chance to see it works before they commit to more.
3. **Phase the ramp.** Better to plan for 450 with a realistic ramp than to assume full output from day one. Build in a buffer for the holidays and flu season.
4. **Name a single accountable owner** for each constraint (anaesthesia, SPD, nursing, physio, beds) and a weekly 30-minute huddle with all of them through March.
5. **Set stop and adjust triggers.** For example, if cancellations exceed a set rate, or staff fill rate falls below a set level, you review. Agree these with the groups in advance so that nobody feels the rules changed.
6. **Plan for April 1.** Say what happens to the extra shifts and sessions when funding ends, so the union does not read this as a permanent change in hours, and so the surgeons don't expect the volume to continue.
7. **Be honest about the target.** If the capacity numbers say 380, tell the CEO that, along with what it would take to reach 450 and what the region will do if you fall short.

## What to bring to the CEO on Friday

Don't bring a plan with a claim that everyone has agreed. Bring:
- the plan, with the weekly capacity numbers and the gap to 450 stated plainly;
- a one-line status for each group: *agreed*, *agreed if X*, or *not yet asked*;
- the decisions you need from the CEO: authority over Support Services hours, a position on the nurses' premium, the funding split, and the region's treatment of a shortfall;
- the pilot as the start date, so you can begin in November without committing everyone to the whole schedule.

## Before Friday, in order

1. Today: check the capacity maths with your own case times and the real calendar.
2. Tomorrow: separate meetings with the union local leaders and the Corvane group lead. These are your two biggest unknowns.
3. Then: the SPD manager together with the Support Services director, the head physio, and the ward manager.
4. Last: confirm the surgeons' allocation rules.
5. Write the CEO paper on the basis of what you learn, with the gaps showing.

A plan that says "here are the five conditions, here is who has agreed to what, and here is the one decision I need from you" is much easier for a CEO to back than one that says "everyone is on board" and then falls apart in week three.
