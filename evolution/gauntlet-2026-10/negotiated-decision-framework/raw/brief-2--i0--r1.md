The first thing to change is the goal. You won't have everyone signed up by Friday, and you shouldn't try to show that. Take the CEO a plan that is feasible on the numbers, shows who has agreed and who hasn't, and says what you still need from the CEO. A plan that claims buy-in nobody has given will fail in November, and it will cost you credibility when it does.

**1. The plan has gaps that matter more than surgeon enthusiasm**

My assumptions are about 20 weeks from November to March 31, and 450 cases on top of your current volume.

- **Volume.** 450 extra cases over about 20 weeks is roughly 22 a week. Two rooms on four extended weekday lists plus two Saturday lists is about 10 added list-sessions a week. That needs about 2 joints per session, and you'd have to hold that pace through holidays, cancellations and sick calls. Check it against your real throughput. Joint cases often run 90 to 120 minutes including turnover, so a 3.5-hour extension may fit 2 cases, and a full Saturday list 3 to 4. "If we don't lose many days" is carrying a lot of weight. Build a version with 15 to 20% slippage and see what you actually reach.
- **Sterile Processing.** They run 7am to 6pm on weekdays and don't run Saturdays. Your extended lists run past their hours, and Saturday lists have no reprocessing at all. Instrument sets are the usual hidden limit on joints. Either Support Services extends SPD hours (a different budget and a different manager, which makes this a negotiation in its own right), or you buy enough loaner or duplicate sets to run a day without reprocessing. This is probably your hardest constraint, and nobody on your list of stakeholders owns it yet.
- **Beds and physio.** The ward is at about 95% occupancy, and every patient stays two nights. Adding about 22 joints a week means roughly 44 more bed-nights a week, which the ward can't absorb. Saturday surgery also means Sunday and Monday discharges, and physio works Monday to Friday, so those patients may not be walking safely until Monday. That adds a night or two each. Either you need weekend physio, a discharge pathway that doesn't depend on it, or more beds. Look at same-day or next-day discharge for suitable patients, a surgical short-stay area, or off-site or home-based recovery. Without that, extra OR time produces a queue in the recovery area and cancellations on the day.
- **Anaesthesia.** The Corvane contract is settled, but that doesn't mean they have capacity. Eight anaesthetists also cover labour and delivery and overnight call, and evening and Saturday lists add to that load. Nothing in your note says they have agreed to this work. Their contract was renewed in June and probably doesn't cover extra sessions, so you'd be negotiating an add-on. Ask them directly what they would need to staff about 10 extra sessions a week and what it costs.
- **Nursing.** "Will consider" voluntary shifts is not agreement. Ten extra sessions a week need scrubs, circulating nurses, recovery nurses and ward staff, which is a lot of shifts. The union is bargaining in April, and you suspended the specialty premium last year, so goodwill is limited. They may see this as a chance to get the premium back, or as one more request when the hospital cut a benefit. Both are reasonable. Time-and-a-half is a floor, not an offer that will make this work on its own.
- **Money.** $2.1M over 450 cases is about $4,700 a case. Add up the real costs of the extra sessions: surgeon per-case fees, anaesthesia, nursing premiums, SPD hours or extra sets, supplies and implants, beds, and physio. If the full cost is above $2.1M you'd be taking on a deficit. Also check whether the funding pays per case completed or as a lump sum, whether cases have to be additional to baseline volume (and how baseline is measured), and whether any funding is lost if you miss 450.

**3. How to get alignment**

1. **Start with the bottleneck owners, this week.** Meet SPD's manager and the Support Services director, the Corvane lead, the ward manager and the physio lead, one on one and short. Ask each: what would it take, what does it cost, and what is the earliest you could start? Do this before you ask anyone to sign on to a plan.
2. **Treat the nurses as a real negotiation.** Meet the union local and nursing leadership together. Be honest about the constraint (one-time money, a deadline) and ask what would make the shifts attractive. Likely items are a guaranteed minimum number of shifts, advance notice, shift bidding by seniority, a time-limited premium, or a commitment on the April bargaining agenda. Be careful about promises that tie into the April bargaining, because the two will get linked whatever you say. Involve HR and labour relations before you offer anything.
3. **Be careful with the surgeons.** They're paid per case, so they have an incentive that the others don't. Ask them for firm session commitments, case-selection rules so that the cases are appropriate and the list is predictable, and their help in persuading colleagues and the anaesthetists. Don't let their enthusiasm stand in for sign-off from others.
4. **Give a choice about how to meet the target.** Different groups can accept different parts. Surgeons and anaesthesia might do more in the existing hours (fuller lists, faster turnover, a dedicated joint room). Extended days and Saturdays might be added only as support is confirmed. This often brings you closer to a realistic number with less friction.
5. **Share one set of numbers.** Put capacity, cost and risk in a one-page model that everyone has seen. People agree more readily to something they can check.

**4. What the plan should look like for the CEO on Friday**

- **A recommendation with phases.** For example, start in November with what is already confirmed, such as surgeon sessions and the weekday extension if SPD and nursing can support it, and add Saturdays when SPD, anaesthesia and physio are confirmed. Set checkpoints, for example the end of November and the end of December, for deciding whether to continue.
- **A realistic target.** Say what you expect (for example 350 to 450 depending on which supports are confirmed), the main assumptions, and what would raise or lower it. If 450 is not reachable at a cost the hospital can bear, say that and talk to the region about partial funding. It's better to ask now than in March.
- **A cost estimate** against the $2.1M, with what is firm and what is a guess.
- **A stakeholder status page** that is honest: agreed, in discussion, not yet asked.
- **Specific asks of the CEO.** These likely include authority to negotiate with Support Services about SPD hours, someone senior to talk with Corvane and the union, bed or discharge flexibility, and a decision on the specialty premium if that becomes part of the nursing solution.
- **Risks and triggers.** Staff fatigue, cancellations, infection-control and quality issues from long days with fewer sterile turnovers, and what you will do if you fall behind (cut back, add lists, ask the region to adjust).
- **A patient-safety and quality plan.** Surgeon, nurse and anaesthetist fatigue on late days, and the lack of weekend physio, are risks the CEO will want to hear you have thought about.
