I'd reframe the goal slightly. A plan whose aim is "get this approved" will look like salesmanship to the 4-3 minority and to members. A plan whose aim is "put a fixed, improved deal in front of members, with honest information, and let them decide" is more likely to reach two-thirds. It's also safer for you personally. Here is how I'd do it.

**1. Close the hard blockers before you campaign. These could sink the deal or the co-op after it passes.**

- **The Vantongeren lease.** It is personal to the co-op, and your upper runs are on that land. If Alderline operates the hill, that may breach the lease or need the family's consent. If you lose the upper runs, the quad chair and snowmaking investment is worth much less. Talk to the Vantongerens now, before the AGM, and get written consent to the arrangement. Ideally also get a longer term or an option to renew, since a 20-year deal sits on a lease you should check the term of. Have a lawyer read the lease first. I'm assuming it allows this with consent and doesn't forbid it outright, so please verify that.
- **The 1987 debenture holders (about 140 of 410 votes).** Lifetime passes are probably contractual rights, not goodwill. Moving them to Alderline's pass product may need each holder's consent, or at least a legal opinion. Even if you can do it, a third of your voters will read it as having their deal taken away. Two-thirds of 410 is about 274 votes if the bylaws count all members, so the debenture holders alone can block the deal. Ask Alderline to honour the lifetime passes for the holders' lives, or to offer something they can actually accept, and cost that.
- **Volunteer patrol and ski school (about 60 people).** You can't open without certified patrollers. A corporate operator changes the picture for them: liability and insurance, who directs them, and whether unpaid people doing work Alderline profits from is acceptable (check labour and volunteer rules with counsel). Their motivation is partly "it's our club". If they walk, the hill doesn't open and the deal fails on its own terms. Meet them early, get their questions on the table, and ask Alderline to commit in writing to a patrol and ski school plan: keep the volunteer model with family passes, or pay for the roles, with named funding.
- **Dariusz and the guarantees.** He and two other directors personally guaranteed the $600k line. That's a real conflict of interest, and it makes his advocacy easy to discredit. Do three things. First, have the guarantors disclose it openly in the member materials. Second, ask Alderline to refinance or retire the line, or have the co-op replace the guarantees, as part of the deal. If it does, say so, because it's a real benefit. Third, consider having the guarantors step back from leading the pitch. Keep the "no" directors in the process too.

**2. What to ask Alderline to change before March**

*Protect the co-op's assets and exit*
- Capital commitments with dates and security: the $4.2M, what's built by when, and a remedy if they miss it (termination, reversion, or a bond or guarantee). A promise isn't enough.
- A real way out: termination for missed milestones, insolvency, change of control, or if Alderline sells the agreement. Ask about step-in rights, and how the hill and the new equipment revert at the end of the term. Say who owns the snowmaking and the quad chair.
- Maintenance and condition standards for the lifts and lodge the co-op still owns, plus the co-op's inspection rights, and who pays for major repairs.
- Co-op governance: the board keeps a seat or consultation rights on pricing, hours, events and community programming. This answers the "club feel" worry in a concrete way.

*Money*
- A guaranteed minimum annual fee, not only a share of profit that thin snow could reduce to nothing. Ask for indexation as well. Ask what the fee has to cover: insurance, inspections, the Vantongeren rent, debt service. Model the co-op's cash in a bad winter, because that's the case the deal needs to survive.
- Clarity on who pays the lease rent, insurance and inspections, and on liability allocation.

*People*
- Employment: "keep all nine" should be a contractual term with a duration, not a statement of intent. Ask for pay and conditions protections for at least a defined period.
- The volunteer plan and the debenture-holder pass terms above.
- Fair member passes: you say the current prices are cheap. So test whether Alderline's pass is a fair deal for ordinary members, with a price cap or a multi-year freeze for members. Being honest that prices will rise makes the pitch more credible.

*Term*
- 20 years is long for a hill that has lost money three years in five, and in which the key counterparty is a family lease. Ask for a shorter initial term with renewal, or a review point (for example year 5 to 7) with exit if the commitments aren't met.

**3. How to build support**

- **Get independent advice.** A lawyer who has done co-op or resort operating agreements, and ideally someone to model the finances. Alderline's offer won't be neutral. Members will trust the result more if it didn't come from the board or from Alderline.
- **Publish the full picture early:** a plain-language summary, the key terms, the status quo alternative (what happens if you stay independent, honestly costed), and the risks. Include the downside. Hiding it will hurt you more than it helps.
- **Compare against real alternatives.** At the moment it's "Alderline vs. doing nothing". Check whether there are other options: a smaller deal, another operator, a grant or community fundraising for snowmaking, a member bond issue, or a shorter pilot. Even if you end up with Alderline, you can show members you looked, and it strengthens your negotiating position.
- **Hold several sessions** (town halls, a Q&A with Alderline present, a separate meeting for debenture holders, one for volunteers). Let the three dissenting directors present their concerns in the materials. A minority report beats a rumour campaign.
- **Turn "club feel" concerns into commitments.** List what members mean by it (local events, volunteer culture, community pricing, a say in decisions), and see which can be written into the agreement. Members who vote no are often voting on a worry that a clause could fix.
- **Count the vote early.** You need roughly two-thirds. Find out who is in favour, against or unsure, particularly among debenture holders and volunteers. Check the bylaws on quorum, proxies and whether it's two-thirds of votes cast or of all members. Don't start the campaign until you know.
- **Plan for the vote failing.** If it fails, what's next? A credible plan B (and a no-fault outcome) lowers the temperature, and it helps you negotiate.

**4. Your role**

You work for the co-op and report to the board, so asking you to bring a plan is reasonable. But you will be trusted most if you present yourself as running a fair process rather than as the campaign manager. Take care that the materials are accurate and balanced, and say plainly where you don't know. If you can't resolve the lease, the lifetime passes or the volunteer question by March, tell the board, because an AGM vote that happens before those are settled is a risk. Consider asking for a later vote or a conditional approval (approval only if the lease consent and key amendments are in place).

**A suggested order of work**
1. This week: lawyer reviews the lease, debenture terms and bylaws. Call the Vantongerens.
2. Next few weeks: send Alderline a written list of required changes (section 2), with the "must-haves" (lease consent, debenture passes, guarantee release, capital security, exit rights, minimum fee) separated from the "nice-to-haves".
3. In parallel: meet the volunteers and the debenture holders, and start the vote count.
4. Winter: publish the materials, hold the sessions, and have independent advice available.
5. Before the AGM: decide whether the deal is ready. If not, ask for more time rather than push a weak deal.

I've made assumptions about the lease, the debenture terms and the bylaws, and I haven't seen the Alderline offer. Please check each with your lawyer before you rely on this.
