You probably can't get everyone to sign up by Friday, and I wouldn't promise the CEO that. You can bring a plan with firm commitments from the groups that are easy to win, named asks for the groups that aren't, and a fallback if 450 turns out to be out of reach. **The plan has more gaps than your list suggests**

Several groups you listed as fine or neutral are actually where this plan could fail.

- **Sterile Processing closes at 6pm on weekdays and doesn't run Saturdays.** Nothing you've described lets a 7:30pm finish or a Saturday list happen without them. They report to Support Services, not to you, so their manager needs to be at the table this week.
- **Anaesthesia isn't settled.** The June renewal fixed the contract, but check whether it covers elective evening and Saturday lists. Eight anaesthetists who also cover labour and delivery and overnight call have limited slack. Two extra rooms on Saturdays plus four evenings a week is a lot of coverage. Ask whether they'd take this on as a defined extra-session arrangement, and at what rate.
- **The ward is the hidden bottleneck.** At 95% occupancy, 450 extra patients with a two-night stay add roughly 900 bed-nights over about 21 weeks. That's about 6 more occupied beds on an average day, on a ward with almost none free. Weekends make it worse. A Thursday-evening or Saturday patient can't be walked safely by physio on the weekend, so they stay until Monday. That's a longer stay, and it ties up the beds you need most.
- **The volume math is tight.** Mon-Thu extensions typically add about one joint case per room per day. Saturdays add perhaps two to three per room. That's roughly 12 to 16 extra cases a week. From November to March 31, minus the holiday period, you have about 19 to 20 usable weeks, so that's only about 230 to 320 cases. Reaching 450 needs about 22 to 24 extra cases a week. Check this against your actual turnover times before Friday. If it holds, you'll need a third room on some days, more Saturday cases per room, or a lower target.

**The nurses**

Last year's suspension of the OR specialty premium is why "consider" is a soft answer. Nurses won't forget it, and the request comes just before an April contract negotiation. Voluntary extra shifts at time-and-a-half is the contractual minimum, not an incentive.

- Talk to the union local and your labour relations lead before Friday. Ask what would make volunteers come forward. Don't guess.
- Consider options beyond the baseline rate. These could include a project premium for the extra sessions, guaranteed shift scheduling, or a clear commitment that these shifts won't be mandatory. Whatever you offer must be consistent with the collective agreement and agreed with labour relations. Offering a side deal that undercuts the April talks would cause more harm than good.
- Say plainly that this is voluntary, one-time and separate from the April bargaining. Don't let it become a bargaining chip, and don't let the nurses feel it will be used against them.
- If the specialty premium can be restored, even only for the project, it would do more to rebuild trust than any amount of persuasion. That's the CEO's call, so put it in front of the CEO as a recommendation.

**What to take to the CEO on Friday**

1. A staffed schedule showing which sessions run, who covers them (surgeon, anaesthetist, nurses, sterile processing, ward) and which are confirmed versus pending.
2. A cost against the $2.1M. Include nurse premium pay, anaesthesia sessions, sterile processing hours, weekend physio and any extra beds. If the incremental cost is close to $2.1M, you need to know that now.
3. Funding terms from the region. Is it paid per case or on reaching the full 450? What counts as "extra" against baseline? Are partial results funded? Are ward and rehab costs eligible? If funding depends on all 450, your risk is much higher.
4. A tiered target. For example: a committed floor you're confident of, a target, and a stretch level. Agree the triggers for adding a third room or ending early.
5. A ramp. Start with the sessions that need the fewest new commitments. Weekday evenings are quicker to set up than Saturdays. Add Saturdays when staff and sterile processing are confirmed. Review after four weeks and drop or adjust whatever is not working.
6. Named decisions only the CEO can make: the premium question, Support Services cooperation, and any discussion with Corvane at executive level.
