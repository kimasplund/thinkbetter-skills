Your proposal is a reasonable core, but as written it only describes what the OR and the surgeons do. Four other groups sit in the critical path (anaesthesia, Sterile Processing, the ward and physio, and the nurses), and none of them has agreed to anything yet. I'd also change what you promise the CEO on Friday. "Everyone has signed up" is unlikely to be true by then, and I think it's the wrong bar. A better bar is a plan where every party's conditions are known, priced and owned, with a date by which you go or no-go.

**1. Check the 450 first**

Between a November start and March 31 you have about 21 weeks. Take out the holiday weeks and a few lost days and you have roughly 18 to 19 working weeks. 450 cases needs about 24 extra cases a week.

- Saturday lists in two rooms: about 3 to 4 joints per room, so 6 to 8 a week.
- Mon to Thu extension in two rooms: an evening to 7:30pm probably gives 1 to 1.5 extra cases per room, so 8 to 12 a week.

That's about 14 to 20 a week, or roughly 260 to 380 cases. Your plan is probably 20 to 40 percent short, not "roughly 450 if we don't lose many days." Please rerun it with your real case times and turnover. If I'm right, you have three options:

- Add a third room on Saturdays.
- Start earlier in the month or add a Friday evening.
- Ask the region now whether the $2.1M can be paid pro rata, or whether it's all-or-nothing at 450.

Find this out before Friday. It changes everything else.

**3. Where the plan is weakest**

- **Anaesthesia.** The June contract being "settled" is a trap. A three-year deal means no leverage either way, and eight people who already cover labour and delivery and overnight call may have no spare capacity for two extra rooms in the evenings and on Saturdays. Check whether the contract covers extra sessions and at what rate. Expect to need a separate sessional agreement. This is probably your tightest limit, so talk to Corvane first.
- **Sterile Processing.** They stop at 6pm on weekdays and don't run weekends. Evening cases and Saturday lists need reprocessing of instruments that are in short supply for joints. Options include extending hours (which needs Support Services to agree and fund it), or buying or renting extra instrument sets so you don't depend on same-day turnaround. The one-time money may suit the second option well.
- **Beds.** 450 extra patients at two nights each is about 900 bed-nights, or roughly 6 more occupied beds a day over the period. A ward at 95% has very little room for that. You need either extra beds, a surge or overflow area, or a faster discharge pathway. Without one, the OR extension just moves the queue onto the ward and you cancel cases for lack of a bed.
- **Physio.** Monday to Friday only. Patients operated on Saturday need first mobilisation on Sunday, and Thursday evening cases can be stuck waiting. Ask for Saturday and Sunday physio cover for the extra period, even a small team or a physio assistant model.
- **Nurses.** You need more than OR nurses. PACU, ward and porters are in the chain too. Voluntary extra shifts at time-and-a-half work only if enough people volunteer. If they don't, the lists get cancelled.

**4. Handling the union**

This is the part most likely to go wrong. The premium suspension is fresh, April bargaining is coming, and the extra shifts end around the time bargaining starts. That cuts both ways: the union has leverage, and you have an interest in not starting a dispute in the spring.

- Approach them formally, through the local's leadership and HR, and not shift by shift. Going around them to individual nurses will poison April.
- Ask them what would make voluntary shifts attractive. Time-and-a-half is the floor. Possibilities include guaranteed shift-swap rights, protected days off afterwards, or a one-time bonus.
- Do not make promises about the premium or any contract term in this plan. Say plainly that the extra shifts are separate from bargaining, and put that in writing. Making side deals now will come back in April.
- Consider a joint staffing committee with a short agenda: how many shifts, how they're allocated, how fatigue is managed.

**5. Handling the surgeons**

They're your supporters, which makes them easy to take for granted. Three things:

- Pay per case is a good incentive for volume, and a risk to case mix. The region is paying for joints, so you can ask them to keep to the normal mix and not just the quickest cases.
- Ask them to commit to the schedule: no late arrivals and no last-minute cancellations. Their enthusiasm needs to turn into reliability.
- Have them speak to the nurses and anaesthetists as peers. A surgeon asking a colleague for help gets further than a manager's email.

**6. What to take to the CEO on Friday**

Don't claim everyone has signed. Take:

1. **The plan**, with the corrected capacity maths and the options for closing any gap.
2. **A short table** of each group, what they've said, what they need and who owns the conversation.
3. **A budget** showing how the $2.1M covers extra staffing, instrument sets, anaesthesia sessions, weekend physio and bed capacity. If the money doesn't cover them, say so.
4. **A go/no-go date**, say 1 December (or earlier if you can start sooner). By then you need written confirmation from Corvane, Sterile Processing and the union local, and a bed plan. If those aren't in, you go back to the CEO and the region with a smaller target.
5. **A fallback.** A smaller number such as 300 with a partial payment is better than a failed 450 and a clawback. Ask the region what happens if you miss.
6. **Exit signals.** Agree in advance what you'd see in the first four to six weeks (cancellation rate, volunteer fill rate, bed occupancy) that would trigger a review.
