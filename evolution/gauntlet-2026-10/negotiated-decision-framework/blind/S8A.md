## Where the plan breaks (the pathway view)

Assumptions: about 4.5 months of delivery (November to March 31), roughly 18 to 20 working weeks, and a case mix where a typical list is 3 to 4 joints per room. Check these against your real numbers.

- **Sterile Processing runs 7am to 6pm on weekdays.** Two ORs running to 7:30pm generate instrument sets after SP closes. Saturday lists have no reprocessing at all. Joint trays are heavy, and you need enough sets to turn rooms and to cover the evening. This is the most likely hidden blocker, and SP sits under Support Services, not under you. Their manager has not been named as a stakeholder.
- **Ward capacity.** The ortho ward is at about 95% already. 450 extra cases at a two-night stay is about 900 extra bed-nights, roughly 6 to 7 extra occupied beds on average over the period, and more at peak because Saturday and Thursday-evening cases cluster. With 95% occupancy you do not have them. Without beds the OR extension just produces cancellations.
- **Physio is Monday to Friday.** A Thursday-evening or Saturday patient will not be walked safely by the usual day. Discharge slips from two nights to three or four, which worsens the bed problem above. You need weekend or extended physio, or a different discharge pathway for those patients.
- **Anaesthesia.** The Corvane contract is renewed, but a renewed contract is not the same as spare capacity. The same eight people cover labour and delivery and overnight call. Extended lists and Saturdays consume the same pool, and post-evening-list fatigue affects overnight cover. Nothing in your note says they have agreed to staff this. Also check whether the contract obliges them to provide extra sessions, or whether it needs a funded amendment.
- **Nursing.** "Will consider" is not a commitment. Context matters: you suspended the OR specialty premium during last year's freeze, and bargaining starts in April. Nurses may read voluntary extra shifts as a test of goodwill, and a bad experience now will carry into bargaining. Also, time-and-a-half voluntary shifts depend on fatigue and sustainability. You need roughly 2 rooms x several roles (circulating, scrub, recovery, plus ward and sterile staff), across about 5 extended days per week.
- **Surgeons.** Keen, and paid per case, which is a feature and a risk. Per-case pay rewards volume, so check that case selection, appropriate indications and quality hold up, and that the surgeons' enthusiasm does not get quietly read by others as "they profit while we absorb the load."
- **The funding is one-time.** It buys extra cases but not a permanent reduction in a 14-month wait. If the extra cases do not come with a plan to stop the queue rebuilding, you will be back here next year. Say that explicitly to the CEO.

## How to get alignment (and what "aligned" should mean)

Treat each group as having something concrete to gain, something to lose, and something it controls. Your job is to find a package each can accept, not to collect enthusiasm.

| Group | What they control | What they need to say yes | Ask |
|---|---|---|---|
| Surgeons | Volume, list fill, case selection | Reliable lists, no cancellations, and reasonable hours | Commit to booking and prep discipline; accept a quality and cancellation audit; consider flexing days to protect recovery |
| OR nurses and union local | Staffing the extra hours | Fair pay, voluntary only, predictable rosters, and no precedent that harms April bargaining | Sit down with the local early; offer a written side letter that the extra shifts are voluntary, time-and-a-half, with no bearing on bargaining positions. Ask what would make them say yes. Consider whether to restore a premium for this project only |
| Anaesthesia (Corvane) | Whether lists run at all | Compensation and cover that do not erode L&D and overnight call | Ask for a written statement of how many extra sessions they can staff, and at what rate; agree on a fallback if they cannot |
| Sterile Processing / Support Services | Instrument supply | Funded overtime or a second shift, and a say in the schedule | Get the SP manager and the Support Services head in the room now, and fund the extra hours from the project money |
| Physio and ward | Discharge and beds | Resourcing for weekend cover and ward beds | Weekend physio funded from the grant; consider a surge or step-down area, or a day-of-surgery-discharge pathway for suitable patients |
| CEO / CFO | Money and political cover | A realistic number and a clear risk statement | See below |
| Patients | Their own readiness | Clear scheduling and pre-op prep | Pre-op clinic capacity also matters, since cancellations at the door kill throughput |

Sequence matters. Start with the people who can say no and who have not been consulted (SP, anaesthesia, nursing, physio, bed management) before you get further with the surgeons. Do this in short one-on-one conversations first, then a single working session with everyone, so nobody is surprised by what another group has asked for. You have until Friday, so realistically: 1 to 2 days of those calls, then write.

Be clear on what you can and cannot offer. You cannot promise the nurses anything about April bargaining. You are not the person who can restore the premium, so involve HR and labour relations before you say anything that sounds like a commitment. Agree with labour relations what you can say.

## What the plan should look like

**Option A (your proposal, with the gaps closed):** two ORs to 7:30pm Monday to Thursday plus Saturdays, funded and staffed in every role, including SP, physio and ward beds.

**Option B (phased and lower risk):** start with extended weekday hours only (no Saturdays) until staffing and bed flow are proven, then add Saturdays if the base is working. This reduces dependence on weekend SP and physio, though it lowers the ceiling.

**Option C (shift the problem):** consider whether an alternative site, a day-surgery or short-stay pathway, or a partner facility with spare beds could take some of the lower-risk joint cases. Even a modest share of day-of-surgery or one-night discharges cuts the bed demand materially. Check clinical eligibility with the surgeons.

I would recommend a version of A built like B: commit to weekday extensions from the start date the key staffing is confirmed, with Saturdays as a gated second step, and a clear tripwire for scaling back.

The plan to the CEO should include:
- The ask and the number: base case, stretch, and what the funding terms say about shortfall.
- Pathway resourcing: OR, anaesthesia, nursing, SP, physio, beds, pre-op, each with a named owner and a confirmed yes, a conditional yes, or an open gap.
- A budget showing how $2.1M is spent and what it does not cover.
- Milestones: for example, by mid-November staffing rosters confirmed; checks at weeks 4 and 8 on cases per week, cancellations, length of stay and bed occupancy; a decision point to adjust.
- Risks and mitigations: staffing falls short, anaesthesia unavailable, bed block, winter surge, equipment failure, nursing fatigue.
- Decisions you need from the CEO: help with Support Services and SP, with labour relations on the nursing arrangement, and with the region on the funding terms.
- A plan for after March: how the waiting list will be held, so the gain is not lost.

## How to frame it on Friday

Instead of "everyone has signed up," say something like: "Here is a plan that can deliver about X cases, with a stretch of 450. I have spoken with each group that controls a piece. These are confirmed, these are conditional, and these are open. These are the three decisions I need from you." A CEO will trust that more, and it protects you if one piece fails.

If by Friday the nurses, anaesthesia or SP are not able to commit, say so, and present the phased option rather than overpromising. A smaller delivered number with a good record is better than a missed 450 and a damaged relationship with nursing going into April.
