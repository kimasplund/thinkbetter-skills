**Short version:** don't walk in Friday with "everyone has signed up." Only the surgeons have, and they are the group with the least to lose. Take the CEO a conditional plan that shows which commitments are firm, which are pending, and what happens if they don't land. The risk is that your proposal only looks like it needs OR time and surgeons. Its real constraints are downstream: sterile processing, anaesthesia, nursing, ward beds and physio.

## Why the plan as drafted probably doesn't reach 450

I'm assuming about 4.5 months of delivery (mid-November to March 31, roughly 20 weeks), 2 ORs, and 2 joints per room per extended day. Check these against your own numbers.

- **Sterile Processing runs 7am-6pm on weekdays.** Cases until 7:30pm need instrument turnaround after 6pm, and Saturday lists need SPD staffing on a day it is closed. Joint sets are large and heavy on trays and implants. If SPD isn't extended, the evening and Saturday lists can't run. SPD reports to Support Services, which is a different chain from yours, so you need its manager and VP on board.
- **Anaesthesia is covered by the contract, but the contract probably doesn't oblige Corvane to staff extra lists.** Eight anaesthetists who also cover labour and delivery and overnight call are a thin pool. Evening and Saturday lists compete with their call rest rules. "Settled in June" means the terms are fixed, not that they will supply extra capacity. You need to ask them directly what they can staff, and at what rate.
- **Nurses.** Volunteering to "consider" extra shifts is not a commitment. The premium you suspended last year, and the April bargaining, mean goodwill is low and the stakes are high. Each extended room needs a full team (circulating, scrub, and recovery/PACU nurses), not just OR nurses.
- **The ward is the likely binding constraint.** At 95% occupancy, 450 extra patients with a two-night stay means about 900 extra bed-nights. Over 20 weeks that is about 6-7 more occupied beds on average, with peaks. You don't have that headroom. Saturday surgery also puts patients in beds Sunday and Monday, and physio works Monday to Friday, so a Saturday patient may not be mobilised until Monday. That stretches the stay beyond two nights and eats bed capacity further. PACU capacity in the evenings needs checking too.
- **Arithmetic.** If each extended room does 2 joints, then 4 evenings plus Saturday is 5 lists per room, or 10 lists a week, or about 20 cases a week, and over 20 weeks only about 400 before cancellations, holidays and bad weeks. "Roughly 450 if we don't lose many days" has almost no margin. Plan for a realistic yield and put the shortfall in front of the CEO.

## How to get alignment by Friday (and after)

Realistically you have days, so use a sequence rather than one big meeting.

1. **Today and tomorrow, one-to-one calls**, in this order: SPD manager and the Support Services director, the Corvane lead, the union local president and the OR nurse manager, the ward manager and physio lead. Use the same five questions each time: What would you need to take part? What can you commit to by when? What worries you? What would make you say no? What can't be done at all?
2. **Share the same numbers with everyone.** Show the 450 target, the grant, weekly volumes, and bed-night maths so people see the same constraints. That also reduces the sense of being told what to do.
3. **Then one short joint session** (60 minutes, Thursday) to resolve the shared problems: SPD hours, the ward bed plan, Saturday physio. Don't open the issues for the first time in the room.
4. **Write down who commits to what.** For each group, capture: what, from when, at what cost, and whether it is confirmed, conditional or unconfirmed.

## What the plan should look like

**Phase 0 (now to mid-November): readiness.** Confirm SPD, anaesthesia and nursing capacity and the bed plan. Do not start the extended lists until the whole chain is staffed. A list that runs without SPD or without recovery staff makes the problem worse.

**Phase 1 (from late November): pilot** one room, 2-3 evenings, perhaps one Saturday a month. Track: cases done, cancellations, turnover, late finishes, ward occupancy, length of stay, staff feedback.

**Phase 2 (January): scale to the second room and Saturdays** only if the pilot data and the staffing support it. Include a review at a fixed date.

**Levers beyond the OR:**
- Use the grant for a temporary ward or step-down bed capacity, or for a day-of-surgery or short-stay pathway for suitable patients (some knees and hips can go home the same day or next day).
- Fund weekend physio, or a discharge-planning nurse.
- Prioritise suitable patients so complex cases don't block beds.
- Consider shifting other elective work (not joint work) out of the extended rooms' regular time, so staff aren't stretched across both.
- Ask whether any outsourced or partner capacity, such as a nearby facility, could take some cases.

**Budget:** $2.1M over 450 cases is about $4,700 per case. That is a thin figure for implants, premium staff pay, SPD, anaesthesia sessions and beds. Check whether it covers marginal costs, and whether implants and consumables are included in the funding. One-time funding also means you can't add permanent staff or commitments that continue after March.

**Decision rule for the CEO:** state a minimum viable number (for example 300-350 cases) and the point (say, end of January) at which you will tell the region you can't reach 450 and renegotiate, or not accept the offer in full. Ask whether the funding is paid per case or has a floor. If it is all-or-nothing at 450, that changes the risk a lot, so find out before Friday.

## Things to avoid

- Announcing the plan before the nurses' local has been consulted. With bargaining in April, a surprise will cost you more than the extra shifts are worth.
- Treating the Corvane contract as a guarantee of capacity.
- Assuming that surgeons who are paid per case will fix the system problems. Their incentive is to do more cases, not to manage beds or SPD.
- Burning out the same staff. If the people who staff extra shifts are the same ones who carry normal lists, cancellations and sick calls will rise by February.
- Pushing patients out early to free beds. Keep the discharge criteria safe.
