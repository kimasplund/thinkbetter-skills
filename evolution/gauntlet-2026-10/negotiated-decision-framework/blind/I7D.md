Short version: don't aim to walk in with everyone "signed up" by Friday. Aim to walk in with a plan where each group that can stop it has either committed to a specific piece or has a named, dated route to commit, plus a go/no-go date. Right now the surgeons are the only group that is clearly on board, and they are the group that matters least to whether you hit 450. Your binding constraints are beds, anaesthesia, Sterile Processing and weekend physio, and none of them is secured.

**What the numbers say**

- November to March is about 21 weeks. After the holidays and a ramp-up, call it 19 productive weeks. 450 cases is roughly 24 a week.
- Your proposal adds 8 weekday evening room-sessions and 2 Saturday lists a week. That reaches about 24 only if every evening yields 2 joints and every Saturday yields 4, with almost no cancellations. Each lost day costs 2 to 4 cases. Treat 450 as a stretch, and ask the region now whether the $2.1M is all-or-nothing or pro-rated. That one answer changes how much risk the CEO is taking.
- 450 cases at two nights each is about 900 bed-nights, or roughly 6 extra occupied beds on average. At 95% occupancy, the ward cannot absorb that.
- $2.1M over 450 cases is about $4,670 a case. Check that against implants, time-and-a-half nursing, extra anaesthesia sessions, Sterile Processing overtime and weekend physio. If it doesn't cover them, the CEO needs to know on Friday.

**What the plan should look like**

1. **Design around the weakest link.** Don't commit to "two ORs, four evenings, two Saturdays" until anaesthesia, sterile processing and beds each give a number. If one gives 60% of what you need, you scale the plan to 60% and pursue the rest by other means, rather than discovering it in December.
2. **Phase it.** Start in November with the pieces that need the fewest sign-offs, probably the surgeons' own time plus a smaller number of evening lists. Add Saturdays only when weekend physio and weekend beds are confirmed. A phased start also gives you real throughput data in the first four weeks, so your forecast stops being a guess.
3. **Consider other ways to reach the number.** Options are a later start rather than a later finish, a pre-admission clinic to cut cancellations, a block-time swap with other specialties, or sending suitable cases to a partner facility or a day-surgery site. Offer the CEO the option set, not just your first design.
4. **Put the rules in writing.** For the union: voluntary, capped, one-time, no precedent. For the surgeons: waiting-time-based selection, with an agreed rule for who gets evening and Saturday slots. For the region: what counts as a completed case and what happens to the money if you fall short.
5. **Set a go/no-go gate.** For example, by mid-November the plan is on track if anaesthesia and nursing rosters cover at least 70% of the planned sessions and weekend physio is in place. If it isn't, scale back and tell the region early rather than late.

**Before Friday**

- Spend the next two days with the five groups above, one at a time, starting with anaesthesia, the union and Sterile Processing. Ask each of them the same question: what would you need, and what is the most you can give?
- Take the CEO's office a one-page summary with three columns: committed, conditional (with the condition and the date), and open. If someone is not on board, say so. The CEO can often resolve a block that you can't, such as funding for the weekend physio or the premium, and will be unhappy to hear about it in December.
- Ask the CEO for three decisions: authority to negotiate the premium with the union, a budget line for Sterile Processing and weekend physio, and a conversation with the region about pro-rating.

One more point on trust. The union has already lost the premium once this year. If the plan looks like asking them to work extra for nothing just before bargaining, you will get "consider" turning into "no". If they see the premium restored for this work, a cap on hours, and a voice in how the shifts are rostered, you have a real chance of getting them on side, and the same applies to the other groups. They will commit more readily to a plan they helped shape than to one handed to them on Friday.

Assumptions: I've assumed standard lists of about 4 joints a day, your OR day ending near 4 to 5pm, and an ortho ward of roughly 30 beds. If those are wrong, the arithmetic above moves, but not the conclusion about which resources limit you.
