**Short version:** don't go to the CEO on Friday with "everyone has signed up." Go with a plan whose assumptions you have tested, a list of who has agreed to what, and a clear list of what is still open. Your proposal only needs surgeons and OR nurses on the surface, but at least five other groups are binding constraints, and several haven't been asked yet.

## Where the plan is weak

Each of these has to hold for 450 cases to happen.

1. **Sterile Processing.** They run 7am to 6pm on weekdays and report to Support Services. ORs running to 7:30pm and on Saturdays will produce instrument sets that can't be reprocessed, so the sets will run out. This is probably your hardest constraint, and nobody in your account has talked to them. Support Services is a different executive reporting line, so you need that leader's agreement and a funded staffing plan (extended weekday shift, Saturday shift).
2. **Ward beds and physio.** The ortho ward is at about 95%. Joint patients stay about two nights and need physio to get them walking safely, and physio works Monday to Friday. A Saturday list means Saturday patients can't be mobilised until Monday, so their stay stretches and they use more bed-days. Thursday evening cases have the same problem. More throughput into a nearly full ward will back up. You need either weekend physio, a bed plan (overflow beds, a day-of-surgery-discharge pathway for suitable patients), or a lower case target.
3. **Anaesthesia.** "Settled" isn't the same as "available." The Corvane contract was renewed in June, but it covers a fixed group of eight who also cover labour and delivery and overnight call. Whether the contract obliges them to staff extended lists, or whether that is a negotiation (and how it is priced), is something you need to check. After a 7:30pm finish, an anaesthetist who is on call overnight is a fatigue and coverage problem. Ask them directly. Don't assume.
4. **OR nurses.** "Consider" voluntary extra shifts is not a commitment. The premium suspension last year and the April bargaining are the backdrop, and the extra shifts will be read through that. Time-and-a-half on voluntary shifts will not settle it by itself. Two ORs on extended lists plus Saturdays is a lot of shifts, and voluntary uptake can fall off after a few weeks. Expect the union to ask about the premium and the April contract. Be careful what you promise: anything that looks like a side deal on the premium can cause trouble in bargaining, so involve HR and labour relations before you make offers.
5. **Surgeons.** They are the easiest group, but paid-per-case enthusiasm doesn't mean they've agreed to a Saturday rota. Check how many sessions each can do, and whether they would accept a case mix that includes harder or slower cases, since 14 months of waiting means some of the list is complex.
6. **The funding and the finish line.** $2.1M is one-time money. Check what counts as one of the "450 extra" (extra relative to what baseline? completed by March 31, or performed and billed?), what happens if you reach 380, and whether it is paid out in full, pro rata, or not at all. You don't have to carry that risk alone. Ask the region these questions this week.

## The arithmetic

450 extra cases in roughly 21 weeks (November to March 31) is about 21 a week. Test it against capacity. Two rooms, four weekday evenings plus a Saturday: if each extended session takes 2 to 3 joints, that's about 8 to 12 on weekday evenings plus 4 to 6 on Saturday, so about 12 to 18 a week, before cancellations, holidays (December especially) and sick days. Those are my assumptions, so replace them with your actual case times. On those numbers 450 looks like a stretch, not a base case. Say that to the CEO plainly and give a range, for example 300 to 450, rather than a single number.

## How to get alignment

Don't try to get everyone to sign up at the same time. Do it in this order.

**Before Friday (days, not weeks)**
- **Sterile Processing and Support Services:** a short meeting with both the manager and their executive. Ask what it would take to extend hours, how many extra staff, and the lead time.
- **Ortho ward manager and physio lead:** ask what they can do about Saturday mobilisation and what bed-days the extra cases will need. Can you get weekend physio, even on a limited basis?
- **Corvane lead:** ask whether they can staff this, and what it costs.
- **Nursing leadership and the union local:** tell them early and be honest. Ask what would make voluntary shifts workable (scheduling, notice, caps on consecutive shifts) and how they want to be approached. Bring in HR.
- **Surgeons:** confirm sessions per surgeon and case mix, and ask them to help with the other groups, because they have more influence than you do.
- **Finance:** cost the whole thing, not only the OR. Include sterile processing, physio, ward, anaesthesia premiums and nursing overtime, and check it against $2.1M. If the extra running costs exceed the one-time money, the CEO will want to know.

**What to bring to the CEO**
1. A recommendation with a phased plan, not an all-or-nothing one. For example, start with one room on extended weekday lists in November, add Saturdays once Sterile Processing, physio and nursing coverage are confirmed, and add the second room if the first runs well. Decide in advance what would trigger each step.
2. A table of each group, what they've agreed to, what they've said they need, and what is unresolved.
3. A realistic range for cases, a cost estimate, and the main risks (sterile processing, ward beds, nurse uptake, anaesthesia, holidays).
4. The decisions you need from the CEO. Typically that's authority over cross-department resourcing (Support Services in particular), a position on the nursing premium given the April bargaining, and a conversation with the region about the target definition and partial payment.

## Things I'd avoid

- Describing groups as "signed up" when they've only been consulted.
- Making private promises to the nurses on pay.
- Starting in November at full scale. A failed rollout burns goodwill with the same people you need for the next push.
- Treating the target as fixed. If it can't be reached safely, a smaller well-run program with a conversation with the region about partial funding is better than a rushed one that cancels cases or overloads the ward.
