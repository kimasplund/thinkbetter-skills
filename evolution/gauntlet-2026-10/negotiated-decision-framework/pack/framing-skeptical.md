I'm the general manager of Kestrel Ridge, a member-owned ski hill: two chairlifts, 14 runs, one base lodge. We've lost money in three of the last five winters, mostly from thin snow, and now the board thinks it has found a fix. I have doubts.

Alderline Mountain Group has offered a 20-year operating agreement. They'd run the hill, put $4.2M into snowmaking and a new quad chair, and pay the co-op an annual fee, while the co-op keeps ownership of the lifts and lodge. They've promised to keep all nine of our employees.

The board voted 4–3 to recommend it. Dariusz, our treasurer, has pushed hardest. He's been anxious about the bank ever since he and two other directors personally co-signed our $600k operating line in 2021. The three no votes say a corporate operator will kill the club feel, and I'm not sure they're wrong.

It needs a two-thirds vote of members at the March AGM. We have 410 voting members, including about 140 original 1987 debenture holders who got lifetime passes for their $500. Alderline would move everyone onto its own season-pass product, which I suppose is fair given how cheap our passes are.

Other context: our ski patrol and ski school are run entirely by volunteers, about 60 people who get family passes in return, and we need certified patrollers on duty to open each day. Our fixed costs are mostly insurance, lift inspections and the $11k a year we pay the Vantongeren family for the upper runs, which sit on their farm under the original 1987 lease. It's personal to the co-op, and the family has never raised the rent.

The board still wants a plan for the AGM that gets this approved. If I'm stuck doing it, how do I get everyone on side, and what should we ask Alderline to change before March?
