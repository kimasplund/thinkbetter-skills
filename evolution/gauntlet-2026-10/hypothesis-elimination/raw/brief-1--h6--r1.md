**Short answer:** This looks like pseudohyperkalemia from pre-analytical handling, not an analyzer or phlebotomy problem. The September 7 courier change put unspun, refrigerated blood on a roughly 4 to 7 hour wait at Ridgeway and Millbrook. I'd hold off quarantining the reagent lot and retraining Tomas until you've checked this, because neither fits your data.

**What's going on**

Red cells leak potassium when whole blood sits unseparated. Cold makes it worse, because refrigeration shuts down the sodium-potassium pump that normally keeps potassium inside the cells. The August 1 cold-hold policy was harmless while your own driver collected samples on a 90-minute loop. On September 7 Ridgeway and Millbrook went to one pickup a day at 3:30 pm. A tube drawn at 9 am now sits in a fridge, unspun, for about 6 hours before it reaches you. Because the pump is off for that whole time, the potassium in the plasma rises.

Your own numbers fit this:

| Observation | What it says |
|---|---|
| Rise started about Sept 8, the day after the courier change | The timing matches the courier change. The cold-hold policy had been in place for five weeks without a rise. |
| Ridgeway and Millbrook are 30% of volume but 83% of high results | Both clinics changed to the once-a-day pickup. |
| Harbour, on the same contractor, is at 5% | Harbour spins samples on site before pickup. Same courier, no problem, so the courier isn't the issue. The unspun wait is. |

| Hemolysis index normal on 93% of flagged samples | Cold-induced leakage raises potassium without visible hemolysis, so this is expected. Traumatic draws would raise the hemolysis index. |
| Calcium and sodium normal | No contamination, such as EDTA carryover. |
| Inpatient and ED rates unchanged | Those samples reach the lab quickly. |
| Both ED patients had normal potassium on repeat | A fresh, promptly processed sample came back normal. |

**Why not the other suspects**

- **Analyzer A reagent lot:** The QC mean shifted by 0.08 mmol/L. That can't turn 1.5% flagged into 6%. Analyzer A ran 57% of flagged samples and carries about 60% of volume, so it is not over-represented. A lot problem also wouldn't be concentrated at two clinics or in morning draws.
- **Tomas and the 23-gauge needles:** A traumatic or slow draw through a small needle would raise the hemolysis index, and most flagged samples have a normal one. He works at Ridgeway only, so he can't explain Millbrook, which has almost the same flag rate. The difficult draws are probably real but are a separate matter.
- **Cold-hold policy alone:** It predates the problem by five weeks. It is a contributing condition, because it makes the delay more harmful, but it isn't what changed on September 8.

**What I'm less sure of**

- The other two clinics are at 12% of high results on roughly 35% of volume. That is not a problem, but I don't know their courier and spin arrangements. Check them to see whether they fit the same pattern.
- I'm inferring the delay from the schedule. Nobody has yet shown that flagged tubes were collected much earlier than they were received or spun.

**What to bring Friday**

1. **A time-based check, which settles it.** For September 8–30, pull collection time, receipt time and spin time. Plot potassium against hours from draw to centrifugation for Ridgeway, Millbrook and Harbour. If potassium climbs with the wait, and Harbour is flat, the cause is confirmed.
2. **A clean comparison of the lot and the analyzer.** Rerun a few flagged retained samples, if you still have them, on Analyzer B. Compare the flag rate by analyzer within the same clinics. Expect no difference.
3. **Immediate actions that don't depend on the check:**
   - Stop refrigerating unspun potassium specimens. Room temperature is better until centrifugation.
   - Spin on site at Ridgeway and Millbrook before pickup, as Harbour does, using gel-separator tubes where possible.
   - Failing that, go back to multiple pickups a day so that no tube waits more than about 2 hours unspun.
   - Reword the cold-hold policy. Refrigeration is right for some tests but wrong for potassium.
4. **Patient safety follow-up.**
   - Identify patients with unconfirmed results above 5.5 since September 8 from the two clinics, especially morning draws. Tell their clinicians that these results may be spurious and that repeat testing should use a prompt-spun sample.
   - Send a short advisory to clinicians now. It will reduce unnecessary recalls and ED referrals.
   - Review whether any patient was treated, for example given potassium binders or had medications stopped, on a false result.
5. **Process fixes.** Add a draw-to-spin time requirement for potassium. Add the contractor's schedule and sample-handling conditions to the contract. Monitor outpatient potassium flag rate by clinic and by draw hour each week.

**On the two requests:** Don't quarantine the lot, since it would disrupt about 60% of your volume without fixing anything. You can ask Ridgeway to coach on hemolysis and difficult draws as routine quality work, but retraining Tomas won't change this problem.
