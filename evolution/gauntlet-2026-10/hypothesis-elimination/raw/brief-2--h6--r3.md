**Short answer:** most of the extra shrink is probably not theft. It looks like a receiving error created by the Greenfold switch. I would hold both £90k decisions until you have run the checks below, which cost almost nothing.

## The likely driver: 60 booked, 50 delivered

- Receiving books the pallet quantity from the item master, and the item master says 60 bags per pallet.
- Greenfold pallets are five layers of ten bags, so 50 bags.
- Every Greenfold pallet scanned in therefore books 10 bags that never arrived. That is 17% of each pallet's booked quantity, or 20% on top of what is physically there.
- Those phantom bags stay on the books until the count. They then show up as shrink, in the lines that moved on 8 July and only in the stores that moved.
- IT confirming that nobody edited the item master fits this. The data didn't change. The supplier did, and the data stopped matching the product.

## Why the other suspects fit worse

| Suspect | What the evidence says |
|---|---|
| Self-checkout | The three southern stores that got it in May are at 1.4%, in line with stores that don't have it. If it were the driver, they should be high too. |
| New security contractor | Eight stores switched, but only five are northern. The other three are presumably at normal levels, and the six northern stores include one that didn't switch. Worth confirming that figure, but it doesn't look like a pattern. |
| Organised power-tool theft | Power tools are only 7% of the excess, and four of the six stores are within 2% of book on tools. A gang hitting the north would show up there first. |
| Bagged landscape (68% of the excess) | This is where the shrink sits, and it is the only thing that changed specifically for these six stores and only for these lines. |

The timing also fits. The excess starts after 8 July, not May, and the north was at normal levels last year. Self-checkout alone would not explain why the three southern stores are clean.

Bagged landscape accounts for about two-thirds of the gap. That leaves roughly a third (paint, power tools, other) that I can't yet attribute. Part of it may be genuine loss, part may be other lines that also moved to direct delivery, and part may be ordinary noise. Treat it as an open question, not as settled.

## What to check before spending anything

1. **Count a Greenfold pallet.** Walk to Store 14 (or any of the six) and count the bags on an unopened pallet. If it is 50, the cause is nearly confirmed.
2. **Compare delivery notes with bookings.** For Q3, set Greenfold's delivered quantities (from their delivery notes or invoices) against what receiving booked. The difference should be about 10 bags per pallet.
3. **Reconstruct the shrink.** Count Greenfold pallets received since 8 July, multiply by 10 bags and by the unit cost, and compare that with the landscape excess. If the numbers roughly match, the landscape shrink is accounted for.
4. **Check earlier deliveries.** Before 8 July the same lines came from the distribution centre at 60 per pallet and the shrink was normal. Confirm the DC pallets really are 60.
5. **Check whether other lines moved.** Ask whether paint or anything else also shifted to direct supplier delivery in those stores. If so, the same pallet mismatch could explain part of the remainder.
6. **Check the three non-northern security-switch stores** and the one northern store that kept its contractor. If their shrink is normal, the contractor change is cleared.
7. **Look at power-tool counts and CCTV only after the above.** If tools are within 2% of book at four stores, that is a few stores to watch, not a chain-wide problem.

## What to do about it

- If the pallet count confirms 50, fix the receiving data for Greenfold lines (a supplier-specific pack quantity, or receiving by case count rather than the pallet label). Then adjust the book stock in the affected stores to reflect the phantom bags, so the shrink figure is restated.
- Check whether any Greenfold bagged lines carry different pallet sizes, since 20–40 kg bags may be stacked differently.
- Then see how much shrink is left. Only if a meaningful gap remains in the northern stores after that adjustment does it make sense to revisit the tool theft or the contractor question, and by then you will know the size of the problem you are paying to solve.

Pulling self-checkout or bringing the old contractor back would cost about £90k each, and the evidence so far gives no reason to expect either to move the number.
