Short answer: the most likely driver is a receiving-data mismatch on the Greenfold bagged landscape lines, which is mostly an accounting artefact rather than theft. I would hold both £90k decisions until you have run a few cheap checks.

**Why the evidence points at Greenfold**

- 68% of the excess shrink is in bagged landscape. Self-checkout and a security contractor change would not concentrate losses in 20-40 kg bags of compost, bark and gravel. Nobody walks out with those, and a tool-theft ring is not interested in them.
- Your receiving process books the pallet quantity from the item master, which is 60 bags per pallet. Greenfold pallets are five layers of ten, which is 50 bags. Every pallet received since July 8 would therefore book 10 bags that never arrived. The item master was not edited, so nothing flagged it. The distribution centre pallets were presumably built at 60, which is why this never showed up before.
- The timing fits. The change started on July 8 and only the six northern stores are affected. The Q3 count then shows the missing stock as shrink.
- Self-checkout does not fit. The three southern stores with self-checkout are at 1.4%, in line with the rest of the chain.
- The security contractor change does not fit well either. It covers eight stores, but only five are among the northern six, so three of the eight are outside the problem. Stores outside the six are at normal shrink.
- The organised power-tool theft is real but small. Tools are 7% of the excess. Four of the six stores are within 2% of book on power tools, so the bulletin does not explain the gap.

**A rough size check**

Take the 68% share of the excess. If you have 5 or 6 stores and pallets arriving regularly, 10 phantom bags per pallet (a 1 in 6 overstatement, about 17% of received quantity) is easily big enough to explain that share. Please compare it with your actual figures, because I am assuming the numbers here.

**What to check before spending anything**

1. Count bags on a few Greenfold pallets as they arrive, at Store 14 and one or two other northern stores. Confirm 50 against 60.
2. Take the Greenfold deliveries since July 8 for the bagged lines and compare the units booked at receipt with the units on Greenfold's delivery notes and invoices. If the gap is about 10 per pallet, that confirms it. Also check what you are being invoiced for, since you may be paying for 50 and booking 60.
3. Check whether any pallets are mixed, partial or a different build, because not all of them will be exactly 50.
4. Compare the landscape variance with pallets received at each store. It should scale with the number of Greenfold pallets, not with the store's footfall, till count or security cover.
5. Look at the bagged landscape lines before July 8 in those stores, which should sit at normal shrink. Also look at the bagged lines in the other 16 stores, which should be normal.
6. Look at the rest of the excess separately. After you strip out the phantom bags, see what is left in the northern six. If shrink is then about 1.5-2%, a smaller real issue may remain, probably in paint and tools, and you can target it.
7. Only after that, look at self-checkout and security. Look at exception data for the three southern self-checkout stores against the northern ones, and incident or after-hours loss data for the five northern stores that changed contractor against the one that did not.

**The fix, if confirmed**

- Set the Greenfold items up with a pack size of 50, or as separate item master records (a separate SKU or supplier-specific pallet quantity), so receiving books the right quantity.
- Write the phantom stock off as an accounting correction, and flag it to your auditors so it is not treated as theft.
- Ask the supplier to confirm pallet configuration in writing, and add a spot-check on receiving for any direct-delivery supplier.
- Consider whether to recover anything from Greenfold if you were invoiced for 60.

**On the two proposals**

Neither the head of loss prevention nor the COO has been shown to be wrong, but neither has evidence behind it yet. Pulling self-checkout does not match the southern stores at 1.4%. Bringing the old contractor back does not match the fact that three of the eight changed stores are not in the problem group. If the Greenfold count confirms the mismatch, you can save the £90k in both cases, though I would still treat the remaining ~1.5% above normal as a question to answer with data before you decide anything.

Assumptions I made: that the northern six all receive Greenfold pallets built the same way as at Store 14, that the distribution centre pallets were genuinely 60, and that no other supplier change happened at the same time.
