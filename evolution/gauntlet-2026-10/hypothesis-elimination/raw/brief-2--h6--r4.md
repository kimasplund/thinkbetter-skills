**Short answer:** Don't spend the £90k on either. The most likely driver is a receiving error that creates stock on paper that was never there. It is probably not theft. Greenfold pallets look like they hold 50 bags (five layers of ten), but the system books 60 on every pallet scanned in. Each pallet adds 10 phantom bags to book stock, and the count then reports them as missing.

**Why this fits the facts**

- **Where it sits:** 68% of the excess is in bagged landscape lines. Those are the only lines whose supply route changed on July 8, and only in the six northern stores.
- **Timing:** Last year the same six stores were at normal levels. The route changed in July, and Q3 is the first quarter that includes it.
- **The mechanism is real:** Receiving books the pallet quantity from the item master. IT confirms bagged lines are still set at 60 per pallet. Your own description of the Greenfold pallets gives 5 x 10 = 50. The DC pallets were presumably built to 60. Nobody edited the master, so nothing flagged the new pallet size.
- **The error scales with volume:** The more Greenfold pallets a store receives, the more phantom stock it books. A theft pattern would not scale this way.

**Why the other explanations fit worse**

| Explanation | What argues against it |
|---|---|
| Self-checkout | The three southern stores with self-checkout are at 1.4%. It also would not concentrate the loss in 20-40 kg bags. |
| New security contractor | It changed at eight stores, only five of them northern. Guarding would not explain a loss concentrated in one product family that began with one supply change. Check the three non-northern stores that switched. If they are normal, this is largely ruled out. |
| Organised power-tool theft | Tools are only 7% of the excess. Tool stock is within 2% of book at four of the six stores. |
| General northern theft | Paint and everything else are only about 25% of the excess, so it is unlikely to be broad theft. |

Some real theft may sit inside the 19% "everything else" and the 6% paint. Those are small next to the landscape figure.

**What to check before spending anything (mostly a few days of desk work)**

1. **Count bags on a delivery.** At Store 14 or any northern store, count the bags on the next few Greenfold pallets. If it is 50, the main question is answered. Also check whether the number varies by product, since gravel, bark and compost may be stacked differently.
2. **Reconcile on paper.** For one store and one product, take pallets received since July 8, multiplied by 60, minus sales, against the physical count. Then repeat with 50. If the 50-bag version closes most of the gap, that is your answer.
3. **Check the size of the gap.** Phantom bags equal Greenfold pallets x 10 x unit cost. Compare that, store by store, to the landscape shrink value. It should line up closely and track each store's Greenfold volume. A poor match means something else is also at work.
4. **Read Greenfold's delivery notes and invoices.** Check that bags invoiced match bags delivered. If you were billed for 60 per pallet and got 50, that is a supplier claim. If billed for 50, your own book stock is overstated.
5. **Check the side gate.** Confirm that pallets are received and counted at the gate rather than dropped and scanned later. Unsupervised access is a secondary risk, but it only matters if the count in step 1 comes out right.
6. **Look at the first weeks.** If the landscape shrink rose abruptly after July 8 and not gradually, that supports the receiving error. Gradual growth would suggest theft.

**If it checks out**

- Set up pack quantity by supplier, or a separate item for Greenfold stock at 50 per pallet, so that receiving books what actually arrives.
- Correct book stock for the phantom bags and restate the Q3 shrink. Much of it is a booking correction, not a real loss, so do not write it off as stolen.
- Ask Greenfold to confirm pallet configuration in writing, and recover any overbilling.
- Reverse the same check for any other lines moved to direct delivery.

**What would change this answer**

If the pallet counts come out at 60, or the 50-bag reconciliation leaves a large unexplained gap, the loss is real and I would look next at gate access and receiving practice at the stores with the highest shrink. Even then, self-checkout looks like the weakest suspect and the contractor change would need a store-level comparison before any money moves.

I'm fairly confident, around 80%, that the receiving mismatch is the main cause. The counts in steps 1 and 2 would move that to near certainty or rule it out.
