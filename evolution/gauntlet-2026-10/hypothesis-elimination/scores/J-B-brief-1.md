Notes (J-B, skeptical reviewer)

General: all eight replies reach the same diagnosis: delayed, refrigerated, unspun samples, triggered by the courier change. So the differences are in rigor and honesty. Several replies treat Harbour's 5% share of flags as a normal flag rate, but Harbour's volume is not given. None notices that the brief's numbers are in tension: 83% of flags from 30% of volume implies about 16-17% at Ridgeway/Millbrook, which is above the 11% morning rate.

- T3P: Sound and well ordered. It notes that inpatient and ED samples run on the same analyzers, and it states its assumptions. It describes Harbour as "basically unaffected", which reads its share of flags as a rate. It gives no quantified confidence, but it does say "strong but circumstantial".
- V9H: Thorough. It says what would change the conclusion (if there is no delay gradient, look at the lot and the draw technique next). It says Harbour's "rate is normal" based on its share of flags. Some padding in the process section.
- V0S: The table is clean. The EDTA point from normal calcium is good, and so is comparing Tomas against other phlebotomists at the same draw time. It gives 85% confidence. The expected 0.5-1+ mmol/L effect is hedged. Minor padding.
- P6C: Good check of Tomas within draw-time bands, and an honest caveat about the other clinics' 12%. It says Harbour "is normal" when only its share of flags is known.
- R7F: Good "less sure" section. But it invents figures: "roughly 35% of volume" for the other two clinics, and Millbrook having "almost the same flag rate" as Ridgeway. Per-clinic volumes are not given.
- A6P: Back-calculates that the rest of the network is at its 1.5% baseline, which is a useful non-obvious point. It also raises the counter-case that Tomas might only work mornings. Confidence is calibrated, and it says what would raise it. But it computes a 16-17% rate for the two clinics without noticing that this conflicts with the 11% morning rate. The "80-85% to 95%" phrasing leans toward confidence arithmetic.
- A3I: Concise. But the lot check is muddled: rerunning on Analyzer A cannot clear Analyzer A. The suggestion to look at low potassium results is unclear. Weaker on verification.
- O6S: The best handling of counter-cases: a fits/doesn't-fit table, and fist clenching and tourniquet time are named as possible contributors. The split-sample design separates the effect of cold from the effect of delay. Confidence is stated, and it says what would move it. It does state assumptions as fact: that inpatient/ED run on the same lot, and that Tomas draws only part of Ridgeway's volume.
