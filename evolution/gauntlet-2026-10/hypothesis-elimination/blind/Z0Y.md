**Short answer:** the most likely driver is a receiving or booking error on the Greenfold pallets, not theft. I wouldn't spend the £90k on either option yet.

**Why the data points that way**

- Your item master has bagged lines at 60 per pallet. Greenfold pallets are five layers of ten bags, which is 50. Receiving books the pallet quantity from the item master, so every Greenfold pallet books 10 bags that never arrived. The stock count then finds them missing and records shrink.
- The timing fits. Direct delivery started on 8 July, and the extra shrink is in the six stores that switched. The same six stores were normal last year.
- The category split fits. Bagged landscape is 68% of the excess. Theft by self-checkout misuse, a guard change or an organised group wouldn't concentrate so heavily in 20–40 kg bags. Those are heavy, low-value, hard to steal and easy to mis-book.
- The size is plausible. A 17% overbooking (10 of 60) on high-volume seasonal lines could produce a large shrink swing. Please check this against actual volumes.
- The other explanations each fail somewhere.
  - Self-checkout: the three southern stores that have it run at 1.4%, which is normal. It was also installed in May, but the problem is tied to the northern stores and landscape lines.
  - Security contractor: the switch covered eight stores, five of them northern. The three non-northern stores that switched aren't elevated (assuming they are within the 1.3–1.5% range). Contractor changes also wouldn't favour 40 kg bags.
  - Organised power-tool theft: power tools are only 7% of the excess, and stock is within 2% of book at four of the six stores. It may be real at a store or two, but it isn't the main driver.

**What to check, cheapest first**

1. Physically count bags on a few Greenfold pallets at Store 14 and the other five stores. If it's 50 against a booked 60, this is confirmed.
2. Compare Greenfold delivery notes and invoices with units booked at receiving. Billing should be for the 50 actually delivered.
3. Quantify the gap. Take the number of Greenfold pallets received since 8 July, multiply by 10, and multiply by unit cost. Compare that with the extra shrink in bagged landscape. If it explains most of the 68%, you have your answer.
4. Check whether any Greenfold pallets are mixed, partial or not five by ten. Also check whether any SKUs have different bag sizes or weights.
5. Check that the stores' shrink on bagged lines is flat by week from 8 July. That would match a per-pallet error instead of random theft. Check the stores' shrink on those lines before 8 July too.
6. For the remaining 32% (power tools, paint, everything else), compare the northern stores with the rest. Paint at 6% is worth a look in case it is also on the new supply route. Check the two stores where power tools are out of line, and talk to police about the bulletin.

**Fixes if confirmed**

- Set up a separate item master record, or a supplier-specific pack quantity, for Greenfold at 50 per pallet. IT's confirmation that nothing was edited is consistent with the problem: the master wasn't updated when the supply route changed.
- Book the shrink correction as a stock adjustment instead of a loss, and restate Q3 shrink for the six stores.
- Add a rule that any change of supplier or pack configuration triggers an item master review.
- Ask Greenfold to confirm pallet configurations in writing, and consider a receiving check on a sample of pallets.

**On the two proposals**

Hold both until steps 1 to 3 are done. They take days and cost almost nothing. If the pallet error explains most of the gap, pulling self-checkout or switching contractors would cost £180k and leave the real problem in place. If a residual gap remains after correcting for the pallets, then look at the other causes with a clean baseline.

**Assumptions:** I've assumed the 50-bag pallet count applies to all Greenfold bagged lines, and that pallets are received complete. I haven't seen your volumes or unit costs, so the size of the effect is something to check, not a figure I can give you.
