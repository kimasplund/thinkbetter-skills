**Short answer:** I don't think you've nailed it yet. The best-supported cause is the September 7 courier change combined with the August 1 fridge rule, not the reagent lot or Tomas. This is a strong inference from your numbers. One quick test would confirm it, and I'd run it before Friday.

**What I think is happening**

Before September 7, a specimen waited at most about 90 minutes for our own driver. Now Ridgeway and Millbrook get one pickup a day at 3:30 pm, so a morning specimen can sit unspun for 5 to 7 hours or more. The cold-hold rule was written for waits of 30 minutes to a couple of hours. Whole blood kept cold and unseparated for hours stops the cell pumps (Na/K-ATPase), and potassium leaks out of the red cells into the serum. That gives falsely high potassium with no real hyperkalemia. Cold-holding on its own was harmless until the delay became very long.

**Why I'd drop the two suspects**

- **Analyzer A lot.** Analyzer A runs 60% of volume and 57% of flagged samples, so it is not over-represented. A 0.08 mmol/L QC shift, inside limits, can't turn a 1.5% flag rate into 6%. It could nudge borderline results by a small amount, so keep an eye on it, but it isn't the driver.
- **Tomas.** Hard draws and 23-gauge butterfly needles usually cause hemolysis, and your hemolysis index is normal. He also can't explain Millbrook, which has 39% of flagged results and no new phlebotomist. Retraining may still be reasonable on its own merits, but it won't fix this.

Quarantining the lot would also disrupt testing on your busiest analyzer without fixing anything.

**What to check before Friday**

1. **Specimen ages.** Pull collection-to-receipt times for Ridgeway and Millbrook specimens, flagged against not flagged. I expect flagged ones to cluster at 4 hours or more. Compare with Harbour.
2. **A split-sample test.** Draw paired tubes from about 20 outpatients. Spin one immediately and hold the other cold and unspun for 6 hours. If the held tubes read 0.5 mmol/L or more higher, the cause is confirmed.
3. **Reagent lot.** Run a few patient samples on Analyzer A and on another analyzer, or on the old lot. This clears the lot for good.
4. **Recollection results.** Look at the repeats from recalled patients. If they came back normal, that supports a pre-analytic artifact.

**Interim actions to bring**

- Stop refrigerating unspun specimens. Where possible, centrifuge at the clinic (as Harbour does) or keep them at room temperature, then send serum or plasma.
- Ask the contractor for an additional midday pickup at Ridgeway and Millbrook, or restore the earlier loop.
- Tell clinicians that outpatient potassium above 5.5 from Ridgeway and Millbrook since September 8 should be repeated before recall or ED referral. Consider a comment on those results.
- Review the roughly 5 weeks of affected results, since some patients may have been recalled or treated unnecessarily.

**Caveats**

- If those times don't differ between flagged and unflagged specimens, I'd reopen the question and look hard at the reagent lot.
