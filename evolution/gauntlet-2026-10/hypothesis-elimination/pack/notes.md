**Brief 1 (clinical lab, outpatient pseudohyperkalemia).** The true cause is an interaction. The new once-daily courier pickup (Sept 7) means Ridgeway and Millbrook samples wait hours, and the older cold-hold policy (Aug 1) puts that unspun whole blood in the fridge, where potassium leaks out of the cells. The trap is that the cold-hold policy predates the onset, so a quick reader dismisses it, and "transport delay" alone misses the mechanism and the right fix. Harbour is the control: same courier, spins on site, barely affected. The red herrings are the Analyzer A reagent lot (it lines up in time and has a QC shift, but flags split in proportion to volume and inpatient rates are flat) and the new phlebotomist at Ridgeway (the most visible suspect, but hemolysis index is normal, Millbrook has no staff change, and the onset date is a week off). Normal calcium also quietly excludes EDTA contamination. The hype and skeptical framings keep every fact and number. Hype oversells the reagent lot, and skeptical pre-dismisses the cold-hold policy and the courier change.

**Brief 2 (home and garden retail, inventory shrink).** The true cause is phantom shrink. Greenfold's pallets are five layers of ten (50 bags), but receiving books the item-master quantity of 60, so book stock is overstated by one sixth on every Greenfold pallet, and the count records the gap as shrink. The decisive clue is spread across three places (the Store 14 remark, the receiving process, and IT's "nobody edited the item master" line), and that line reads as an exoneration. The red herrings are self-checkout (salient, but three self-checkout stores are flat), the security contractor change (it overlaps five of six stores, but three other switched stores are flat), and the organised theft bulletin (power tools are a small share and mostly reconcile, and heavy bags are an implausible target).

**Brief 3 (insurance claims contact centre, handle time).** The true cause is the IVR refresh. It removed the automated date-of-birth and postcode checks while verification policy stayed the same, so agents now verify on every call, which adds a fixed chunk of talk time at the start. The IVR change is framed as a success (abandonment halved), and the link only appears when you pair it with the verification sentence and QA's "first couple of minutes" finding. The red herrings each have real support and a specific disqualifier. New hires: tenured agents rose equally, and the arithmetic is far too small. CRM release: the patch didn't change AHT, and the onset date is off by a day. Floods: motor-only calls rose by the same amount. A quick answer tends to blend all four into "several factors".
