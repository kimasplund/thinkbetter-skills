I'm warden of the Brannock Ridge Hut, which the Ostkamm Alpine Club runs at 2,640 m: 48 beds, open 1 July to 20 September, reachable only on foot (about 3.5 hours) or by helicopter for supplies. I'll be honest, I'm not convinced there's a good answer to this.

Our water comes from a spring 300 m above the hut, piped into a 12 m³ tank. At full occupancy we use about 6 m³ a day. Last August the spring stopped for 19 days. We closed for 11 of them, refunded bookings and cancelled two guided groups. It has dwindled in late summer in three of the last five years, and our hydrologist says that's the pattern from now on as the snowfield above us shrinks. In late June, by contrast, it runs so hard the overflow floods the path to the toilet block, for what that's worth.

Other background. We replaced the old dry toilets with flush toilets and added two showers in 2016, after guest complaints. From mid-August occupancy drops to around 55% anyway, because the glacier route gets badly crevassed and the guiding companies stop booking it. The Grauberg alp, 1.1 km down the valley and 190 m lower, runs a dairy all summer; its spring has never failed and the family who farm it are friendly enough. Our solar array was upgraded last year and has plenty of spare capacity.

The committee has two proposals, and I doubt either: drill a borehole by the hut (€140,000 quoted; the geologist gives only about a 60% chance of usable water), or fly water in when we run short (one helicopter rotation carries 0.8 m³ and costs €1,100). We have €75,000 in reserves, which doesn't go far up here.

Are there really any other options? The AGM is in November, and I suspect members will end up with the same two choices, but I'd rather check.
