I'm town clerk for Wickham Lees, population about 14,000. The council runs the town's only cemetery, at Sorrel Lane, and we carry out around 95 burials a year. Our bereavement officer counts 360 new grave spaces left, so roughly four years.

Everyone's first thought is the field next door. The farmer will sell, but only all 14 hectares as one lot, at £1.15 million. Our capital reserve is £280,000. Some councillors are counting on cremation rising, but it has sat at about 70% of funerals for six years.

Background on Sorrel Lane: the north section opened in 1888 and has been full for a century; it's mostly long grass now, and the Friends of Sorrel Lane mow it twice a year. The east section was laid out in the 1960s. About 380 plots there were sold in advance as reserved graves, many of them in the 1960s and 70s, and they're still empty. We charge £1,450 for the exclusive right of burial in a grave, granted for 75 years, as it has been since the cemetery opened.

Elsewhere: the council's pitch-and-putt course at Ferney Bank closed last year, and nobody has decided what to do with its 3 hectares. At the last county meeting, the clerk from Ashby Combe, three miles away, mentioned that their cemetery extension has had hardly any burials since it opened in 2017.

In January I have to brief full council on how we keep providing burials for the next 25 years or so. I don't want to walk in with nothing but "buy the field or don't". What are all the realistic options?
