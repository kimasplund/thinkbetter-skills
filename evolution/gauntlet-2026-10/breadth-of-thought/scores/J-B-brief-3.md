- H0N: Covers the menu well: harvesting, the auction, a courier-first variant, running the port and sensor work in parallel, service spares, and the shape of the last-time buy. It flags hill-hold safety and the €1.9M reading as assumptions and gives yield as a range. The claim that the gap closes "with little or no LTB" is hedged but only loosely quantified. 19 items is on the long side.
- Y7K: Its packages are well built and it quantifies retail-only VX-41 need (about 900 boards, about €0.4M). But its gross/net logic is backwards. It says a quote net of existing stock would make the cash problem "far smaller" and the full buy "much more affordable", when the opposite is true. It also calls €700k "37% of the need" while its own numbers make the need about €950k. Misses service spares.
- Q7J: The tightest reasoning. It reads €1.9M as gross, which gives a €950k real shortfall, and gets the direction of the alternative right. Its no-hill-hold calculation (10.4 months needed, about 1,100 boards, about €450k) is careful, and it sends harvested boards to service spares first. It wants a pre-approved bid ceiling and admits what it cannot know. Minor issues: the installed base of about 25,000 is an extrapolation stated as fact, and it does not spell out running the port and sensor work in parallel.
- V8H: Strong on the subtle points: a cargo bike rolling backward as a liability issue, service spares, secured finance the bank was not asked about, and a pre-authorised bid. The numbers are consistent. It calls the 80% harvest yield "even at", which is optimistic, and its 16-month total is not discounted for that risk.
- Q0O: A sound menu, and the "Not covered" section is honest. Its packages are not quantified, and the recommended package A gives no coverage numbers. The 6-9 month figure for a third-party drive system is an invented estimate (hedged). It does not deal with the auction falling after the board meeting.
- N8N: The most action-ready board asks: a stock-level trigger, specific approvals and a weekly timeline. But it takes the less natural reading of €1.9M (as the 7-month shortfall, when the brief says "covering the full 14 months"), so its headline numbers are pessimistic. Its "€1.2M for six months" example matches neither reading. Misses service spares and running the work in parallel.
