Notes

K9C: Very thorough. It treats the reserved graves as stock on top of the 360 and audits how many burials actually take a new space. It has the right checklist for Ferney Bank, and it offers an option on the field as cheap insurance. It also gets the legal position right: a town council has a power to provide burials, not a duty, and it flags the doubt over CPO. It runs long, though: 13 options, a table and two lists that overlap.
C3G: The only reply to spot that 95 burials at 30% means about 315 funerals, which is a lot for 14,000 people. That suggests heavy non-resident use and changes who should pay. The arithmetic is sound and it lands on a clear view (Ferney Bank first). The packages are useful. It slightly overstates the gain from double depth.

D0Q: It tests the "four years" against new-space consumption first, which is a good move, and ends on a clean decision point. But it calls 380 plots "more than a year's worth" (they are about four years' worth), and its table says reserved graves deliver in "months". It also leaves out the wind-down baseline.
W2Q: Compact, with sharp points: the timing needs a bridge, 3 ha at the asking rate fits the reserve, a land swap, and a cemetery is a perpetual obligation. But it is optimistic about reusing the north section, it doesn't flag that a town council can't make a CPO itself, and the survey cost claim has no support.

E5E: A solid menu. It handles CPO correctly through the principal authority and mentions Band D and PWLB. The recommendation is less sharp, and it offers to draft the table rather than giving it. It adds a growth uplift that is a labelled assumption.
M3T: Reasonable structure. But it advises telling the farmer "you have compulsory purchase powers", which a town council doesn't hold directly. It says no single option closes the gap while its own Ferney Bank figure roughly does. "Options I set aside" is a slight trace of method.
