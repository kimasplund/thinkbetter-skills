A few practical points before you say yes:

- **Tell stage management now.** The stage manager has to call the hold, and the cast and crew need to know the new time. It's best if it's a single five-minute hold and not a rolling "just another minute".
- **Set a hard limit.** Agree that 19:35 is the latest start, however long the queue is. Otherwise a five-minute hold can drift into ten or fifteen, and that eats the margin you have.
- **Make an announcement.** Tell the audience over the PA or at the bar ("curtain is running about five minutes late"). That keeps people from rushing their drinks and stops them from drifting back into the bar queue.
- **Check any fixed times that depend on the end.** Examples are a live stream or recording call, a sign-language or captioning schedule, a hired-in crew with a finish time, and the last public transport that many of the audience would use. At 21:40 you are still fine for most of these, but confirm the ones you know about.
- **Keep the interval as planned.** Don't let the delay turn into a longer interval. If the bar is the bottleneck, the better fix for tonight is more staff or a pre-order option for the interval.

If none of those is a problem, go ahead and hold to 19:35.
