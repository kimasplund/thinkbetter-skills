Do not let the tanker take this milk yet, and do not dump it. Hold it on the farm until you have a test result.

**Why your wife's dilution sum doesn't hold**
- Twenty litres in 11,600 is about 1 in 580. Antibiotic screening tests work in parts per billion. The penicillin limit is around 4 ppb.
- Milk from a cow on penicillin tubes can carry thousands of ppb, and the first milkings are the worst. Divided by 580, that is still likely to be above the limit.
- So the milk may well fail. If it does, you pay for the whole 26,000-litre load plus a penalty. Taking the risk is not worth it. Holding the tank costs you far less than a failed load.

**Now, 06:35 to 06:50**
1. Phone the co-op or milk-quality contact and the driver. Tell them straight: "Treated cow's milk went in the tank by mistake. We're holding collection pending a test." Telling them early usually helps with the contract. Letting it go quietly and failing at the plant does not.
2. Tell the driver to skip you today. He doesn't have to wait. Collecting you tomorrow instead is a smaller problem than a failed load.
3. Ask the co-op whether they can run a rapid beta-lactam test on a tank sample. If you have an on-farm kit (a Delvotest, Snap or Charm-type strip), use it now on a well-agitated tank sample. A rapid test is only a guide. Your insurer needs the lab result.

**By 10:00**
- Agitate the tank thoroughly and take a clean sample, labelled and chilled.
- Drop it at the Penmorrow depot before 10:00. The result should come mid-afternoon. It also gives you the lab result your insurance needs.
- Keep a written note of the timeline: when Callum told you, the cow's number, the 9.8 and 10.4 litre volumes, and the treatment dates. Take a photo of the parlour software record.

**The 16:00 milking**
- The tank has 11,600 litres and the milking adds about 2,900. That makes 14,500, over the 13,000 capacity.
- Clean and sanitise the old 6,000-litre tank, and put the afternoon milk in that. Keep it separate from the suspect milk.
- If the lab result is clean, the milk can be collected as normal. If it fails, the 11,600 litres are discarded and claimed on insurance, and the afternoon milk in the small tank is unaffected.

**If the result is positive**
- Don't pour it down the yard drain to the beck. Milk is a serious pollutant on its own, and antibiotic residue makes it worse. It also breaks the lab-result condition on your insurance claim. The cooler failure two years ago was a different situation.
- Ask the co-op, your insurer and the environment agency how to dispose of it. Options usually include a slurry store or a licensed collector, depending on volume and local rules.
- Check the insurance conditions on disposal before you dump anything.

**Cow 274**
- Put a new band on her, a visible mark or a collar, and tell Callum her milk goes on the dump line until the end of the withdrawal period. Check the product's label, because it runs from the last tube, not from today.
- Keep her out of the main string where you can, and mark the pen as well.

**Assumptions**
- I'm assuming a UK or Irish-style co-op with penicillin-type tubes.
- If your co-op has a different rule on reporting, follow theirs. Ring them first.

In short: call the co-op and driver now, skip collection, test and sample the tank, use the small tank at 16:00, and dump nothing until the lab result is in.
