Cancel tonight. Hanne is right, and your director is solving the wrong problem.

**Why holding for the X-ray doesn't work**

- The X-ray answers one question: is Signe's ankle broken? That isn't the main risk.
- The main risk is a lift that dropped 40 cm without warning, that nobody has inspected, and that nobody qualified can inspect until tomorrow morning. Act 2 puts an actor on it in front of 540 people. A clear X-ray doesn't change that.
- The timing is also bad. The result lands around 19:00, just as doors open. If you wait for it, you either cancel with people already on their way, or you open with a lead who has a freshly injured ankle and a fight scene to do.
- Waiting also removes the one thing the duty manager is offering you, which is cancelling before people leave home. That is the real cost of holding.

**Why Liv doesn't rescue it**

- Moving Liv on swaps the injured performer for one who has only done the Act 2 fight at half speed. Add the six critics on press night and no technician in the building, and you are asking the least-rehearsed person to take the highest-risk scene.
- Liv is a good plan for a normal night. Tonight the problem is the lift, not the cast list.

**The only way I'd go ahead**

- Someone competent has to lock the lift out and stop it being used, and you have to restage Signe's entrance so it doesn't touch the lift. Both need to be confirmed by about 18:25.
- With no technician, an unexplained failure and a fight scene on a newly changed set, I don't think you get that confirmation in time. If you can't get it, don't open.

**What to do in the next 20 minutes**

1. Decide by about 18:25 and tell the duty manager.
2. Before you press anything, ask whether the system can postpone or move the performance. The cancel button refunds every card booking and releases the seats, and that can't be undone. If there is no postpone option, press it anyway. A refund can be reversed by rebooking. Opening night on a failed lift can't be undone.
3. Message ticket holders straight away, by email and text, and put it on your social channels and the venue's. Say it is a postponement for safety reasons and that you will contact them with new dates. That is true and it protects you.
4. Call the six critics yourself. Critics who are phoned personally and offered another night usually take it well. A critic who turns up to a dark theatre, or watches a rushed night with an injured lead, will not.
5. Put tape and a sign on the lift, cut its power if you can safely, and tell the stage manager that nobody uses it tonight.
6. Write up the incident now while it is fresh, with times, who saw it and what was reported. Call your insurer in the morning.
7. Get the lift inspected first thing tomorrow, and don't reschedule until you have the inspector's findings. Signe's X-ray result decides when she can return, but it doesn't decide when the lift is safe.

**The cost**

- You lose a press night, you take the refund and rebooking hit on 540 seats, and the company is disappointed.
- All of that can be recovered with new dates and a decent apology. A second fall on press night, with a lead or an understudy hurt in front of critics, could not be recovered. That asymmetry is why this is cancel and not hold.
