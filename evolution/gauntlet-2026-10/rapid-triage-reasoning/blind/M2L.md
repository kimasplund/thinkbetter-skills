Don't let the tanker load this tank, and don't dump it. Hold the milk, get a sample to Penmorrow by 10:00, and sort out the 16:00 milking separately. Your wife's dilution idea is the one thing I'd rule out first.

**Why not "it'll dilute to nothing"**

The 20.2 litres from 274 aren't ordinary milk. She's mid-course on penicillin tubes, so her milk carries penicillin at levels far above the legal limit. Diluting 20 litres into 11,600 is about 1 in 575. Penicillin is detected at around 4 parts per billion, and a treated cow's milk can carry thousands of ppb. Divided by 575, that can plausibly still be above the detection line. I can't promise it fails, but I can't promise it passes either. The downside is a failed load: about 26,000 litres across four farms charged to you, plus the penalty. The upside is saving €5,000 of milk that you may well be able to claim for anyway. That's not a bet worth making.

**Right now (06:35 to 06:45)**

1. Tell the driver before he arrives, or as soon as he does: you have a possible antibiotic contamination and your tank is not to be collected today. He loses ten minutes at most and moves on to his next farm. Don't load it and hope the plant misses it.
2. Phone the co-op (the Penmorrow office or whoever handles supply) and tell them plainly what happened. Ask three things:
   - Will they take your sample and test it, and how soon?
   - If it tests clean, can they send a collection earlier than your next scheduled one?
   - If it's positive, how do they want the milk disposed of, and will they send a tanker for waste milk?
   Telling them yourself now will look much better than a failed load turning up later.
3. Take a sample. Make sure the agitator has been running for a few minutes so it's mixed, draw a clean sample, label it with the time and date, and chill it. If you keep a beta-lactam test kit on the farm, run it too. A positive tells you what you're dealing with. A negative from a quick kit doesn't clear it, so still send the lab sample.
4. Get the sample to the Penmorrow depot by 10:00. That gives you a lab result by mid-afternoon, and that result is also what your insurance needs for the discard claim.

**Don't open the outlet to the beck**

Running milk into the drain and beck is a pollution incident. Milk is very damaging to a watercourse, and you can be prosecuted for it whatever happened two years ago. It also destroys your insurance claim, because you'd have no lab result for milk you've already thrown away. Keep the tank until the result is in. If it's positive, the co-op or your environment agency contact can tell you the proper route, which is usually a waste-milk tanker or the slurry store if they say yes.

**The 16:00 milking**

The tank holds 11,600 and takes 13,000. Adding 2,900 at 16:00 brings it to 14,500, so you'd be about 1,500 litres over. You have three outcomes depending on the lab:

- Clean, and the co-op collects before 16:00: nothing changes, and you milk into an empty tank.
- Positive, and the tank is emptied before 16:00: you discard the tank properly and milk into a clean one. Your insurance covers it with the lab result.
- No result or collection by 16:00: the evening milk goes into the old 6,000-litre tank. It's been unused since spring, so give it a full wash and sanitise now, check that it cools below about 4 degrees, and make sure it's fully separate from the main tank. If it can't cool properly, ask the co-op what they want you to do rather than milking into a tank you can't keep cold.

**Stop it happening again**

- Cow 274 goes on the dump line from now until the withdrawal period is over. Check the tube label for the exact milk withhold time. It usually runs well past the last tube, so work out the date and write it on the wall.
- Put a new band or a leg spray mark on her now, and a clear sign in the parlour.
- Talk to Callum about what to do when a band falls off in the yard (hold the cow, check the record, ask) so this isn't a one-off.

**Assumptions**

I'm assuming you're in the UK or Ireland, that your co-op has the usual rules on antibiotic-positive milk, and that your tank isn't due for another pickup before this one. Ring the co-op and the insurer if either of those isn't right, and ask the insurer to confirm in writing that the claim stands.

**Short version:** keep the tank, stop the driver, ring the co-op, sample to Penmorrow by 10:00, and plan the 16:00 milking around the result.
