# Handover: blossom-end rot in compartment 3 (for Daniel Achterberg)

## The problem
Since mid-August, 6-9% of fruit has blossom-end rot (BER), nearly all of it in compartment 3. The crop runs to week 2. Your job is to choose and implement a fix.

## Where we stand
Two explanations are on the table. They probably work together, and we never separated them.

1. **Low night-time transpiration.** Compartment 3 runs 92-95% humidity at night, against 85-88% elsewhere. Its old vent motors only open to 40%. Humid air means little water moves through the plant at night, and calcium travels with that water, so the fruit gets too little. This was our best explanation. It fits the pattern, but we never tested it directly.
2. **High root-zone salinity.** Week 36 drip EC was 3.9 in compartment 3, against 2.8 (comp 1) and 3.0 (comp 2). High EC makes it harder for the plant to take up water, and BER gets worse with it. Nobody found out why compartment 3 is a full point higher. This is the loose end I would pull first.

## What the data says
- Feed solution calcium was on target in weeks 32, 34 and 36. Leaf tissue calcium is normal. So calcium is reaching the plant. The problem is getting it into the fruit.
- The week 36 EC figures are the only EC data we have. I don't know whether 3.9 is what the system delivers, or what builds up in the slab, or whether it is still 3.9 now. Check this on day one.

## What not to spend time on
- **Raising calcium nitrate.** The feed supplier's rep keeps recommending it. Calcium is already on target, and extra calcium nitrate pushes EC up further in the compartment where EC is already too high. It would probably make things worse.
- **Re-testing feed or leaf calcium.** It has been done three times and the answer is stable.
- **Foliar calcium sprays.** This is the usual BER rescue, but our buyer Grenmark Foods renewed us in their "Clean Leaf" programme in July. It allows no foliar applications of any kind after first fruit set. Breaking it risks the contract. This rules out calcium chloride and also any foliar product sold as a calcium or anti-stress treatment. Check with me before trying anything that touches leaves or fruit.

## Options, and the constraints on each
- **Bring compartment 3 EC down toward 2.8-3.0.** It is cheap, fast and fully inside your control, and it addresses the one clear difference between this compartment and the others. First find out why it is high: feed recipe, irrigation volume or frequency, drainage, or salt build-up in the slabs. Then correct it in steps and watch how fast new fruit responds. BER shows up on fruit that set a few weeks earlier, so wait before judging.
- **Raise night-time air movement and dry the air.** Look at horizontal air-flow fans, a small amount of pipe heat combined with whatever vent opening the motors allow, and the timing of the evening and early-morning routines. Heating costs gas, see below.
- **Check the old vent motors.** The 40% limit may be a mechanical or setting fault, not an age limit. Find out before anyone spends money.
- **Reduce fruit load on compartment 3 plants** (for example truss thinning) so each fruit gets a larger share of the available calcium. This is a lower-cost option. I haven't tried it.
- **New vent motors for compartment 3:** quoted at EUR 48,000 with a 10-week lead time. Ordering this week puts delivery around the end of December, leaving only a few weeks of crop. So they are unlikely to help this crop. They may still be worth buying for next season, which is a decision for me, not a fix for now. Don't count on them.

## Gas: the main limit on humidity control
Our fixed-price contract covers 410,000 m³ to the end of March. We have used 296,000, so 114,000 m³ remain. Anything above that is billed at spot, currently about four times the contract rate. Winter consumption will be higher than what we have used so far, and I haven't worked out whether the remaining volume lasts to March. Please do that calculation early, because it sets how much extra heating you can use to dry compartment 3. If heating for humidity is likely to push us into spot pricing, I'd want to weigh that cost against the value of the fruit it saves.

## What I don't know
- Why EC is higher in compartment 3.
- Whether the EC gap and the humidity gap each cause part of the BER, and how much each contributes.
- Whether anything else differs in compartment 3 (variety, plant age, crop load, irrigation layout). Please check.
- How the daily BER rate changes with the weather.

## Suggested first week
1. Get current EC and pH from the drip and the slab drain in all three compartments, and find out why compartment 3 is high.
2. Look at the vent motors and settings in compartment 3.
3. Work out gas use per week to the end of March against the 114,000 m³ remaining.
4. Start a simple BER log by compartment and date, so we can see whether changes work.
5. Talk to me before any spray, new purchase or contract-affecting change.

I'm confident compartment 3's problem is about getting calcium into fruit, not about calcium supply. I'm less sure of the split between humidity and EC, and the EC one is the quicker thing to test.
