**Short version:** Pattern C is not safe to take to the board as written. Both analysts rate it well (70% and 80%), but their work shares two weak points that neither flagged. I would put it at about 40% until those are fixed.

## State of play

**Problem:** With the MV Tamsin retiring in April, choose a timetable for the MV Elder alone on Vellmouth–Inch Harbour (55 min each way). The board decides in a week.

**What both agree on**
- Seven patterns were considered and three kept. Pattern C (near-hourly, first sailing from Vellmouth at 05:15 to connect with the 06:20 mainland train) is the front-runner.
- Owen's stress test found that C is workable on crew hours (split shift keeps everyone within the 12-hour duty limit) and on fuel (up 4%).

**Where they differ**
- **The MV Dunlin charter.** Priya rules it out because its stern ramp doesn't fit the Vellmouth linkspan. Owen rules it out only on cost and says it is "back on" if money appears. These are different reasons. If Priya is right, extra money doesn't help. Someone needs to check the ramp and linkspan fit directly. Until then, treat the charter as not available.

## Problems neither note caught

1. **The 05:15 departure breaks the harbour bylaw.** Vessel movements at Vellmouth are banned from 23:00 to 05:30. Priya cites the bylaw but her own first sailing falls inside the ban. Owen's stress test didn't cover it. The sailing can't leave before 05:30, and a 05:30 departure arrives at 06:25. That is after the 06:20 train. So the main purpose of Pattern C, getting islanders to that train, fails as designed. Options to explore:
   - Ask the harbour authority for a dispensation.
   - Overnight the Elder at Inch Harbour, if that is feasible, so the first run is a mainland-to-island one.
   - Accept a later train connection.
   - Treat the 06:20 train as a design target that may not be achievable.
2. **The passenger data is probably distorted, and both analysts relied on it.** The September 2024 survey was taken while the Corran Bridge was closed (March to November 2024). Ferry demand then was probably inflated because the road route round the estuary was cut. The Inch Harbour park-and-ride, opened in January, will also have changed how people reach the ferry. Both analysts used the same survey, so Owen's work is not independent confirmation of Priya's loadings. His agreement counts for less than it looks. If demand is lower than the survey says, a near-hourly service may be over-built. If the park-and-ride lifts mainland-side use, peaks could be higher than assumed.
3. **Check what "near-hourly" means with one vessel.** A round trip is at least 110 minutes before turnarounds. One boat cannot give an hourly sailing in each direction. It can give roughly one sailing an hour in total, alternating ends, or about one per direction every two hours. I assume the notes mean the former, but confirm this. The answer changes what islanders get and what the crew and fuel figures should be.

## How sure can we be

- **About 40% that Pattern C works as the board will read it.** The analysts' own figures are 70% and 80%. I've marked it down because it conflicts with the bylaw, because the shared survey is weak evidence, and because the two notes disagree on the charter.
- **Fairly firm:** the crew-hours and fuel results, if the timetable is similar after fixing the first sailing. A later start probably helps rather than hurts them, but Owen should rerun it.
- **Not yet known:** whether the Dunlin fits Vellmouth, whether a bylaw exemption is possible, and what current demand really is.

## What to do this week, in order

1. Confirm with the harbour authority whether the 05:30 rule is firm and whether an exemption has ever been granted.
2. Get a quick pass at current demand. Use recent ticket sales or counts, and compare them with the 2024 survey, in particular for the bridge closure and the park-and-ride.
3. Check the Dunlin ramp against the Vellmouth linkspan, so the charter is either dead or alive on facts.
4. Ask Owen to rerun crew hours and fuel on a corrected Pattern C, with the first sailing no earlier than 05:30.
5. Confirm what "near-hourly" means in practice.
6. If the 06:20 connection can't be saved, tell the board that now. Pattern C's main selling point goes with it, and the other two shortlisted patterns should be looked at again.

Since you're away again after the decision, I'd put items 1 to 3 in front of the board as stated risks. I wouldn't present C as settled.
