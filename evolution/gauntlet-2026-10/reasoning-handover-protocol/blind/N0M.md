# Handover: water-damaged survey maps (for Tobias)

**The job:** choose and contract a drying method for 212 hand-coloured survey maps (1790s-1840s) soaked nine days ago when a sprinkler head failed over the map store. They are in our chest freezers at -20 °C. The freezing stops further damage, so you have time to choose carefully. Don't rush into the cheapest option.

## The decision in one paragraph

Freeze-drying is the only commercial method we have a conservator's backing for. It is also the dearest. The 61 Brekke maps cannot leave the building without written consent from the lender, so any offsite plan covers at most our own 151 maps until that consent arrives. Insurance pays up to €6,000, and we have €1,100 of conservation budget on top. The practical route is probably: test run on two maps, then freeze-dry our 151 offsite, and settle the Brekke 61 separately.

## Facts

**Collection**
- 151 maps are ours outright.
- 61 maps are the Brekke deposit. The 2009 loan agreement, clause 7, says: "Deposited items shall not be removed from the Archive premises for any purpose without the prior written consent of the Lender."
- The Brekke executor can only be reached through their solicitor. Contact details are not in these notes, so check the deposit file.

**Conservation advice (Dr Aino Pell, freelance conservator, examined samples)**
- The maps have watercolour washes in gum arabic, and verdigris green on the boundaries.
- Heat is the danger. Kestrel's process thaws the maps and heats them to 40-60 °C. She says that is fine for office paper but will bleed and cockle water-soluble media like ours. This is why we dropped Kestrel.
- She thinks freeze-drying is "probably fine" for the verdigris. That is not a guarantee. She wants a test run on two of the least significant maps first.

**Money**
- Insurer: Valtera Mutual's adjuster says they reimburse drying up to €6,000 total, with any accredited vendor. This is a verbal statement and is not confirmed (see the to-do list).
- Our remaining conservation budget this year is €1,100.

Cost notes:
- Our 151 maps at Nordlicht come to €6,158, which is €158 over the insurance cap. The €1,100 budget covers that gap with room to spare.
- All 212 at Nordlicht come to €8,476, which is €2,476 over the cap. Even with the €1,100 budget we would be about €1,376 short.
- The flat €420 transport fee is probably per shipment. Ask Nordlicht whether the test run needs its own transport charge. If it does, two test maps cost about €496.

**On Kestrel's 30% offer:** the discount is welcome but it does not remove the reason we rejected them. The problem is the heat, not the price. Only reconsider Kestrel if Dr Pell, shown the discounted offer, says in writing that a lower-temperature setting would be safe for these media. Don't treat the offer as a reason to reopen it by default. If you do talk to them, get the discount in writing and ask for their actual temperature profile.

## What I'd do, in order

1. **Get the insurance cover in writing this week.** Ask Valtera Mutual's adjuster to confirm the €6,000 limit, the definition of "accredited vendor", and whether Nordlicht qualifies. Ask whether they want to approve the vendor before work starts. Don't sign anything with a vendor until you know.
2. **Ask the Brekke solicitor for written consent.** Request consent to move the 61 Brekke maps offsite for freeze-drying. Explain that the water damage is insured, that the alternative is in-house drying, and that waiting makes things worse. This may take weeks, so start now. If consent doesn't come, the Brekke maps are air-dried in-house or stay frozen. They must not go offsite.
3. **Book the test run with Dr Pell.** She chooses the two least significant maps from our 151 (not Brekke). Send them to Nordlicht for freeze-drying and have her inspect them on return. She should look for bleeding, cockling and any change in the verdigris. Her sign-off is the go/no-go for the rest.
4. **Contract Nordlicht for the remaining 149 of ours** if the test passes. Ask for a written quote with the per-map price, the transport terms, turnaround time, and what happens if an item is damaged in processing.
5. **Brekke maps.** If consent arrives and the test is good, add them to the Nordlicht job (budget and insurance needed, see the cost notes). If not, air-dry them in-house in batches of about 25 (3 batches). Ask Dr Pell whether air-drying is acceptable for gum arabic watercolour and verdigris before you start. I have not asked her that.

## What we don't know yet

- Whether Valtera Mutual will actually pay, and up to what amount. This is the biggest financial risk.
- Whether Nordlicht is on the insurer's accredited list.
- Whether the test run will go well. Dr Pell's "probably fine" is the only evidence we have.
- Whether the Brekke executor will consent, and how long it will take.
- Whether Dr Pell considers in-house air-drying safe for these media.
- Whether the maps have any mould or other damage from the nine days. Check the condition of the freezer contents when you next open them.

- Nordlicht's €420 transport is a single charge for one shipment of any size.
- Staff and materials cost for in-house drying is not in the figures above.
- Nothing has been signed with any vendor yet.
