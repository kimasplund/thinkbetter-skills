# Handover: blossom-end rot in compartment 3

**For:** Daniel Achterberg, head grower. **Task:** choose and implement a fix. **Crop runs to:** week 2.

## The problem

Since mid-August, 6-9% of fruit has blossom-end rot (BER). Nearly all of it is in compartment 3 (compartments 1 and 2 are largely unaffected).

## What we know

| Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Calcium supply is not the problem | Feed solution lab tests in weeks 32, 34 and 36 were on target every time. Leaf tissue calcium is normal. |
| Compartment 3 is much more humid at night | 92-95% night-time humidity, against 85-88% in the other compartments. Its old vent motors only open to 40%. |
| Compartment 3 has a much higher drip EC | Week 36: compartment 1 is 2.8, compartment 2 is 3.0, compartment 3 is 3.9. |

## Working explanation

BER is usually a calcium *delivery* problem, not a calcium *supply* problem. Calcium moves to the fruit with the transpiration stream. At night, when humidity is 92-95%, the plants transpire very little, so little calcium reaches the fruit even though there is plenty in the feed and the leaves.

The EC in compartment 3 is about 1 point above the others. High EC makes it harder for roots to take up water, and that adds to the same effect. We have not yet explained why it is higher. It could be a deliberate setting, a dosing or mixing fault, or a drift in that compartment's recipe. **Finding this out is your quickest first step.** I'd treat humidity and EC as the two likely contributors working together. We haven't tested that, and it is the main open question.

## What not to spend time on

- **Raising calcium nitrate.** Our feed supplier's rep keeps suggesting it. Calcium is already on target and the leaf tissue is normal. More calcium nitrate would also raise EC further in the compartment where EC is already too high, which could make things worse.
- **Re-testing feed calcium.** Three clean lab results are enough.
- **Foliar calcium sprays or any other foliar application.** Our main buyer, Grenmark Foods, renewed us in their "Clean Leaf" programme in July. It allows no foliar applications of any kind after first fruit set. Breaking it risks the contract.
- **Compartments 1 and 2.** They are working as expected and are useful as the reference.

## Options to weigh

1. **Bring compartment 3's EC down toward 3.0.** This is cheap and fast, and it falls within the feed recipe. First find out why it is at 3.9, and change it in steps while watching fruit and the crop's response.
2. **Manage night humidity with what we have.** Options include a heating and venting routine at night, and running the existing vents as far as they go. The limit is gas, which is the constraint below.
3. **Replace the vent motors in compartment 3.** The quote is €48,000 with a 10-week lead time. Ten weeks after ordering is already well into winter, leaving only a few weeks before the crop ends in week 2. Whether the spend pays back on this crop is doubtful. It may be better treated as an investment for next season, so check the payback before ordering.

## Gas constraint

The fixed-price contract covers 410,000 m³ to the end of March, and we have used 296,000, which leaves 114,000 m³. Anything above that is billed at spot, currently about four times the contract rate. Any plan that dries the air by heating and venting costs gas, and the burn rate rises through winter. Work out whether 114,000 m³ will last to the end of March before committing to a heating strategy.

## Open questions

- Why is compartment 3's EC 3.9, and has it been like that since August?
- Is the fruit loss concentrated at certain times or on certain trusses (for example, fast growth after a warm, bright day)?
- How much humidity can we remove with the current vents and a modest heating pulse, and what does that cost in gas?
- What does a 6-9% loss cost per week, to compare with the fix options?

## My confidence

I'm fairly confident (about 65%) that humidity is a main driver. I'm less sure about the EC contribution, because we haven't tested it. I'd start with the EC check, which costs almost nothing, before spending money on the vents.
