**Short version:** Pattern C is still the front-runner, but not as written, and I'd put our confidence at roughly 50%, not the 70-80% in the notes. Two problems need fixing before the board sees it. Both analysts' figures also rest on the same shaky data.

## State of play

- **Situation:** From April the MV Elder is alone on Vellmouth-Inch Harbour (55 minutes each way).
- **Pattern C:** Near-hourly service, with the first sailing from Vellmouth at 05:15 so islanders make the 06:20 mainland train. Priya picked it as best of three from seven patterns. Owen stress-tested it: crew stay within the 12-hour duty limit using a split shift, and fuel is up 4% on today.
- **Second vessel:** Chartering the MV Dunlin was rejected, but the two notes give different reasons (see below).

## Problems to resolve

1. **The 05:15 first sailing breaks the harbour bylaw.** Priya's own notes say there can be no vessel movements at Vellmouth between 23:00 and 05:30. A 05:15 departure is inside that window. Neither note spots this.
   - Moving to the earliest legal departure, 05:30, means arriving at 06:25. That is after the 06:20 train, so the main purpose of the early sailing is lost.
   - Owen's crew-hours and fuel checks probably assumed the 05:15 start, so they need re-running on a legal timetable.
   - Options: ask the harbour authority for a dispensation, or find another way to serve that train. I haven't tested either.

2. **The two notes disagree on why the Dunlin is out.**
   - Priya says it can't work: its stern ramp doesn't fit the Vellmouth linkspan.
   - Owen says it is out only on cost, and that it comes back if the board finds money.
   - These can't both be right. If Priya is correct, extra funding does not rescue the charter, and the board should not be told it might. Check the ramp and linkspan fit first; it is a factual question and quick to settle.

3. **The passenger data is probably unreliable, and both analysts used it.** Both used the September 2024 survey. The Corran Bridge, the road route round the estuary, was closed from March to November 2024, so that survey was taken when driving was much harder. Ferry demand then was probably inflated. Since then the bridge has reopened and the Inch Harbour park-and-ride has opened (January), which could shift demand either way. Using the same data in both notes means Owen's work does not independently confirm Priya's. His result is a check on crew and fuel, not on demand.

## How sure can we be

- Both analysts back Pattern C, which counts for something. But they share the same survey, so their agreement is worth less than it looks.
- The bylaw conflict means C as written can't run, so the stated 70% and 80% overstate where we stand.
- My view is roughly 50% that a Pattern C variant is the right recommendation. That could go up if the bylaw issue has a clean fix and fresh demand figures support near-hourly service. I'd put much less confidence in the exact 05:15 timing, the fuel figure and the crew roster until they are redone.

## Open items for your week

1. Settle the first-sailing time with the harbour authority, and whether the 06:20 train can still be served.
2. Confirm the Dunlin ramp and linkspan fit, and tell the board the right reason for the exclusion.
3. Get updated demand figures, or at least a quick recount, to replace the 2024 survey, taking account of the bridge reopening and the park-and-ride.
4. Have Owen redo crew hours and fuel on the corrected timetable.

Of these, I'd do the first two straight away; they are quick and could change the recommendation.
