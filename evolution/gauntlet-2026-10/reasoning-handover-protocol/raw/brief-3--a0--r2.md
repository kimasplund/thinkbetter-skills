# Handover for Daniel Achterberg: blossom-end rot in compartment 3

## 1. The problem in one paragraph

Since mid-August, 6-9% of fruit has blossom-end rot (BER), nearly all of it in compartment 3. Compartments 1 and 2 are essentially clean. The crop runs to week 2. Your job is to choose and implement a fix. Two things limit the choice: a gas contract that gets expensive if we overrun it, and a buyer programme that bans foliar sprays.

## 2. What we know (solid)

- **Calcium supply is not the problem.** Feed solution lab tests in weeks 32, 34 and 36 all showed calcium on target. Leaf tissue calcium is normal.
- **Compartment 3 differs from the others in two measured ways.**
  - Night-time humidity is 92-95%, against 85-88% elsewhere. The old vent motors only open to 40%.
  - Drip EC (week 36) was 3.9 in compartment 3, against 2.8 in compartment 1 and 3.0 in compartment 2.
- **Constraints.**
  - Gas: the fixed-price contract covers 410,000 m³ to the end of March, and we have used 296,000, so 114,000 m³ remain. Anything above that is billed at spot, currently about 4 times the contract rate.
  - Grenmark Foods "Clean Leaf" renewal (July): no foliar applications of any kind after first fruit set.
  - New vent motors for compartment 3: €48,000 quoted, 10-week lead time.

## 3. What we think (hypotheses, not proven)

1. **Low night-time transpiration from high humidity.** This is the explanation we favoured. Fruit calcium moves with the transpiration stream, and fruit is a weak competitor for it. Humid nights starve the fruit even when supply is fine. It fits the pattern (only the humid compartment is affected), but we never confirmed it with an intervention.
2. **High root-zone salinity in compartment 3.** I want to flag this plainly, because we may have under-weighted it. An EC of 3.9 is about 1 point above the other compartments. High EC cuts water uptake and calcium delivery to fruit, and it is a classic BER driver independent of humidity. We noted the reading but did not establish why it is high or whether it is the feed EC, the drain or the slab EC.
3. **Both together.** This is plausible, since both point the same way in the same compartment. Treat the humidity-only story as our best guess and not as settled. I would put it at roughly 60% likely to be the main cause, with EC the most likely co-factor or alternative.

Caveat on the data: the EC reading and the lab tests are 6 or more weeks old. Re-measure before you act on any of it.

## 4. What I would do first (cheap, fast, discriminating)

1. **Re-measure EC in compartment 3** at feed, at the dripper and in the drain, and compare with compartments 1 and 2. Find out why it is 3.9: a dosing or injection fault, a blocked or unequal emitter set, a different recipe, or slab salt build-up. This is a quick check, and if it is a fault it may be the whole answer.
2. **Bring compartment 3 EC down toward the other compartments' level** and watch the BER rate over the next 2-3 weeks. BER shows up in fruit that set weeks earlier, so judge by newly set trusses, not by the existing fruit.
3. **Work on night humidity without the new motors.**
   - Test horizontal air-flow fans.
   - Test a modest minimum pipe temperature or heat-and-vent pulses in the evening and pre-dawn.
   - Check the screen and ventilation strategy for compartment 3.
   - Log night-time RH and BER by truss date, so you can tell which intervention worked.
4. **Keep a simple record.** Record BER percentage per compartment per week, night RH, EC and the date of each change. We had too little of this.

## 5. Decisions and tradeoffs you will have to make

- **Gas.** Extra night heating to cut humidity burns gas. We have 114,000 m³ left on contract. I cannot tell from here whether that covers the rest of the season at our normal burn plus any extra, because I do not have our monthly usage. Work that out in your first days, as it sets how much night heating you can afford. Anything over contract costs about 4 times as much, so a humidity fix that overruns the contract may cost more than the fruit it saves.
- **Vent motors (€48,000).** If ordered now, 10 weeks of lead time plus installation lands around the end of the crop (week 2). They will therefore give little or no benefit this season. Treat them as a decision for next season's budget, unless you can show that this season's losses justify an early order. Do not order them as a rescue for this crop.
- **What the losses are worth.** At 6-9% of fruit, mostly in one compartment, put a euro figure on it first. Any fix costing more than the fruit it saves is not worth it.

## 6. What not to spend time on

- **Raising calcium nitrate.** The feed supplier's rep keeps recommending it. Calcium is already on target in feed and leaf. More calcium nitrate would also raise EC, and compartment 3 EC is already high, so it could make things worse. Do not do it unless a new test shows a calcium shortfall.
- **Foliar calcium sprays.** They are the usual quick BER rescue, but Grenmark's Clean Leaf programme forbids any foliar application after first fruit set. Breaching it risks the renewal with our main buyer. Do not use them, and do not use any other foliar product.
- **Re-testing feed calcium and leaf calcium again and again.** Three clean feed tests and a normal leaf test are enough.
- **Compartments 1 and 2.** They are performing fine. Use them as your comparison, not as a problem.
- **The new vent motors as a short-term fix** (see section 5).

## 7. Open questions I could not answer

- Why is compartment 3 EC 3.9? Is that the feed, the drain or the slab, and is it a fault or a recipe?
- Is the 60% weight on humidity right? The EC finding could shift it substantially.
- What is our real monthly gas burn, and what does the remaining 114,000 m³ cover?
- How much fruit value are we losing, in euros, in compartment 3?
- Does Grenmark's rule cover non-nutrient foliar products such as fungicides or biologicals? I have read it as "no", and you should confirm it with them before using anything.

## 8. Suggested first week

- Day 1-2: re-measure EC and run the compartment 3 dosing and emitter check. Get the gas burn figures and the BER-by-truss-date record.
- Day 3-5: adjust EC, start the air-flow and night-temperature trial, set up the logging.
- End of week: review, and tell me which of the two hypotheses the first data favours.

Assumption: I have taken today's date as mid-October for the motor lead-time arithmetic. If it is later, the case against ordering motors for this crop only gets stronger.
