My recommendation is not to buy any of the three kilns to fix the crazing, and not to let Friday's deadline force the decision. Your own data points away from the kiln as the cause.

**Why the kiln is probably not the problem**

- Crazing went from 2% to 9% starting in May. The one thing that changed is the clay body, switched in April. The glaze recipe is the same, and the kiln's elements were replaced in January, so nothing about the kiln changed around May.
- Your two-year-old test kiln, a different machine, shows about 8% since May. If the old kiln were at fault, the test kiln should still be near 2%. Both kilns show the same jump, which points to something common to both: the new clay body.
- Crazing is a glaze-fit problem. The glaze shrinks more than the clay body as it cools, so it goes into tension and cracks. A different clay body changes thermal expansion, so a glaze that fit the old body for six years can fail on the new one. The delay until the dishwasher fits this: crazing often appears over days or weeks, and thermal cycling and moisture speed it up.

I can't be certain from here. But the evidence is strong enough that a €15,000 to €22,000 purchase should not be justified as a crazing fix.

**What to do this week (cheap, and it settles the question)**

1. Run a test batch of the new clay body with your glaze, plus the old clay body if you still have any, through the same firing. Do the same in both kilns. If the old body stays clean and the new one crazes in both kilns, the cause is confirmed.
2. Test accelerated crazing on samples: boil-and-chill cycles, or an autoclave if a local lab has one. This gives results in days rather than weeks.
3. Call your clay supplier and ask for the body's thermal expansion data and whether they changed the formulation. Ask your glaze supplier or a ceramics technician about adjusting the glaze, for example less sodium or potassium flux, more silica, or a small alumina or boron adjustment. Alternatively, go back to the old clay body and absorb the price increase. Compare that cost to 7 extra points of remakes. At 2,400 pieces a week, 7% is about 170 pieces a week made twice, plus the lost capacity, which is likely why you are turning away accounts.
4. A slower, longer cooling cycle can modestly help glaze fit. Kiln B's programmable controlled cooling is relevant here, but it is a mitigation. It does not replace fixing the clay and glaze match. Check whether your current kiln's controller can slow the cooling already.
