## What the timeline allows (day 0 = today, festival = day 49)

**Saison, brewed in FV4 today:**
- Brew day, 10 days primary, 16 days conditioning and 1 day to keg comes to about day 28.
- That leaves roughly three weeks of slack.
- Brewing in FV2 doesn't work. It frees up on day 23, so the beer would be ready around day 51, two days after the festival.
- FV4 is the only route. It also holds 10 hl, and you pour about 5 hl on draft, so the volume is fine.
- Draft needs no registry approval, because the registry only deals with packaged product.

**Saison in cans for the festival: not possible, even in the best case.**
- The application needs the final lab ABV, which you can't have before primary fermentation finishes (about day 11).
- Then there are 35 calendar days at the registry, which puts approval around day 46.
- Then 5 days of can printing, which puts cans around day 51.

**Amber renamed, with new can art: possible, but only if you apply almost immediately.**

- Approval lands around day 35 and printing around day 40. That leaves about 9 days of slack for packaging.
- Every day you delay the filing comes straight out of that slack. Past about day 9, it's gone.
- A public holiday or a registry query could eat the rest, so treat this as a week-one decision.

## A range of options

**A. Both, on separate tracks (my pick).**
- Marguerite brews the saison in FV4 now, as a draft-only festival debut.
- Dov files the amber rebrand this week, with new cans at the merch tent.

**C. Saison cans as the sequel.**

**D. Rebrand only, no saison.**

**E. Saison only, amber untouched.**
- This is the safest for the brewhouse, but it leaves Dov with nothing and sharpens the sides.

**F. Amber twist that needs no registry.**

**G. Smoked plum as an amber variant.**
- A small amount of the saison could be blended or served as a "split pour" next to the amber, so the two get sold together rather than against each other.

**H. Let customers choose.**
- Pour the saison and a draft-only amber side by side, and put a vote token or a scorecard at the exit.

**I. Pilot-batch fallback.**
- Hold a taste checkpoint at about day 11 to 14.

## Getting Marguerite and Dov to agree

1. **Take the either/or off the table first.** Show them the calendar above. Most of the heat comes from each of them believing only one idea can win. Once they see both fit, the argument becomes about sequencing, which is much easier.
2. **Give each person ownership of their own win and a say in the other's.**
   - Marguerite owns the saison brew and sets its quality bar.
   - Dov owns the amber rebrand and the launch story for both beers.
   - Each reviews the other's work once, at a fixed point.
3. **Name the risk Dov is actually worried about, and answer it.**
   - Draft-only means no registry exposure, no can run and no inventory left over.
   - A tasting checkpoint on the saison gives him a clear exit.
   - Ask him what failure at the festival would look like to him. His answer is likely narrower than "new recipe is risky".
4. **Name what Marguerite is actually worried about.** It is probably being overruled, or her beer being treated as a nice-to-have. Putting her brew first on the timeline, with the date she needs, tells her it is a priority.
5. **Agree the success measure before the festival.** For example, pints sold per hour for the saison, can sales for the amber, and sentiment scores from the exit card. Two people who agreed the scoreboard in advance don't re-argue the result.
6. **Run a joint tasting early.** Do it at the day 11 to 14 checkpoint with both of them in the room, plus a few staff who haven't taken a side. Shared data and shared tasting calms things faster than more discussion.
7. **Make the decision yourself, quickly, and own it.** Two weeks of circling is costly, and the filing deadline is real. Tell them: "Both go ahead. Here is the plan, here are the dates, and here is who owns what." Then say plainly that you value them both and that this isn't a ruling for one against the other.
8. **Tell the rest of the team the same story at once.** A single short message prevents the side-picking from hardening.
