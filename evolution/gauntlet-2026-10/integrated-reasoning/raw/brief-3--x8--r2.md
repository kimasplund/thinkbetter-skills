## Options for the festival

1. **Do both, in different formats.** The saison runs on draft only, brewed in FV4. The renamed Harvest Amber goes out on cans at the merch tent. This is the plan I would lead with.
2. **Draft-only saison as a festival exclusive.** "Only here, only this weekend" is a good story. The scarcity helps rather than hurts, and you can apply for a can label afterwards using the real ABV for a later release.
3. **Dov's relabel only.** It is the safe route. It still has to be submitted this week, and it gives up the festival buzz of a new beer.
4. **Saison only, with the amber unchanged.** This is the least work and the most risk to the story, and it makes Dov the loser.
5. **Amber story without a registry filing.** Put the new "Harvest Amber" name and artwork on tap handles, menus, posters, glassware, tees and tent signage. None of that is a can label. Check that the registry doesn't regulate draft naming, then let the festival crowd meet the new look before cans carry it.
6. **A split pour.** Run the saison and the amber side by side as a tasting flight. Add a "pick your festival beer" vote, and use the results to decide on cans for next year.
7. **Name the saison together.** Marguerite and Dov co-name it, and Dov writes the tap-room and tent copy. His marketing then serves the beer instead of competing with it.
8. **A fallback in case the amber relabel slips.** Keep enough stock of the current cans to cover the weekend. If approval is late, you sell the old cans and show the new art on merch and signage only.
9. **A small saison can run after the festival.** Once you have the real ABV and a free big fermenter, a later can release is possible. That lets Marguerite see the beer as the start of a range, not a one-off.

- **Show them the calendar first.** The two weeks of argument assumed they were competing for the same slot. They aren't. The saison goes in FV4 and on draft. The amber relabel goes through the registry and onto cans. Put this on one page and walk them through it together.

  - Marguerite gets her beer in front of the biggest crowd of the year.
  - Dov gets the rebrand and a real say in how the saison is presented.
  - Dov's concern was risk. A draft-only 10 hl batch limits it: if the beer disappoints, nothing is on a shelf and no label has been printed. The amber remains the safe anchor of your offer.
- **Set go/no-go gates in advance.**
  - Around day 11, check gravity and flavour at the end of primary.
  - Around day 20, hold a joint tasting.
  - If the beer misses the mark, Marguerite pulls it, and you still have the amber on cans.
  - Agreeing the criteria now means nobody is arguing about it later.
- **Set a deadline.** The registry clock forces a decision. Decide this week and submit the amber application within a few days. Tell them the reason is the calendar, not a verdict on either idea.
- **You make the call and say why.** Pick the plan in one short meeting, thank both for the work they put into their arguments, and write down who owns what. People rarely resent a decision when they see their own concern reflected in it.
- **Get ahead of side-taking.** Tell the wider team that both ideas are going ahead. This ends the sense of a winner and a loser.
