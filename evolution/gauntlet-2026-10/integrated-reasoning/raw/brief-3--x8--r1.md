## What the timeline allows (today = day 0, festival = day 49)

**Saison in FV4 (10 hl, free now)**
- Brew day, 10 days primary, 16 days conditioning and 1 day to keg add up to about 27 days.
- If you brew this week, it is kegged around day 30. That leaves roughly 2.5 weeks of slack for a slow or stalled fermentation, which saisons and fruit/smoke additions can have.
- The last safe brew date is about day 14 (kegged day 41). I'd treat day 7 as the real deadline.
- FV2 is not an option. It frees up on day 23, and 23 + 27 = 50, one day too late.
- 10 hl is twice what you poured on draft last year (5 hl), so the saison can cover the festival taps with some left over.

**Saison in cans is not possible.**
- The registry needs the final lab-measured ABV, so you can't apply until fermentation is done (about day 10 at the earliest).
- Then there are 35 calendar days of approval and 5 days of printing. That puts cans at about day 50 at the very best, and realistically after the festival.
- A rushed application on day 10 still lands at day 50. That is already too late, and the plan would have no room for a rejection.

**Dov's amber rename and new artwork is feasible, but only if it starts now.**
- The ABV is unchanged and known, so you can apply immediately.
- Approval takes 35 days and printing takes 5, so cans are ready around day 40 if you apply today.
- The latest possible submission is about day 9. After that you have no margin at all.
- There is no time for a resubmission. The application needs to be right the first time, so have someone check the label text and ABV statement against the registry's rules before it goes in.

**Assumptions to confirm with the registry this week**
- I'm assuming kegged beer for the festival taps isn't "packaged product" under the registry's rules, so draft needs no approval. They only deal with packaged product, but confirm that kegs are outside it.

## Creative options for the festival

1. **The split (my recommendation).** Pour the smoked plum saison on draft as a festival debut. Sell the rebranded Harvest Amber in cans at the merch tent. Each person owns a headline product.
2. **Saison as a festival-only draft.** The scarcity helps: "You can only drink this here, this weekend." Announce that cans follow after the festival, and apply for the label as soon as the lab ABV is in. Cans could be on sale about 5-6 weeks after the festival.
3. **Side-by-side flight.** Offer "Old Harvest and New Harvest" as a tasting flight: the amber under its new name next to the saison. Collect votes with tokens, a QR form or a tally board. The festival becomes the experiment that settles the debate with real data.
4. **Use the extra saison volume.** About 5 hl beyond festival needs could go to the taproom, a few local bars, or a pre-festival keg preview for club members and press. That builds buzz and also gives you real tasting feedback before the event.
5. **Draft-only twists on the amber.** Because draft doesn't need label approval, you can run limited one-offs: an amber served with a splash of the saison, a plum-hopped or oak-aged variant, or a firkin. These cost nothing in registry time.
6. **Dov's artwork beyond the can.** Put the new artwork on tap handles, posters, tasting glasses, t-shirts, tote bags and festival tokens. None of these need registry approval, so the rebrand is visible all weekend even if the can arrives at day 40.
7. **A local-story angle.** The festival is a harvest celebration. Source the plums from a Vellmoor-area orchard or ask the orchard to co-host a tasting. The smoked saison and the "Harvest" branding then tell one story instead of competing.
8. **A "pilot series" label on the saison.** Frame it as the first of a numbered small-batch series from FV4. That gives Dov a marketing story (collectible, limited, you were there) and keeps the risk of a new recipe small and visible.
9. **A fallback plan, so the new recipe isn't a gamble.** If the saison fermentation goes wrong, you still pour the amber on all the taps and the festival is unaffected. Set a tasting gate around day 12 and again around day 24. That directly answers Dov's "too risky" worry.
10. **A reveal moment.** Launch the new amber name and the saison together on festival day, with a short staff briefing and a one-line story for each.

## Getting Marguerite and Dov to agree

**Reframe the argument.** The two ideas aren't competing for the same slot. One is a draft debut and the other is a can relaunch. Start the conversation with the timeline above, because it replaces opinions with facts both of them can check.

**Give each a lane and a deadline.**
- Marguerite brews the saison within the week and owns the brew plan and the tasting gates.
- Dov submits the amber label application within about 3-5 days (hard limit day 9) and owns the rebrand and the festival marketing for both products.
- Each of them is accountable for something that matters, and each depends on the other doing theirs.

**Address the real worries.**
- Dov's worry is risk. The fallback plan, the tasting gates and the fact that the amber relabel proceeds regardless all contain it.
- Marguerite's worry is probably creative recognition. A top-billing draft debut at the biggest event of the year gives her that.
- Ask each to state the other's best argument in their own words before you decide anything. It lowers the temperature, and it matters most because the team is starting to pick sides.

**Decide quickly and announce it together.**
- Hold one 30-minute meeting with both of them in the room, and settle it that day. The longer it drags, the less time the amber label has and the more the team polarises.
- Announce the decision to the team jointly, with both of them credited. Say plainly that neither idea won. The calendar decided the format, and both ideas are going to the festival.
- Agree in advance what you'll look at afterwards (flight votes, sell-through of each product, comments) and set a date to review it. Next year's decision then comes from results rather than from a two-week stalemate.

**One caution.** If the amber application slips past about day 9, Dov's can goes to the post-festival period, and then the split plan loses half its value. Treat that date as the real deadline for this whole discussion.
