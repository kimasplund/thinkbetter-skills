## The options

1. **Split the festival by format (my recommendation).** The smoked plum saison is a draft-only debut from FV4. The renamed Harvest Amber is the canned, take-home product at the merch tent. Marguerite gets her debut. Dov gets his new can on the year's biggest sales weekend.
   - FV4 holds 10 hl and last year's whole draft run was about 5 hl. The saison can therefore be the headline tap without taking over the festival.
   - Draft-only also helps Dov's risk worry. If the saison flops, you have lost one pilot-sized batch and no new can inventory.

2. **Dov's rename alone, with no saison.** This is the safest choice and it is feasible. It also gives up the one new idea the brewing team is excited about, and it keeps the argument alive.

3. **Marguerite's saison alone, on draft, with the amber unchanged.** This is feasible, but it spends goodwill with Dov and leaves the merch tent with nothing new to sell in cans.

4. **Make the saison scarce on purpose.** Run it as a "tap-only, one keg at a time" release. Advertise the end of each keg and sell it in small pours or flights next to the amber. A rationed beer feels like an event, and it hides any risk about volume.

5. **Pair them.** Put a "Harvest Amber and Smoked Plum" flight or pairing on the tasting menu. Amber and a smoked fruit saison make an interesting contrast, and one product sells the other.

6. **Use the festival as the saison's market test.** Hand out tasting cards that ask for a score, a name vote and "would you buy this in a can?" Use the results for the can decision, so the saison earns its place instead of being argued into one.

7. **Tease the saison on the new amber can.** Put a small "something smoky is coming" line or a QR code on the amber label. Because the label is going through approval anyway, this costs nothing extra. Check with the registry that the text is acceptable.

8. **Brew a second saison batch in FV2 on day 23.** It would be ready on day 51, just after the festival. File the saison can label around day 11, when the first batch gives a measured ABV. Approval lands about day 46 and print about day 51. That gives you a saison can launch a few days after the festival, in time to keep the momentum. Two caveats. The label ABV has to match both batches closely. This also uses FV2 for something other than whatever it is booked for now.

9. **Pilot-size insurance.** If the saison batch goes wrong, FV4 has to restart by about day 18 to be safe, and day 21 at the very latest. Decide on day 10, when primary fermentation finishes, with a taste and gravity check. If it is off, rebrew in FV4 straight away. This is why the decision cannot wait.

10. **Growler or crowler fills.** Filling these at the tent might count as packaged product and need approval. Treat this as off unless the registry says otherwise.

11. **Blend amber with saison for a one-off festival cask.** This is draft-only and needs no label, so it might work. It is a stunt rather than a plan. Don't can it, because a changed product could trigger a new approval.
