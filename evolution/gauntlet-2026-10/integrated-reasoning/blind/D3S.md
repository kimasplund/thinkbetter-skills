The engine is almost certainly not buggy, so IT is right and Odile is wrong. The cause is the overbooking factor, which is a setting and not a fault. I'm about 85% sure of this from the numbers you gave me. The check below would take you about an hour and would confirm it.

**What the engine does**
- 62 x 1.06 = 65.72, which rounds down to 65 units sold per sailing.
- At a 1.8% no-show rate, you'd expect 65 x 0.982 = 63.8 units to turn up for 62 units of deck.
- So on a fully sold weekend sailing, the engine is built to put about two units too many on the pier. It does exactly what it was configured to do.

**Why it "always worked" until now**
- The 6% was sized for a 7.5% no-show rate. That rate was measured from November to February, when people cancel for weather and for the sake of cancelling. Summer weekend bookers rarely do.
- The right factor is roughly the no-show rate you actually expect, so about 2% now. A 6% factor was only safe while no-shows really ran near 6 to 7%.
- Your timetable change made it worse. With one fewer sailing on each of Friday and Saturday, the same demand is squeezed into fewer sailings, so more of them sell to the limit. A full sailing is the only kind where overbooking can bite. That is why every incident is on a Friday or Saturday.
- Last summer's three incidents fit this. The setting was already too aggressive, but with more sailings, fewer weekend sailings sold out.

**The campervan problem is probably part of this**
You've parked it as a separate issue, but I'd put it in the same investigation. If campervans are booked as "car", the engine counts them as 1 unit when they take 2. A few mis-declared vehicles on a sailing sold to 65 units can push the real deck load past 62 even with zero no-shows. It may be the reason some of the 11 sailings overflowed by more than the arithmetic above predicts.

**How to confirm (this week)**
For each of the 11 sailings, compare units sold, units that actually turned up, and the true unit count after correcting vehicle types (use the deckhands' loading records or gate photos).
- If turned-up units, after correcting vehicle types, are only 1 to 3 above 62, the factor is the cause. This is what I expect.
- If some sailings overflow by much more than that, vehicle mis-declaration is a big contributor.
- Only if sold units on a sailing exceed 65 would there be a genuine engine fault. In that case IT would need to look at how it counts vehicles and rounds.

**What to do before the bank-holiday weekend**
1. Set the overbooking factor to 0 for Friday and Saturday sailings now. If you want a little overbooking back, 0.02 is the most the 1.8% figure supports, and even that will still leave you occasionally over.
2. Keep the higher factor, if you want it, only on low-demand weekday and winter sailings, where it can't cause pier-side overflow.
3. Make the booking form's vehicle type a required choice with no default, and show the car-equivalent rule next to it. This is cheap and can go in before August.
4. Reset the factor from a rolling measure of no-shows by season and day, not a single winter number from 2023.

**The cost**
At 62 units, 65 sold versus 62 sold is about 3 units, perhaps 3 cars, per full sailing. Compared with stranded passengers, bad reviews and council complaints, that's a small price.

**For the meeting with IT and Odile**
- Tell IT the engine is correct, and ask them to help pull sold, arrived and corrected-unit numbers for the 11 sailings.
- Tell Odile the setting is stale. That's no blame on her, because the 2023 rate was a reasonable method for winter. What changed was the season, the timetable and the vehicle mix.

My main assumption is that your 1.8% figure is measured on weekends only, as you described. If it's an all-days figure, the weekend rate could be even lower, which makes the case for lowering the factor stronger.
