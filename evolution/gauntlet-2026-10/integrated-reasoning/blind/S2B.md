Congratulations on the growth. Before you pick a kiln, though, I don't think any of the three will fix the crazing, and I'd hold off on presenting one as "the winner" on Friday.

**Why the kiln is probably not the cause (about 80% confident)**

- Your small test kiln is two years old and has crazed at about 8% since May. Your 14-year-old kiln runs at about 9%. If the old kiln were the problem, the new one should be clean. It isn't.
- The timing fits the clay body. You switched suppliers in April, and rejects went from 2% to 9% from May. Your glaze recipe and your elements (replaced in January) both predate the jump.
- Crazing usually means the glaze shrinks more than the clay body as it cools, so the glaze is under tension and cracks. A new clay body with a different thermal expansion under the same six-year-old satin white is the textbook way to get this.
- Late crazing after dishwasher cycles fits too. Repeated heat and moisture finish off a glaze that was already marginal.

Better cooling control (Kiln B) could shift things a little, but it won't correct a body and glaze that don't match.

**What I'd do this week, and it costs almost nothing**

1. Ask the new clay supplier for the body's thermal expansion data and the maturation range. Compare it with your old body.
2. Fire a small test batch of the old clay body with the current glaze, in both kilns. If it comes out clean in both, the clay is confirmed as the cause.
3. Try a few glaze adjustments on the new body, such as slightly more silica or less flux, or a modest change to the firing and cooling schedule. A ceramics technician or glaze consultant can often sort this out in days.
4. Do a dishwasher-cycle test on the test pieces yourselves, so you find crazing in days, not weeks.

If the old body fixes it, compare the saving from the cheaper clay against the cost of the remakes. At 9% rejects you are remaking about 215 pieces a week, versus about 50 at 2%. That is roughly 165 pieces a week of lost capacity, about 7% of output, plus the free remakes, shipping and goodwill. The cheaper clay is probably costing you money.

- B is cheap and comes with a useful feature, but it won't solve either of your two problems.

**On Friday's deadline**

A quote expiring is a sales tactic, not a reason. Ask all three suppliers to extend their quotes by 2-4 weeks. Most will, and if they won't, a rushed purchase of a €18,000 machine on a guess about the cause would be the more expensive mistake. If Kiln A would still be your choice after the clay is sorted out, that decision loses nothing by waiting a few weeks.
