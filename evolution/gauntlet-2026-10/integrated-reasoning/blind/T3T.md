I can't give you a kiln winner that fixes the crazing, because the evidence says the kiln probably isn't causing it. I'd keep Friday's deadline from driving the decision.

**Why the kiln is unlikely to be the cause (roughly 80-85% confident)**

- Crazing went from 2% to 9% starting in May. The clay body changed in April, and the glaze recipe stayed the same. Crazing is usually a thermal expansion mismatch between glaze and clay body. A new body under an unchanged glaze is the textbook way to create one.
- Your two-year-old test kiln shows about 8% since May. A different, newer kiln gives nearly the same rate, so the old kiln isn't what's driving it. A kiln fault would not usually show up equally in two machines.
- The new elements in January don't fit the timing either. The problem began in May, not after January.
- The delayed failures, appearing after dishwasher cycles, are typical of a marginal glaze fit that gets worse with repeated heat and moisture stress.

What would change my mind: if the test kiln fires a different clay body, or fires very different schedules, the comparison is weaker. Please check that.

**What I'd do before Friday**

1. Run a quick test this week. Fire a few pieces of the same glaze on the old clay body and the new one in the same kiln load. If the old body doesn't craze and the new one does, you have your answer, usually within a firing or two.
2. Ask your clay supplier for the new body's thermal expansion data, and ask your glaze supplier or a ceramics technician about adjusting the glaze (for example, a slightly lower-expansion glaze, or more silica) to fit. This typically costs far less than any kiln.
3. Consider a dishwasher or thermal shock test on a few pieces so you can catch crazing in days rather than waiting two to three weeks for restaurant reports.
4. Quantify the cost. At 2,400 pieces a week, 9% rejects is about 215 pieces a week remade for free, versus about 50 at 2%. That's roughly 165 extra pieces a week, which is also lost capacity. Fixing the crazing may free up a good share of the capacity you think you need before you buy anything.
5. Ask Kiln A's supplier for an extension of a week or two, or whether the price holds. Quotes often have more flexibility than the stated deadline suggests. If they refuse, losing one quote is cheaper than an €18,400 purchase made on the wrong diagnosis.
