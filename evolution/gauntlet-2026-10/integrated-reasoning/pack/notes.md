# Pack notes

**Brief 1 (pottery kiln purchase).** The request asks for a best-option comparison under a Friday deadline, with three partners pulling in different directions. The decisive evidence sits in a "background" paragraph. The two-year-old test kiln crazes at nearly the same rate as the old main kiln, and the rise follows the April clay body switch, with delayed, post-dishwasher crazing. So this is a diagnosis problem, and no kiln fixes it (k1). The quote deadline is a salesman's clock, not a real constraint (k2). The partner disagreement covers three separate questions (defect, capacity, new product line), which need decoupling rather than one negotiated vote (k3). Fixing the reject rate also frees about 7% of capacity, which undercuts the assumed case for the larger kiln (k4). k1 and k4 are the ones a fast comparison-style answer is most likely to miss. The hype and skeptical framings carry the same facts and numbers.

**Brief 2 (island ferry overbooking).** The user frames this as a bug hunt and a referee call between IT and revenue. The arithmetic (floor(62 x 1.06) = 65 bookings at 1.8% no-shows) shows the engine is behaving as configured, so the miscalibrated factor is the cause (k1). The campervan complaint is deliberately framed as an unrelated loading gripe. It is actually a second, independent source of oversells that remains after the factor is fixed (k2). The "who's right" dispute hides the real question, a policy trade-off between overbooking revenue and stranding islanders, which belongs to management (k3). The jump from 3 to 11 incidents is explained by the timetable cut concentrating sell-outs, not by any system change (k4). k2 and k4 are the hard ones.

**Brief 3 (brewery festival launch).** The user explicitly asks for a wide range of creative options and help with the two people's disagreement, which invites brainstorming and mediation. The problem is actually a dependency timeline. The label application needs the final ABV, so a canned saison cannot make day 49 (k1). The registry only covers packaged product, so the saison can still go on draft (k2). The obvious fermenter is too late, but the dismissed 10 hl pilot fermenter is big enough for about 5 hl of pours (k3). Together these show that both proposals can run at once, with concrete start-by dates, so no compromise is needed (k4). k2 and k3 only surface after the canned route is ruled out, and a quick answer tends to miss them.
