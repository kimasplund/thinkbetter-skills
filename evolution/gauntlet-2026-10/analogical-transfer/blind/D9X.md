**Short answer:** the gym is the wrong model for Thrale, and your own numbers show why. A better model is an off-peak pass, closer to a ski pass, a rail off-peak card or ClassPass. It sells the seats you can't otherwise sell and keeps the seats you already sell. The licensing terms also decide the design, so start there.

## Why the gym model doesn't fit

A gym makes its money from members who pay and rarely come. Its marginal cost per visit is close to zero, and it limits crowding with class caps. A theatre membership is the reverse. Nearly everyone who joins will use it, because attending is the whole appeal. Your peak nights are already full, which is the gym's 6pm rush. Maren's proposal lets members book any performance two weeks ahead, so they would fill Friday and Saturday seats before full-price buyers do.

Two groups you already have would take the offer, and the sums are bad.

- **Your 1,800 frequent attenders.** They bought about a third of your single tickets. On my arithmetic that is roughly 27,000 tickets, about 15 each, or around €915,000 at €34. This probably includes guests they bring. Unlimited at €180 a year brings that to €324,000 if all of them switch, a loss of about €590,000. You would need roughly 3,300 new members who displace no paying seat just to break even.
- **Your 1,100 subscribers.** They pay €182 for seven seats. Unlimited at €180 is cheaper and gives them far more, so they would all move. Revenue stays flat, but they would take more seats, and many of those seats would be the peak ones.

The claim that it draws in people who would never pay €34 is probably true. But those people would fill the seats your best customers now pay for.

## The licensing terms are the real constraint

Any admission issued at under half its face value counts as complimentary, and complimentary admissions are capped at 4% of the house. That is 20 seats a performance.

An unlimited €15 membership falls under half face value after about one visit a month. At €34 face value, half is €17. So almost every member admission would count as complimentary. The cap would then limit you to about 20 member seats per performance, and those seats would also have to cover press, access and school comps. Unlimited access at €15 can't be sold as designed. Nor can any plan where the amount you can assign to each admission drops as the member attends more.

Two things follow.

1. Each admission must be worth at least half the face value of the seat it uses. A pass that works in credits does this. A flat unlimited fee does not.
2. Ask your rights holders now, not after the board. Ask how membership revenue counts toward gross box office for the 10% royalty, and whether an agreed per-credit value would count as a paid admission. Get the answer in writing. I can't tell from here how they will read it.

## What the other fields teach

| Field | What they do | What it means for Thrale |
|---|---|---|
| **Cinema unlimited cards** (Cineworld Unlimited, AMC A-List) | Flat fee, fixed per-visit limits, usually exclusions. MoviePass collapsed because it paid venues full price for every visit while charging a low flat fee. | The fee has to cover the cost of each visit. Here that cost is the 10% royalty and the seat you could have sold. |
| **Ski passes** (Epic, Ikon) | Cheaper tiers carry blackout dates. The full-access tier costs much more. | Keep Friday and Saturday out of the base tier and offer them in a pricier one. |
| **Rail off-peak cards and airline fare buckets** | A discount works only on trains and seats that would run empty. Fares are allocated per departure. | Open only the empty midweek seats, and set the number for each performance. |
| **ClassPass** | Credits, not unlimited use. Each venue decides how many spots to offer. Busier classes cost more credits. | A credit system gives you a ceiling per performance. It also helps with the half-face rule. |
| **Gyms** | Annual terms, or a minimum period. Peak times are managed by booking caps. | Offer a membership year or a 3-month minimum instead of "cancel any time". Add a late-cancellation rule, because your no-show rate on free tickets is 14% against 3% on paid ones. |

## How I would set it up

**Thrale Midweek, pilot season.**

- **Product:** a monthly fee that buys credits, with each credit worth at least half the face value of the seat it redeems. At €34 average face value, that is about €17 a credit. A monthly fee of €15 for an entry only works if midweek face value is €30 or less. Check your actual midweek prices. Extra tickets are available at a member price that never falls below half face value.
- **Where members can book:** Tuesday to Thursday, where occupancy is 49%. That is about 265 empty seats per performance. Fridays and Saturdays open to members only in a last-minute standby window, for example 24 hours ahead, and only if the seats are unsold.
- **Allocation:** hold member seats to a fixed number per performance, set below the 20-seat cap so some stays for other comps. Set it from your sell-through data, not from the membership total.
- **No-shows:** a credit is lost if the member doesn't come and doesn't cancel by an agreed time.
- **Size:** limit the pilot to a few hundred members, with a waiting list. Don't aim for thousands of members in year one.
- **Protect what you have:** keep the Season Seven subscription as it is. Offer the 1,800 frequent attenders a plan that suits them, such as a priority tier. Don't expect them to give up peak nights.
- **Measure:** the share of members who are new to you, the share of midweek credits that go to people who used to buy tickets, and the effect on Friday and Saturday sales.

For scale, here is an illustration. Suppose midweek is about 100 of your 250 performances. That is my guess, so please replace it with your actual count. That would leave around 26,500 empty midweek seats. Selling half of them at an average of €17 would bring in about €225,000 a season. It would also bring new faces into the house without touching the peak nights that already sell out.

## Where the analogies are weakest

- Theatre demand differs by production. A hit show fills on a Tuesday too, and a weak one doesn't fill on a Saturday. So the midweek-only rule should be tested against the individual shows.
- A member's seat has no use to them unless the show appeals. A gym or a rail card is used regardless of the content.
- I'm treating the 1,800 as one group that all behaves alike. They won't. The first-year data will show which of them switch.
- I don't know your actual midweek prices or how many of your 250 performances are midweek. Both change the figures above.

## Before the board meets

1. Get the rights holders' answer on royalty and the half-face rule.
2. Pull midweek face prices and the count of Tuesday to Thursday performances.
3. Check how many of the 1,800 attend midweek today. That is the cannibalisation you would face.
4. Bring Maren a pilot proposal, not a rejection. Her instinct for steady monthly income and for reaching new audiences is sound. The structure needs to change.

My confidence is fairly high that unlimited at €15 loses money for you. It is moderate that the midweek credit design is the best alternative, because the royalty reading and the midweek data could change it.
