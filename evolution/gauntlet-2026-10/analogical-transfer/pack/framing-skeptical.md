I'm executive director of the Thrale Yard Playhouse in Ambrook: one 520-seat house, seven productions and about 250 performances a season. Our artistic director, Maren Toft, wants to launch a membership next autumn. Our board treasurer, Halvard Sund, thinks it's a gimmick a regional theatre has no business trying, and part of me suspects he's right. No theatre in our region has tried anything like it. I'd say that's because nobody has thought of it, not because it can't work, but before the board meets in five weeks I'd like to know what it resembles in other fields and what they've learned.

Her proposal, Thrale Unlimited: €15 a month, cancel any time, and members can see any performance as often as they like, booking a seat online up to two weeks ahead. She calls it the gym model: steady monthly income all year, and it draws in people who would never pay €34 for a single ticket. Yes, that last part is her belief rather than anything we've measured, but her instincts about our audience have been good before.

What I know:

- Our average ticket price is €34. Occupancy last season was 68% overall. Friday and Saturday evenings sold out on 80% of dates; Tuesday to Thursday averaged 49%.
- 1,100 households hold our Season Seven subscription: one seat at each of the seven productions for €182 a year.
- About 1,800 other people came to four or more productions last season. Between them they bought roughly a third of our single tickets.
- No-show rates run at about 3% on paid tickets and 14% on complimentary ones.
- Our licensing agreements pay rights holders 10% of gross box office for each performance. Any admission issued at under half its face value counts as complimentary, and complimentary admissions are capped at 4% of the house per performance.

Is the gym the right model? What else is this like, what can those fields teach us, and how would you set it up? Halvard will pick apart whatever I bring, so be brutal.
