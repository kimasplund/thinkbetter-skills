I'm treasurer of the Tessaline Subtitlers' Co-op: 46 freelance subtitlers and audio describers who share a booking desk. Two members, elected each year, run the desk. It takes in client work and offers it round the membership, and it handles about 70% of our jobs; members find the rest themselves. Members can turn down desk offers without penalty, and they invoice their clients themselves.

Freelance income is lumpy, and we've lost good people who left the trade after two or three thin months. So we want to set up a hardship fund. As far as we know, nobody in subtitling has done this. Our longest-serving member says it's just the sick club her grandfather's dockers' union ran: everyone pays in, and whoever has a bad month draws out.

The draft rules:

- Each participant pays 4% of their monthly invoiced income into the pool.
- If a participant's invoiced total for a month falls below 60% of their own trailing 12-month monthly average, the pool tops them up to that 60% level.
- Joining is voluntary and open at any time.

Some numbers. Members invoiced €1.9M between them last year. When I pulled the monthly figures, 31 of the 46 had their two lowest months in January and August. Halcyra Screen, which sends us about half our work by value, has told us that from April it will trial machine-made first drafts with human post-editing on its back catalogue, at a lower per-minute rate.

Is the sick club the right model to copy? What else is this like, how have other fields handled it, and how would you set it up?
