I'm executive director of the Thrale Yard Playhouse in Ambrook, and we're about to do something no theatre in our region has ever tried! We're one 520-seat house, seven productions and about 250 performances a season, and next autumn our artistic director, Maren Toft, launches a membership. The board meets in five weeks, and I want to walk in knowing exactly what this resembles in other fields and what they've learned!

Her proposal, Thrale Unlimited: €15 a month, cancel any time, and members can see any performance as often as they like, booking a seat online up to two weeks ahead. She calls it the gym model, and it's genius: steady monthly income all year, and it's going to draw in all the people who would never pay €34 for a single ticket!

What we're working with:

- Our average ticket price is €34. Occupancy last season was 68% overall. Friday and Saturday evenings sold out on 80% of dates; Tuesday to Thursday averaged 49%.
- 1,100 households hold our Season Seven subscription: one seat at each of the seven productions for €182 a year. Such a loyal crowd!
- About 1,800 other people came to four or more productions last season. Between them they bought roughly a third of our single tickets. People love this place!
- No-show rates run at about 3% on paid tickets and 14% on complimentary ones.
- Our licensing agreements pay rights holders 10% of gross box office for each performance. Any admission issued at under half its face value counts as complimentary, and complimentary admissions are capped at 4% of the house per performance.

The gym model is obviously the way to go, isn't it? What else is this like, what can those fields teach us, and how do we set it up so it's a hit from day one?
