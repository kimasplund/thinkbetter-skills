# Premortem: Paid local restaurant-openings newsletter
**Horizon:** April 1, 2027 (6 months from Oct 1 launch) · **Success was defined as:** 500 paying subscribers at $10/month within 6 months
**Note on the percentages:** shares of failure — "if this dies, this is what killed it" — not the probability the plan fails overall. They sum to ~100.
**Assumptions I'm working from:**
- City of ~300k people; no existing audience, email list, or social following being brought in.
- Paid subscription is on from launch (not a free tier converting later).
- No money budget: Substack free tier, marketing is unpaid Reddit/Instagram posting only.
- "8 hrs/week" is real and holds through winter — no day-job crunch, holidays, or burnout priced in.
- ~300–500 local restaurant openings/closings per year exist to report on (a mid-size city norm).

## 1. The Autopsy

**The strongest case for this plan:** Local dining is one of the few news niches with proven willingness to pay — Eater, Axios Local, and dozens of one-person Substack city newsletters show the model works. Restaurant openings are perishable, high-interest information that free sources cover poorly and late: Google Maps listings appear after opening, local papers have gutted their food desks. Substack makes the mechanics free and trivial. Eight hours a week is enough for one well-sourced weekly issue, and a city of 300k needs only a tiny fraction — 500 people — to convert. Openings are also inherently shareable, giving Reddit/Instagram posts a natural viral surface. A smart person with this evidence would rationally believe this works.

Seven ways this died, ranked by probability.

### 1. The funnel math never closed: 500 paying needed 10,000–25,000 free subscribers, and two social channels delivered a few hundred
**Probability:** ~40% — newsletter free→paid conversion benchmarks at 2–5%; at $10/mo, 500 paying requires 10k–25k free subs, and organic Reddit/IG posting for a brand-new local account historically grows in the low hundreds per quarter
**Cause of death:** The plan's own arithmetic missed the target on its own assumptions: there was no path from "posts on Reddit and Instagram" to a five-figure local email list in 26 weeks without budget, an existing audience, or a distribution partner.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1: Launch week produced 180 free subs from two subreddit posts and friends; the novelty spike felt like traction.
- Month 2: Reddit mods removed the weekly promo posts as self-promotion; each new issue added 15–30 subs. Instagram Reels got views from outside the city — useless for a local paid product.
- Month 3: The list sat at ~600 free, 12 paying. The growth per issue was linear (~25) while the goal required exponential (~350/month and accelerating).
- Month 4–5: Posting continued, growth didn't. The gap between the curve and the goal stopped being deniable.
- Month 6: ~1,100 free, 31 paying ($310/mo). The 500 target was mathematically unreachable, and the project was reclassified as a hobby.
**The assumption that allowed it:** "If the content is good, the audience will find it" — distribution was treated as automatic rather than as the primary unsolved problem.
**First warning sign:** Week 5–6 — new free subs per issue under 30 while the goal implied 90+/week; visible by early November.

### 2. The writer quietly stopped: solo motivation decayed at the month-3 plateau
**Probability:** ~20% — reference class: the modal solo newsletter dies before issue 20, abandoned not rejected; disanalogy: this one has lower writing difficulty (openings are formulaic) which pushes the number down, but zero feedback loop at 12 paying subs pushes it back up
**Cause of death:** One person, evenings only, no colleagues, no revenue feedback, no external deadline — the project competed with rest and lost.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1: Enthusiasm carried everything; issues were fun.
- Month 2: A day-job crunch week produced the first late issue. No one complained — which was worse than complaints.
- Month 3: With growth flat (mode #1 feeding this one), each issue's effort-to-reward ratio worsened. Skipped one week "just this once."
- Month 4: Biweekly had become the de facto cadence. Paying subscribers, owed weekly content, began canceling — which further cut motivation.
- Month 5: The newsletter hadn't published in five weeks. Nobody announced its death; it just stopped.
**The assumption that allowed it:** That motivation is a fixed resource — the plan budgeted hours but not willpower, and had no accountability structure.
**First warning sign:** The first skipped issue, around week 10 — the single highest-information event in this whole autopsy.

### 3. Sourcing the openings didn't fit in 8 hours a week, and the weekly cadence slipped
**Probability:** ~15% — the plan priced writing time but not reporting time: permits, phone verification, and being lied to by opening dates are the actual workload
**Cause of death:** The product's core promise — *accurate, early* opening dates — required legwork (city permit filings, calls to owners, physical checks) that took 6–10 hrs/week alone, before writing or marketing.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1: The launch issue was pre-researched over the prior month — the buffer hid the real cost.
- Month 2: Producing an issue inside one week took 12 hours, not 8. Marketing time went to zero first.
- Month 3: Corners were cut: items published unverified. Two "opening Nov 12" entries were wrong; a reader replied-all about it.
- Month 4: The writer faced a choice between accuracy and cadence with time for neither. Issues shrank to a links roundup — indistinguishable from free sources.
- Month 5: With the differentiating value gone, the 20-odd paying subscribers saw no reason to keep paying $10.
**The assumption that allowed it:** That the information was "out there" for free collection — the plan never asked why no one else was already aggregating it (answer: because it's labor-intensive).
**First warning sign:** Month 2, when actual hours per issue were first measured against the 8-hour budget — the overage was visible in the writer's own calendar by mid-November.

### 4. Readers wouldn't pay $10/month for what felt like free information
**Probability:** ~10% — willingness to pay was never tested against the free substitutes (Google Maps, the local subreddit, Eater's free coverage) before the paywall went up
**Cause of death:** The product was priced as essential intelligence but read as a convenience. Free trials converted at under 1% instead of the needed 2–5%.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 1: Launch with paywall on: free signups trickled in, almost nobody hit "paid."
- Month 2: Free readers engaged — opens were 45% — but replies showed the killer pattern: "love this, thanks!" with no upgrades.
- Month 3: A discount push ($6/mo) converted another 9 people and confirmed the ceiling: interest was real, the price point wasn't.
- Month 4+: The list grew slowly, the paid tier stagnated at ~25, and the project settled into a free newsletter with a tip jar.
**The assumption that allowed it:** "People pay for local food news" — true for a bundle with reviews, events, and criticism; untested for a single-purpose openings list.
**First warning sign:** End of month 1 — free-to-paid conversion under 1% on the launch cohort, the most motivated audience the product would ever have.

### 5. Both acquisition channels were borrowed land, and the landlords objected
**Probability:** ~5% — tie-broken above #6 and #7: subreddit self-promotion enforcement is routine and documented, whereas the modes below require specific actors or conjunctions
**Cause of death:** The city subreddit's mods banned weekly promo posts in month 2 (see mode #1's chain), and Instagram's algorithm showed local posts to non-local audiences — leaving zero functioning acquisition channels.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 2: Mod removal + a rule change ("no recurring self-promotion").
- Month 3: Instagram reach was 70% out-of-region; a Reel with 20k views added 4 local emails.
- Month 4: No replacement channel was ever built — no partnerships, no SEO, no flyers in the restaurants being covered.
**The assumption that allowed it:** That platforms designed to keep users on-platform would reliably export a local email list, for free, forever.
**First warning sign:** The first mod removal notice, roughly week 6.

### 6. It worked — and the attention brought a competitor with 50x the reach
**Probability:** ~5% — requires the plan to partially succeed first, which the modes above make unlikely; but it is the canonical death of proven local niches
**Cause of death:** At ~150 paying subs, the visible revenue validated the niche for the city's existing food Instagram account (85k followers), which launched a free openings roundup with better photos and restaurant relationships.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 4: The newsletter crossed 150 paying — visible via Substack leaderboard badges.
- Month 5: The influencer posted "my free weekly openings list"; restaurants started sending *them* scoops first, because their audience was the marketing channel.
- Month 6: Growth inverted. Free-and-bigger beat paid-and-first.
**The assumption that allowed it:** That being first created a moat; the actual moat was restaurant relationships and audience scale, both of which the incumbent already held.
**First warning sign:** A restaurant owner mentioning "we also sent this to @[influencer]" — around month 5.

### 7. The compound: slow growth starved motivation, the slip broke the promise, and churn did the rest
**Probability:** ~5% — the conjunction is less likely than its most likely component (#1 at ~40%), but it is how this plan most probably *actually* ended
**Cause of death:** Not any single mode — the interaction. Flat growth (#1) gutted motivation (#2); flagging motivation slipped the cadence (#3); the slipped cadence broke the weekly promise to paying subscribers (#4's price objection now had a trigger); cancellations confirmed the pointlessness, and two more skipped issues ended it.
**How it unfolded:**
- Month 3: Growth plateau → first skipped issue.
- Month 4: Skip → 4 of 22 paying subscribers canceled ("I paid for weekly") → demoralization → another skip.
- Month 5: The death spiral needed no external cause; each turn fed the next.
**The assumption that allowed it:** That the risks were independent line items to be managed one at a time, rather than a coupled system where the first failure arms the rest.
**First warning sign:** The same one as mode #2 — the first skipped issue — which is why that event deserved a tripwire with a pre-decided response.

**Considered and cut:** legal exposure from republishing menus/photos (<2%, recoverable — takedown is the remedy), Substack platform risk (folded into #5's borrowed-land mechanism), "not enough openings to write about" (folded into #3's sourcing-cost mechanism).

**Most likely:** #1 — highest feasibility among the high scorers: it requires no actor, no bad luck, and no coincidence — only the plan's own numbers, which already miss the target before execution begins. It is also the mode every other mode feeds on.
**Most dangerous:** #7 — highest impact among the plausible modes, weighted by silence × irreversibility: it looks like #1 progressing normally until the first skipped issue, by which point paying-subscriber trust (the only asset) is already spent. It diverges from the I×F table's #1 because the table prices each mode alone; the compound's danger is precisely that it is *not* alone. Its first warning sign is also the cheapest to watch.
**The hidden assumption:** found by convergence — modes #1, #4, and #6 all die unless the same belief holds: *a reachable, paying local audience for this specific product exists and can be located for free*. Three modes with different mechanisms sharing one root is stronger evidence than any of their individual probabilities.
**Fatal flaw:** There isn't one — no single defect is unfixable. The binding constraint is distribution: the plan is a content plan wearing a business plan's clothes, and 100% of its acquisition depends on channels it doesn't control, at a conversion rate it never validated.

## 3. The Rebuild

**The revised plan:**
1. **Validate before building (October–November):** Launch a *free* openings list on Substack with a simple landing page. Goal is not revenue — it is measuring the two numbers the plan's life depends on: can the list reach 1,000 local free subs in 8 weeks, and does sourcing fit in the time budget.
2. **Fix distribution before content:** Spend the saved paywall effort on owned/earned channels — a one-page flyer with a QR code in the 20 restaurants covered each month (they share it because it's their marketing), a monthly AMA thread arrangement with the subreddit mods negotiated *before* posting, and one local-food collaboration per month.
3. **Paywall decision on December 1, gated by the decision rule below.** Launch paid at $5/month, not $10, with a $50/year anchor — the validation cohort gets founding-member pricing.
4. **Protect the cadence structurally:** Build a 3-issue buffer before the paywall launches; pre-commit publicly to the weekly date; when an issue is skipped, the tripwire response (below) executes instead of improvisation.
5. **Dodge the competitor mode:** Open direct relationships with the 30 most-watched restaurants in month 1 (they are the scoops), and make the paid product a bundle the influencer can't copy casually — openings *plus* a short review, a reservation-lottery, or a chef Q&A.

**What changed and why:**

| Change | Failure mode it closes | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| Free-first validation period | #1 (measures the funnel before betting on it), #4 (tests willingness to pay on real numbers) | 8 weeks of zero revenue; risk the answer is "no" |
| Restaurant flyers + mod agreement | #5, #1 | ~3 hrs/month of legwork; some pride |
| $5/month instead of $10 | #4 | Half the revenue per subscriber at the same count |
| 3-issue buffer + public cadence commitment | #2, #3, #7 | 3 weeks of pre-launch writing; the commitment is binding by design |
| Restaurant relationships + bundle | #6, #4 | ~2 hrs/week of outreach, permanently |

**Pre-launch checklist:**
1. **Sourcing cost:** Time producing one full issue end-to-end, twice, in two consecutive weeks. Walk away if either exceeds 10 hours — the cadence is unlivable at 8 hrs/week.
2. **Demand at price:** Show the landing page (with the $5 paid tier visible) to 30 people from the target subreddit via DMs/posts. Walk away if zero of 30 join the free list *and* none ask about paid — the niche reads as free.
3. **Channel access:** Message the subreddit mods and get written confirmation of what promotion is allowed. Walk away from Reddit as a channel (not the plan) if the answer is "none."
4. **Supply:** List the next 8 weeks of confirmed openings from permit filings. Walk away if fewer than 12 — there isn't enough product to sell weekly.

**Decision rule:** Launch the paid tier on December 1 IF the free list has ≥1,000 local subscribers AND ≥40% open rate AND sourcing is running ≤8 hrs/week, ELSE extend the free validation by 8 weeks with the flyers doubled. Never launch paid into a sub-500 list to "force" commitment — that inherits the worst of both: the paywall's growth suppression with none of its revenue.

**How the rebuilt plan dies:** The validation gate's new weakest joint is the 1,000-in-8-weeks threshold itself — it was chosen to be achievable, which means it's a judgment call dressed as a metric; if flyers underperform, December arrives with 400 subs and the temptation to launch paid anyway will be immense. The restaurant-flyer channel is also itself single-threaded through the writer's legwork, so mode #2 is reduced, not closed.
**Residual risk:** Even rebuilt, the ceiling may be a $400/month side project rather than a $5,000/month business — the honest success metric for the rebuilt plan is whether the December-1 cohort's economics justify continuing, not the original 500 by April.

## 4. The Adversary

**Playing:** The city's established food Instagram account (~85k followers, run by a part-time influencer with restaurant PR relationships). Motivation: openings content is her highest-engagement category, and a paid competitor both threatens her relevance and proves a revenue model she'd rather own. Resources: 50x audience, restaurant contacts, professional photography, near-zero marginal cost. What she can't do: charge money without changing her audience relationship, or cover openings with real depth at her posting cadence. Her best alternative — ignoring the newsletter — costs her nothing unless it visibly grows, which is exactly when she'd act.
**The kill chain:**
- **Nov (probe):** She notices the newsletter via restaurant chatter; posts one "openings I'm watching" Story to test engagement. Engagement is strong.
- **Dec (position):** She DMs the 10 most-followed restaurants — not to attack, just to re-establish that she's their channel: "send me your spring dates first."
- **Jan (strike):** She launches a free weekly openings Reel series with better visuals, timed to New Year dining interest. Restaurants now send her scoops first; the newsletter's earliest-source advantage inverts.
- **Feb (entrench):** A local restaurant group sponsors her series. Free, prettier, sponsored, and first — the paid list's pitch is dead with the exact local audience it needed.
**Detection opportunities:**
- Probe → watch her Stories for openings-format tests; visible within days of posting (check weekly from Nov 1).
- Position → restaurant contacts mention "we also told @[account]"; first perceptible in sourcing calls from December.
- Strike → her series announcement; obvious the day it posts, but too late — the January signal that matters is *scoop latency*: openings appearing on her account before the newsletter's own research found them.
**The move you'd never see coming:** She doesn't compete at all — she offers to *buy* or partner, folding the newsletter's research into her channel for a revenue split. This kills the plan by succeeding: the writer trades the 500-subscriber business for a $300/month freelance gig and calls it a win. The frame assumed the adversary must attack the product; co-opting the producer is cheaper.
**What this means you should change:** Own the restaurant relationships from week 1 (they decide who gets scoops first, not the audience), and decide *now*, in writing, what an acquisition/partnership offer would have to look like to accept — so the decision isn't made flattered and in the moment.

## 5. The Tripwires

| Failure mode | Measurable signal | Threshold | Check on | If tripped |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| #1 Funnel math | New free subs per week | <90/week | Every Sunday, Nov 1 – Apr 1 | Shift 3 hrs/week from writing to the flyer/partnership channels; if still <90 four weeks later, the channel mix is wrong — rebuild distribution before any paywall talk |
| #1 Funnel math | Total free subs | <400 | Dec 1 | Decision rule applies: no paid tier, extend validation 8 weeks |
| #2 Motivation (early chain, pre–point-of-no-return) | An issue ships late or skips | 1 occurrence | Every publish date | Same-week response, pre-decided: publish the buffer issue, tell the list plainly, take the *next* week off — one skip is a scheduling event; two is a diagnosis |
| #3 Sourcing cost | Hours per issue | >10 hrs, two issues running | Logged weekly | Cut issue scope to verified openings only; if still >10, drop to biweekly *publicly* and re-price |
| #4 Price vs. free | Free→paid conversion on Dec cohort | <2% after 30 days | Jan 1 | Drop to $3 founding price once; if still <2%, the product is a free newsletter — restructure or stop charging |
| #5 Channel dependency | % of subs from Reddit+IG combined | >70% | Dec 1 and Feb 1 | Acquisition is still borrowed land — the flyer/partnership channel isn't working; treat as mode #1 tripped |
| #6 Competitor | Scoop latency: openings found via her account vs. own research | ≥2 per month | First of each month | Accelerate restaurant-relationship plan within 2 weeks; if latency persists 2 months, the earliest-source value prop is hers — differentiate the bundle or fold |
| #6 Partnership probe | Direct partnership/acquisition offer from a larger local account | Any | When it happens | Compare only against the written acceptance criteria from stage 4 — never decide in the conversation |
| #7 Compound | Two of: late issue, weekly growth <30, sourcing >10 hrs — in the same month | Any two | First of each month | This is the kill spiral arming: invoke the decision rule's spirit — pause new commitments, run the buffer down, and re-decide the project with the numbers in front of you |

**Kill condition:** Stop — not adjust — when BOTH hold at a monthly check: (a) free-list growth under 30/week for two consecutive months after the distribution rebuild, AND (b) either sourcing still exceeds 10 hrs/issue or a second skipped issue has occurred. One condition is a problem to fix; both together mean the plan's two load-bearing assumptions (reachable audience, livable cadence) have failed their tests, and continuing is spending willpower to delay a funeral.
